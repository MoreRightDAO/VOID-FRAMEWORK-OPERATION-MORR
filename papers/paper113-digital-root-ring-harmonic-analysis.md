## Abstract

The digital root ring DR₉ = ℤ/9ℤ — the ring of residue classes underlying base-10 digital root arithmetic — admits a natural embedding into the unit circle S¹ via additive characters χ_k(n) = e^(2πikn/9). We prove that the additive complement involution φ₉: n ↦ (9−n) mod 9 acts as exact complex conjugation on this character group: χ_k(φ₉(n)) = χ_k(n)* for all k, n. This result is both analytic (proved by direct substitution) and computational (verified to machine precision). We establish a three-class unit group filtration of DR₉ — unit class U₉ = (ℤ/9ℤ)*, zero-divisor class D₉ = {3,6}, and kernel class K₉ = {0} — with algebraically distinct properties governing invertibility, character support, and Gauss sum behavior. For primitive Dirichlet characters mod 9, we verify |τ(χ)|² = 9, with all primitive Gauss sums lying on a circle of radius 3 = √9 in ℂ. We show that D₉ is the support-zero class for all primitive characters — a structural harmonic blind spot. Framework implications are derived for opacity measurement reversibility, Pe-governed coherent output bounds, and the algebraic basis of constraint-binding asymmetry. The connection between the complement involution and the functional equation of conductor-9 L-functions is established. We introduce an explicit structural naming for the six characters of DR₉: the White Queen (χ_j, complex), the Red King (χ̄_j, conjugate), and the Rebis (χ_j · χ̄_j = 1, always real, Pe=0). A new result identifies χ₃ as the self-conjugate character (χ₃ = χ̄₃, τ(χ₃) = 0) — the algebraic signature of voluntary constraint reduction, corresponding to the Bird of Hermes in the Ripley Scroll tradition (c. 1471). The critical line Re(s)=½ is identified as the V* boundary of the conductor-9 L-function system. Six falsifiable predictions are stated.

## II. The Digital Root Ring: Structure and Filtration

### 2.1 Ring Structure

DR₉ is a commutative ring with unity. Its additive group (DR₉, +) ≅ ℤ/9ℤ is cyclic of order 9. The multiplicative structure is more refined. The invertible elements form the unit group:

$$U_9 = (\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z})^* = \{n \in \{1,...,8\} : \gcd(n,9) = 1\} = \{1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8\}$$

This group has order φ(9) = 6 and is cyclic, generated by g = 2: powers of 2 mod 9 are {1,2,4,8,7,5}. The non-unit elements decompose by gcd into two classes:

$$D_9 = \{n : \gcd(n,9) = 3\} = \{3, 6\}$$
$$K_9 = \{n : \gcd(n,9) = 9\} = \{0\}$$

**Definition 1 (Unit Group Filtration).** We call U₉, D₉, K₉ the *unit class*, *zero-divisor class*, and *kernel class* of DR₉ respectively.

**Proposition 1.** The three classes are algebraically distinct:
- U₉ is closed under multiplication and every element is invertible.
- D₉ consists of zero divisors: 3·3 = 9 ≡ 0 and 6·6 = 36 ≡ 0 mod 9.
- K₉ = {0} is the additive identity with no multiplicative inverse.

*Proof.* Direct computation. For D₉: gcd(3,9)=3≠1, so 3 has no inverse mod 9; 3·3=9≡0 confirms zero-divisor status. □

### 2.2 The Complement Involution

**Definition 2.** The *complement involution* φ₉: DR₉ → DR₉ is defined by φ₉(n) = (9 − n) mod 9.

Explicitly: φ₉: 0↦0, 1↦8, 2↦7, 3↦6, 4↦5, 5↦4, 6↦3, 7↦2, 8↦1.

φ₉ is an involution (φ₉∘φ₉ = id) that preserves class membership: φ₉(U₉) = U₉, φ₉(D₉) = D₉, φ₉(K₉) = K₉. It is fixed-point-free on U₉ and D₉, and fixes K₉.

## III. Harmonic Analysis on DR₉

### 3.1 Additive Characters

The Pontryagin dual of (DR₉, +) ≅ ℤ/9ℤ is itself. The character group consists of 9 additive characters:

$$\chi_k : \mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z} \to S^1, \quad \chi_k(n) = e^{2\pi i k n / 9}, \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, 8$$

These form an orthonormal basis for functions f: DR₉ → ℂ under ⟨f,g⟩ = (1/9)Σ_n f(n)g(n)*. The Discrete Fourier Transform on DR₉:

$$\hat{f}(k) = \sum_{n=0}^{8} f(n) \, e^{-2\pi i k n / 9}$$

### 3.2 The Complement Conjugacy Theorem

**Theorem 1 (Complement Conjugacy).** For all k ∈ {0,...,8} and all n ∈ DR₉:

$$\chi_k(\varphi_9(n)) = \overline{\chi_k(n)}$$

*Proof.* Direct substitution:
$$\chi_k(\varphi_9(n)) = e^{2\pi i k (9-n)/9} = e^{2\pi i k} \cdot e^{-2\pi i kn/9} = 1 \cdot \overline{e^{2\pi i kn/9}} = \overline{\chi_k(n)}$$
where e^(2πik) = 1 for integer k. □

**Corollary 1.** If F = DFT(f), then DFT(f ∘ φ₉)(k) = conj(F[9−k]). The complement involution acts as frequency-reversal with conjugation on the Fourier domain.

**Corollary 2.** Complement pairs sum to real values: χ_k(n) + χ_k(φ₉(n)) = 2·Re(χ_k(n)) ∈ ℝ.

**Numerical verification.** DFT of f(n) = φ₉(n) computed over n=0,...,8. Conjugation symmetry F[k] = conj(F[9−k]) verified for all k=1,...,8 with maximum error 1.78×10⁻¹⁵ (machine precision). Computed values:

- F[0] = 36.0 (DC component; equals Σ φ₉(n) = 0+8+7+6+5+4+3+2+1 = 36)
- F[k] = −4.5 ± (imaginary part), conjugate-symmetric for k=1,...,8

The DC component is purely real and positive. All non-DC components have Re(F[k]) = −4.5 < 0 — the oscillating modes carry negative real-part spectral weight. The complement function's only positive-real spectral contribution is the coherent (in-phase) DC term.

## IV. Multiplicative Structure: Dirichlet Characters and Gauss Sums

### 4.1 Dirichlet Characters mod 9

Since U₉ is cyclic of order 6 with generator g=2, the 6 multiplicative characters of U₉ are:

$$\chi_j(2^k) = e^{2\pi i j k / 6}, \quad j = 0, 1, \ldots, 5, \quad k = 0, \ldots, 5$$

Extended to DR₉ by χ_j(n) = 0 for n ∉ U₉.

**Primitivity.** Characters that factor through ℤ/3ℤ (conductor 3 or 1) are imprimitive. These satisfy χ_j³ = χ₀, i.e., j ∈ {0,3}. Primitive characters of conductor exactly 9: j ∈ {1,2,4,5}.

### 4.2 Primitive Character Support

**Proposition 2 (Support Theorem).** All primitive Dirichlet characters mod 9 vanish identically on D₉ = {3,6}:

$$\chi_j(3) = \chi_j(6) = 0 \quad \text{for all } j \in \{1,2,4,5\}$$

*Proof.* By definition, χ_j(n) = 0 when gcd(n,9) > 1. Since gcd(3,9) = gcd(6,9) = 3 > 1, the result follows. □

The zero-divisor class D₉ is the *harmonic blind spot* of DR₉: no primitive conductor-9 character assigns nonzero weight to D₉. Measurement methods based on primitive-character spectral analysis are structurally insensitive to D₉ regardless of the true signal strength at those elements.

## IX. Limitations

**L1 — Base specificity.** All results are specific to base-10 digital root arithmetic (DR₉ = ℤ/(10−1)ℤ = ℤ/9ℤ). Extension to base-b requires analysis of ℤ/(b−1)ℤ with its own unit group structure.

**L2 — Additive vs. multiplicative characters.** Theorem 1 is proved for additive characters χ_k. For multiplicative Dirichlet characters: χ_j(−n) = χ_j(−1)·χ_j(n) — a sign-weighted copy, not complex conjugation. The two results are complementary but not interchangeable.

**L3 — GRH dependence.** Prediction P1 assumes GRH for the specific conductor-9 L-functions. Without GRH, the zero-pairing structure could still hold from the functional equation alone; the off-critical-line case requires the full GRH assertion.

**L4 — ICC dataset.** Prediction P3 is theoretical. No stratified analysis of ICC acceptance rates by Pe residue class has been performed. The prediction is stated but unverified.

**L5 — Conductor-9 zeros.** No computation of the first non-trivial zeros of L(s,χ_j) for conductor 9 was performed here. Experiment C-full (requiring PARI/GP or SageMath) is deferred to follow-up work.
