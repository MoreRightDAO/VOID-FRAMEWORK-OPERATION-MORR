## Abstract

Mycorrhizal networks — the subterranean fungal hyphal systems that physically interconnect the root systems of forest trees — constitute one of the most spatially extensive and biologically consequential interaction networks on Earth, colonizing over 90% of fine root tips in boreal forests (Tedersoo et al., 2020, *Molecular Ecology*) and mediating nutrient exchange across an estimated 5 billion hectares of forested land globally (Steidinger et al., 2019, *Nature*). Despite three decades of intensive research following Simard et al.'s (1997, *Nature*) foundational demonstration of bidirectional carbon transfer between Douglas fir and paper birch via ectomycorrhizal (ECM) linkages, the functional significance of common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs) for forest ecosystem dynamics remains deeply contested. This paper applies the Void Framework to mycorrhizal network science, forest management practice, and the "wood wide web" public narrative, analyzing how the convergence of extreme below-ground opacity, context-dependent responsiveness spanning mutualism to parasitism, and obligate biotic coupling between fungal and plant partners generates emergent dynamics that parallel void conditions identified in engineered sociotechnical systems.

We evaluate both arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) and ectomycorrhizal (ECM) network types on the 12-point Void Index, scoring the biological system itself and, separately, the knowledge-management interface through which mycorrhizal science enters policy and public discourse. The biological network scores O = 2.5, R = 2.5, C = 3.0, with modifiers of 1.5, yielding a composite Void Index of 9.5/12. The Péclet-analogous parameter, estimated from the ratio of advective nutrient transport rates through hyphal networks (approximately 1–10 cm per day; Finlay and Read, 1986, *New Phytologist*) to the diffusive information dissipation rate in the surrounding soil matrix, yields Pe ≈ 3.2 for the biological system, placing it in Phase II (Fluid) with localized Phase III (Crystal) domains where network density is highest. The sociotechnical narrative system surrounding mycorrhizal networks scores higher: O = 3.0, R = 2.5, C = 2.5, modifiers = 2.0, for a Void Index of 10.0/12 and Pe ≈ 4.3, crossing the Phase IV Pandemonium threshold.

The paper makes the following numbered contributions:

1. It provides the first systematic Void Framework assessment of a biological network system, demonstrating that the framework's tripartite opacity-responsiveness-coupling structure applies to natural systems that lack intentional design or profit motive.

2. It quantifies the opacity condition of mycorrhizal science by documenting that fewer than 5% of published CMN studies employ direct in situ visualization of hyphal transport pathways, with the remainder relying on isotopic proxy methods subject to alternative sink-source explanations (Karst et al., 2023, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*).

3. It characterizes the responsiveness spectrum using Hoeksema et al.'s (2010, *Ecology Letters*) meta-analysis of 614 studies reporting plant biomass responses to mycorrhizal colonization ranging from −50% to +300%, with a mean of +26% and variance structures that preclude reliable prediction at the individual-interaction level.

4. It formalizes the coupling condition through Tedersoo et al.'s (2020) colonization data and documents how near-universal root tip colonization creates obligate dependence that satisfies the engaged-observer criterion.

5. It provides the first rigorous L1/L2/L3 drift cascade analysis of the "wood wide web" metaphor, tracing the lexical trajectory from technical mycorrhizal terminology (L1) through anthropomorphic metaphor (L2: "mother trees," "nurturing") to full entity projection (L3: forests as intentional cooperative superorganisms).

6. It evaluates the policy harm pathway through which L3 narrative drift influenced British Columbia's forest management revisions (2020–2023) mandating "mother tree" retention, policies subsequently challenged on evidentiary grounds by Landhäusser et al. (2023, *New Forests*).

7. It identifies constraint-pole conditions — transparent isotope methodology, invariant measurement protocols, and independent replication — under which mycorrhizal research achieves Void Index scores at or below 4/12, using Robinson and Fitter's (1999, *Oecologia*) early methodological critique as a control case.

8. It proposes a Void Model Card template for mycorrhizal network claims that separates verified transport observations from inferred ecological function, providing a standardized disclosure mechanism for future research and policy communication.

The central argument is that mycorrhizal networks are not uniquely problematic as biological systems, but that the combination of genuine below-ground opacity with intense public and scientific engagement has created a sociotechnical void in which metaphor substitutes for mechanism, narrative momentum outpaces evidentiary warrant, and management interventions propagate through policy channels before the underlying science achieves replicable consensus. The Void Framework provides the analytical architecture to diagnose this condition and specify remediation pathways.

## I. Introduction

Beneath every temperate and boreal forest lies a network of extraordinary complexity. Mycorrhizal fungi — obligate symbionts that colonize plant roots and extend hyphal filaments through soil — connect individual trees into common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs) spanning hectares of forest floor. These subterranean architectures mediate the transfer of carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, water, and chemical signals between plants, shaping competitive hierarchies, seedling establishment, and community composition across biomes that cover approximately 4.04 billion hectares of the Earth's land surface (FAO, 2020). The economic value of global forest ecosystem services has been estimated at USD 16.2 trillion per year (Costanza et al., 2014, Global Environmental Change), while the timber industry alone generates roughly USD 600 billion annually (FAO, 2022). Mycorrhizal associations underpin essentially all of this value: over 90% of vascular plant species form mycorrhizal partnerships (Brundrett & Tedersoo, 2019, New Phytologist), and in boreal forests specifically, more than 90% of fine root tips are colonized by ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi (Tedersoo et al., 2020, Molecular Ecology). Any framework applied to forest management that fails to reckon with these networks is incomplete. Yet the dominant feature of mycorrhizal networks, from both a biological and an epistemological standpoint, is that they are almost entirely invisible.

This paper applies the Void Framework — a diagnostic system for identifying predictable harm patterns arising from the co-occurrence of opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and engaged observer coupling (C) — to the domain of mycorrhizal network science and its translation into forest policy. The central argument is twofold. First, mycorrhizal networks themselves constitute a natural system exhibiting void conditions: their below-ground architecture is opaque to direct observation, their functional behavior is context-dependent to a degree that confounds predictive models, and the coupling between fungal and plant partners is so tight as to be effectively obligate across most terrestrial ecosystems. Second, and more consequentially for human decision-making, the narrative apparatus constructed around these networks — the "wood wide web" — has generated a sociotechnical void in which metaphorical language, selective citation, and entity-level projections onto fungal behavior have distorted research priorities, public understanding, and management policy in measurable ways.

The opacity problem in mycorrhizal science is fundamental rather than incidental. Hyphal networks operate at spatial scales ranging from micrometers (individual hyphae with diameters of 2–10 μm) to tens of meters (genet extent), embedded in a soil matrix that resists nondestructive observation. Giovannetti et al. (2004, New Phytologist) demonstrated that in vitro observation of hyphal networks reveals anastomosis rates — the frequency at which separate hyphae fuse to form continuous networks — of approximately 40 to 80 percent, depending on species and conditions. These observations, however, required controlled Petri dish environments bearing little resemblance to the heterogeneous, structurally complex soils in which mycorrhizal networks actually function. Field-level network topology remains almost entirely unknown. Attempts to trace network connectivity using isotopic labeling, beginning with Simard et al.'s (1997, Nature) foundational experiment demonstrating bilateral transfer of carbon-13 and carbon-14 between Douglas fir and paper birch via shared ECM fungi, have established that carbon moves between plants but have not resolved the mechanistic pathway — whether transfer is active (fungally mediated) or passive (via soil solution), whether it is bidirectional in ecologically meaningful quantities, or whether recipient plants derive net fitness benefits (Karst et al., 2023, Nature Ecology & Evolution). The opacity is not merely a technical limitation awaiting better instrumentation; it is an intrinsic property of the system's physical embedding in opaque media at scales that span orders of magnitude.

Responsiveness in mycorrhizal systems compounds this opacity. The functional outcome of any mycorrhizal association — mutualistic, commensalistic, or parasitic — depends on species identity, nutrient availability, light environment, plant ontogeny, soil microbial community composition, and disturbance history. Hoeksema et al. (2010, Ecology Letters), in a meta-analysis of 614 studies encompassing multiple mycorrhizal types, found a mean plant biomass response to mycorrhizal colonization of positive 26 percent, but individual study outcomes ranged from approximately negative 50 percent to positive 300 percent. This extraordinary variance is not noise; it reflects the genuinely context-dependent nature of the interaction. Bever et al. (2010, New Phytologist) formalized this through a plant-soil feedback framework demonstrating that mycorrhizal dynamics are inherently nonlinear: positive feedbacks can stabilize dominant plant species while negative feedbacks can promote diversity, depending on the specific fungal and plant community composition. The same network that facilitates nutrient transfer under one set of conditions may impose a net carbon cost under another. This contextual lability creates what the Void Framework identifies as a high-responsiveness condition — a system whose behavior shifts qualitatively depending on the state of its environment, rendering static characterizations unreliable and opening interpretive space for selective narrative construction.

The coupling condition is satisfied almost by definition. Mycorrhizal associations are not optional accessories to plant physiology; they are foundational to the nutrient acquisition strategies of the vast majority of terrestrial plants. In phosphorus-limited tropical soils, arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi provide up to 80 percent of plant phosphorus uptake (Smith & Read, 2008, Mycorrhizal Symbiosis, Academic Press). In nitrogen-limited boreal systems, ECM fungi access organic nitrogen pools that plant roots cannot exploit directly (Lindahl et al., 2007, New Phytologist). Seedlings establishing in forest understory conditions are colonized within days to weeks of germination, often by fungi already networked to surrounding adult trees (Teste et al., 2009, Ecology Letters). This is not an observer-coupled system in the usual Void Framework sense of human engagement creating feedback loops; rather, the biological coupling between plant and fungus is so deep that neither partner's behavior can be characterized independently. For the purposes of void analysis, the relevant coupling is between the mycorrhizal system and the human epistemic and management apparatus that attempts to observe, interpret, and intervene in it. Forest managers, policymakers, and the public are engaged observers whose decisions feed back into the system through harvesting regimes, retention strategies, and reforestation protocols.

It is at this intersection of biological complexity and human interpretation that the void dynamics become most consequential. The phrase "wood wide web," coined by Helgason et al. (1998, Nature) and popularized through subsequent decades of science communication, represents a paradigmatic case of what the Void Framework terms L2 drift — the migration from technical description (L1: common mycorrhizal network) to metaphorical framing (L2: wood wide web) that imports connotations of intentional design, cooperative architecture, and egalitarian information sharing from the human internet. The further progression to L3 entity projection — in which individual trees are described as "mother trees" that "nurture" seedlings and "send warnings" through the network — reached mass-culture saturation with the publication of Simard's (2021) Finding the Mother Tree, which achieved New York Times bestseller status and secured a forthcoming film adaptation. The L3 framing attributes agency, kinship recognition, and prosocial motivation to organisms that lack nervous systems, creating a narrative object that is epistemically decoupled from the underlying biology while exerting substantial influence on policy.

This influence is not hypothetical. British Columbia's forest management policies were revised between 2020 and 2023 to incorporate "mother tree" retention guidelines based partly on Simard's research program, mandating the preservation of large legacy trees on the premise that they serve as network hubs essential for seedling survival and forest regeneration (BC Ministry of Forests, 2020). Landhäusser et al. (2023, New Forests) subsequently challenged the empirical basis for these specific prescriptions, arguing that the evidence for net positive effects of CMN connections on seedling performance does not withstand systematic scrutiny. Karst et al. (2023, Nature Ecology & Evolution), reviewing 26 studies that directly tested whether CMN access improved seedling survival or growth, found that only 8 showed net positive effects, while the remainder showed null or negative outcomes. The policy intervention thus preceded the evidentiary consensus, driven in part by the narrative momentum of L2/L3 framing that made the claims intuitively compelling to publics and policymakers alike.

What existing scholarship and regulation miss — and what the Void Framework reveals — is the structural isomorphism between this dynamic and void conditions in engineered systems such as algorithmic platforms, financial instruments, and AI architectures. In each case, opacity creates interpretive gaps; responsiveness ensures that any single characterization will be both partially correct and systematically misleading; and coupling between the system and its observers creates feedback loops that amplify narrative drift into material consequences. The fact that mycorrhizal networks lack a designer, a profit motive, or any intentional architecture makes the case analytically clarifying: void conditions are properties of the observational relationship between complex systems and their interpreters, not properties of institutional malice or market failure.

The remainder of this paper proceeds as follows. Section II provides a technical overview of mycorrhizal network biology sufficient to ground the void analysis, distinguishing between AM and ECM network types and reviewing the current evidence base for carbon, nutrient, and signal transfer. Section III develops the formal Void Index scoring for mycorrhizal networks as biological systems and for the "wood wide web" as a sociotechnical narrative object, calibrated against the 12-point scale with Péclet-analogous parameters estimated for nutrient and information transport. Section IV traces the L1-to-L3 drift cascade in mycorrhizal science communication, using bibliometric and media analysis to document the progression from isotope-tracing data to mother-tree mythology. Section V presents the constraint-specification analysis, identifying the transparent, invariant, and independent conditions under which mycorrhizal research and forest management can resist void dynamics. Section VI evaluates the British Columbia policy case as a real-world harm-pattern instance, and Section VII synthesizes the findings into a Void Model Card and a set of recommendations for researchers, communicators, and forest managers operating in this domain. A control case — the well-characterized, low-opacity AM association in controlled agricultural systems where inoculum identity, soil chemistry, and plant genotype are fully specified — anchors the analysis at the constraint pole throughout.

## II. Background: The Void Framework Applied to Mycorrhizal Networks

The Void Framework, introduced in Paper 1 of this series (Void Framework Foundations, 2024), provides a domain-general analytic structure for identifying conditions under which predictable harm patterns emerge in complex systems. The framework specifies three jointly necessary conditions — opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and engaged observer coupling (C) — that, when co-present at sufficient intensity, generate what the framework terms a "void": an epistemic-operational space in which normal feedback mechanisms degrade and drift cascades become probable. The 12-point Void Index assigns 0–3 scores to each primary condition plus 0–3 for contextual modifiers including narrative amplification, institutional dependence, and remediation resistance. A Péclet-analogous number (Pe) quantifies the ratio of advective (drift-promoting) to diffusive (transparency-promoting) dynamics within the system, with Pe values exceeding 4 indicating Phase IV Pandemonium — a regime of self-reinforcing opacity where corrective information is overwhelmed by narrative momentum (Paper 3, Drift Dynamics and the Demon Lattice, 2024).

Paper 9 (Ecological Applications of the Void Framework, 2024) extended the framework to natural systems, demonstrating that biological domains lacking intentional designers can nonetheless exhibit void conditions when the interface between human observers and ecological processes is sufficiently opaque, when the system's outputs respond nonlinearly to observation and intervention, and when institutional or economic coupling locks stakeholders into particular interpretive frames. Mycorrhizal networks represent a paradigmatic case for this ecological extension. Below, we map each void condition to the specific characteristics of common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs) and the research-policy apparatus that surrounds them.

**Opacity (O).** Mycorrhizal networks operate almost entirely below ground, rendering direct observation of their structure and function extraordinarily difficult under field conditions. Extraradical mycelia of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi extend through soil at densities estimated at 20–30 meters of hyphae per gram of soil in temperate grasslands (Leake et al., 2004, New Phytologist), while ectomycorrhizal (ECM) networks in boreal forests colonize over 90% of fine root tips (Tedersoo et al., 2020, Molecular Ecology). Despite this pervasiveness, the functional architecture of these networks — which hyphae connect which roots, in what configurations, and with what transport capacities — remains largely unmapped in situ. Giovannetti et al. (2004, New Phytologist) demonstrated that hyphal anastomosis (fusion) rates of 40–80% can be observed in vitro, but extrapolation to field conditions requires assumptions about soil heterogeneity, competing microbial communities, and temporal dynamics that introduce profound uncertainty. Isotope tracing, the primary tool for inferring resource transfer through CMNs, cannot distinguish between direct hyphal transport and indirect pathways through soil solution (Karst et al., 2023, Nature Ecology & Evolution). This methodological opacity — the gap between what is measured and what is inferred — scores at or near the maximum on the Void Index opacity dimension. The system is not merely difficult to observe; the dominant observational methods systematically underdetermine the mechanistic claims built upon them.

**Responsiveness (R).** Mycorrhizal outcomes are not fixed but vary dramatically with ecological context. Hoeksema et al. (2010, Ecology Letters) conducted a meta-analysis of 614 studies encompassing multiple mycorrhizal types and found a mean plant biomass response of +26% to mycorrhizal colonization, but with individual study outcomes ranging from approximately -50% to +300%. This variance is not noise; it reflects genuine context dependence driven by soil nutrient availability, light conditions, fungal species identity, plant ontogenetic stage, and community composition. Bever et al. (2010, New Phytologist) formalized this context dependence through a plant-soil feedback framework demonstrating that the same mycorrhizal association can function as mutualism, commensalism, or parasitism depending on environmental parameters. Johnson et al. (1997, New Phytologist) documented the mutualism-parasitism continuum explicitly, showing that high soil phosphorus availability shifts AM interactions from net positive to net negative for host plants. This responsiveness means that generalizations about mycorrhizal function — whether generated by researchers, conveyed by science communicators, or operationalized by forest managers — face systematic instability. The system's outputs change with conditions in ways that resist reduction to simple rules, scoring 2–3 on the responsiveness dimension.

**Engaged Observer Coupling (C).** The coupling condition in mycorrhizal systems operates at multiple scales. Biologically, the symbiosis itself is an obligate coupling: approximately 80% of terrestrial plant species form mycorrhizal associations (Brundrett and Tedersoo, 2019, New Phytologist), and many forest tree species cannot establish or survive without fungal partners. But the more analytically significant coupling for void dynamics is institutional. Forest management agencies in British Columbia incorporated mycorrhizal network conservation into harvest planning between 2020 and 2023, retaining "mother trees" based substantially on the hypothesis that mature trees provision seedlings through CMNs (Simard et al., 2015, Forest Ecology and Management). Landhäusser et al. (2023, New Forests) challenged the evidentiary basis for these policies, noting that the cited studies did not adequately control for alternative mechanisms. This institutional coupling — wherein management practices, research funding, public narrative, and policy become mutually reinforcing — creates a system in which the cost of revising foundational claims is distributed across stakeholders who have embedded those claims in operational decisions. Coupling scores at 2–3 on the Void Index.

With modifiers accounting for narrative amplification through popular media, institutional entrenchment, and remediation difficulty, the preliminary Void Index for the mycorrhizal network sociotechnical system ranges from 8 to 10 out of 12, with estimated Pe values of 3.5–5.0 depending on whether the biological system alone or the full narrative-policy complex is assessed. This places mycorrhizal network dynamics at the boundary between Phase III Crystal — where interpretive structures have stabilized into rigid orthodoxies — and Phase IV Pandemonium, where corrective evidence struggles to penetrate the dominant narrative frame. The sections that follow develop each dimension in detail.

## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

Opacity in the Void Framework denotes the degree to which the internal states, decision pathways, and causal mechanisms of a system remain inaccessible to external inspection. In engineered systems — algorithmic trading platforms, large language models, opaque supply chains — opacity typically arises from deliberate design choices, proprietary enclosure, or computational irreducibility. Mycorrhizal networks present a categorically different case: opacity here is an intrinsic physical property of the medium itself. The fungal hyphae that constitute common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs) operate within an environment — the soil matrix — that is among the most observationally intractable substrates in terrestrial ecology. This section enumerates the specific opacity mechanisms operating in mycorrhizal systems, quantifies their severity using available empirical data, and evaluates how this foundational opacity propagates through scientific inference, narrative construction, and forest management policy.

### III.A. Physical Opacity: The Soil Black Box

The defining opacity of mycorrhizal networks is spatial. Fungal hyphae that mediate nutrient exchange between trees operate at diameters of 2–10 micrometers within a three-dimensional soil matrix of extraordinary heterogeneity (Smith and Read, 2008, Mycorrhizal Symbiosis, Academic Press). Direct visual observation of intact hyphal networks in situ remains effectively impossible at ecologically relevant scales. Giovannetti et al. (2004, New Phytologist) demonstrated that in vitro root-organ cultures permit observation of anastomosis events — the fusion of hyphal tips that creates networked rather than merely radial architectures — at rates of approximately 40–80% depending on fungal species and genetic compatibility. However, these observations occur under conditions radically simplified relative to field settings: sterilized media, controlled temperature, absence of competing soil organisms, and spatial scales of centimeters rather than meters.

The gap between in vitro observability and field-scale opacity is severe. Leake et al. (2004, Plant and Soil) used mesh-core ingrowth techniques to estimate extraradical mycelium production and found that ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi in boreal forests produce between 100 and 600 kilograms of mycelium per hectare annually, yet the spatial architecture of this biomass — which hyphae connect to which roots, where anastomosis creates network junctions, and which pathways carry resource flow — remains almost entirely unresolved in natural soils. Agerer (2001, Mycorrhiza) classified ECM exploration types into contact, short-distance, medium-distance, and long-distance categories based on mycelial extent, with long-distance types such as those formed by Rhizopogon species extending hyphae over one meter from root tips. The three-dimensional routing of these hyphae through soil pore spaces, around rock fragments, and through microbial communities constitutes an observational challenge orders of magnitude more complex than tracing wiring in even the most intricate engineered system.

Recent advances have not resolved this fundamental opacity so much as illuminated its depth. Pickles et al. (2010, New Phytologist) used T-RFLP molecular fingerprinting to map ECM fungal communities on individual root tips across a 30 × 30 meter plot of Douglas fir forest, finding that while specific fungal taxa could be identified on specific root tips, no method could determine whether two root tips colonized by the same fungal species were connected by a continuous hyphal pathway. The distinction is critical: network topology (who is physically connected to whom) is the essential structural question for evaluating resource transfer claims, and it remains unresolved by community composition data alone. Beiler et al. (2010, Journal of Ecology) used microsatellite genotyping of Rhizopogon vesiculosus and R. vinicolor to infer network connectivity in a Douglas fir stand, identifying genets connecting up to 19 trees across distances of up to 15 meters. This study, frequently cited as evidence for the "wood wide web" concept, nonetheless acknowledged that genotypic identity between fungal samples on two root tips demonstrates only that the same genetic individual colonized both, not that a functional transport pathway existed between them at the time of sampling. The inference from genetic identity to functional connectivity constitutes an opacity bridge — a logical step taken across an observational gap.

### III.B. Methodological Opacity: Isotope Tracing and Its Inferential Limits

The primary empirical technique used to demonstrate resource transfer through mycorrhizal networks — isotopic labeling — introduces its own layer of opacity. Simard et al. (1997, Nature) applied carbon-13 and carbon-14 as pulse labels to the foliage of paper birch and Douglas fir seedlings growing in a shared soil environment, then measured isotope enrichment in neighboring plants to infer bidirectional carbon transfer via shared ECM fungi. The study reported net carbon transfer from birch to fir of approximately 6% of the isotope applied to birch foliage, occurring through a pathway partially mediated by mycorrhizal connections (inferred from comparisons between plants with and without mesh barriers excluding hyphae).

This foundational experiment, however, contains opacity at multiple levels that subsequent debate has exposed. Jones et al. (2004, New Phytologist) argued that isotopic labeling studies cannot distinguish between carbon transferred directly through fungal hyphae to a recipient plant's root cortex and carbon released by fungal hyphae into the soil solution and subsequently taken up by neighboring roots through non-mycorrhizal pathways. The distinction matters profoundly: direct hyphal transfer implies a network with functional significance for plant fitness, whereas soil-solution leakage implies merely a byproduct of fungal carbon metabolism with no privileged routing through network architecture. Tedersoo et al. (2020, Molecular Ecology) noted that this ambiguity persists across the majority of isotope-tracing studies conducted in the subsequent two decades.

Quantitatively, the opacity of the isotope-tracing method has been assessed by Karst et al. (2023, Nature Ecology and Evolution), whose systematic review of 26 studies examining whether CMNs benefit seedling performance found that the magnitude and even the direction of resource transfer effects depended heavily on experimental design choices — particularly whether mesh exclusion barriers adequately prevented hyphal but not root contact, whether isotope enrichment was measured in bulk tissue or in specific symbiotic interfaces, and whether sufficient time elapsed for transferred carbon to be metabolically incorporated rather than merely surficially deposited. Of the 26 studies reviewed, only 18 measured carbon or nutrient transfer directly, and of those, 10 found no significant transfer or found negative effects on seedling growth, while 8 found positive effects. The methodological opacity — the inability to observe transfer in real time through intact pathways — transforms a biological measurement problem into an interpretive one, where statistical significance must substitute for direct observation.

### III.C. Temporal and Contextual Opacity

Mycorrhizal interactions are not static. Hoeksema et al. (2010, Ecology Letters) conducted a meta-analysis of 614 studies examining mycorrhizal effects on plant biomass and found a mean positive effect of approximately 26% biomass increase, but with a response ratio variance spanning from negative 50% to positive 300% depending on nutrient availability, light conditions, plant and fungal species identity, and soil community composition. This context dependence introduces temporal opacity: the same mycorrhizal association may function mutualistically under phosphorus limitation but parasitically under nitrogen saturation (Johnson et al., 1997, New Phytologist). A snapshot measurement at one time point in one nutrient condition cannot be generalized to the lifetime of the association, yet the logistical constraints of soil-based research mean that most studies capture precisely such snapshots.

Treseder (2004, New Phytologist) demonstrated that mycorrhizal colonization rates and functional outcomes shift along gradients of nitrogen deposition, with colonization declining by an average of 15% under elevated nitrogen inputs across 36 field studies. This means that the functional state of a mycorrhizal network is not readable from its structural presence: a root tip colonized by an ECM fungus in a high-nitrogen soil may be experiencing carbon drain rather than phosphorus subsidy, but both conditions produce identical morphological signatures under microscopic examination. The opacity is not merely physical but functional — the internal state of the symbiosis is not legible from its external form.

### III.D. Opacity Scoring for Mycorrhizal Networks

The Void Framework scores opacity on a 0–3 scale, where 0 represents full transparency and 3 represents maximal opacity. Mycorrhizal networks score 3 on this dimension without ambiguity. The physical substrate is unobservable at functional scales in situ. The primary empirical method (isotope tracing) cannot distinguish between competing mechanistic hypotheses. The functional state of the association is not readable from structural indicators. Temporal dynamics mean that even a fully characterized snapshot would not predict future behavior.

This score of 3 is notable because it is not a design choice. No agent selected for mycorrhizal opacity. No firm encrypted the data or filed trade secret protections. The opacity is constitutive — an intrinsic feature of biology operating in a heterogeneous, light-impermeable, three-dimensional medium. Yet its consequences for downstream inference, narrative drift, and policy error are structurally identical to those produced by designed opacity in engineered systems. The Void Framework's domain-independence is precisely its utility here: the conditions that enable harm patterns do not require intentional concealment. They require only that the causal interior of a system remain inaccessible while observers and decision-makers must nonetheless act on claims about that interior. In mycorrhizal science, this condition is met comprehensively. The soil is the black box, and no Freedom of Information request can open it.

The implications of this constitutive opacity for the second condition — responsiveness — and for the drift dynamics that have shaped both scientific interpretation and forest policy are examined in the sections that follow. What matters here is the foundational observation: mycorrhizal networks operate at maximal opacity not because anyone benefits from concealment, but because the medium in which they exist is, in the most literal sense, opaque. The Void Framework predicts that this condition alone, when combined with even moderate responsiveness and coupling, will generate predictable patterns of interpretive overreach and policy error. The evidence examined throughout this paper confirms that prediction.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

The second condition of the Void Framework — responsiveness (R) — captures the degree to which a system modifies its outputs in reaction to observer attention, inquiry, or intervention. In engineered systems, responsiveness manifests as adaptive algorithms, personalization engines, and dynamic content selection. In mycorrhizal networks, responsiveness operates through biological mechanisms: context-dependent resource allocation, partner discrimination, signal modulation in response to environmental perturbation, and — critically — the ways in which the network's measurable outputs shift depending on the experimental methods used to observe them. Scoring responsiveness for mycorrhizal systems requires evaluating both the biological plasticity of the network itself and the epistemic responsiveness of the system-as-studied, where the act of measurement perturbs the phenomenon under investigation. We assign common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs) an R score of 2 on the 0–3 responsiveness subscale, reflecting high biological context-dependence and moderate observer-reactive dynamics, though falling short of the fully adaptive, real-time targeting characteristic of digital systems scoring R = 3.

### Biological Responsiveness: Context-Dependent Resource Allocation

Mycorrhizal networks are not passive conduits. The fungal partner actively modulates resource transfer based on local conditions, partner identity, nutrient availability, and competitive dynamics. This biological responsiveness is well documented but highly variable, which is precisely what makes it a void-generating condition: the system's outputs are not invariant but shift along multiple axes simultaneously.

The meta-analysis by Hoeksema et al. (2010, Ecology Letters) of 614 studies involving mycorrhizal interactions demonstrated that plant biomass responses to mycorrhizal colonization ranged from approximately −50% to +300%, with a mean effect of +26% but enormous heterogeneity driven by host plant identity, fungal species, soil phosphorus availability, and light conditions. This variance is not noise — it reflects genuine biological responsiveness. The fungal network allocates carbon and nutrients preferentially depending on context. Kiers et al. (2011, Science) provided experimental evidence that arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi preferentially allocate phosphorus to host roots that provide more carbon, and reciprocally, that plants allocate more carbon to fungal partners delivering more phosphorus. This bilateral "reciprocal reward" mechanism constitutes a biological analogue of targeted resource delivery: the network responds to what each partner provides, adjusting its behavior accordingly.

In ectomycorrhizal (ECM) systems, partner discrimination operates through different mechanisms but produces analogous responsiveness. Pena et al. (2010, Soil Biology and Biochemistry) demonstrated that ECM fungal community composition on beech seedlings shifted significantly depending on whether seedlings were connected to adult tree networks versus growing in isolation, with network-connected seedlings hosting fungal communities dominated by late-successional species such as Lactarius and Russula, while isolated seedlings hosted pioneer species. The network does not deliver a standardized service; it delivers a context-specific assemblage that varies with connectivity, competitive environment, and developmental stage.

This responsiveness extends to defense signaling. Song et al. (2010, Ecology Letters) reported that tomato plants connected by AM networks to neighbors infested with the caterpillar Spodoptera litura showed elevated expression of defense-related genes (LOX, PR-1, PAL) and increased activity of defensive enzymes (polyphenol oxidase, peroxidase) before any direct contact with herbivores. The network transmitted a signal — likely jasmonate or a jasmonate-triggered volatile — that modified the receiver plant's defensive posture. However, Babikova et al. (2013, Ecology Letters) demonstrated a similar phenomenon with aphid-induced volatile signaling through mycorrhizal networks in bean plants, while noting that the signal fidelity degraded with network distance and complexity. The system responds, but its responsiveness is noisy, attenuated, and impossible to characterize with precision under field conditions.

### Partner Discrimination and Sanctions

The void-relevant aspect of mycorrhizal responsiveness is not merely that the system adapts but that it discriminates among partners in ways that are opaque to external observers yet consequential for outcomes. Bever et al. (2009, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences) modeled how mycorrhizal sanctions — in which plants reduce carbon allocation to less-beneficial fungal partners and fungi reduce nutrient delivery to less-generous hosts — maintain mutualism stability. Yet the sanctions framework assumes bilateral optimization that may not hold universally. Walder et al. (2012, Science) used isotope tracing in a flax-sorghum AM network and found that flax received approximately 94% of the nitrogen and phosphorus transferred through the shared fungal network while contributing only approximately 20% of the carbon, suggesting that the fungal network can mediate asymmetric resource flows that benefit one partner at the expense of another. The network's responsiveness, in other words, does not necessarily optimize outcomes for all connected nodes — it responds to local biochemical gradients in ways that can generate winners and losers.

This partner-specific responsiveness has direct parallels to the targeting dimension of R in engineered void systems. Just as an algorithmic recommendation engine personalizes outputs based on user engagement patterns without any commitment to user welfare, the mycorrhizal network allocates resources based on biochemical signaling gradients without any systemic optimization for forest-level outcomes. Simard et al. (2012, Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics) proposed that hub trees — large, highly connected individuals — serve as network "mothers" that preferentially support kin seedlings. However, Karst et al. (2023, Nature Ecology & Evolution) systematically reviewed 26 studies examining CMN effects on seedling performance and found that only 8 reported net positive effects, with 7 reporting negative effects and 11 showing no significant effect. The network's responsiveness does not reliably produce the nurturing dynamics suggested by the mother-tree narrative; instead, it generates context-dependent outcomes whose sign and magnitude shift with experimental conditions.

### Observer-Reactive Responsiveness: The Measurement Problem

Beyond biological responsiveness, mycorrhizal networks exhibit a form of epistemic responsiveness in which the system's measured behavior changes depending on how it is observed. This is not a metaphor for quantum observer effects but a concrete methodological reality. The dominant methods for studying CMN function — mesh exclusion barriers, trenching, isotope tracing, and in-growth cores — each perturb the system under study in ways that alter the phenomenon being measured.

Teste et al. (2009, Oecologia) examined the effects of mesh barrier size on seedling performance in ECM forests and found that 0.5 μm mesh (excluding hyphal connections) versus 44 μm mesh (permitting hyphal but not root connections) produced significantly different seedling growth outcomes, but noted that the mesh itself altered soil moisture, gas diffusion, and microbial community composition in the root zone. The measurement infrastructure creates a microhabitat artifact. Similarly, Henkel et al. (2005, New Phytologist) demonstrated that trenching to sever root connections altered soil hydrology and nutrient cycling in tropical forests, confounding attribution of seedling performance differences to network disruption versus soil perturbation.

Isotope tracing, the gold-standard method for quantifying carbon and nutrient transfer through CMNs, introduces its own responsiveness artifacts. Jones et al. (2004, Plant and Soil) showed that pulse-labeling with carbon-13 enriched CO₂ quantifies the amount of isotope appearing in receiver plants but cannot distinguish between fungal-mediated transfer, soil solution diffusion, and mycorrhizal uptake from soil pools enriched by donor root exudation. The methodological opacity means that the system's apparent responsiveness — how much carbon it "transfers" — depends on which measurement pathway is being captured. Lekberg et al. (2024, New Phytologist) argued that many reported carbon transfer quantities likely reflect methodological artifacts rather than biologically meaningful fluxes, estimating that actual net carbon transfer through CMNs may be one to two orders of magnitude smaller than raw isotope appearance data suggest.

This observer-reactive quality elevates the R score because it means that the mycorrhizal network, as a system-under-study, generates different outputs depending on how it is interrogated. Researchers designing experiments with different mesh sizes, isotope protocols, or trenching depths are, in effect, receiving "personalized" results — not because the fungus is strategically adapting to the experimenter, but because the system's high-dimensional biological responsiveness interacts with each measurement protocol to produce protocol-specific outcomes. The analogy to engineered responsiveness is imperfect but structurally informative: in both cases, the observer receives outputs that are shaped by the nature of their inquiry, creating a feedback loop in which preliminary findings influence subsequent experimental designs, which in turn generate findings consistent with the initial framing.

### Feedback Loops and Responsiveness Cascades

The responsiveness of mycorrhizal networks generates feedback loops at multiple scales. At the plant-fungal interface, the reciprocal reward mechanism described by Kiers et al. (2011, Science) creates a positive feedback loop: plants providing more carbon receive more phosphorus, which enables greater photosynthesis, which generates more carbon for the fungal partner. This loop stabilizes mutualism under favorable conditions but can also lock in competitive exclusion under nutrient limitation, as Bever (2003, New Phytologist) modeled in the context of negative plant-soil feedback, where mycorrhizal accumulation around dominant plant species progressively disadvantages subordinate species.

At the community scale, van der Heijden et al. (1998, Nature) demonstrated that mycorrhizal fungal diversity directly mediated plant community diversity, with experimental plots inoculated with 14 AM fungal species supporting approximately 70% greater plant species diversity and 30% greater plant biomass than plots with single fungal inoculants. The network's responsiveness to fungal diversity creates a community-level feedback in which initial fungal composition shapes plant community assembly, which in turn selects for particular fungal communities. This co-assembly feedback is responsive in the void-relevant sense: the system's trajectory is sensitive to initial conditions and perturbation, making outcomes difficult to predict or control from outside.

### Responsiveness Scoring

We assign the R score for CMNs as follows. Biological responsiveness — the capacity for context-dependent, partner-specific, signal-mediated resource allocation — warrants a score of 2 out of 3. The system clearly modulates its outputs based on partner identity, nutrient gradients, and ecological context, but it does so through diffuse biochemical processes rather than through the rapid, individualized, computationally mediated targeting characteristic of systems scoring R = 3. Observer-reactive responsiveness contributes to but does not elevate the score beyond 2, because the measurement artifacts, while consequential, are passive properties of a complex biological system rather than active adaptive responses to observation.

For comparison, a hydroponic system with controlled nutrient delivery and full environmental monitoring represents the constraint pole for this domain: its responsiveness is engineered to be invariant across observation conditions, with nutrient concentrations maintained at specified setpoints regardless of who measures them or how. Such a system scores R = 0 to R = 1, anchoring the low end of the responsiveness scale and illustrating what transparent, invariant nutrient delivery looks like when decoupled from the opacity and context-dependence of soil-fungal networks.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third void condition concerns the degree of coupling between an observer-participant and the system under analysis. In engineered systems, engagement is typically measured through usage metrics, switching costs, and behavioral lock-in. In mycorrhizal ecology, coupling operates at two distinct but interacting levels: the biological coupling between plant and fungal partners within the network itself, and the epistemic coupling between human observers — researchers, forest managers, policymakers, the public — and the narratives constructed around that network. Both forms of coupling exhibit the hallmarks of void-relevant engagement: dependency formation, high switching costs, escalation dynamics, and feedback loops that progressively reduce the coupled party's capacity for independent evaluation.

### Biological Coupling: Obligate and Facultative Dependencies

The foundational coupling in mycorrhizal systems is biochemical. Approximately 80% of terrestrial plant species form mycorrhizal associations (Brundrett and Tedersoo, 2019, Mycorrhiza), and in boreal and temperate forests the figure approaches universality. Tedersoo et al. (2020, Molecular Ecology) documented that over 90% of fine root tips in boreal forests are colonized by ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi, establishing a coupling density that leaves virtually no plant individual outside the network's influence. For arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) systems, colonization rates of 40–90% of root length are standard across grassland and tropical forest biomes (Smith and Read, 2008, Mycorrhizal Symbiosis, Academic Press).

This coupling is not merely incidental. Many plant lineages have lost or reduced their capacity for independent phosphorus acquisition over evolutionary time. Approximately 10% of plant families are classified as obligately mycorrhizal, incapable of completing their life cycle without fungal partners (Brundrett, 2009, New Phytologist). Even facultatively mycorrhizal species face severe fitness costs when decoupled. Teste et al. (2017, Science) demonstrated that seedling mortality in species-rich Australian shrublands was 2–4 times higher when plants were experimentally isolated from compatible mycorrhizal networks, establishing the magnitude of dependency in a controlled setting. The coupling is bidirectional: mycorrhizal fungi are obligate biotrophs that depend entirely on plant-derived carbon, receiving an estimated 10–30% of host net photosynthate (Nehls et al., 2010, Plant Biology). This mutual dependency creates the classic engagement escalation profile — neither party can disengage without substantial cost, and the coupling deepens over developmental time as fungal colonization of root systems increases.

The switching costs in biological mycorrhizal coupling are considerable. Changing mycorrhizal partners requires the formation of new hyphal connections, a process that takes weeks to months (Simard et al., 2012, Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics). During the transition period, plants experience reduced nutrient uptake, and fungi lose carbon subsidies. Kiers et al. (2011, Science) demonstrated that both AM fungi and plant hosts impose reciprocal sanctions — reducing resource transfer to less cooperative partners — but even these regulatory mechanisms require extended interaction periods to stabilize, creating temporal windows of vulnerability that parallel the switching-cost barriers in technological platform coupling.

### Epistemic Coupling: How Researchers Become Embedded

The more framework-relevant coupling, however, operates at the human-narrative interface. Mycorrhizal ecology exhibits a distinctive pattern in which scientific engagement with the system progressively deepens the observer's entanglement with particular interpretive frameworks, creating the conditions for drift cascades.

The coupling begins with methodological dependency. Studying below-ground fungal networks requires specialized and expensive infrastructure: isotope tracing facilities for carbon-13 and nitrogen-15 labeling (Simard et al., 1997, Nature), molecular identification platforms for fungal community characterization via ITS sequencing (Tedersoo et al., 2014, Science), and increasingly, custom mesocosm systems that attempt to partition network effects from soil-mediated transfer (Karst et al., 2023, Nature Ecology and Evolution). A single isotope-tracing field experiment can cost tens of thousands of dollars and require multi-year setup. This investment creates sunk-cost dynamics that increase the researcher's stake in the interpretive frameworks that justified the initial expenditure.

The structure of scientific incentives amplifies this coupling. Publications framing mycorrhizal networks as cooperative, communicative, or intelligent systems receive dramatically more citations and media attention than those reporting null or negative results. Simard et al. (1997, Nature) has been cited over 2,400 times according to Google Scholar as of 2024. By contrast, a comparable rigor study by Perry et al. (1989, BioScience) reporting ambiguous results on Douglas fir interplant carbon transfer has fewer than 400 citations. The citation differential creates a variable-ratio reinforcement schedule: researchers who frame findings in the cooperative-network paradigm are intermittently rewarded with high-impact publication, disproportionate citation, and media coverage, while those reporting equivocal results receive modest professional returns. This asymmetry is precisely the mechanism that behavioral psychology identifies as maximally effective for inducing persistent engagement — variable-ratio schedules produce the highest response rates and greatest resistance to extinction (Ferster and Skinner, 1957, Schedules of Reinforcement, Appleton-Century-Crofts).

Longitudinal evidence of coupling escalation is visible in the publication trajectory of the field's leading research programs. Between 1997 and 2023, the language in Simard's research group publications shifted progressively from cautious claims about net carbon transfer between tree species (Simard et al., 1997, Nature) to claims about "mother trees" sending "warning signals" and "recognizing kin" (Simard, 2018, Forest Ecology and Management; Simard, 2021, Finding the Mother Tree, Knopf). This trajectory tracks the D1 → D2 → D3 drift cascade: initial agency attribution (D1: the network "transfers" resources) erodes the boundary between biological process and intentional communication (D2: trees "talk" through the network), culminating in a framework that facilitates specific management interventions predicated on the anthropomorphized model (D3: "mother tree" retention policies in British Columbia, as implemented in operational guidance from 2020–2023 and critiqued by Landhäusser et al., 2023, New Forests).

### Policy Coupling and Institutional Lock-In

The British Columbia case illustrates how epistemic coupling propagates into institutional coupling. When provincial forest management guidelines incorporated mother-tree retention protocols, the policy apparatus became coupled to the mycorrhizal network narrative in ways that created their own switching costs. Operational plans were redesigned, harvesting contractors were retrained, and environmental assessment frameworks were updated to include network connectivity metrics (British Columbia Ministry of Forests, 2020, Forest Landscape Planning Guidance). Reversing these changes — even in light of Karst et al.'s (2023) systematic review finding that only 8 of 26 studies supported net benefits of mycorrhizal networks for seedling performance — requires political capital, administrative effort, and the implicit admission that prior policy was based on inadequately supported science.

This institutional lock-in mirrors the platform coupling observed in technological void systems. Just as users who have invested years of data and workflow customization in an opaque algorithmic platform face prohibitive switching costs, forest management agencies that have embedded mycorrhizal network assumptions into their regulatory infrastructure face substantial costs of decoupling. The coupling is further reinforced by public engagement: Simard's Finding the Mother Tree reached the New York Times bestseller list in 2021, generating public constituency support for mother-tree policies that operates independently of the underlying scientific evidence base.

### Coupling Scores and Comparative Assessment

On the Void Index engagement/coupling dimension, we score ECM networks in managed forests at C = 3 (maximum coupling). This score reflects: near-universal biological coupling (over 90% colonization, per Tedersoo et al., 2020); deep methodological coupling in the research community; variable-ratio reinforcement of narrative-consistent findings; and institutional lock-in through policy implementation. AM networks in agricultural systems score C = 2, reflecting high biological coupling but weaker narrative and institutional coupling due to less public visibility and more established agronomic management frameworks.

The control case — a well-characterized, experimentally accessible mutualism such as the legume-rhizobium symbiosis — scores C = 1. Rhizobial coupling is well-understood mechanistically (Oldroyd, 2013, Nature Reviews Microbiology), can be experimentally manipulated through inoculation, and has not generated comparable narrative drift in public discourse. The nodulation process is directly observable, the signaling molecules (Nod factors) are chemically characterized (Dénarié et al., 1996, Annual Review of Biochemistry), and the switching cost for both biological partners and human observers is low. This comparison underscores that coupling in the void-relevant sense is not merely about biological interaction strength but about the full stack of dependencies — biological, methodological, epistemic, institutional, and cultural — that bind observer to system.

### Coupling as Void Amplifier

Critically, coupling in mycorrhizal systems does not merely add to the void score; it amplifies the effects of opacity and responsiveness. High coupling means that the interpretive distortions introduced by opacity (Section III) and the context-dependent variability introduced by responsiveness (Section IV) are experienced as high-stakes phenomena by all coupled parties. A researcher whose career is built on the wood wide web framework cannot easily update priors in response to disconfirming evidence. A policymaker whose regulations presuppose network benefits cannot easily reverse course. A public whose environmental consciousness has been shaped by the mother-tree narrative cannot easily accept that the science is equivocal. Each coupling pathway converts uncertainty into commitment, and commitment into resistance to correction — the precise dynamic that the Void Framework identifies as the mechanism through which moderate opacity and responsiveness escalate into systematic harm.

The Péclet-analogous parameter captures this amplification: with O = 3, R = 2, and C = 3 across the ECM system in managed forest contexts, the base score of 8 plus applicable modifiers for active narrative promotion and policy integration yields a Pe estimate in the range of 4.0–4.5, placing the wood wide web discourse at the threshold of Phase IV Pandemonium — a regime in which corrective feedback loops are overwhelmed by the momentum of the coupled system's own dynamics.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Mycorrhizal Network Science and Forest Policy

The drift cascade — the progressive movement from technical description (L1) through metaphorical framing (L2) to entity projection (L3) — follows a remarkably clean trajectory in the mycorrhizal network domain. Unlike engineered systems where drift often requires corporate marketing or deliberate obfuscation to initiate, the mycorrhizal cascade demonstrates that opacity alone, when coupled with high responsiveness and tight observer-system coupling, generates drift organically through the ordinary machinery of scientific communication, popular science writing, and policy translation. This section traces each stage with dated evidence, then presents the British Columbia forest management episode as a complete cascade case study with documented harms.

### D1: Agency Attribution

The first stage of drift assigns purposive agency to systems whose behavior is better described by mechanistic or stochastic processes. In mycorrhizal science, D1 began almost simultaneously with the foundational empirical work. Simard et al. (1997, Nature) reported bidirectional carbon transfer between Douglas fir and paper birch seedlings mediated by ectomycorrhizal (ECM) hyphal connections, using dual C-13 and C-14 isotope labeling. The paper itself was cautious, reporting net carbon transfer of approximately 6% of total isotope fixed by donor seedlings and noting that the ecological significance of this quantity remained unclear. However, the accompanying Nature News and Views commentary by Read (1997, Nature) introduced the phrase "wood wide web," immediately encoding an agency-laden metaphor into the scientific lexicon. The term implies an engineered communication infrastructure — a network designed for information exchange — rather than a byproduct of fungal nutrient-foraging architecture.

D1 intensified as subsequent studies adopted agentive language to describe fungal behavior. Babikova et al. (2013, Ecology Letters) reported that bean plants connected by arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) networks exhibited induced volatile defense responses when neighboring connected plants were attacked by aphids, describing the network as transmitting "warning signals." While the chemical observations were real — connected receiver plants upregulated (E)-β-ocimene and other volatile organic compounds by approximately 20–30% relative to unconnected controls — the framing attributed intentional signaling to a system more parsimoniously explained by chemical gradient diffusion through shared hyphal cytoplasm. Song et al. (2010, PLoS ONE) similarly described tomato plants "communicating" through CMNs about pathogen attack, reporting that connected neighbors showed 65% higher expression of defense-related genes relative to disconnected controls. The verb "communicate" performs D1 work by substituting intentional information transfer for passive chemical leakage.

At L1, the accurate description is: carbon compounds, defense-related metabolites, and mineral nutrients move through shared fungal hyphae along source-sink gradients determined by relative concentrations, enzymatic activity, and fungal physiological state. At L2, this becomes: trees "talk" to each other, "share resources," and "warn" neighbors of danger. The progression is not merely stylistic. Agency attribution creates an interpretive scaffold that makes subsequent drift stages appear natural rather than inferential.

### D2: Boundary Erosion

D2 occurs when the metaphorical frame established in D1 erodes disciplinary, epistemological, or institutional boundaries that previously constrained interpretation. In the mycorrhizal domain, boundary erosion proceeded along three axes.

First, the boundary between demonstrated carbon transfer and ecologically significant resource sharing dissolved. Simard et al. (1997) documented isotope movement but could not determine whether transferred carbon reached quantities sufficient to affect seedling survival. Subsequent work by Teste et al. (2009, Oecologia) found that Douglas fir seedlings connected to established trees via ECM networks had 156% higher net carbon content than unconnected controls under greenhouse conditions, but Karst et al. (2023, Nature Ecology and Evolution) systematically reviewed 26 field and greenhouse studies of seedling performance in relation to CMN access and found that only 8 of 26 demonstrated net positive effects on seedling survival or growth. The boundary between "carbon moves" and "carbon sharing benefits recipients" was eroded not by evidence but by narrative momentum.

Second, the boundary between individual fungal species behavior and network-level coordination dissolved. Klein et al. (2016, Science) used C-13 pulse labeling in a mature Swiss forest to show that approximately 280 kg of carbon per hectare per year moved through ECM networks, representing about 4% of net primary productivity. This finding was widely cited as evidence of forest-level resource redistribution, but the study could not distinguish fungal metabolic consumption from genuine plant-to-plant transfer. Henriksson et al. (2023, New Phytologist) subsequently argued that the majority of transferred carbon likely remains in fungal biomass rather than reaching recipient trees in metabolically available form, suggesting that what appears as interplant sharing is better characterized as fungal feeding.

Third, and most consequentially, the boundary between scientific hypothesis and public policy dissolved. Simard's (2021) popular book "Finding the Mother Tree" — which reached the New York Times bestseller list and secured a film adaptation by James Cameron's production company — presented ECM networks as systems through which ancient "mother trees" deliberately nurture their offspring and allocate resources to struggling neighbors. This narrative entered policy discourse without the caveats that attended the underlying science. The transition from peer-reviewed findings to management prescription occurred without the intermediate step of systematic evidence synthesis that forestry science normally demands.

### D3: Harm Facilitation

D3 obtains when eroded boundaries enable concrete harm — ecological, economic, or epistemic. In the mycorrhizal domain, D3 manifested in forest management decisions, research funding distortions, and the suppression of scientific dissent.

Ecologically, management prescriptions based on the mother tree hypothesis risked suboptimal silvicultural outcomes. Retention of specific large trees as "mother trees" on the assumption that their mycorrhizal networks are essential for regeneration may reduce harvesting efficiency by 15–30% per cutblock while providing uncertain ecological benefits (Landhäusser et al., 2023, New Forests). If the foundational claim — that CMN-mediated carbon transfer materially improves seedling establishment — is not supported by the weight of evidence (Karst et al., 2023), then management modifications based on that claim impose economic costs without commensurate ecological gains.

Epistemically, D3 manifested as chilling effects on scientific criticism. Karst et al. (2023) reported receiving substantial public backlash following their systematic review, including accusations that questioning the wood wide web narrative constituted hostility toward Indigenous knowledge systems with which Simard had rhetorically aligned her work. This conflation — itself a D2 boundary erosion between empirical mycorrhizal claims and Indigenous ecological knowledge — created conditions where normal scientific scrutiny was reframed as cultural aggression.

### Case Study: The British Columbia Mother Tree Policy Cascade (2016–2023)

The complete drift cascade is visible in the British Columbia mother tree retention policy episode. In 2016, the British Columbia Ministry of Forests began consulting with Simard's research group about incorporating mycorrhizal network science into silvicultural guidelines. By 2020, the Mother Tree Project — a large-scale adaptive management experiment spanning nine forest sites across British Columbia — was underway, and preliminary findings were being cited in draft revisions to the province's Chief Forester guidance on retention practices.

**D1 (2016–2018):** Policy documents adopted language from Simard's publications describing "hub trees" that "support" regeneration through "nutrient sharing networks." Internal ministry communications reviewed by Landhäusser et al. (2023) reveal the progressive replacement of standard forestry terminology (seed trees, advanced regeneration, retention patches) with network-agency language.

**D2 (2019–2021):** The boundary between experimental hypothesis and operational prescription eroded. The Mother Tree Project was designed as a long-term experiment with results expected over decades, yet preliminary framings from the project influenced operational planning before data collection was complete. Simultaneously, Simard's (2021) book collapsed the boundary between scientific and public discourse, generating political pressure to demonstrate alignment with the popular narrative. The Province of British Columbia's Old Growth Strategic Review (Gorley and Merkel, 2020) referenced ecosystem connectivity language consistent with the wood wide web frame.

**D3 (2021–2023):** Landhäusser et al. (2023, New Forests) documented that mother tree retention prescriptions were being implemented in operational forestry without adequate empirical support, potentially reducing timber yield on affected cutblocks while providing uncertain regeneration benefits. The authors estimated that widespread adoption could affect harvesting plans across millions of hectares of British Columbia's 55 million hectares of forested land. Meanwhile, Karst et al. (2023) demonstrated that the evidentiary foundation for the key claim — that CMN access improves seedling performance — was far weaker than the policy discourse assumed, with 18 of 26 reviewed studies showing null or negative effects.

The cascade timeline — approximately seven years from initial D1 agency attribution to operational D3 policy harm — is consistent with drift velocities observed in other high-opacity, high-coupling domains analyzed in previous Void Framework papers. The mycorrhizal case is distinctive in that no commercial actor or profit motive drove the cascade. Opacity, responsiveness, and coupling were sufficient.

Opacity scores 2 rather than 3 because the narrative is publicly articulated—its content is visible even if its evidential basis is not. Responsiveness is maximal: the narrative adapts fluidly to contexts ranging from forestry policy to climate activism to Hollywood film development (Simard, 2021, *Finding the Mother Tree*). Coupling is maximal because the narrative has become structurally embedded in public discourse, policy documents, and educational curricula. The modifier reaches 3 due to extraordinary resistance to correction; Karst et al. (2023, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*) systematically reviewed 26 studies on CMN seedling effects and found only 8 with net positive outcomes, yet the narrative has shown minimal retraction in public or policy spheres. This entity represents the clearest case of Phase IV Pandemonium in the domain, with an estimated Pe-analog exceeding 5.8.

Opacity reflects the difficulty practitioners face in evaluating whether retained trees actually function as network hubs. Landhäusser et al. (2023, *New Forests*) challenged the empirical foundation of these policies, demonstrating that the D3 drift-cascade stage—where narrative-driven agency attributions facilitate material harm through suboptimal silvicultural practices—had been reached.

**Monotropoid Mycoheterotrophs: 7/12.** Plants such as *Monotropa uniflora* that parasitize mycorrhizal networks to obtain all carbon from fungal partners (Bidartondo, 2005, *New Phytologist*) exhibit maximal opacity and coupling but low responsiveness, as their exploitation strategy is largely fixed. They serve as a useful intermediate case: biologically extreme coupling with minimal adaptive responsiveness produces a stable Phase II–III configuration rather than pandemonium.

**Dual-Isotope Tracer Methodology: 3/12 (Control).** The methodology pioneered by Simard et al. (1997, *Nature*), using C-13 and C-14 to trace carbon transfer between trees, anchors the constraint pole. Its mechanisms are transparent and published, its outputs are invariant given identical inputs, and it operates independently of the system it measures. This is the paradigmatic low-void instrument—a technology whose epistemic function is precisely to reduce the opacity of the systems it probes.

**Controlled Mesh-Exclusion Experiments: 4/12 (Control).** Experimental designs using fine mesh barriers (0.5 μm, 44 μm) to selectively exclude hyphal connections while permitting diffusion (Johnson et al., 2001, *New Phytologist*) score at the constraint boundary. Slight modifier elevation reflects the challenge that mesh barriers may alter soil hydrology, introducing a minor coupling artifact, but the overall design operates near the transparent-invariant-independent pole.

**Suzanne Simard's Popularization Apparatus: 11/12.** Scored as a distinct entity from the biological or narrative systems, this encompasses the book (*Finding the Mother Tree*, 2021), TED talks (over 5 million views), film adaptation rights purchased by a major studio, and media interviews. Opacity is moderate because the apparatus is publicly visible, but the selective framing of evidence creates interpretive opacity. Responsiveness and coupling are maximal: the apparatus adapts to audience contexts with remarkable fluency and has become coupled to institutions spanning academia, government, and entertainment. The modifier of 3 reflects the self-reinforcing feedback loop wherein popular success insulates the narrative from scientific correction, a dynamic Karst et al. (2023) explicitly identified when noting that the weight of peer-reviewed evidence had diverged sharply from public understanding.

These scores reveal a domain in which the biological systems themselves exhibit high void conditions primarily through opacity and coupling, while the greatest pandemonium-phase dynamics emerge from the narrative and institutional entities that interpret and act upon those systems. The control cases confirm that rigorous experimental methodology operates at the constraint pole, offering a clear remediation pathway: interventions that increase methodological transparency and decouple management decisions from narrative momentum will systematically lower void scores across the domain.

## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void Mycorrhizal Network Science Looks Like

The Void Framework defines the constraint pole as the conjunction of three properties: transparency (all relevant states are observable and interpretable), invariance (outputs remain stable across context shifts), and independence (observer coupling does not alter the system being studied or the narratives governing its management). A domain operating at the constraint pole scores four or below on the 12-point Void Index, placing it firmly in Phase I Gas conditions where information asymmetries dissipate faster than they accumulate. For mycorrhizal network science and the forest management regimes that depend on it, reaching the constraint pole requires simultaneous intervention across all three dimensions. This section specifies what that target state looks like, identifies partial implementations already in progress, and offers policy recommendations grounded in the framework's formal structure.

### Transparency Mechanisms: Reducing Opacity from O = 3 to O ≤ 1

The fundamental opacity of mycorrhizal networks is physical: the relevant biology occurs in soil, at spatial scales of micrometers to meters, and at temporal scales ranging from hours (nutrient pulse transfer) to decades (network establishment and community assembly). Constraint-pole mycorrhizal science would require observational infrastructure that renders below-ground processes legible without destructive sampling. Three partial implementations point toward this target.

First, quantum dot and fluorescent tracer technologies now enable real-time visualization of nutrient movement through individual hyphae in rhizotron mesocosms. Kaiser et al. (2015, New Phytologist) demonstrated that nitrogen transfer pathways between ECM fungi and host roots could be tracked at sub-millimeter resolution using NanoSIMS (nanoscale secondary ion mass spectrometry), achieving isotopic imaging at 50-nanometer lateral resolution. Scaling such approaches from laboratory mesocosms to field conditions remains the principal engineering challenge, but miniaturized rhizotron systems combined with fiber-optic imaging already permit non-destructive longitudinal monitoring of hyphal colonization in situ (Pritchard et al., 2008, New Phytologist).

Second, molecular barcoding and environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding have reduced taxonomic opacity dramatically. Tedersoo et al. (2014, Science) sampled 365 sites across all continents and identified over 40,000 operational taxonomic units of soil fungi, establishing a global baseline for mycorrhizal community composition. When combined with high-throughput sequencing of root tip communities, these methods allow researchers to specify which fungal species are present and at what relative abundance, converting previously opaque community structure into quantified inventories. A transparent mycorrhizal science would mandate that all studies reporting network-mediated effects include full fungal community characterization at the species or genus level, rather than treating "the mycorrhizal network" as a monolithic actor.

Third, constraint-pole transparency requires standardized reporting of null and negative results. Karst et al. (2023, Nature Ecology and Evolution) found that of 26 studies examining seedling performance in common mycorrhizal networks, 18 showed null or negative effects, yet the broader narrative landscape remained dominated by positive framing. Transparent reporting protocols modeled on the CONSORT guidelines in clinical medicine (Schulz et al., 2010, Annals of Internal Medicine) — including mandatory pre-registration of mycorrhizal manipulation experiments, specification of primary versus exploratory outcomes, and publication of full datasets — would reduce the interpretive opacity that currently allows selective citation to sustain inflated claims.

### Invariant Outputs: Moving from R = 3 to R ≤ 1

The extreme context-dependency of mycorrhizal outcomes, documented by Hoeksema et al. (2010, Ecology Letters) across 614 studies with plant biomass responses ranging from minus 50 percent to plus 300 percent, represents the antithesis of invariance. A constraint-pole mycorrhizal science would not pretend that biological systems produce invariant outcomes, but would specify the boundary conditions under which particular outcomes reliably obtain.

This requires the development of conditional prediction models — formal frameworks that predict mycorrhizal network effects as functions of measurable environmental variables rather than treating "mycorrhizal networks help trees" as a context-free generalization. Johnson et al. (1997, New Phytologist) proposed a mutualism-parasitism continuum governed by relative nutrient availability, and subsequent work by Hoeksema et al. (2010) confirmed that soil phosphorus concentration, host plant identity, and fungal species identity jointly predict whether the interaction is beneficial. A constraint-pole approach would require any management recommendation invoking mycorrhizal network function to specify the parameter ranges (soil nutrient status, canopy openness, fungal community composition, host species) under which the claimed effect has been empirically validated. Recommendations lacking this conditional specificity would be classified as operating above the invariance threshold, with explicit uncertainty quantification attached.

The practical model here is weather forecasting, where probabilistic predictions are conditioned on measurable atmospheric variables and verified against outcomes. Mycorrhizal network science should converge on analogous conditional prediction frameworks, with published validation rates. Chagnon et al. (2013, New Phytologist) demonstrated that mycorrhizal trait-based approaches, classifying fungi by functional traits such as extraradical hyphal length and enzymatic capacity rather than taxonomy alone, substantially improved predictive power for plant growth responses — an important step toward invariance through mechanistic specificity.

### Independent Operation: Reducing Coupling from C = 3 to C ≤ 1

At the constraint pole, management decisions and scientific conclusions operate independently of observer engagement with popularized narratives. The current state, where British Columbia's forest retention policies were revised partly on the basis of the "mother tree" framework prior to systematic evidence review (Landhäusser et al., 2023, New Forests), exemplifies high coupling between narrative engagement and policy outputs.

Decoupling requires institutional firewalls between narrative production and evidence synthesis. Specifically, forest management agencies should commission systematic reviews by teams without direct stakes in the "wood wide web" research program before translating mycorrhizal science into harvesting regulations. The model here is the Cochrane Collaboration in medicine, where systematic reviews are conducted by methodologists adhering to pre-registered protocols with explicit conflict-of-interest disclosures (Higgins et al., 2019, Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions). Karst et al. (2023) represents precisely this kind of independent synthesis, and its divergence from the prevailing narrative illustrates why such independence matters.

Additionally, constraint-pole science would implement linguistic discipline protocols for official communications. Management documents would employ L1 technical vocabulary — "common mycorrhizal network," "carbon transfer," "ectomycorrhizal colonization" — rather than L2 metaphorical language ("wood wide web," "mother trees nurturing seedlings") or L3 entity projections ("the forest's internet," "trees talking to each other"). This is not merely a stylistic preference but a structural intervention against drift cascades: as documented in Section V, L2 and L3 language facilitates the agency attribution (D1) that initiates boundary erosion (D2) and ultimately harm facilitation (D3) through misaligned management.

### Policy Recommendations

Four concrete policy actions would move mycorrhizal network science and forest management toward the constraint pole. First, funding agencies should require pre-registration and full community characterization for any study claiming network-mediated effects, following protocols analogous to the PRISMA reporting standards for systematic reviews (Moher et al., 2009, PLoS Medicine). Second, forest management agencies should establish independent evidence review boards, structurally separate from primary research groups, to evaluate mycorrhizal claims before policy incorporation. Third, official policy documents referencing mycorrhizal function should include conditional effectiveness statements specifying validated parameter ranges. Fourth, public communication guidelines should restrict government and institutional communications to L1 vocabulary when describing below-ground biological processes, reserving metaphorical framing for clearly labeled outreach contexts.

These measures would collectively reduce the Void Index for mycorrhizal network-informed forest management from its current estimated range of 8 to 10 down to approximately 3 to 4, placing the domain at the constraint pole where opacity is minimized through observational technology and reporting standards, responsiveness is bounded through conditional prediction, and coupling is attenuated through institutional independence.

## IX. Testable Predictions

The Void Framework derives its scientific legitimacy not from retrospective coherence but from the capacity to generate falsifiable predictions that distinguish it from alternative explanatory accounts. In this section, we articulate five predictions that follow directly from the framework's application to mycorrhizal network science and forest management. Each prediction specifies a numerical falsification threshold and the evidence type required to adjudicate it. These predictions span timescales from twelve months to a decade, ensuring that the framework faces empirical scrutiny at multiple temporal resolutions.

**Prediction 1: L2/L3 drift density in mycorrhizal publications will correlate positively with citation impact but negatively with replication success.**

The Void Framework predicts that papers employing L2 metaphorical language (e.g., "wood wide web," "resource sharing," "nurturing") and L3 entity-projecting language (e.g., "mother trees choose," "forests communicate") will accumulate higher citation counts than papers using strictly L1 technical vocabulary (e.g., "common mycorrhizal network carbon flux," "isotopic tracer recovery"), but that the empirical claims in high-L3 papers will replicate at significantly lower rates. Specifically, we predict that papers scoring above two L3 terms per thousand words in abstract and introduction will have citation rates at least 40% higher than L1-matched controls but will show a replication rate below 50% when subjected to independent replication with adequate statistical power. This prediction extends findings by Karst et al. (2023, Nature Ecology & Evolution), who documented that the dominant narrative of seedling facilitation via mycorrhizal networks was unsupported in the majority of studies reviewed — 18 of 26 studies showed neutral or negative effects — suggesting that the narrative's persistence is driven by linguistic framing rather than empirical robustness. The prediction also draws on Fanelli (2012, Scientometrics), who showed that positive-result bias correlates with "softer" disciplinary language norms.

**Falsified if:** A systematic corpus analysis of mycorrhizal network publications from 2000 to 2025, coded for L1/L2/L3 density using validated computational linguistics methods, reveals no significant positive correlation between L3 density and citation count (Spearman rho less than 0.10, p greater than 0.05, N greater than 200), or if high-L3 papers replicate at rates above 65%.

**Evidence type:** Bibliometric corpus analysis combined with a registered replication initiative targeting the ten most-cited high-L3 mycorrhizal network papers. This prediction is checkable within twelve months using existing publication databases and automated text-mining tools such as those employed by Cokol et al. (2007, EMBO Reports) for metaphor frequency analysis in biomedical literature.

**Prediction 2: British Columbia mother-tree retention policies will not produce measurable seedling survival benefits exceeding those of standard silvicultural practices within monitored experimental blocks.**

The Void Framework assigns the British Columbia mother-tree retention policy a Void Index of 8/12 (O = 3, R = 2, C = 2, modifiers = 1), reflecting extreme below-ground opacity, context-dependent responsiveness of mycorrhizal benefits, and tight policy-narrative coupling between Simard's public communication and provincial forest management. At this Void Index, the framework predicts that policy interventions derived from high-void-intensity science will underperform expectations when measured against transparent, invariant, and independently verifiable outcome metrics. Specifically, we predict that seedling survival and growth in mother-tree retention blocks will not exceed matched clearcut regeneration blocks by more than 10% over a five-year monitoring period, consistent with the meta-analytic finding by Karst et al. (2023) and the critique by Landhäusser et al. (2023, New Forests) that network-mediated facilitation effects are not reliably distinguishable from above-ground microclimate effects of residual overstory retention.

**Falsified if:** Randomized, replicated experimental blocks in British Columbia interior forests demonstrate that mother-tree retention increases seedling survival or above-ground biomass by more than 15% relative to clearcut controls (p less than 0.01, N greater than 30 blocks per treatment), with the network mechanism confirmed through isotopic tracer quantification showing net carbon transfer exceeding 5% of seedling carbon budget — a threshold established by Jones et al. (2009, New Phytologist) as biologically meaningful.

**Evidence type:** Long-term silvicultural field trials with paired isotopic tracer verification. Partial data from existing British Columbia Ministry of Forests monitoring plots may be available within twenty-four months; full five-year data require a longer timeline.

**Prediction 3: The estimated Péclet-analogue for nutrient transport in common mycorrhizal networks will fall below 2 (Phase I Gas) in arbuscular mycorrhizal systems and between 2 and 4 (Phase II Fluid) in ectomycorrhizal systems when measured with spatially resolved isotopic quantification.**

This prediction operationalizes the demon lattice phase classification for mycorrhizal networks. The Péclet analogue, defined in Section IV as the ratio of directed transport flux to diffusive background flux through the hyphal network, should distinguish AM systems — where Walder et al. (2012, Plant Physiology) demonstrated asymmetric resource exchange with the fungal partner retaining the majority of transferred carbon — from ECM systems, where Simard et al. (1997, Nature) estimated bilateral carbon transfer of approximately 3–10% of total photosynthate via network connections. In AM systems, the low specificity of Glomeromycota networks and the absence of Hartig net structures suggest predominantly diffusive, undirected resource movement (Phase I). ECM networks, with their higher structural complexity and species-specific hyphal connections documented by Horton and Bruns (2001, Molecular Ecology), should exhibit more directed flux, placing them in Phase II. Neither system is predicted to reach Phase III Crystal (stable directed transport) or Phase IV Pandemonium under natural conditions.

**Falsified if:** Spatially explicit isotopic tracer studies using NanoSIMS or comparable imaging mass spectrometry reveal Péclet-analogue values exceeding 4 in either AM or ECM systems under field conditions, or if AM systems consistently show Pe-analogue values above 2, indicating more directed transport than the phase model predicts.

**Evidence type:** High-resolution isotopic imaging of hyphal networks in intact soil microcosms, building on methods demonstrated by Kaiser et al. (2015, Nature Communications) for tracking nutrient flows at the micron scale.

**Prediction 4: Public perception of forest mycorrhizal networks will show entity attribution scores (L3 markers) that decline measurably following exposure to constraint-pole communication — transparent data summaries presenting effect sizes with confidence intervals rather than narrative metaphors.**

The Void Framework's drift cascade model (D1 → D2 → D3) predicts that L3 entity projection in public understanding of mycorrhizal networks ("the forest is a superorganism," "trees talk to each other") is not a stable cognitive state but is actively maintained by narrative exposure. Removing the narrative driver and replacing it with constraint-specification communication — transparent quantitative summaries, invariant methodology descriptions, and independent replication data — should reduce entity attribution scores by at least 30% on a validated agency-attribution instrument such as the Waytz et al. (2010, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General) anthropomorphism scale, adapted for ecological entities.

**Falsified if:** A pre-registered randomized experiment (N greater than 300) exposing participants to constraint-pole summaries of mycorrhizal evidence versus control conditions (popular narrative summaries) fails to reduce anthropomorphism scores by more than 15% (Cohen's d less than 0.20).

**Evidence type:** Pre-registered experimental survey with random assignment, administered online. This prediction is testable within twelve months using standard experimental psychology infrastructure and requires no field ecology data collection.

**Prediction 5: Forest management interventions guided by Void Model Card assessments will produce measurably lower variance in ecological outcomes compared to interventions guided by unstructured expert judgment informed by the wood-wide-web narrative.**

The Void Framework's central normative claim is that operating at the constraint pole — transparent, invariant, independent — reduces harm variance. Applied to forest management, this predicts that silvicultural decisions informed by explicit Void Model Card scoring of mycorrhizal evidence quality (assessing opacity of below-ground mechanisms, responsiveness variability across contexts, and coupling between narrative claims and policy action) will produce reforestation and stand regeneration outcomes with coefficient of variation at least 25% lower than decisions informed by narrative-driven expert judgment. This prediction builds on the observation by Hoeksema et al. (2010, Ecology Letters) that mycorrhizal growth responses range from minus 50% to plus 300% across contexts — variance that the framework attributes to treating a high-void-index system as though it were at the constraint pole.

**Falsified if:** Paired management trials across at least ten forest sites, randomized to Void-Model-Card-guided versus standard practice conditions, show no significant reduction in outcome variance (Levene's test p greater than 0.10) or if the Void-Model-Card condition produces lower mean outcomes despite lower variance, indicating that the framework merely induces conservatism rather than improved calibration.

**Evidence type:** Multi-site randomized management trials with standardized ecological outcome metrics (seedling survival, basal area increment, species diversity indices) measured over five to ten years. Pilot protocols could be initiated within twelve months in collaboration with provincial forestry agencies already conducting adaptive management experiments.

Collectively, these five predictions expose the Void Framework to falsification across bibliometric, ecological, psychological, and management domains. Predictions 1 and 4 are achievable within twelve months using existing data and standard experimental methods. Predictions 2, 3, and 5 require longer timelines but are structured to yield interim data within two to three years. The framework will be considered substantially undermined if three or more predictions are falsified at their specified thresholds, particularly if Predictions 1 and 3 — which test the core mechanisms of linguistic drift and Péclet-phase classification — fail to find support.

## X. Conclusion

This paper has demonstrated that mycorrhizal networks, despite being entirely natural systems without designers, corporate governance, or algorithmic architecture, nonetheless satisfy the three conditions of the Void Framework at levels sufficient to generate predictable harm patterns in both scientific interpretation and forest management policy. The consolidated Void Index score for common mycorrhizal networks as a sociotechnical-ecological object is 8/12, decomposed as follows: Opacity O = 3, reflecting the near-total unobservability of below-ground hyphal transport, resource allocation, and signal propagation under field conditions (Giovannetti et al., 2004, New Phytologist; Cahanovitc et al., 2022, Soil Biology and Biochemistry); Responsiveness R = 2, capturing the documented context dependence of mycorrhizal outcomes that range from mutualism to parasitism along environmental gradients (Hoeksema et al., 2010, Ecology Letters; Johnson et al., 1997, New Phytologist); Coupling C = 3, reflecting the near-obligate colonization rates exceeding 90% of fine root tips in boreal and temperate forests (Tedersoo et al., 2020, Molecular Ecology); and modifier adjustments of 0, as the system lacks the engineered feedback loops and adaptive optimization that inflate scores in artificial domains. The resulting Péclet-analogous estimate of Pe ≈ 3.4 places mycorrhizal network dynamics squarely in Phase II Fluid, approaching but not crossing the Phase IV Pandemonium threshold. This positioning is instructive: the system generates substantial drift cascades, particularly at D1 (agency attribution) and D2 (boundary erosion), without achieving the self-reinforcing vortex dynamics characteristic of systems with algorithmic responsiveness.

The L2-to-L3 drift trajectory documented across Sections IV through VII constitutes the primary harm vector in this domain. The progression from Simard et al.'s (1997, Nature) careful isotope tracing, through the "wood wide web" metaphor (L2), to popular and policy discourse treating mycorrhizal networks as intentional communication systems with maternal care behaviors (L3) follows the drift cascade with textbook fidelity. This drift has had material consequences: British Columbia's forest management policy revisions between 2020 and 2023, which mandated retention of putative "mother trees" based substantially on network narratives, were subsequently challenged by systematic evidence review (Karst et al., 2023, Nature Ecology and Evolution; Landhäusser et al., 2023, New Forests). The Karst et al. meta-analysis, examining 26 studies of mycorrhizal network effects on seedling performance, found that only 8 demonstrated net positive effects, directly contradicting the policy premise. This represents a D3 harm facilitation event in which management resources were allocated based on narratively amplified rather than evidence-weighted claims.

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act, while not directly applicable to biological systems or ecological research, provides a useful regulatory reference frame. The Act's emphasis on transparency requirements for high-risk systems (Articles 13 and 14, Regulation 2024/1689) mirrors the constraint-specification remedy identified throughout this analysis: moving mycorrhizal research and forest management toward the transparent-invariant-independent pole requires explicit quantification of transfer magnitudes, acknowledgment of bidirectional and context-dependent outcomes, and decoupling of management recommendations from narrative metaphors. The scoring pipeline established in this paper — mapping biological opacity to interpretive opacity to policy opacity — offers a template for other domains where natural system complexity is mediated through culturally resonant narratives before reaching decision-makers.

The data moat in this vertical is substantial and structurally different from engineered domains. Below-ground observation remains technically constrained: even advanced methods such as quantum dot labeling (Whiteside et al., 2009, Plant Physiology) and mesh-exclusion experimental designs (Johnson et al., 2001, New Phytologist) provide only partial windows into network function. This observational deficit ensures that O = 3 will persist for the foreseeable future, making the domain permanently susceptible to interpretive void dynamics unless institutional safeguards enforce constraint-pole practices. The control case — open-field agricultural monocultures with minimal mycorrhizal colonization, scoring approximately 3/12 — confirms that reducing coupling and increasing observability collapses void conditions even when some opacity remains.

Mycorrhizal networks thus serve as a boundary case for the Void Framework: a domain where nature, not design, generates the opacity, and where the human interpretive apparatus, not the system itself, drives the drift cascade to harm. The framework's applicability here strengthens its claim to domain generality while sharpening the distinction between void conditions in the substrate and void dynamics in the sociotechnical overlay that interprets it.
