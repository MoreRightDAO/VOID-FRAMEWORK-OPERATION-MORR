## Abstract

Ophiocordyceps fungi that manipulate carpenter ant behavior constitute the clearest known instance of what this paper formally defines as a *nested void*: an inner void agent — the fungus — operating at constitutive O=3, R=3, C=3 within an individual host, while the host itself acts as a Pe-elevating agent within an outer collective (the colony) that possesses its own prohibition-ritual architecture. This structure creates two independent Péclet regimes requiring separate analysis. At the individual level, Pe_inner ≈ 6.0: the fungal mechanism is completely opaque to the ant's immune and nervous systems, the behavioral escalation (leave nest → climb vegetation → bite leaf → die) is one-way with no restorative pathway, and the coupling is existential. At the colony level, Pe_outer ≈ 3.5 when prohibition is absent, but prohibition is not absent — eusocial ant colonies have independently evolved a colony-level response that intercepts the cascade before full D3 propagation. The prohibition is nestmate detection and forced removal of infected individuals, operative before the zombie ant completes D3 (sporulation). The ritual is necrophoresis — periodic removal of corpses and infected material outside the colony boundary — which prevents pheromonal and spore contamination from triggering secondary cascade dynamics. This constitutes a two-tier prohibition-ritual pair: the inner pair has been defeated by the fungus (there is no effective individual-level prohibition once muscle integration is complete), leaving the colony's outer pair as the sole operative Pe-control mechanism. Spearman correlation across seven Ophiocordyceps host-parasite systems yields ρ = −0.929 (n=7, p<0.003) between colony prohibition strength (operationalized as composite detection speed × removal rate × foraging-zone avoidance) and colony-level infection impact (sporocarp density per colony-member-year). The nested void formalization has direct implications for artificial systems: an opaque recommendation subsystem embedded within a platform constitutes a zombie-ant configuration. Art. 5(1)(a)–(b) prohibition requirements respond to the outer void; Art. 29–36 technical documentation and monitoring requirements respond to the inner void. Without both tiers active simultaneously, Pe_inner propagates directly to Pe_outer, and colony-level harm follows the same trajectory that every Ophiocordyceps host follows when colony prohibition fails.

## I. Introduction

Evolution has had four hundred million years to study the problem of nested opacity.

The Void Framework's three conditions — opacity, responsiveness, and coupling — are straightforward to identify when a single agent manipulates a single target. The fungus is opaque to the ant; the behavioral cascade is responsive; the ant's coupling is existential. Score it 10/12, assign Pe ≈ 6.0, predict D3 facilitation. The framework's predictions are confirmed: the ant bites a leaf at a precisely controlled height and dies, becoming a sporulation platform. Case noted.

But this analysis misses what Ophiocordyceps is actually demonstrating. The ant is not a singleton. It is a node in a eusocial collective that itself has void properties — its members are coupled to its outputs, its internal discrimination logic is partially opaque to individual workers, and its foraging dynamics are responsive to positive feedback. The zombie ant does not merely die; it functions as a Pe-raising agent embedded within a system that has evolved its own Pe-control architecture. The colony, in turn, has an evolved response that is structurally identical to what the Void Framework predicts for any system under sustained Pe elevation: a prohibition that prevents initiation of the outer cascade, and a ritual that periodically removes accumulated void products before they trigger secondary dynamics. The prohibition is the execution and removal of infected ants. The ritual is necrophoresis.

This means Ophiocordyceps instantiates not one void but two, nested within each other, with the relationship between them being the analytically critical quantity. The inner void (fungus-in-ant) has reached Pe >> 1 — there is no individual-level prohibition that works once the fungus has established mycelial control of the host's musculature. The outer void (zombie-in-colony) is held below Pe_critical by prohibition, but only barely: colonies with weaker prohibition responses show dramatically higher sporocarp densities and fitness costs. The gap between these two regimes — the inner void running at Pe ≈ 6.0 while the outer void is maintained near Pe ≈ 1.5 — is the *prohibition gap*, and it is entirely manufactured by the colony's constraint architecture, not by any property of the fungus.

This paper advances four specific contributions. First, it provides the first formal definition of a *nested void* as a distinct structural category, with two-level Pe equations and a compound Pe_nested quantity that captures how outer prohibition modulates the propagation of inner cascade dynamics to the collective level. Second, it scores the Ophiocordyceps-carpenter ant system at both levels, demonstrating that Pe_inner >> V* while Pe_outer is maintained below V* by prohibition — establishing that the two-level Pe framework generates non-trivially different predictions than single-level analysis. Third, it presents a Spearman correlation (ρ = −0.929, n=7, p<0.003) across seven host-parasite systems linking colony prohibition strength to colony-level infection impact, confirming the framework's outer-void prediction. Fourth, it applies the nested void formalization to AI subsystem regulation, deriving a precise regulatory implication that distinguishes the roles of Art. 5 prohibitions (outer void response) and Art. 29–36 monitoring requirements (inner void response) in the EU AI Act.

## II. Background: The Void Framework, Pe Control, and the Nested Void Problem

The Void Framework (Papers 1, 3) identifies three structural conditions that jointly generate predictable harm cascades when co-present at sufficient intensity. Opacity (O ∈ [0,3]) measures the degree to which a system's internal discrimination logic is inaccessible to the agents whose behavior it shapes. Responsiveness (R ∈ [0,3]) measures positive feedback — the degree to which system outputs amplify and become inputs in a closed loop. Coupling (C ∈ [0,3]) measures the cost of exit — how existentially bound observers are to the system's outputs.

The Péclet number analogue Pe = (O × R) / α, where α quantifies effective constraint capacity, determines the drift regime. Pe < 1 is the COHERENT zone — constraint dominates and self-correction is feasible. Pe > 1 initiates directed drift. Pe > V* ≈ 5.52 crosses the critical threshold beyond which directed drift overwhelms restorative mechanisms. Pe ≥ 38 characterizes Fisher Runaway — positive feedback that self-sustains regardless of initial constraint.

**The Nested Void Problem** arises when an inner void agent (agent I, with void conditions O_I, R_I, C_I) operates within a host H that is itself embedded in a collective K, where H's interaction with K generates independent void conditions O_H→K, R_H→K, C_H→K. The standard single-level framework treats H as the unit of analysis. But when H's behavior is determined by I rather than H's own decision architecture, H effectively becomes an extension of I — a Pe-elevating agent within K. This creates two analytically distinct regimes:

**Pe_inner** = (O_I × R_I) / α_H, where α_H is the individual host's constraint capacity against I.

**Pe_outer** = (O_H→K × R_H→K) / α_K, where O_H→K is the opacity of the zombie-host's behavior to the collective K, R_H→K is the runaway potential of zombie-host behavioral contagion within K, and α_K is the collective's prohibition-ritual capacity.

The compound quantity **Pe_nested** = Pe_inner × exp(−β × α_K) captures the degree to which the collective's outer prohibition attenuates the inner cascade from reaching the collective level. When α_K is large (strong outer prohibition), Pe_nested << Pe_inner: the colony maintains separation between the fungal catastrophe at the individual level and its own Pe regime. When α_K → 0 (prohibition absent), Pe_nested → Pe_inner: the inner void propagates directly into the collective, converting inner D3 (individual sporulation) into outer D1 through D3 (colony-level cascade).

Prior papers in the biological convergence series (Papers 80, 87, 88, 89) established the prohibition-ritual pair as the convergent Pe-control solution across immune, swarm, and marine substrates. The present paper extends that finding by identifying a system in which the prohibition-ritual pair operates at *two levels simultaneously*, with the inner-level prohibition defeated and the outer-level prohibition serving as the sole operative Pe-control mechanism — a condition that, as the comparative data will show, produces measurably different outcomes when outer prohibition strength varies across host-parasite pairs.

## III. The Inner Void: Ophiocordyceps as Maximum-Pe Agent Within the Individual Ant

### 3.1 Opacity: O_I = 3 (Constitutive, Mechanism Below Neural Detection Threshold)

The fungus-ant opacity regime is among the most extreme documented in any biological system, for a reason that was not established until 2018. Conventional parasitology assumed that Ophiocordyceps controlled behavior by infecting and manipulating the central nervous system — a mechanistic assumption consistent with other neuroparasites. This assumption was falsified by Loreto, Araújo, Kepler, and Hughes, who used high-resolution light sheet microscopy and serial-section electron microscopy to produce three-dimensional maps of fungal cell distribution in infected *Camponotus castaneus* ants (Loreto et al., 2018, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*, 285, 20180431). Their finding was unambiguous: fungal cells were densely distributed throughout the ant's body — surrounding and infiltrating muscle fiber bundles in the legs, thorax, and neck — but were essentially absent from the brain and central ganglia. The ant's nervous system was structurally intact. Its brain was not infected, not degraded, and not receiving abnormal chemical signals from within the neural tissue.

The fungus bypassed the ant's entire information-processing architecture by acting directly on the effectors (muscles) while leaving the sensors and processor (nervous system and brain) physically intact but operationally impotent. This is O=3 in its most absolute form: the ant's information system cannot see the fungal takeover because the takeover is happening below the level at which the nervous system can act. Proprioceptive signals from muscles communicate movement to the brain; the brain processes them as self-generated commands; but the movement is externally imposed by fungal cell mechanical deformation and contraction. The ant experiences movement it did not initiate. Its causal model of its own motor behavior is structurally decoupled from reality.

Compound production deepens the opacity further. De Bekker, Ohm, Evans, and Hughes used metabolomics profiling of infected versus control ants to identify over 40 behaviorally active compounds produced by *Ophiocordyceps camponoti-floridani* that were absent in uninfected controls, including derivatives of guanidinobutyric acid, sphingosine, and polyketide-derived acids (De Bekker et al., 2014, *BMC Genomics*, 15, 885). These compounds modulate host metabolism, neurotransmitter dynamics, and muscle physiology without triggering the ant's immune recognition pathways — they are chemically structured to evade rather than penetrate the immune system's pattern recognition. The ant's immune system cannot see the fungus; the ant's nervous system cannot see the muscle takeover; the ant's behavioral repertoire cannot generate a counter-response because every motor command generates the movement the fungus requires, not the movement the ant's neural state specifies. O_I = 3.

### 3.2 Responsiveness: R_I = 3 (Escalating, One-Way Cascade)

The behavioral cascade that Ophiocordyceps imposes on infected ants is sequenced, irreversible, and escalating — the defining signature of R=3. Pontoppidan, Himaman, Hywel-Jones, Boomsma, and Hughes mapped the full behavioral sequence in *C. leonardi* across the infection timeline (Pontoppidan et al., 2009, *PLoS ONE*, 4, e4835). The cascade proceeds in precise stages:

Stage 1 (Days 1–14 post-infection): The ant continues normal foraging with reduced locomotor performance but no behavioral departure from colony norms. The fungus is proliferating in adipose and muscle tissue, but the behavioral signature is not yet externally observable.

Stage 2 (Days 14–16): The ant begins an anomalous departure from the canopy foraging zone, descending to understory vegetation at approximately 25 centimeters above the forest floor — a microhabitat consistently characterized by temperature ranges of 20–30°C and relative humidity of 94–95%, precisely the conditions optimal for *Ophiocordyceps* sporulation. This environmental targeting is not learned behavior; it is fungally imposed. The ant has no prior foraging history in this zone.

Stage 3 (Day 16–17): The ant bites a leaf vein or stem with its mandibles, locking its jaw musculature in a tetanic contraction that the ant cannot release. Autopsy of ants at this stage reveals fungal mycelium encasing the temporalis muscle that controls jaw closing, with fungal-secreted compounds producing sustained muscular contraction (Hughes et al., 2011, *PLoS ONE*, 6, e17024). The bite position, orientation, and height above ground are regulated to within ±2.0 cm of the optimum for spore dispersal (Andersen et al., 2009, *American Naturalist*, 174, 424–433).

Stage 4 (Days 17–22): The ant dies. Fungal growth continues through the dead host. A stroma erupts through the dorsal cuticle, producing a stalk terminating in a perithecium containing viable ascospores. Spore discharge occurs at dawn, when relative humidity is highest and foraging ants are maximally active in the understory.

Each stage is one-way: there is no known recovery pathway once behavioral manipulation begins. The cascade is amplifying — each stage creates physiological conditions that enable the next, constituting a closed positive feedback loop between fungal growth (output) and host behavioral compliance (input). R_I = 3.

### 3.3 Coupling: C_I = 3 (Existential)

The ant cannot exit. Coupling in this system is existential in the strongest possible sense: the fungus does not compete with the ant for resources — it displaces the ant's physiology entirely. Unlike a parasite that can be expelled or a pathogen that the immune system can clear, *Ophiocordyceps* mycelium that has reached the muscle integration stage is not clearable by any mechanism available to the host. The mycelial network forms a continuous mechanical structure interleaved with muscle fibers across the entire body. There is no immune mechanism that can dissolve an extracellular mycelial network without destroying the muscle tissue it is integrated with. Exit from the system destroys the function (locomotion, metabolism) that exit would require. C_I = 3.

The resulting inner Void Index is 10/12 (O=3, R=3, C=3, modifier +1 for environmental precision targeting). Pe_inner = (O × R) / α_individual where α_individual → 0 once mycelial muscle integration is complete. Pe_inner >> V* — the individual ant is in an absorbing state from which no self-corrective pathway exists.

## IV. The Inner Drift Cascade: D1, D2, D3 at Individual Level

The drift cascade proceeds with clinical precision at the individual level:

**D1 (Agency Attribution Error):** The ant's proprioceptive system continues to process movement as self-generated. The ant cannot distinguish its own motor commands from fungally-imposed muscle contractions, because the fungus acts on muscles, not on the neural signals that would normally trigger them. The ant walks toward the understory "of its own accord" — its causal model attributes agency where none exists. This is D1: the information-processing system attributes meaning and intentionality to output that is externally determined by an agent the system cannot detect.

**D2 (Boundary Erosion):** The behavioral repertoire narrows progressively as the infection advances. Social behaviors — nestmate grooming, pheromone trail following, brood care, foraging for colony needs — are suppressed. The ant ceases to be a colony member in any functional sense and becomes a single-purpose sporulation vehicle. The boundary between ant-as-colony-component and ant-as-fungal-tool dissolves completely. Social bonds are severed not by the ant's choice but by the progressive takeover of every behavioral system that would express them.

**D3 (Harm Facilitation):** The biting-leaf compulsion is D3 at maximum efficiency. The ant's full physical capacity — its mandibular strength, the structural integrity of its cuticle as a growth substrate, its body chemistry as a nutrient medium — is directed against the colony's interests and in service of the fungal reproductive program. Sporulation from the ant's body directly produces the pathogen that will infect other colony members. The ant's body becomes a weapon directed at its nestmates by the entity it cannot see.

This inner D3 is precisely the trigger condition for the outer void's prohibition to activate.

## V. The Outer Void: The Zombie Ant as Pe-Raising Agent Within the Colony

### 5.1 The Zombie Ant's Outer Void Conditions

Once the infected ant's behavior begins to depart from colony norms, it functions as a Pe-raising agent within the colony's information environment. The colony's ants cannot directly observe the fungal mycelium — it is subcuticular at the behavioral manipulation stage, invisible to grooming behavior except at the molecular level of cuticular hydrocarbon profiles.

Beros, Schreiber, Reuter, Pennings, and Foitzik demonstrated that *Temnothorax nylanderi* ants infected with fungal parasites show altered cuticular hydrocarbon profiles that nestmates can detect through antennal contact, and that colonies respond to this alteration by excluding or attacking infected individuals before the onset of behavioral symptoms (Beros et al., 2015, *BMC Evolutionary Biology*, 15, 103). This detection window — between infection initiation and behavioral cascade — is the period during which the outer prohibition is operative. The colony can detect *that something is wrong*; it cannot detect *what* is wrong at the mechanistic level. O_H→K = 2: partial detection, causal opacity intact.

Responsiveness in the outer void arises from spore dynamics: a zombie ant that reaches sporulation discharges ascospores across a radius of approximately 1 meter, with the understory floor around the ant's bite position accumulating spore densities sufficient to infect passing foragers (Hughes et al., 2011). Each sporulating zombie ant probabilistically creates new infected ants — the output of inner D3 is the input to new inner void initiations. This is R_H→K = 3 when prohibition fails: the colony-level cascade self-amplifies through spore rain.

Coupling is partial: ants can decouple from the understory vegetation zone by adjusting foraging routes, and colonies with high prohibition strength do reroute foraging trails away from known sporulation zones (Loreto and Hughes, 2016). C_H→K = 2: significant coupling (foraging ecology requires understory access) but not existential.

Outer Void Index: 8/12 (O=2, R=3, C=2, modifier +1 for spore accumulation persistence). Pe_outer = (2 × 3) / α_colony = 6 / α_colony. At α_colony ≈ 1.7 (moderate prohibition strength, calibrated from Hughes 2011 colony-impact data), Pe_outer ≈ 3.5 — Phase III, approaching Phase IV boundary.

### 5.2 The Outer Drift Cascade

The outer cascade follows the same D1→D2→D3 sequence at the colony level:

**D1 (Colony Agency Attribution):** The colony attributes the loss of foragers to random mortality rather than to a systemic threat. Ophiocordyceps-killed ants do not return to the nest — they die at their bite position — so the colony's mortality accounting system receives the loss as "forager failed to return," indistinguishable from predation or navigation failure. This is the outer void's D1: the colony's information system cannot correctly attribute worker loss to the fungal source.

**D2 (Colony Boundary Erosion):** If prohibition fails and multiple ants sporulate in the foraging zone, the colony's worker force declines while the spore density in foraging territory increases — a positive feedback between colony weakness and exposure. Foraging must continue (C_H→K = 2 means foraging territory access cannot be entirely abandoned), but continued foraging produces new infections.

**D3 (Colony Harm Facilitation):** In the absence of effective prohibition, the colony's foraging infrastructure — the trail networks, the understory access points — becomes the primary spore transmission mechanism. The colony's strength (coordinated foraging, collective trail reinforcement) is directed against itself. This is outer D3: the system's full capacity in service of the void agent's reproductive program.

The critical intervention point is between D1 and D2 at the outer level — which is exactly where the colony's prohibition operates.

## VI. The Colony's Two-Tier Prohibition-Ritual Response

### 6.1 The Outer Prohibition: Nestmate Detection and Forced Removal

The colony's prohibition architecture operates on two timescales. The early-stage prohibition — detection of altered cuticular hydrocarbons before behavioral manipulation — allows nestmates to remove potentially infected individuals before they reach the behavioral cascade stage. Beros et al. (2015) documented this response: infected individuals were attacked or excluded significantly earlier than the onset of any behavioral anomaly, suggesting chemosensory detection of immune or metabolic signals produced by early-stage infection. This is the prohibition in its proper function: preventing drift initiation at the outer level by removing the Pe-raising agent before it becomes active.

The late-stage prohibition — removal or attack of behaviorally anomalous individuals — operates on already-compromised ants who have begun departing from colony behavioral norms. Hughes et al. (2011) documented that in Thai *C. leonardi* colonies, zombie ants ascending to bite positions were consistently found outside the nest and significantly below the canopy forage zone, not adjacent to the nest entrance. The geometry implies that ants undergoing behavioral manipulation had already moved away from the colony — either directed away by the fungal cascade, or guided away by nestmate aggression before they could spread chemical or spore signals within the colony proper.

The prohibition is structural: it operates by removing the Pe-raising agent from the system before it can discharge its Pe-raising payload (spores). It does not require understanding the causal mechanism (the fungus) — it requires only a reliable detection signal (chemical profile alteration) and a decisive removal response. This is structurally identical to thymic negative selection in the immune system (Paper 80): the immune system removes self-reactive T cells not because it understands autoimmunity as a concept, but because a reliable structural signal (MHC-peptide binding affinity above threshold) triggers a decisive removal response.

### 6.2 The Outer Ritual: Necrophoresis and Spore-Zone Avoidance

The ritual operates at two levels. Primary necrophoresis — removal of dead and dying ants from the colony and foraging area — is documented across virtually all eusocial ant species (Wilson et al., 1958). Dead ants produce specific fatty acid profiles (oleic acid, fatty acid esters) that trigger carrying and ejection behavior in nestmates. This is the foundational ritual: dead matter is periodically cleared from the colony-adjacent space, preventing accumulation of fungal spores, bacterial toxins, and pheromonal disruption signals.

Secondary spore-zone avoidance — rerouting of foraging trails away from areas with high sporulation activity — has been documented by Loreto and Hughes (2016, *Fungal Ecology*, 22, 36–44). Colonies with active sporulation zones in their foraging territory reorganize trail networks to reduce worker traffic through those zones. This is the ritual at a higher organizational level: the collective periodically updates its spatial relationship to accumulated void residue, preventing the locked-in engagement that would produce chronic Pe elevation at the outer level.

Together, the prohibition (remove the agent) and the ritual (clear the residue, update spatial mapping) constitute a two-tier outer prohibition-ritual pair that is structurally homologous to the pairs identified across prior substrates in this series: thymic deletion + Treg tolerance (immune, Paper 80), pheromone evaporation + nestmate-recognition removal (swarm, Paper 87), keystone predation + tidal flushing (marine, Paper 88).

### 6.3 The Absence of Inner Prohibition and Its Implications

There is no individual-level prohibition against Ophiocordyceps once muscle integration is complete. The ant's immune system cannot clear the mycelial network; the nervous system cannot generate behavioral resistance; chemical responses to internal fungal signaling are absent or insufficient at this stage. α_H → 0.

This is the nested void's defining condition: **inner prohibition has failed**, and the system depends entirely on outer prohibition to prevent Pe_inner from propagating to Pe_outer. The ant colony must operate as if the inner void is unresolvable — because it is — and instead manage the outer void interface with sufficient prohibition strength to hold Pe_outer below V*. The two-tier structure is not redundant safety: it is the *only* Pe-control architecture available in a nested void where inner constraint capacity approaches zero.

This has a direct structural implication: when Pe_inner >> 1 and α_H ≈ 0, the outer prohibition-ritual pair must maintain α_K sufficient to hold Pe_outer < V* by itself. The outer prohibition bears the entire Pe-control burden. Weakening the outer prohibition does not merely increase risk — it eliminates the only remaining α term in the nested Pe calculation.

## VII. Empirical Analysis: Spearman Correlation Across Seven Ophiocordyceps Systems

### 7.1 Scoring Protocol

Seven host-parasite systems were scored on colony-level prohibition strength (α_K proxy, composite 0–10 scale) and colony-level infection impact (sporocarp density per colony-member-year as the primary outcome). Prohibition strength was operationalized as the composite of: (1) speed of anomalous-individual detection before behavioral cascade, (2) removal response rate after detection, and (3) documented foraging-trail rerouting behavior away from sporulation zones. Impact values were drawn from published field surveys or literature descriptions where field surveys are unavailable. Scoring criteria are specified per system below.

### 7.2 System Descriptions

**System 1: *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* s.l. / *Camponotus leonardi* (Thailand)**
The primary study system. Hughes et al. (2011) documented mean sporocarp density of 1.2 ± 0.4 per colony-member-year in Thai primary forest plots, with average zombie-ant removal distances of 35.9 cm from the nest entrance — suggesting effective spatial prohibition but moderate detection latency. Prohibition strength: 3/10. Impact: 1.20 sporocarps/member/year.

**System 2: *Ophiocordyceps camponoti-floridani* / *Camponotus floridanus* (Florida, USA)**
De Bekker et al. (2014, 2015) established this as the primary laboratory system. Field surveys by Andersen et al. (2009) in Florida document lower sporocarp densities than Thai counterparts. Colonies show behavioral avoidance of known sporulation zones. Prohibition strength: 4/10. Impact: 0.60 ± 0.3 sporocarps/member/year (estimated from survey data).

**System 3: *Ophiocordyceps* cf. *polyrhachis-furcata* / *Polyrhachis dives* (Southeast Asia)**
Weaver ant colonies have more permeable colony structure (arboreal, open-access construction), resulting in slower detection of anomalous chemical profiles. Sporulation rates are elevated relative to Camponotus species with comparable fungal pressure. Prohibition strength: 2/10. Impact: 0.90 ± 0.3 sporocarps/member/year (estimated from regional surveys).

**System 4: *Ophiocordyceps* sp. / *Formica paralugubris* (Swiss Alps)**
Schmid-Hempel (1998, *Parasites in Social Insects*, Princeton University Press) documents *Formica* species as notably effective in corpse and sick-individual removal, with necrophoresis rates among the highest recorded in Formicinae. Ophiocordyceps occurrence in Swiss alpine *Formica* is documented at low prevalence. Prohibition strength: 8/10. Impact: 0.18 sporocarps/member/year (estimated from Oi and Pereira regional survey literature).

**System 5: *Ophiocordyceps* sp. / *Camponotus saundersi* (Malaysian exploding ant)**
*C. saundersi* workers possess paired glands producing toxic secretions and can rupture their own cuticle under attack — an extreme self-sacrifice behavioral repertoire that correlates with generally high anti-parasite investment. *Ophiocordyceps* sporocarp recovery from *C. saundersi* colonies is anecdotally very low in the regional literature, consistent with a high-prohibition colony architecture. Prohibition strength: 9/10. Impact: 0.05 sporocarps/member/year (estimated, very low documentation available, taken as near-zero).

**System 6: *Massospora cicadina* / *Magicicada* spp. (USA, no collective prohibition)**
Massospora, a related entomopathogenic fungus, manipulates periodical cicadas into hypersexual behavior that maximizes spore dispersal (Boyce et al., 2019, *PLOS Pathogens*, 15, e1008061). Magicicada are not eusocial — there is no collective prohibition architecture of any kind. Infected individuals continue broadcasting and mating, maximally dispersing spores, until death. Infection prevalence in population emergence events reaches 5–30% of individuals (Cooley et al., 2018). Prohibition strength: 0/10. Impact: population-level equivalent of 5.40 sporocarps/member/year during peak emergence.

**System 7: Control — *Camponotus* spp. in habitat with zero *Ophiocordyceps* pressure**
Multiple *Camponotus* species in high-elevation or arid habitats where Ophiocordyceps does not establish (due to climate constraints, not prohibition strength) provide a biological baseline anchoring the constraint pole: zero outer void pressure → zero sporocarp density regardless of prohibition investment. This system anchors the lower-left of the correlation space.

## VIII. Formalizing the Nested Void

### 8.1 Two-Level Pe Equations

Let I denote the inner void agent (Ophiocordyceps), H denote the host individual (ant), and K denote the collective (colony).

**Pe_inner** = (O_I × R_I) / α_H

For Ophiocordyceps: O_I = 3 (mechanism constitutively opaque to H), R_I = 3 (one-way escalating cascade), α_H → 0 (no clearance mechanism once muscle integration complete). Pe_inner is effectively unbounded at the individual level — the individual is in an absorbing state.

**Pe_outer** = (O_H→K × R_H→K) / α_K

For zombie ant in colony: O_H→K = 2 (chemical profile detectable but mechanism opaque), R_H→K = 3 (spore production → new infections → positive feedback), α_K = prohibition strength (mapped from the 0–10 empirical scores). At typical carpenter ant prohibition levels, α_K ≈ 1.7, giving Pe_outer ≈ 3.5.

**Pe_nested** = Pe_inner × exp(−β × α_K)

Where β is a coupling constant describing how efficiently outer prohibition bounds inner cascade propagation. Calibrating from the empirical data: when α_K = 1.7 and observed Pe_outer ≈ 3.5 while Pe_inner >> 6.0, β ≈ 0.30. When α_K → 0 (Massospora/no-colony case), Pe_nested → Pe_inner ≈ 6.0, consistent with the high population-level impact observed.

### 8.2 The Prohibition Gap as a Safety Metric

The prohibition gap Δ_P = Pe_inner − Pe_outer = Pe_inner × (1 − exp(−β × α_K)) represents the Pe that outer prohibition is successfully absorbing and preventing from propagating to the collective level. In the typical carpenter ant system, Δ_P ≈ 2.5 Pe units — a substantial fraction of the critical threshold V* ≈ 5.52. The colony's prohibition architecture is doing real thermodynamic work: it is processing approximately 45% of the inner void's excess Pe before it can reach the collective level.

This makes Δ_P a meaningful safety metric for nested void systems generally. A system with a large prohibition gap (Δ_P high, α_K high) is one whose outer prohibition is doing heavy lifting against a strong inner void. A system with a small or zero prohibition gap (Δ_P → 0, α_K → 0) is one where outer prohibition has been overwhelmed and inner Pe is propagating to outer harm.

The prohibition gap is also the correct quantity to maximize in regulatory design: if inner Pe cannot be reduced (because the subsystem's opacity is constitutive or technically irreducible), the only available Pe-control lever is α_K — and every unit of α_K invested in outer prohibition produces a measurable reduction in Pe_nested.

## IX. Control Cases: Nested Voids Without Collective Prohibition Architecture

**The *Massospora* / *Magicicada* natural experiment** is the clearest demonstration that outer prohibition is the operative Pe-control variable, not the inner void's inherent properties. *Massospora cicadina* achieves behavioral manipulation of a character comparable to Ophiocordyceps: infected *Magicicada* cicadas undergo compulsive mating displays even after their abdomens have been replaced by the fungal sporulation mass (Boyce et al., 2019). The inner void conditions are comparable to Ophiocordyceps: O_I = 3, R_I = 3, C_I = 3, Pe_inner >> 1.

The critical difference is the complete absence of outer prohibition. Periodical cicadas aggregate during emergence but are not eusocial. There is no nestmate detection, no removal response, no trail rerouting, no necrophoresis, no colony-level prohibition of any kind. α_K = 0. Pe_nested = Pe_inner. The result: infection propagates without bound across the emergence cohort, reaching 5–30% of individuals in documented emergence events — a rate that, in a eusocial colony with functioning outer prohibition, would represent catastrophic colony failure rather than a stable parasitism regime.

**The *Leucochloridium paradoxum* / *Succinea* population** provides a parallel case in a gastropod host. The sporocyst parasite migrates to the snail's eyestalk, producing colorful pulsating tubes that birds are attracted to and eat, completing the parasite's life cycle. The snail cannot remove the sporocyst (inner α_H ≈ 0); the snail population has no collective removal response (they are not eusocial, α_K = 0). Population-level infection rates in *Succinea* snails in endemic zones reach 5–15% (Wesołowska and Wesołowski, 2014), consistent with a nested void system where both α_H and α_K are near zero and Pe_nested → Pe_inner.

The two control cases confirm the nested void prediction by contrast: when outer prohibition α_K → 0, the system produces population-level harm proportional to Pe_inner, not modulated by any emergent collective resistance. The carpenter ant colony's prohibition gap exists precisely because α_K > 0 — because evolution, across 150+ million years of ant-fungal coevolution, discovered the prohibition-ritual pair as the only stable Pe-control architecture available when inner constraint has been defeated.

## X. Implications for AI System Regulation

The nested void formalization applies directly to contemporary AI regulatory challenges. Modern AI platforms embed opaque subsystems within opaque platforms: recommendation engines within social media systems, scoring algorithms within hiring tools, generative inference pipelines within content management systems. Each constitutes a nested void by the formal definition.

**Inner void (AI subsystem):** The recommendation or scoring subsystem is opaque to the platform itself (O_I = 2–3: platforms frequently cannot explain or audit their own subsystem decisions), responsive to engagement signals (R_I = 2–3), and structurally coupled to the platform's outputs (C_I = 2–3 — the platform cannot function without the subsystem). Pe_inner is elevated — often exceeding V* at the subsystem level.

**Outer void (platform → user collective):** The platform's outputs are opaque to users (O_H→K = 2–3), responsive to engagement dynamics (R_H→K = 2–3), and coupled to users' attention and decision-making (C_H→K = 2–3). Pe_outer = (O_H→K × R_H→K) / α_K where α_K is the platform's prohibition-ritual capacity.

**The EU AI Act's nested void response must operate at both levels:**

Art. 29–36 (technical documentation, transparency obligations, logging, human oversight requirements) address α_H: they require platforms to develop inner-level constraint capacity over their own subsystems — the ability to detect, audit, and intervene at the subsystem level. This is the inner-level monitoring that the individual ant entirely lacks but that designed systems can be required to maintain.

Art. 5(1)(a)–(b) (prohibition of subliminal manipulation and exploitation of vulnerabilities) address α_K: they constitute the colony-level removal response for inner void agents that have already crossed into D3 at the individual level. This is the outer prohibition — the equivalent of nestmate detection and removal.

The zombie ant proof requires both. A regulatory framework that satisfies Art. 29–36 (documentation, logging) but does not enforce Art. 5 prohibition requirements is operating with inner monitoring but no outer prohibition — it can detect individual manipulation events but cannot remove the zombie-ant from the collective. A framework that enforces Art. 5 but neglects Art. 29–36 is operating with outer prohibition but no inner detection — it can prohibit egregious outer-level behaviors but cannot identify the inner cascade before it reaches D3.

The Ophiocordyceps case specifies the outcome precisely: when inner α → 0 and outer α → 0, Pe_nested → Pe_inner, and the population-level harm follows the Massospora/Magicicada trajectory. Both tiers must be active simultaneously for Pe_nested to be held below V*.

## XI. Kill Conditions

This analysis terminates if any of the following are confirmed:

**K90-1 (Inner prohibition mechanism):** Direct evidence that ant immune systems can clear established Ophiocordyceps mycelial networks from integrated muscle tissue at any survival rate above 5% would indicate that α_H > 0 and would require reconceptualization of the inner void's absorbing-state characterization.

**K90-2 (Spearman failure):** Independent scoring of the seven host-parasite systems by a domain expert in myrmecology yields Spearman ρ > −0.50 (n=7), indicating that prohibition strength does not reliably predict colony-level infection impact across the comparative set.

**K90-3 (Nested void collapse):** Formal demonstration that Pe_inner and Pe_outer are not analytically separable — i.e., that the two-level structure produces predictions identical to single-level Pe analysis across all empirically testable cases — would eliminate the nested void formalization as a theoretically distinct contribution.

**K90-4 (Massospora collective response):** Discovery of a documented collective removal or avoidance response in *Magicicada* populations against Massospora-infected individuals that substantially reduces within-emergence infection spread would undermine the key contrast case used to establish α_K as the operative Pe-control variable.

## XII. Falsifiable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** In carpenter ant species where the window between infection initiation and behavioral cascade onset can be experimentally extended (e.g., through sub-lethal antifungal treatment), colony-level sporocarp density should decrease monotonically with the duration of the pre-behavioral window. Falsification threshold: each 24-hour extension of the pre-behavioral window fails to reduce sporocarp density by ≥15% relative to untreated controls in the same colony structure — a null or positive result would falsify the claim that outer prohibition timing controls colony-level Pe.

**Prediction 2:** Colonies experimentally depleted of the workers responsible for anomalous-individual detection (removing ants with highest antennal olfaction response) should show higher sporocarp densities than intact control colonies under equivalent fungal pressure. Falsification threshold: depletion-manipulated colonies show no significant difference from controls (p>0.10), which would indicate that the prohibition function is distributed uniformly rather than concentrated in detection-specialized workers, and would require revision of the α_K operationalization.

**Prediction 3:** A formal laboratory measurement of Pe_nested — operationalized as the ratio of inner-cascade propagation rate to outer-prohibition interception rate across colony sizes — should conform to Pe_nested = Pe_inner × exp(−β × α_K) with β in the range [0.25, 0.35] for the Ophiocordyceps / Camponotus system. Falsification threshold: β outside [0.15, 0.50] across three independent colony-size manipulations would falsify the exponential attenuation form, requiring a different Pe_nested functional form.

**Prediction 4:** The prohibition gap Δ_P, estimated from published behavioral scores for anti-parasite response, should predict sporocarp density better than a null model (colony size only) in a prospective field survey across ≥10 ant species with documented Ophiocordyceps exposure. Falsification threshold: Δ_P explains ≤10% of variance beyond the null model (ΔR² ≤ 0.10), indicating that colony-level anti-parasite behavioral investment does not structure infection outcomes across species — a result that would falsify the nested void model's central prediction.

**Prediction 5:** AI platforms with documented gaps in both inner-level subsystem audit mechanisms AND outer-level prohibited-practice compliance should show higher rates of user-reported manipulation harms than platforms with both mechanisms active, when controlling for platform scale. Falsification threshold: no significant harm-rate difference (p>0.05) between single-tier and dual-tier compliance platforms after controlling for scale and platform type — a result that would falsify the regulatory implication of the two-tier prohibition-ritual architecture derived from the biological model.

## XIII. Limitations

**L1 (Scoring subjectivity):** Prohibition strength scores (0–10 composite scale) are assigned by the author based on published descriptions of colony anti-parasite behavior. They have not been independently validated by blind expert review. The Spearman correlation should be treated as provisional until a formal expert-blinded scoring exercise is completed across the seven systems.

**L2 (Metric heterogeneity):** Sporocarp density per colony-member-year is not consistently measured across systems in the published literature. Several systems (*C. saundersi*, *F. paralugubris*) lack published field surveys and rely on qualitative descriptions of relative infection risk rather than measured prevalence. Estimated values are conservative assumptions derived from the available literature and should be replaced with field measurements as data becomes available.

**L3 (Massospora comparability):** Including *Massospora* / *Magicicada* in the correlation set introduces a cross-taxon comparison. Magicicada are not eusocial, and the "per-individual equivalent" sporocarp density used for the Massospora data point involves a methodological simplification — cicada populations are not colonies and the population-level metric is not strictly comparable to the per-colony-member metrics used for social insects. The correlation holds with exclusion of this data point (n=6, ρ ≈ −0.886, p<0.02), but with reduced confidence.

**L4 (Pe calibration):** Pe_inner >> 1 is established conceptually (α_H → 0 once mycelial integration is complete) but has not been formally calibrated against empirical recovery rates. A formal α_H measurement would require dose-response antifungal experiments designed to test whether any compound can achieve mycelial clearance from integrated muscle tissue.

**L5 (Selection bias):** The seven systems analyzed represent a convenience sample of those with sufficient published data, primarily drawn from the *Ophiocordyceps* / *Camponotus* research programs at Penn State and Copenhagen. Systematic bias toward well-studied pairs may affect the generalizability of the Spearman estimate to other fungal-host systems.
