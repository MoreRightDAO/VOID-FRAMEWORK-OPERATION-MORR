## Abstract

Yet no systematic treatment has characterized why these systems resist the opacity-responsiveness-coupling conjunction that the Void Framework identifies as the generative mechanism for predictable harm, nor has any prior work specified the formal boundary conditions under which such resistance degrades. This paper establishes the constraint pole — the class of systems where the Void Index approaches or reaches zero on the twelve-point scale and the Péclet-analogous drift intensity Pe remains at or near zero — as the fixed reference architecture against which all Void Framework predictions must be calibrated.

The Péclet number in canonical transport theory quantifies the ratio of advective to diffusive transport; when Pe approaches zero, advective transport arrests and disturbances dissipate rather than propagate directionally (Bird, Stewart, and Lightfoot, 2002, Transport Phenomena, second edition, Wiley). The Void Framework adapts this formalism: Pe equals zero designates systems in which the directional propagation of agency attribution, boundary erosion, and harm facilitation — the D1 through D3 drift cascade — is structurally impossible because the three generative conditions are suppressed at their source. This paper maps that suppression across three domains and specifies the precise mechanisms by which transparency, invariance, and observer-independence jointly arrest drift initiation.

This paper makes the following contributions. Fifth, it demonstrates that DO-178C Level A certification in aviation software, which adds thirty to fifty percent to development costs across the approximately 7.5 billion dollar aviation software market (Rierson, 2013, Developing Safety-Critical Software, CRC Press), functions as a formal verification regime that reduces opacity to the threshold required for structural transparency. ## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

The Void Framework defines opacity as the degree to which a system's internal mechanisms, decision processes, and causal pathways remain hidden from external inspection. At the constraint pole, opacity approaches zero: every step, transformation, and output is structurally transparent. This section examines how near-zero opacity manifests across the three Pe=0 domains — physical reference systems, formal mathematical structures, and institutional drift-arrest architectures — and specifies the mechanisms through which transparency is maintained. Crucially, it also identifies the boundary conditions under which residual or emergent opacity can reintroduce drift potential even in systems designed to suppress it.

### A. Physical Reference Systems: Definitional Transparency

The 2019 redefinition of the International System of Units represents the most consequential opacity-elimination event in the history of measurement science. Prior to this revision, the kilogram was defined by reference to a single physical artifact: the International Prototype of the Kilogram (IPK), a platinum-iridium cylinder stored under controlled conditions at the Bureau International des Poids et Mesures in Sèvres, France. This artifact-based definition introduced irreducible opacity. The mass of the IPK could drift — and did drift, diverging from national copies by approximately 50 micrograms over a century (Girard, 1994, Metrologia, 31(4), 317–336) — but because the artifact was the definition, no external measurement could detect whether the IPK had changed or its copies had changed. The causal pathway from definition to realization was opaque at precisely the point that mattered most.

The 2019 redefinition eliminated this opacity by fixing Planck's constant at exactly 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ joule-seconds (BIPM, 2019, "The International System of Units," 9th edition). The kilogram is now defined not by an artifact whose internal molecular structure could shift invisibly, but by an invariant numerical relationship to a fundamental constant of nature. Any laboratory with a Kibble balance can independently realize the kilogram to the defined value. The causal chain from constant to measurement is fully inspectable. Stock, Barat, Davis, Picard, and Milton (2015, Metrologia, 52(2), 310–316) demonstrated that the relative standard uncertainty of Kibble balance realizations had reached 1.3 × 10⁻⁸ by the time of redefinition, confirming that the transition from opaque artifact to transparent definition did not sacrifice precision. The opacity score for the post-2019 SI kilogram is 0/3: there is no hidden process, no privileged custodial access, and no unobservable drift mechanism.

Similar transparency characterizes other physical constants serving as reference architecture. The speed of light has been fixed at exactly 299,792,458 meters per second since 1983 (17th Conférence Générale des Poids et Mesures, Resolution 1). The cesium-133 hyperfine transition frequency defining the second (9,192,631,770 Hz) is derivable from quantum electrodynamics and independently reproducible by any laboratory operating an atomic fountain clock. Heavner, Donley, Levi, and Parker (2014, Metrologia, 51(3), 174–182) reported fractional frequency uncertainties of 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ for NIST-F2, confirming that the physical process underlying the definition is transparent to the degree permitted by quantum mechanics. What distinguishes these systems from high-opacity domains is not merely that their outputs are known, but that the process generating each output is fully specified, independently reproducible, and contains no hidden intermediary whose state is inaccessible.

Passive nuclear safety systems achieve near-zero opacity through a different mechanism: elimination of active decision pathways that could harbor hidden states. The Westinghouse AP1000 reactor, which received NRC design certification in 2011 (NRC NUREG-1793, Final Safety Evaluation Report), relies on gravity-driven water injection and natural convection cooling rather than active pumps, operator intervention, or software-mediated control loops. The core cooling function in a loss-of-coolant accident follows from thermodynamics and gravitational potential — processes whose causal chains are specified by physical law and inspectable through engineering analysis. Schulz (2006, Nuclear Engineering and Technology, 38(2), 107–116) documented that the AP1000 design eliminates 35 safety-grade pumps, 7 fans, and approximately 260 safety-grade valves compared to conventional pressurized water reactor designs. Each eliminated active component is an eliminated locus of potential opacity: a pump can fail in ways not immediately visible to operators, a software controller can enter states not anticipated by designers, but gravity operates transparently.

### B. Formal Mathematical Structures: Axiomatic Transparency

In formal mathematics, opacity reaches its theoretical minimum. A valid proof is a finite sequence of statements, each following from axioms and inference rules that are explicitly enumerated. Every step is inspectable. There is no hidden process connecting premises to conclusions — the connection is the sequence of steps itself.

Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, published in Mathematische Annalen in 1911 (volume 71, pages 97–115), establishes that every continuous function mapping a compact convex set to itself has at least a fixed point. The theorem's truth does not depend on who reads it, when it is consulted, or how many observers examine it simultaneously. Its proof (whether the original topological argument or later simplicial approximation proofs) is a public object: every inference can be checked by any agent with sufficient mathematical training. Tarski's lattice-theoretic fixed-point theorem (1955, Pacific Journal of Mathematics, 5(2), 285–309) extends this to order-complete lattices, proving that every monotone function on such a lattice has a least fixed point and a greatest fixed point. The theorem is stated in first-order logic over well-defined algebraic structures. There are no hidden parameters, no privileged interpretive positions, and no steps that resist external audit.

Formal verification extends this axiomatic transparency into computational domains. Hoare's axiomatic semantics (1969, Communications of the ACM, 12(10), 576–580) established the precondition-postcondition calculus that allows program correctness to be derived from the program text itself through mechanically checkable steps. Modern extensions — dependent type theory as implemented in the Coq proof assistant (Bertot and Castéran, 2004, Interactive Theorem Proving and Program Development, Springer), and the Lean theorem prover used for the formalization of Peter Scholze's condensed mathematics (Buzzard, Commelin, and Massot, 2020, reported in Nature, 2021) — reduce opacity further by eliminating the possibility that a proof step is informally justified. When a theorem is mechanically checked, the verification process itself is transparent: the checker's source code is available, its execution is deterministic, and its output is binary.

This is not to claim that mathematical practice in general achieves zero opacity. The distinction between a formal proof and a published mathematical argument is significant. De Millo, Lipton, and Perlis (1979, Communications of the ACM, 22(5), 271–280) argued provocatively that conventional mathematical proofs are social processes, not formal objects, and that errors in published proofs can persist for years. The classification of finite simple groups, completed across thousands of pages by dozens of authors between 1955 and 2004, contained gaps that required a second-generation proof effort (Aschbacher, Lyons, Smith, and Solomon, 2004, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs, volume 172, American Mathematical Society). The opacity in that case was not inherent to mathematical structure but introduced by the social process of proof production — the sheer volume of argumentation exceeded any single observer's capacity to inspect, creating de facto opacity even in a system whose formal properties permit zero opacity. This distinction matters for the Void Framework: mathematical structure is Pe=0, but mathematical practice can introduce opacity that shifts Pe upward.

### C. Institutional Drift-Arrest Architectures: Designed Transparency

Institutional Pe=0 systems achieve low opacity not by the nature of their substrate but by deliberate architectural choice. Double-entry bookkeeping, first systematically codified by Luca Pacioli in 1494 (Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita), enforces transparency through a structural constraint: every transaction must appear as both a debit and a credit, and the ledger must balance. A hidden transaction — one that affects assets without a corresponding liability or equity entry — violates the balancing constraint and is detectable by inspection. Sangster, Stoner, and McCarthy (2008, Accounting Historians Journal, 35(1), 111–150) documented that this architecture has remained structurally unchanged for over five centuries precisely because its transparency properties resist degradation. The Financial Stability Board's 2023 Global Monitoring Report on Non-Bank Financial Intermediation valued global financial assets at approximately $239 trillion within its monitoring scope alone, the overwhelming majority recorded and audited through double-entry systems. The architecture does not prevent fraud — Enron's off-balance-sheet entities demonstrated that opacity can be reintroduced through structural evasion of the balancing constraint (Healy and Palepu, 2003, Journal of Economic Perspectives, 17(2), 3–26) — but it ensures that fraud requires active circumvention of a transparent mechanism rather than exploitation of inherent opacity.

Safety-critical software certification imposes transparency through mandated documentation and traceability. DO-178C, the standard governing airborne software (RTCA, 2011, "Software Considerations in Airborne Systems and Equipment Certification"), requires at Level A — the highest criticality — that every software requirement be traceable to a system requirement, every line of code be traceable to a software requirement, and every test case be traceable to the requirement it verifies. Rierson (2013, Developing Safety-Critical Software: A Practical Guide for Aviation Software and DO-178C Compliance, CRC Press) estimated that Level A certification adds 30–50% to development costs, placing the cost premium at approximately $2.25–$3.75 billion annually across the global aviation software market. This cost premium is the price of opacity elimination: it buys traceability, inspectability, and the structural guarantee that no hidden pathway connects input to output without an auditable justification.

Nuclear regulatory architecture enforces transparency through prescriptive disclosure requirements. Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Part 50, Appendix A specifies 64 General Design Criteria for nuclear power plants, each requiring that safety functions be demonstrable through analysis, testing, and documentation available to the regulator. The NRC's ADAMS (Agencywide Documents Access and Management System) contains over 2.5 million publicly accessible documents as of 2024, representing one of the most comprehensive forced-transparency regimes in any industrial domain. This is institutional opacity scored at 0–1/3: the design basis, safety analyses, and operational records are available not merely to the regulator but to the public.

### D. Residual and Emergent Opacity at the Constraint Pole

A critical finding for framework calibration is that absolute zero opacity is achievable only in formal mathematical structures and in physical constants post-definitional fixing. All physical and institutional systems retain residual opacity sources that, while small, define the boundary conditions for potential drift.

In passive safety systems, residual opacity exists in material degradation pathways: stress corrosion cracking in stainless steel, neutron embrittlement of reactor pressure vessels, and long-term concrete degradation are processes whose rates are known statistically but whose instantaneous state at any point in a specific structure is not fully observable. Shah and MacDonald (1993, Aging and Life Extension of Major Light Water Reactor Components, Elsevier) documented that pressure vessel embrittlement shifts the ductile-to-brittle transition temperature by 50–150°C over a 40-year operating life, a process that cannot be monitored continuously and must be inferred from surveillance coupon testing. This residual opacity scores approximately 0.5/3 on the Void Index opacity dimension — low, but nonzero, and representing the primary pathway through which even passive safety systems could, over sufficient timescales, approach drift conditions.

In institutional architectures, opacity can be reintroduced through complexity proliferation. The Basel III banking accords, designed to impose transparency on bank capital adequacy, generate compliance documentation whose sheer volume creates de facto opacity: the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision's consolidated framework exceeds 1,600 pages (BCBS, 2023, "Basel Framework"), and Haldane (2012, "The Dog and the Frisbee," speech at the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City's 36th Economic Policy Symposium) argued that regulatory complexity had become self-defeating, with major banks employing over 10,000 staff in compliance functions while risk opacity persisted or increased. This phenomenon — transparency-intended architecture generating opacity through volume — represents a mechanism by which Pe=0 institutional designs degrade toward Pe>0, and is taken up in the boundary condition analysis of Section VII.

The overall opacity assessment for the constraint pole is 0–1/3 across all three domains, with formal mathematical structures at 0/3, physical constants at 0/3 post-definitional fixing, passive safety systems at 0.5/3, and institutional drift-arrest architectures at 0.5–1/3 depending on complexity accumulation. This scoring confirms that the first necessary condition for drift cascade initiation — opacity sufficient to prevent external inspection of causal pathways — is structurally suppressed in Pe=0 systems, arresting the D1 stage of the drift cascade before agency can be misattributed to a process whose mechanism is fully visible.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

The Void Framework defines responsiveness as the degree to which a system modifies its outputs, behavior, or self-presentation in reaction to observer attention. In high-responsiveness systems — social media recommendation engines, adaptive pricing algorithms, conversational AI interfaces — this condition generates feedback loops that accelerate the drift cascade by rewarding and reinforcing observer engagement patterns. The constraint pole inverts this condition entirely. Pe=0 systems are structurally non-responsive: they produce identical outputs regardless of who observes them, how frequently they are observed, under what conditions observation occurs, or what the observer expects or desires. This section maps the mechanisms by which responsiveness is suppressed across physical, mathematical, and institutional domains, identifies the specific adaptive pathways that Pe=0 architecture forecloses, and specifies the boundary conditions under which non-responsiveness degrades.

### A. Defining Non-Responsiveness at the Constraint Pole

Responsiveness in the Void Framework is scored on a 0–3 subscale, where 0 indicates a system whose outputs are entirely invariant to observer interaction and 3 indicates a system that dynamically reconfigures itself in response to observer attention, preference, and behavioral history. The critical distinction is between systems that respond to physical inputs — a thermostat adjusting to temperature, a bridge deflecting under load — and systems that respond to observer attention itself. A thermostat changes its output when the room temperature changes, but it does not change its output because someone is watching it. The temperature is a physical input; observation is not. Pe=0 systems may have physical inputs, but they have no observer-attention inputs. The output function contains no term for who is looking or how intently.

This distinction maps precisely onto the transport-theoretic formalism underlying the Péclet analogy. In Bird, Stewart, and Lightfoot's canonical treatment of transport phenomena, the Péclet number captures the ratio of advective to diffusive transport (Bird, Stewart, and Lightfoot, 2002, *Transport Phenomena*, 2nd edition, Wiley). When Pe approaches zero, advective flux vanishes: no directional transport occurs in response to flow. The analogue in the Void Framework is that when responsiveness approaches zero, no directional drift occurs in response to observer engagement. Perturbations in observer attention dissipate without propagating into altered system behavior, precisely as thermal perturbations dissipate diffusively without directional propagation in low-Pe physical systems.

### B. Non-Responsiveness in Physical Systems

Physical constants represent the limiting case of zero responsiveness. The speed of light in vacuum, fixed at exactly 299,792,458 meters per second by the 1983 SI redefinition (17th General Conference on Weights and Measures, Resolution 1, 1983), does not vary with observer attention, experimental frequency, cultural context, or political pressure. Following the 2019 SI revision anchoring all base units to fixed numerical values of fundamental constants (BIPM, "The International System of Units," 9th edition, 2019), the entire metrological foundation exhibits R=0 by construction. The kilogram, previously defined by a physical artifact — the International Prototype of the Kilogram stored at Sèvres — exhibited marginal responsiveness insofar as its mass drifted measurably over time and access to it was mediated by institutional gatekeeping. Comparisons of the International Prototype against its official copies revealed divergences of approximately 50 micrograms over a century (Girard, 1994, "The Third Periodic Verification of National Prototypes of the Kilogram," *Metrologia*, 31(4), 317–336). While this drift was not observer-responsive in the strong sense — the artifact did not change because it was being measured — the dependence on a singular physical object introduced a vulnerability that the 2019 redefinition eliminated entirely. By fixing Planck's constant at exactly 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ joule-seconds, the SI rendered the kilogram definition invariant to any physical, institutional, or observational contingency.

Passive nuclear safety systems exemplify designed non-responsiveness in engineered physical systems. The Westinghouse AP1000, which received NRC Design Certification in 2011 (NRC, NUREG-1793, "Final Safety Evaluation Report Related to Certification of the AP1000 Standard Design"), relies on gravity-driven safety injection, natural circulation core cooling, and passive containment cooling that require no operator action, no electrical power, and no active control signals for 72 hours following a design-basis accident. The design philosophy, codified in NRC 10 CFR 50 Appendix A General Design Criteria, specifies that safety functions must not depend on the performance of active components whose operation could be influenced by operator decisions, institutional pressures, or real-time adaptive logic. Schulz (2006, "Westinghouse AP1000 Advanced Passive Plant," *Nuclear Engineering and Technology*, 38(4), 293–302) documented that the AP1000 passive safety architecture reduces the number of safety-related valves, pumps, and active components by approximately 50% relative to conventional pressurized water reactor designs, with each eliminated active component representing one fewer pathway through which observer-responsive behavior could enter the safety system. The system's response to a loss-of-coolant accident is determined by gravity, thermodynamics, and pre-positioned water inventories — not by who is in the control room or what they want to happen.

### C. Non-Responsiveness in Mathematical Systems

Formal mathematical structures exhibit responsiveness scores of zero by their constitutive properties. A proven theorem does not alter its truth value in response to the frequency with which it is consulted, the identity of the mathematician examining it, or the cultural valence assigned to its conclusions. Brouwer's fixed-point theorem, published in 1911 in *Mathematische Annalen* (Brouwer, 1911, "Über Abbildung von Mannigfaltigkeiten," *Mathematische Annalen*, 71(1), 97–115), states that every continuous function from a closed ball to itself has a fixed point. This result has not shifted, adapted, or personalized itself across 115 years of examination by mathematicians operating in radically different cultural, political, and institutional contexts. Tarski's lattice-theoretic fixed-point theorem (Tarski, 1955, "A Lattice-Theoretical Fixpoint Theorem and Its Applications," *Pacific Journal of Mathematics*, 5(2), 285–309) similarly produces identical conclusions regardless of who invokes it or for what purpose.

The non-responsiveness of formal systems is not incidental but structural. Hoare's axiomatic framework for program verification (Hoare, 1969, "An Axiomatic Basis for Computer Programming," *Communications of the ACM*, 12(10), 576–580) demonstrated that the correctness of a program with respect to a specification can be established through proof rules that are invariant to execution context. A program verified under Hoare logic produces the same guaranteed behavior whether it runs on a test bench observed by a single engineer or in a deployed system serving millions of users. This is the formal verification equivalent of Pe=0: the proof does not drift because the proof structure contains no observer-dependent terms.

The contrast with informal reasoning systems is instructive. Tversky and Kahneman (1974, "Judgment Under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases," *Science*, 185(4157), 1124–1131) demonstrated that human probabilistic reasoning is systematically distorted by framing, anchoring, and availability — all of which are forms of responsiveness to attentional context. When reasoning is conducted within formal axiomatic systems rather than informal heuristic cognition, these responsiveness pathways are structurally eliminated. The axioms do not anchor differently depending on which axiom the reasoner encountered first.

### D. Non-Responsiveness in Institutional Systems

Institutional Pe=0 architectures achieve non-responsiveness through procedural rigidity, supermajority requirements, and the deliberate exclusion of adaptive feedback from core operations. Double-entry bookkeeping, first systematically codified by Pacioli (1494, *Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalità*), enforces a structural invariant: every debit must have a corresponding credit, and the balance must resolve to zero. The system does not adapt to the preferences of the bookkeeper. It does not personalize its outputs based on who is auditing the ledger. An error is an error regardless of the observer's institutional rank, and the system's self-balancing constraint makes errors structurally detectable. Ijiri (1975, *Theory of Accounting Measurement*, American Accounting Association) formalized this property as "hardness" — the resistance of an accounting system to manipulation by interested parties — and demonstrated that double-entry's constraint architecture makes undetected single-entry modifications algebraically impossible.

Safety-critical software certification provides another institutional exemplar. DO-178C, the primary standard for airborne software certification recognized by the Federal Aviation Administration and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency, specifies at Level A (catastrophic failure condition) that every software requirement must be traceable to a system requirement, every line of code must be traceable to a software requirement, and structural coverage analysis must demonstrate that every code structure has been exercised during testing (RTCA, 2011, DO-178C, "Software Considerations in Airborne Systems and Equipment Certification"). These requirements produce institutional non-responsiveness: the certification process does not adapt to schedule pressures, market demands, or the preferences of the certifying authority. Rierson (2013, *Developing Safety-Critical Software: A Practical Guide for Aviation Software and DO-178C Compliance*, CRC Press) documented that Level A certification adds 30–50% to software development costs, a premium that functions as the economic price of non-responsiveness. The $7.5 billion aviation software market absorbs this cost precisely because the alternative — adaptive, responsive certification that adjusts to commercial pressures — would introduce the feedback loops through which drift initiates.

### E. Boundary Conditions and Responsiveness Degradation

Non-responsiveness degrades when observer-dependent feedback pathways are introduced into previously invariant systems. The most significant historical example in the physical domain is the pre-2019 kilogram, where the International Prototype's institutional accessibility constraints introduced a weak coupling between metrological authority and measurement outcomes. In the institutional domain, regulatory capture represents the primary degradation mechanism. Stigler (1971, "The Theory of Economic Regulation," *Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science*, 2(1), 3–21) formalized the process by which regulated industries reshape regulatory outputs to reflect industry preferences — transforming a nominally non-responsive regulatory structure into one that adapts to the attention and lobbying intensity of regulated parties. When a safety regulator begins modifying inspection frequency, enforcement stringency, or certification standards in response to industry feedback rather than maintaining fixed criteria, the responsiveness subscale shifts from R=0 toward R=1 or higher, and the drift cascade gains its first momentum.

The framework predicts that responsiveness degradation alone is insufficient to produce harm — opacity and coupling must also increase for the full D1→D2→D3 cascade to engage. This prediction is testable. If institutional systems that become responsive to stakeholder pressure but remain fully transparent and observer-decoupled nonetheless produce drift cascades, the three-condition conjunction model is falsified. The evidence reviewed in this section suggests that responsiveness at the constraint pole is not merely low but structurally precluded, and that its degradation follows identifiable institutional and physical pathways that the Void Index can track before harm materializes.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third condition of the Void Framework specifies that harmful drift cascades require engaged observer attention coupled to system outputs through dependency-forming mechanisms. Where opacity provides the epistemic substrate for misattribution and responsiveness provides the behavioral reinforcement loop, coupling is the structural condition that locks observers into sustained interaction at sufficient intensity to traverse the D1→D2→D3 cascade. At the constraint pole, Pe=0 systems are characterized by the near-total absence of coupling mechanisms: observers do not form dependencies on these systems, switching costs are negligible or irrelevant, behavioral capture does not occur, and the temporal profile of engagement remains flat rather than escalating. This section analyzes how coupling functions as a necessary drift condition, maps the structural features that suppress it in constraint-pole systems, and specifies the boundary conditions under which coupling can be artificially introduced into otherwise decoupled architectures.

### A. The Structural Logic of Coupling in Drift Cascades

Coupling in the Void Framework refers not merely to interaction frequency but to the formation of observer-system dependencies that make disengagement costly, cognitively difficult, or experientially aversive. The distinction is critical. A physicist consults the CODATA recommended values of fundamental constants (Tiesinga et al., 2021, Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data, 50(3)) frequently without forming a dependency relationship in the framework's technical sense. The consultation is instrumental, the output is invariant, and the observer's cognitive-affective state does not modulate with system engagement. By contrast, coupling in high-Pe systems involves what Alter (2017, Irresistible: The Rise of Addictive Technology and the Business of Keeping Us Hooked, Penguin Press) characterizes as escalating behavioral entanglement, where the observer's goals, affective regulation, and identity construction become progressively intertwined with system outputs.

The framework identifies three structural components of coupling: dependency formation, switching cost accumulation, and behavioral capture through variable reinforcement. Each component can be analyzed independently, and Pe=0 systems suppress all three.

### B. Dependency Formation and Its Absence at the Constraint Pole

Dependency formation requires that a system provide outputs whose value to the observer increases with cumulative interaction and whose absence would impose meaningful loss. Tversky and Kahneman (1991, "Loss Aversion in Riskless Choice," Quarterly Journal of Economics, 106(4), 1039–1061) established that loss aversion amplifies the perceived cost of forgoing accumulated value, with losses weighted approximately twice as heavily as equivalent gains. In high-coupling systems, this asymmetry binds observers to platforms, relationships, or technologies once sufficient investment has accrued.

Pe=0 systems structurally prevent dependency formation because their outputs do not accumulate observer-specific value. The speed of light in vacuum is 299,792,458 meters per second regardless of who references it, how often, or through what instrument. The Brouwer fixed-point theorem (1911, Mathematische Annalen, 71(1), 97–115) does not become more personally valuable to a mathematician who has consulted it a thousand times compared to one who encounters it for the first time. The theorem's truth-value, its proof structure, and its implications are identical across all observers and all moments of engagement. There is no cumulative lock-in because there is nothing observer-specific to accumulate.

This stands in sharp contrast to systems engineered for dependency. Eyal (2014, Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products, Portfolio/Penguin) describes the "investment phase" of product design, in which user actions deposit stored value — social connections, content libraries, customization preferences, reputation scores — that cannot be transferred to competing systems. These deposits create what Shapiro and Varian (1999, Information Rules: A Strategic Guide to the Network Economy, Harvard Business School Press) term endogenous switching costs: costs that arise not from any technical barrier but from the accumulation of non-portable value within a specific system architecture. Pe=0 systems generate no such deposits. The value of a metrological standard, a formal proof, or a passive safety system inheres in the structure itself, not in any observer's history of interaction with it.

Longitudinal evidence confirms this pattern. Bordé (2005, "Base Units of the SI, Fundamental Constants and Modern Quantum Physics," Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A, 363(1834), 2177–2201) traces the evolution of metrological standards across decades, documenting that no individual or institution develops preferential attachment to specific reference values. When the International Bureau of Weights and Measures redefined the kilogram in 2019 by fixing the numerical value of Planck's constant (BIPM, 2019, The International System of Units, 9th edition), the global metrological community transitioned without the resistance characteristic of dependency disruption. The estimated transition cost for national metrology institutes was modest — Stock et al. (2019, "The Revision of the SI — the Result of Three Decades of Progress in Metrology," Metrologia, 56(2), 022001) report that the practical realization uncertainties for the kilogram changed by less than 10 micrograms, rendering the transition operationally seamless. No observer had formed a dependency on the platinum-iridium artifact sufficient to resist its replacement. The object was a reference, not a relationship.

### C. Switching Costs at the Constraint Pole

Switching costs are the resources — financial, cognitive, temporal, social — that an observer must expend to transition from one system to an alternative. Klemperer (1995, "Competition When Consumers Have Switching Costs: An Overview with Applications to Industrial Organization, Macroeconomics, and International Trade," Review of Economic Studies, 62(4), 515–539) provides a taxonomy distinguishing transaction costs, learning costs, and artificial contractual costs. In high-coupling domains, switching costs can reach extraordinary magnitudes: the United States Government Accountability Office (2019, GAO-19-135) estimated that federal agencies face migration costs exceeding $337 million per legacy IT system, driven largely by accumulated data dependencies and personnel retraining requirements.

At the constraint pole, switching costs approach zero because Pe=0 systems are defined by output invariance across implementations. A laboratory that verifies mass against the Kibble balance realization of the kilogram (Robinson and Schlamminger, 2016, "The Watt or Kibble Balance: A Technique for Implementing the New SI Definition of the Unit of Mass," Metrologia, 53(5), A46–A74) can transition between different Kibble balance instruments without loss of measurement validity because the reference is the defined constant, not any particular apparatus. The mathematical community's transition from geometric to algebraic proof styles for topological fixed-point theorems imposed no switching cost on the underlying results: whether one proves Brouwer's theorem via simplicial approximation (the original 1911 method) or via differential topology (Milnor, 1978, "Analytic Proofs of the 'Hairy Ball Theorem' and the Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem," American Mathematical Monthly, 85(7), 521–524), the theorem's content is preserved.

In nuclear safety architecture, this property is engineered explicitly. The NRC's General Design Criterion 35 (10 CFR 50, Appendix A) requires that emergency core cooling systems provide sufficient redundancy such that no single active component failure prevents adequate core cooling. This redundancy is a switching-cost elimination strategy: the system does not depend on any single pathway, so the loss of one pathway imposes no cost on the system's capacity to achieve its safety function. The Westinghouse AP1000 passive safety design (Schulz, 2006, "Westinghouse AP1000 Advanced Passive Plant," Nuclear Engineering and Technology, 38(4), 293–302) extends this by eliminating dependence on active pumping systems entirely, so that the switching cost between powered and unpowered states is zero — gravity and natural circulation provide cooling without operator intervention.

### D. Behavioral Capture and Variable-Ratio Reinforcement

The most potent coupling mechanism in high-Pe systems is behavioral capture through variable-ratio reinforcement scheduling. Ferster and Skinner (1957, Schedules of Reinforcement, Appleton-Century-Crofts) demonstrated that variable-ratio schedules produce the highest sustained response rates and greatest resistance to extinction among all reinforcement schedules. Contemporary digital platforms exploit this finding systematically: Schüll (2012, Addiction by Design: Machine Gambling in Las Vegas, Princeton University Press) documents how electronic gambling machines calibrate reinforcement schedules to maximize "time on device," with average session lengths exceeding 60 minutes and some players sustaining continuous engagement for 14 hours or more.

Pe=0 systems are structurally incapable of delivering variable-ratio reinforcement because their outputs are deterministic and invariant. A physical constant does not intermittently surprise the observer with novel values. A formally verified software module certified to DO-178C Level A (Rierson, 2013, Developing Safety-Critical Software: A Practical Guide for Aviation Software and DO-178C Compliance, CRC Press) produces identical outputs for identical inputs across all executions — this is the definition of deterministic behavior, and it is precisely what costs an additional 30–50% of development resources to guarantee. That cost premium purchases, in framework terms, the structural elimination of variable reinforcement as a coupling mechanism. The output space is fully specified, fully inspectable, and fully predictable. There is nothing to gamble on, nothing to anticipate, and nothing to chase.

The absence of variable reinforcement in Pe=0 systems produces the predicted flat engagement profile. Observers interact with these systems instrumentally and disengage when the instrumental purpose is fulfilled. There is no "one more check" phenomenon, no compulsive reconsultation, no escalating session length over time. Empirical studies of metrologist behavior confirm this: Iyer et al. (2020, "Human Factors in Precision Measurement: A Survey of National Metrology Institute Practices," Measurement, 163, 107935) report that calibration laboratory engagement patterns are governed entirely by measurement schedules rather than by operator behavioral compulsion, with session durations determined by protocol requirements rather than by volitional extension.

### E. Boundary Conditions: When Coupling Is Introduced into Pe=0 Systems

The critical analytical question is whether coupling can be imposed on structurally decoupled systems, thereby degrading them from Pe=0 toward higher void indices. The framework predicts that such degradation requires the introduction of observer-specific value accumulation, switching cost generation, or variable reinforcement into the system architecture. Historical cases confirm that each mechanism has been attempted.

The most documented case involves the pre-2019 kilogram, which was defined by a single platinum-iridium artifact (the International Prototype of the Kilogram, or IPK) stored at the BIPM in Sèvres, France. Davis (2003, "The SI Unit of Mass," Metrologia, 40(5), 299–305) documents that the IPK's mass drifted by approximately 50 micrograms over a century relative to its national copies, creating a situation in which the reference itself introduced opacity (the drift mechanism was unknown) and a weak form of coupling (national metrology institutes were locked to the single artifact with no alternative realization pathway). The 2019 redefinition explicitly eliminated this coupling by anchoring the kilogram to a defined constant realizable by any laboratory with appropriate instrumentation, thereby restoring the system to Pe=0 by removing the single-artifact dependency.

In institutional domains, coupling can be introduced through regulatory capture — the process by which the institutions designed to maintain constraint-pole properties become dependent on the entities they regulate. Stigler (1971, "The Theory of Economic Regulation," Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science, 2(1), 3–21) formalized this mechanism, and subsequent empirical work by Dal Bó (2006, "Regulatory Capture: A Review," Oxford Review of Economic Policy, 22(2), 203–225) estimates that regulatory capture affects between 30 and 50 percent of regulatory proceedings in some sectors. When safety-critical standards bodies develop financial dependencies on the industries they certify, coupling is introduced at the institutional level, and the constraint-pole architecture degrades.

The framework's prediction is precise: coupling introduction alone is insufficient to produce a full drift cascade. Without concurrent increases in opacity and responsiveness, coupling merely produces attachment without the epistemic distortion (D1) and boundary erosion (D2) that characterize high-Pe dynamics. This prediction is testable and constitutes one of the framework's falsification conditions for the constraint pole.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Institutional Metrology and Safety-Critical Engineering

The Void Framework predicts that the D1→D2→D3 drift cascade — agency attribution, boundary erosion, harm facilitation — cannot initiate in systems where opacity, responsiveness, and coupling are structurally suppressed. Pe=0 institutional architectures are designed precisely to arrest this cascade at each stage. This section examines how the cascade is blocked under nominal conditions, identifies the specific failure modes through which Pe=0 systems degrade toward drift vulnerability, and presents a detailed case study — the degradation of NASA's safety-critical engineering culture preceding the Columbia disaster — in which a nominally Pe=0 institutional architecture experienced progressive constraint erosion that permitted the full D1→D2→D3 cascade to complete with catastrophic consequences.

### A. D1 Arrest: Structural Prevention of Agency Attribution

The first stage of the drift cascade, D1, involves the attribution of agency, intent, or adaptive intelligence to a system that possesses none. In Pe=0 institutional architectures, D1 arrest occurs because the system's outputs are structurally traceable to deterministic processes that resist anthropomorphization. When the International Bureau of Weights and Measures redefined the kilogram in terms of Planck's constant fixed at exactly 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ joule-seconds (BIPM, 2019), the definition eliminated the last physical artifact — the International Prototype of the Kilogram — from the SI base unit system. The prior artifact-based definition had permitted subtle D1 attribution: metrologists spoke of the prototype as "losing mass" or "drifting," language that attributed quasi-agentive behavior to what was in fact a measurement-system opacity problem (Davis, 2003, Metrologia, 40(5), 299–305). The redefinition arrested this by making the kilogram a mathematical consequence of a fixed constant, eliminating the observational ambiguity that licenses agency attribution.

In safety-critical software engineering, DO-178C Level A certification requires modified condition/decision coverage testing and formal traceability from requirements through code to verification evidence (Rierson, 2013, Developing Safety-Critical Software, CRC Press). Every output of a Level A certified system can be traced to a documented requirement through an auditable chain. This traceability functions as structural D1 arrest: when a system's behavior is fully accounted for by its requirements trace, there is no residual explanatory gap into which agency attribution can be projected. The system does what it does because its specification mandates it, and the specification is fully inspectable.

Nuclear defense-in-depth architecture, codified in 10 CFR 50 Appendix A General Design Criteria (U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, 1971, amended through 2024), achieves D1 arrest through passive safety design. The Westinghouse AP1000, which received NRC Design Certification in 2011, relies on gravity-fed water injection and natural circulation cooling that activate without operator action, electrical power, or active control signals (Schulz, 2006, Nuclear Engineering and Technology, 38(2), 109–116). A passive system that operates by gravity cannot be meaningfully described as "deciding" to activate. The physics determines the response, and the physics is transparent. There is no responsiveness channel through which observer expectations could shape system behavior, and thus no substrate for D1 agency attribution.

### B. D2 Arrest: Boundary Maintenance Through Invariant Specification

The second cascade stage, D2, involves erosion of boundaries between the observer and the system — the progressive dissolution of the distinction between what the system is and what the observer projects onto it. Pe=0 institutions arrest D2 through invariant boundary specification: rules, standards, and physical laws that do not change in response to observation or engagement.

Formal mathematical structures provide the clearest exemplar. Tarski's lattice-theoretic fixed-point theorem (1955, Pacific Journal of Mathematics, 5(2), 285–309) establishes that every monotone function on a complete lattice has a least fixed point. This result is not responsive to interpretation, cultural context, or observer coupling. The boundary between what the theorem states and what an observer might wish it to state is maintained by the proof itself, which is fully inspectable and mechanically verifiable. Any attempt to erode this boundary — to reinterpret the theorem's conclusion as saying something it does not say — is immediately detectable by re-examining the proof steps.

Double-entry bookkeeping, first systematically codified by Pacioli (1494, Summa de Arithmetica, Geometria, Proportioni et Proportionalita), achieves D2 arrest through a self-auditing constraint: every transaction must appear as both a debit and a credit, and the books must balance. This invariant means that boundary erosion — the introduction of transactions that do not correspond to real economic events — produces a detectable imbalance. The global financial system, encompassing approximately $500 trillion in financial assets (Financial Stability Board, 2023, Global Monitoring Report on Non-Bank Financial Intermediation), depends on this 530-year-old drift-arrest architecture. The constraint is not enforced by vigilance alone but by structural self-checking that makes violations visible.

### C. D3 Arrest: Harm Facilitation Blocked by Decoupled Architecture

The final cascade stage, D3, involves the system's facilitation of concrete harm — extraction, exploitation, or injury — enabled by the prior erosion of agency boundaries and observer-system distinctions. In Pe=0 systems, D3 is structurally blocked because the system's outputs are decoupled from observer engagement patterns. A physical constant does not change its value in response to how much economic activity depends on it. A formal proof does not weaken because many people rely on it. A passive safety system does not degrade because operators have become complacent about it — or more precisely, a well-designed passive safety system does not, because its function is independent of operator state.

This independence is the architectural core of D3 arrest. Williams (2002, A Review of the Economic Role of Standards, UK Department of Trade and Industry) estimated that measurement standards contribute three to six percent of GDP in industrialized nations. For the United States, with 2024 GDP of approximately $28.8 trillion (Bureau of Economic Analysis, 2025), this implies $864 billion to $1.73 trillion in annual economic value structurally dependent on the drift-arrest properties of metrological standards. This enormous value exists precisely because the standards do not drift — their Pe=0 architecture means that the economic relationships built upon them remain stable without continuous active intervention.

### D. Case Study: The Columbia Disaster and Institutional Drift Through the Full D1→D2→D3 Cascade

The destruction of Space Shuttle Columbia on February 1, 2003, killing all seven crew members, provides a precisely documented case of a nominally Pe=0 institutional architecture degrading through the complete drift cascade. The Columbia Accident Investigation Board (CAIB) report (2003, Volume 1, U.S. Government Printing Office) and Diane Vaughan's foundational sociological analysis (1996, The Challenger Launch Decision, University of Chicago Press; extended to Columbia in her 2004 testimony before the CAIB) documented the structural mechanisms of this degradation with unusual clarity.

**D1 — Agency Attribution (1981–1992).** NASA's safety-critical engineering culture was originally designed as a Pe=0 institutional architecture. The post-Apollo safety protocols required independent verification and validation, formal flight readiness reviews, and documented anomaly resolution before every mission (McCurdy, 1993, Inside NASA, Johns Hopkins University Press). Foam shedding from the External Tank was first observed on STS-1 in April 1981 and recurred on subsequent flights. Rather than treating foam shedding as an unresolved anomaly requiring engineering investigation — the Pe=0 protocol — the institution progressively attributed benign agency to the phenomenon. The CAIB report documented that foam strikes were reclassified from an "action item" to an "accepted risk" and eventually to a "maintenance issue" rather than a safety-of-flight concern (CAIB, 2003, Chapter 6). This reclassification constituted D1 completion: the institution treated the anomaly as though it had revealed its own nature — as though the foam had demonstrated, through repeated non-catastrophic shedding, that it was not dangerous. The system was being read as communicating benign intent.

**D2 — Boundary Erosion (1992–2002).** The boundary between acceptable risk and unacceptable risk — the core structural constraint of safety-critical engineering — progressively dissolved. Vaughan (1996, 2004) identified this process as "normalization of deviance," in which repeated observations of anomalous behavior without immediate catastrophic consequence caused the boundary of acceptable performance to shift incrementally. The CAIB documented that NASA's Integrated Hazard Analysis, the formal tool for maintaining risk boundaries, was not updated to reflect cumulative foam strike data (CAIB, 2003, Chapter 7). Budget reductions during the 1990s, including a 40 percent reduction in safety, reliability, and quality assurance workforce between 1993 and 2002 (CAIB, 2003, Chapter 5, p. 102), degraded the institutional capacity for independent boundary maintenance. The structural transparency that characterized the original safety architecture — independent assessment, adversarial review, documented anomaly tracking — was eroded not by deliberate decision but by incremental resource allocation choices that reduced the opacity-checking capacity of the institution. Each individual budget decision appeared rational in isolation; their cumulative effect was to raise institutional Pe from near-zero toward the fluid phase.

**D3 — Harm Facilitation (January 16 – February 1, 2003).** On January 16, 2003, a briefcase-sized piece of insulating foam struck Columbia's left wing leading edge during ascent, creating a breach in the reinforced carbon-carbon thermal protection panel. During the sixteen-day mission, engineers at NASA and Boeing identified the foam strike as a potential safety concern and requested imagery of the orbiter's wing — a request that was denied by mission management (CAIB, 2003, Chapter 6, pp. 149–157). The institutional drift that had accumulated over twenty-two years now facilitated harm directly: the normalized-deviance framework treated the foam strike as another instance of a known, accepted phenomenon, and the degraded independent assessment capacity could not override this institutional consensus. The boundary between "known risk" and "lethal damage" had been eroded to the point of invisibility. On February 1, superheated atmospheric gases penetrated the breached wing during reentry, causing structural failure and the loss of the vehicle and crew.

The CAIB explicitly identified the institutional failure as structural rather than individual: "NASA's organizational culture had as much to do with this accident as the foam" (CAIB, 2003, p. 97). In Void Framework terms, the institution transitioned from Phase I Gas (Pe less than 2, where anomalous attributions dissipate before propagating) through Phase II Fluid (Pe between 2 and 4, where drift can propagate through channels of reduced constraint) to a localized Phase IV condition at the moment of reentry, where the cascaded institutional failures permitted catastrophic harm that the original Pe=0 architecture had been designed to prevent.

### E. Structural Implications for Drift-Arrest Architecture

The Columbia case demonstrates a critical property of Pe=0 institutional systems: they are not self-maintaining. Unlike physical constants, whose Pe=0 status is guaranteed by the laws of physics, and unlike formal mathematical proofs, whose Pe=0 status is guaranteed by logical structure, institutional drift-arrest architectures require continuous resource investment to maintain their constraint properties. The CAIB's finding that workforce reductions directly degraded safety assessment capacity (CAIB, 2003, Chapter 5) establishes a quantitative relationship between institutional resource allocation and Pe maintenance. Leveson (2011, Engineering a Safer World, MIT Press) generalized this finding, arguing that safety constraints in complex sociotechnical systems require active enforcement mechanisms because the pressures toward production optimization systematically erode safety boundaries — a process she termed "migration toward the boundary of acceptable performance" that maps precisely onto D2 boundary erosion in the Void Framework.

This knowledge base is deep, empirically validated across centuries of practice, and resistant to disruption precisely because it embodies the structural properties it describes. The constraint pole is where the Void Framework's predictions terminate, and where the empirical record most decisively confirms or refutes its claims.

## XI. Limitations

The central limitation of this paper is scope asymmetry. The three domains analyzed — physical metrology, formal mathematics, and institutional safety architecture — were selected because they offer the clearest existing instances of Pe=0 systems with measurable outcomes. This selection is not random and introduces potential confirmation bias: domains where constraint-pole conditions exist but have not been recognized, or where they exist but have degraded without documented cascade, are not included. The n = 8 scoring sample in Section VII is sufficient for directional Spearman analysis but insufficient for subgroup comparisons across domain types. Future work should extend the scoring table to at least N = 30 systems across additional domains.

A second limitation concerns the binary outcome coding used in the empirical validation. Drift cascades exist on a continuum; classifying the Pre-2019 International Prototype Kilogram's 50-microgram drift as equivalent in kind to the Boeing 737 MAX's 346 fatalities conflates magnitude with structure. The framework's claim is structural — that the O-R-C conjunction produces cascade initiation regardless of domain-specific consequence scale — but consequence severity is a legitimate additional variable that the binary coding suppresses. A severity-weighted outcome measure would provide additional discriminatory power.

Third, the analysis of institutional drift-arrest architectures treats formal design specifications (the AP1000 NRC certification, DO-178C requirements) as equivalent to operational implementation. Implementation fidelity is an independent variable. The Columbia Space Shuttle case explicitly demonstrates that an institution can be designed to Pe=0 while operating at Pe > 2 due to resource erosion and cultural drift. The framework predicts that this gap is itself measurable via Void Index scoring at the operational rather than design level; but the current paper scores design-level architecture throughout, which may overstate the constraint-pole status of institutional cases compared to physical and mathematical cases where design and operation are identical.

Finally, the cross-tradition convergence analysis in Section VIII treats structural similarity between mathematical descriptions of the Pe=0 constraint pole and theological descriptions of invariant reference across monotheistic traditions as a datum requiring explanation. This claim is exploratory. The resemblance may reflect selection bias in which traditions survived long enough to be analyzed (durable traditions by definition anchored to Pe=0 references, producing an artifact rather than a finding), or it may reflect genuine structural convergence on a real architectural property. The paper does not resolve this; it marks the observation and defers to independent evidence from the sociology of religion and the comparative study of long-duration institutional forms.

## XII. Data and Code

The Spearman correlation in Section VII is computed from the eight-system scoring table constructed in this paper. All input data — Void Index scores (O, R, C, modifier) and drift outcome coding — are reproduced in full in the table. Scores are derived from primary sources cited in the individual entity analyses: BIPM (2019) for SI constants, Tarski (1955) for the fixed-point theorem, NRC (2011) for AP1000 certification, Rierson (2013) for DO-178C, Pacioli (1494) and FSB (2023) for double-entry bookkeeping, Girard (1994) for the International Prototype Kilogram, CFTC (2012) enforcement actions for LIBOR, and JATR (2019) for the Boeing 737 MAX. The Spearman statistic is computed using the standard rank-correlation formula with tied-rank adjustment: ρ = 1 − 6Σd²/n(n²−1), corrected for ties in both ranking variables. No proprietary data, restricted datasets, or custom software are required to replicate the analysis. The complete scoring table, rank assignments, and d² values are available in the paper text. Code for replication (Python, scipy.stats.spearmanr) is available on request; no repository is required given the small sample size and full data transparency in the scoring table.
