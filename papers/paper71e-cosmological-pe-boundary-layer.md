## Abstract

Precision cosmology has invested in excess of thirteen billion dollars in observational infrastructure across the Planck satellite (approximately €700 million), the James Webb Space Telescope ($10 billion), the Euclid mission (approximately €1.4 billion), the Vera C. Rubin Observatory ($473 million), and the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument ($75 million), yet three persistent parameter tensions have resisted resolution for over a decade despite this unprecedented empirical campaign and the publication of more than one thousand proposed theoretical remedies catalogued by Di Valentino, Mena, Pan, Visinelli, and Yang (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001). The Hubble constant measured locally via the cosmic distance ladder yields H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al. 2022, Astrophysical Journal Letters 934, L7), while the same parameter inferred from the cosmic microwave background under ΛCDM returns H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 641, A6), a discrepancy exceeding five standard deviations. The amplitude of matter fluctuations measured through gravitational lensing surveys yields S₈ = 0.766 with asymmetric uncertainties of +0.020 and −0.014 from KiDS-1000 (Heymans et al. 2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 646, A140), falling two to three standard deviations below the Planck CMB prediction and confirmed at comparable suppression levels by the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 result of S₈ = 0.776 ± 0.017 (Abbott et al. 2022, Physical Review D 105, 023520). Cosmic void statistics exhibit sensitivity to the matter-to-dark-energy transition that renders them simultaneously powerful cosmological probes and sources of anomalous tension with ΛCDM N-body predictions (Pisani et al. 2019, Bulletin of the American Astronomical Society 51, 40). Verde, Treu, and Riess (2019, Nature Astronomy 3, 891) declared cosmology to be in a state of "crisis" over this parameter discordance, and Abdalla et al. (2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics 34, 49) reviewed over five hundred proposed solutions without identifying any single universally accepted resolution.

This paper advances the hypothesis that these three tensions are not independent anomalies but structural consequences of a shared epistemic boundary layer in cosmic evolution: the matter-to-dark-energy transition regime near redshift z ≈ 0.3–0.7, where inference chains achieve maximal sensitivity to modeling assumptions. We define a cosmological Péclet number, Pe-Λ, as the dimensionless ratio of dark energy advective expansion to matter-dominated diffusive structure formation, and map the resulting epistemic architecture onto the Void Framework's tripartite condition structure. The specific contributions of this paper are as follows. (1) We introduce Pe-Λ as a formal transport parameter for the matter-Λ boundary layer, demonstrating that all three tensions concentrate in the redshift range where Pe-Λ transitions through unity. (2) We establish a precise mapping between constitutive cosmological opacity — the irreducible unobservability of dark energy's equation of state, dark matter's microphysics, and the initial condition space — and the Void Framework's O-dimension, scored at 2/3 to reflect opacity that is physically fundamental rather than designed. (3) We identify model-dependent responsiveness in the cosmological inference chain, whereby ΛCDM's six-parameter structure generates correlated parameter shifts in response to measurement perturbations, scored at R = 2/3. (4) We characterize observer-model coupling as the irreducible dependence of cosmological observables on assumed background cosmology, priors, and likelihood architecture, scored at C = 2/3. (5) We trace a drift cascade from D1 agency attribution — the premature reification of tensions as evidence for specific new physics — through D2 boundary erosion — the proliferation of extended models that weaken the falsifiability boundary of ΛCDM — to D3 harm facilitation — the potential misallocation of billions in experimental resources toward resolving tensions that may be artifacts of boundary-layer epistemics. (6) We construct a control case analysis using baryon acoustic oscillation measurements, which occupy the constraint pole at Void Index 3/12 due to their geometric directness, model independence, and minimal observer coupling. (7) We identify explicit kill conditions: the unified boundary layer hypothesis fails if the three tensions are shown to arise from independent, unrelated systematic errors with no shared structural origin in the matter-Λ transition. (8) We situate this analysis within Peebles's (2022, Annals of Physics 447, 169159) broader concern that ΛCDM's empirical successes may mask unresolved foundational questions, arguing that the Void Framework provides diagnostic vocabulary for precisely this masking structure. The aggregate Void Index for precision cosmology's tension architecture is assessed at 7/12, with Pe-Λ estimated at 3.2, placing the domain in a Phase II Fluid regime where epistemic tensions are expected to remain unstable and resistant to resolution through incremental measurement improvement alone, but have not yet entered the Phase IV Pandemonium threshold that would indicate irreversible epistemic failure.

## I. Introduction

Three numbers define the crisis of modern cosmology. The Hubble constant measured from the local distance ladder stands at 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al., 2022, *The Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 934, L7). The same constant inferred from the cosmic microwave background under the standard ΛCDM model yields 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration, 2020, *Astronomy and Astrophysics*, 641, A6). The discrepancy exceeds five standard deviations — a threshold that, in particle physics, would constitute discovery of new phenomena. Meanwhile, the amplitude of matter clustering measured through gravitational lensing surveys consistently falls below the value predicted by the CMB: the KiDS-1000 survey reports S₈ = 0.766 +0.020/−0.014, sitting two to three standard deviations beneath the Planck expectation of approximately 0.832 (Heymans et al., 2021, *Astronomy and Astrophysics*, 646, A140). The Dark Energy Survey Year 3 cosmic shear analysis finds S₈ = 0.776 ± 0.017, intermediate but still suppressed (Abbott et al., 2022, *Physical Review D*, 105, 023520). The universe appears to be expanding faster and clumping less than the standard model predicts, and the two anomalies have resisted resolution for over a decade despite a combined observational infrastructure investment exceeding thirteen billion dollars across Planck (approximately €700 million), JWST ($10 billion), Euclid (approximately €1.4 billion), Rubin Observatory ($473 million), and DESI ($75 million).

The persistence of these tensions is not for lack of theoretical effort. Abdalla and colleagues catalogued over five hundred proposed solutions to the Hubble tension alone, spanning early dark energy, modified gravity, interacting dark sectors, decaying dark matter, extra relativistic species, and dozens of other mechanisms, concluding that no single proposal achieves universal acceptance or resolves all tensions simultaneously (Abdalla et al., 2022, *Journal of High Energy Astrophysics*, 34, 49). Di Valentino and colleagues compiled over one thousand papers addressing the discrepancy and reached a similar verdict: the tension is robust against known systematics and no compelling new physics has emerged (Di Valentino et al., 2021, *Classical and Quantum Gravity*, 38, 153001). Verde, Treu, and Riess declared cosmology to be in a state of "crisis," arguing that the discordance among independently calibrated parameters signals either unidentified systematic errors or genuine failure of the concordance model (Verde, Treu, & Riess, 2019, *Nature Astronomy*, 3, 891). Freedman and colleagues used the James Webb Space Telescope to recalibrate Cepheid photometry — the most scrutinized potential systematic in the distance ladder — and found that the tension persists at comparable significance even after eliminating crowding and blending concerns that had been proposed as explanations (Freedman et al., 2024, *The Astrophysical Journal*, 969, 56). Peebles, reflecting on six decades of cosmological model-building, observed that ΛCDM's remarkable empirical successes may have masked unresolved foundational questions about the nature of the dark sector and the limits of parameter inference from finite observational windows (Peebles, 2022, *Annals of Physics*, 447, 169159).

This paper proposes that the three tensions — Hubble, σ₈ suppression, and anomalous void statistics — are not independent puzzles but symptoms of a shared structural condition in the epistemic architecture of cosmological measurement. All three localize at a common boundary in cosmic evolution: the redshift range z ≈ 0.3–0.7, where the universe transitions from matter-dominated deceleration to dark-energy-dominated acceleration. This transition regime is the epoch at which the Hubble parameter's sensitivity to cosmological model assumptions peaks, where growth-rate measurements diverge most from CMB extrapolations, and where cosmic void statistics become maximally informative about the equation of state of dark energy (Pisani et al., 2019, *Bulletin of the American Astronomical Society*, 51, 40). The localization is not coincidental. It reflects the physical regime where two competing dynamical processes — gravitational collapse (structure formation) and metric expansion (dark energy advection) — operate at comparable magnitude, creating a boundary layer in both the physical and inferential senses of the term.

To formalize this localization, we introduce the cosmological Péclet number Pe-Λ, defined as the ratio of the dark energy advective expansion rate to the matter-dominated diffusive rate of structure formation. In fluid dynamics, the Péclet number governs transport regime transitions: when advection dominates diffusion (Pe >> 1), the system enters a regime where boundary layers thin, gradients steepen, and small perturbations in boundary conditions propagate nonlinearly through the solution. We argue that Pe-Λ performs an analogous function in cosmological inference. At high redshift (z >> 1), matter dominates and Pe-Λ << 1; structure formation proceeds diffusively and CMB-calibrated predictions propagate smoothly to observable consequences. At low redshift (z → 0), dark energy dominates and Pe-Λ >> 1; the expansion is advection-dominated and local measurements become relatively insensitive to early-universe boundary conditions. At z ≈ 0.3–0.7, however, Pe-Λ passes through values of order unity, and the inference chain connecting early-universe calibration (CMB) to late-universe observables (distance ladder, lensing, void counts) traverses a regime of maximal sensitivity to modeling assumptions, prior choices, and unresolved physics. The tensions concentrate here precisely because this is where the epistemic transfer function is least constrained.

The Void Framework, developed to analyze harm patterns in human-coupled opaque systems, identifies three jointly sufficient conditions for predictable drift toward failure: opacity (O), the degree to which internal system states are inaccessible to external observation; responsiveness (R), the degree to which system outputs depend on observer interaction; and observer coupling (C), the degree to which observers become entangled with the system through feedback loops that compromise independent assessment. In standard applications — algorithmic recommendation, financial instruments, therapeutic interventions — these conditions describe designed architectures where profit motives or institutional incentives actively maintain opacity. In cosmology, the structural homology is real but the etiology is fundamentally different. Cosmological opacity is constitutive rather than designed: the dark sector is opaque not because anyone profits from concealing it but because dark energy and dark matter interact with the electromagnetic spectrum only gravitationally. Responsiveness is measurement-dependent rather than algorithmic: the value of H₀ one infers depends irreducibly on which rung of the distance ladder one occupies and which model priors one adopts. Coupling is epistemic rather than behavioral: the scientific community's investment in ΛCDM as the concordance model creates inference-chain dependencies where early-universe calibrations propagate through model-dependent transfer functions to constrain late-universe predictions, and the degree to which those predictions can be tested independently of the model is limited by the same opacity that generates the tensions.

## II. Background: The Void Framework Applied to Cosmological Epistemics

The Void Framework, as formalized in Papers 1, 3, and 9 of this series (Williams 2024a; Williams 2024c; Williams 2025a), identifies a triadic structure — opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and engaged observer coupling (C) — whose joint presence generates predictable harm cascades across domains ranging from algorithmic recommendation systems to financial derivatives markets. The framework's core claim is not that any single condition is sufficient to produce systematic failure, but that their co-occurrence creates a self-reinforcing geometry in which inference degrades along a stereotyped drift cascade: D1 (agency attribution to the opaque system), D2 (boundary erosion between observer and system), and D3 (facilitation of harm through the coupling itself). The present section maps this triadic architecture onto the epistemic structure of precision cosmology — a domain that differs categorically from the framework's original behavioral applications, yet instantiates each condition through mechanisms that are constitutive rather than designed.

This mapping requires a preliminary methodological disclaimer. In standard Void Framework applications, opacity is engineered by platform architects, responsiveness is coded into algorithmic feedback loops, and observer coupling emerges from behavioral vulnerabilities that system designers exploit. Cosmological epistemics presents none of these features. The universe is not a platform. Dark energy is not an algorithm. The scientific community is not a vulnerable user population in the sense addressed by the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act or analogous regulatory frameworks. What the cosmological domain does share, however, is a structural configuration in which the three conditions converge at a specific boundary — the matter-to-dark-energy transition regime near redshift z approximately 0.3 to 0.7 — producing inference failures that resist resolution despite extraordinary measurement investment exceeding thirteen billion dollars across Planck, JWST, Euclid, Rubin Observatory, and DESI combined (ESA 2020; NASA 2023; ESA 2023; NSF 2022; DESI Collaboration 2016). The mapping is therefore epistemological rather than behavioral, and every claim below is flagged as exploratory with the kill conditions specified in the source analysis.

**Opacity in cosmological inference.** The Void Framework defines opacity (O) as the degree to which a system's internal state is inaccessible to the observer through any available channel. In precision cosmology, opacity is not designed but constitutive: it arises from the fact that approximately 95 percent of the universe's energy-matter content consists of dark energy and dark matter, neither of which couples to electromagnetic radiation and neither of which has been directly detected in laboratory experiments (Peebles 2022, Annals of Physics 447, 169159). The Cosmic Microwave Background, the single most informative cosmological observable, encodes the state of the universe at recombination (z approximately 1100) and must be propagated forward through 13.8 billion years of nonlinear evolution to yield present-day parameter predictions. This propagation is entirely model-dependent: the Planck Collaboration's reported value of H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 641, A6) is not a direct measurement of the current expansion rate but an inference conditional on the full ΛCDM parameter space. The opacity here resides not in any single measurement but in the inferential chain connecting observables to parameters — a chain whose intermediate nodes (reionization history, neutrino mass hierarchy, baryon feedback in structure formation) are themselves opaque. We assign O = 2 on the framework's 0–3 scale: substantial but not maximal, because the opacity is principled (governed by known physics) rather than adversarial, and because independent cross-checks between CMB, baryon acoustic oscillation, and nucleosynthesis constraints partially illuminate the chain.

**Responsiveness in the measurement-model interface.** Responsiveness (R) in the Void Framework measures the degree to which a system's apparent output changes in response to observer interaction. Cosmological observables do not, of course, respond to human measurement in any physical sense. However, the extracted parameter values are exquisitely sensitive to the model assumed during inference — a feature that functions as epistemic responsiveness. Di Valentino and colleagues (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001) catalogued over one thousand papers proposing modifications to ΛCDM, many of which shift H₀ by several km/s/Mpc simply by altering assumptions about early dark energy, modified gravity, or neutrino self-interactions. The S₈ tension similarly migrates: Abbott and colleagues (2022, Physical Review D 105, 023520) showed that the DES Y3 value of S₈ = 0.776 ± 0.017 shifts depending on intrinsic alignment models and photometric redshift calibration choices. Void statistics, as Pisani and colleagues (2019, Bulletin of the American Astronomical Society 51, 40) demonstrated, are acutely sensitive to void-finding algorithm definitions, tracer density, and survey mask geometry. The system "responds" not physically but inferentially: different analytical choices applied to the same photons yield discrepant cosmologies. We assign R = 2, reflecting genuine model-dependent responsiveness that falls short of the maximum score because the community maintains rigorous blinding protocols and systematic error budgets that partially constrain this sensitivity.

**Engaged observer coupling at the Pe-Λ boundary.** The framework's third condition, engaged observer coupling (C), captures the degree to which the observer's inference process is structurally entangled with the system being observed. In cosmology, this coupling is irreducible and arises from a specific feature: human observers are embedded within the universe they measure, observing from a single spacetime location with a finite past light cone. This is not a philosophical abstraction but an operational constraint that shapes every measurement. The cosmic distance ladder, which produces the local H₀ value (Riess et al. 2022, Astrophysical Journal Letters 934, L7), samples a volume within approximately 400 megaparsecs — a region that may not be representative of the global expansion rate if local structure (supervoids, overdensities) biases the measurement (Kenworthy, Scolnic, and Riess 2019, Astrophysical Journal 875, 145). The σ₈ tension may partially reflect baryonic feedback processes that are calibrated against observations within our cosmic neighborhood (Amon and Efstathiou 2022, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 516, 5355). The void fraction anomaly is directly shaped by survey geometry and the observer's embedding within the cosmic web. Crucially, all three tensions concentrate at redshifts z approximately 0.3 to 0.7 — precisely the epoch where the universe transitions from matter domination to dark energy domination, and where the observer's inferential leverage is maximally compromised by the competing effects of gravitational clustering and accelerated expansion. We assign C = 2 for this irreducible but partially characterized coupling.

## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

### The Epistemic Inaccessibility of the Matter-Λ Transition Regime

Opacity in the Void Framework denotes the degree to which the internal states, causal mechanisms, and decision-relevant parameters of a system are inaccessible to external observation. In standard behavioral applications, opacity is typically engineered — a platform conceals its recommendation algorithm, a financial instrument obscures its risk structure. Cosmological opacity is categorically different: it is constitutive. The universe does not hide its parameters through strategic design; it renders them inaccessible through the fundamental structure of physical law, the finite speed of light, the thermodynamic erasure of early-universe information, and the observer's irreducible embeddedness within the system under study. This distinction is critical. The opacity score assigned here (O = 2.5 on a 0–3 scale) reflects not malicious concealment but the genuine, irreducible character of the inferential barriers confronting precision cosmology at the matter-to-dark-energy transition epoch near redshift z ≈ 0.3–0.7.

This section enumerates five distinct opacity mechanisms that jointly render the Pe-Λ boundary layer — the cosmological regime where dark energy's expansionary influence overtakes matter's gravitational clustering — a zone of maximal epistemic inaccessibility. Each mechanism is documented with primary-source evidence and quantitative constraints.

### III.1 Opacity Mechanism 1: Dark Energy as a Constitutively Opaque Substrate

The dominant energy component of the present-day universe is dark energy, constituting approximately 68.3% of the total energy density (Planck Collaboration 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 641, A6). Despite this dominance, dark energy has exactly one observationally constrained property: its equation of state parameter w, measured to be consistent with w = −1.03 ± 0.03 by the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument first-year results (DESI Collaboration 2024, arXiv:2404.03002, submitted to Physical Review D). No laboratory detection of dark energy has ever been achieved. No microphysical model has been empirically confirmed. The vacuum energy density predicted by quantum field theory exceeds the observed value by approximately 120 orders of magnitude — the so-called cosmological constant problem (Weinberg 1989, Reviews of Modern Physics 61, 1). This is not a gap that improved instrumentation can straightforwardly close; it reflects a fundamental disconnection between the theoretical frameworks (quantum field theory and general relativity) that must be jointly invoked to characterize the substance that governs cosmic expansion.

The opacity this generates is not merely quantitative imprecision but categorical inaccessibility. When cosmological parameters are inferred from CMB observations, the inference chain must propagate forward through approximately 13.4 billion years of cosmic evolution, traversing the entire matter-Λ transition, using a dark energy model whose physical basis remains unknown. The Planck Collaboration's derivation of H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc is not a direct measurement of the present expansion rate; it is the output of a six-parameter ΛCDM model fit to temperature and polarization power spectra at z ≈ 1100, projected forward through a dark energy epoch whose microphysics is opaque (Planck Collaboration 2020). As Peebles (2022, Annals of Physics 447, 169159) noted in his historical assessment, ΛCDM's empirical successes have masked the fact that two of its three dominant energy components — dark matter and dark energy — lack any confirmed particle physics identification.

### III.2 Opacity Mechanism 2: Distance Ladder Compounding and Systematic Propagation

The local measurement of H₀ via the cosmic distance ladder introduces a distinct form of opacity: the compounding of calibration uncertainties across sequential rungs. The SH0ES program (Riess et al. 2022, Astrophysical Journal Letters 934, L7) employs a three-rung ladder — geometric parallaxes to anchor Cepheid period-luminosity relations in the Milky Way, Cepheids in Type Ia supernova host galaxies to cross-calibrate supernova luminosities, and the Hubble flow supernovae to determine H₀. Each rung introduces systematic uncertainties that propagate multiplicatively rather than additively.

The critical quantitative feature is the narrowness of the calibration base relative to the inferential reach. The geometric anchor consists of 75 Milky Way Cepheids with Gaia EDR3 parallaxes (Riess et al. 2021, Astrophysical Journal 908, L6), from which the entire extragalactic distance scale and hence the global expansion rate are derived. Freedman et al. (2024, Astrophysical Journal 969, 56) used JWST near-infrared photometry to test whether crowding and blending effects in Hubble Space Telescope Cepheid measurements introduced systematic bias, finding that while individual Cepheid fluxes shifted, the aggregate distance ladder result remained robust. This is informative but does not resolve the deeper opacity problem: that systematic errors correlated across rungs are extremely difficult to detect because they shift all rungs coherently. As Efstathiou (2021, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 505, 3866) demonstrated, different treatments of Cepheid outlier rejection and photometric crowding corrections shift H₀ by 1–2 km/s/Mpc — comparable to the formal uncertainty — without any treatment being unambiguously preferable.

The distance ladder is therefore opaque not because any single rung is poorly measured but because the inter-rung calibration transfer functions embed modeling choices whose effects on the final H₀ are difficult to disentangle from the physical signal. This is structurally homologous to what the Void Framework terms "pipeline opacity" in algorithmic domains: the output is deterministic given the inputs, but the sensitivity of the output to intermediate modeling decisions is not transparent to downstream consumers of the result.

### III.3 Opacity Mechanism 3: The Lensing Kernel and Projection Effects in σ₈ Measurement

Weak gravitational lensing — the primary observable constraining σ₈ at low redshift — measures the coherent distortion of background galaxy shapes by intervening large-scale structure. The lensing signal is an integrated quantity: the shear at any point on the sky is the projection of all matter along the line of sight, weighted by a broad lensing efficiency kernel that peaks at roughly half the distance to the source galaxies (Bartelmann and Schneider 2001, Physics Reports 340, 291). This projection integrates over the entire Pe-Λ boundary layer, mixing matter-dominated and dark-energy-dominated epochs in a single observable.

The opacity this introduces is twofold. First, the lensing signal cannot be decomposed into contributions from specific redshift slices without additional information (photometric redshift distributions), which themselves carry calibration uncertainties at the level of Δz ≈ 0.01–0.02 per tomographic bin (Hildebrandt et al. 2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 647, A124). The KiDS-1000 analysis (Heymans et al. 2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 646, A140) employed five tomographic bins spanning 0.1 < z < 1.2, but the broad lensing kernel means that each bin's signal is contaminated by structure at redshifts outside the nominal bin boundaries. Second, the conversion from measured shear to inferred σ₈ requires assuming a matter power spectrum shape, which is itself derived from the same ΛCDM framework under test. Amon et al. (2022, Physical Review D 105, 023514) showed in the DES Y3 analysis that different choices for intrinsic alignment models — the astrophysical contamination from physically aligned galaxy shapes mimicking the lensing signal — shift S₈ by up to 0.5σ. Since the intrinsic alignment amplitude is degenerate with σ₈ in the lensing signal, the parameter of interest is partially occluded by an astrophysical foreground whose properties are independently uncertain.

This mechanism localizes opacity precisely at the Pe-Λ boundary layer: the lensing kernel's peak sensitivity falls at z ≈ 0.3–0.5 for typical source redshift distributions, which is the epoch where dark energy transitions from subdominant to dominant. The measurement is maximally opaque precisely where the cosmological model is most sensitive to the dark energy prescription.

### III.4 Opacity Mechanism 4: Void Identification as a Definition-Dependent Observable

Cosmic void statistics introduce a qualitatively different opacity mechanism: the observable itself is not uniquely defined. Unlike the CMB temperature power spectrum, which is a well-defined statistical quantity derivable from first principles, a "void" in large-scale structure is an operational construct that depends on the identification algorithm, the tracer population (galaxies, clusters, or dark matter halos in simulations), and the density threshold used to define the void boundary. Colberg et al. (2008, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 387, 933) compared thirteen void-finding algorithms applied to identical simulation outputs and found that the number of voids identified varied by factors of two to five, void sizes differed by 30–50%, and void profiles showed systematic shape differences depending on the algorithm's topological assumptions. Pisani et al. (2019, Bulletin of the American Astronomical Society 51, 40) identified void statistics as powerful cosmological probes with particular sensitivity to dark energy, but acknowledged that standardizing void definitions across surveys remains an open methodological challenge.

This is definitional opacity: the quantity being measured is partially constituted by the measurement prescription. The void fraction fᵥ — the volume of the universe classified as underdense — ranges from approximately 60% to 80% in survey analyses depending on the void finder and tracer density (Hamaus et al. 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 633, L10). This 20-percentage-point variation is not statistical noise; it reflects genuine ambiguity in what constitutes a void. When void statistics are used to constrain cosmological parameters, the results inherit this definitional opacity, and deviations from ΛCDM predictions may arise from algorithmic choices rather than from new physics.

### III.5 Opacity Mechanism 5: Covariance Blindness Across Tension Domains

The final opacity mechanism is meta-analytic: the three tensions (H₀, σ₈, void fraction) are conventionally analyzed as independent problems, yet their covariance structure is not well characterized. Di Valentino et al. (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001) catalogued over 1,000 publications addressing the Hubble tension alone, yet noted that most proposed solutions are evaluated against H₀ in isolation without checking consistency with σ₈ constraints. Abdalla et al. (2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics 34, 49) reviewed over 500 proposed modifications to ΛCDM and found that solutions resolving the Hubble tension frequently exacerbate the σ₈ tension or introduce new inconsistencies with baryon acoustic oscillation measurements. This pattern — where the literature is partitioned into tension-specific subcommunities with limited cross-validation — constitutes a form of collective epistemic opacity. The system's behavior (three correlated tensions in the same redshift regime) is obscured by a disciplinary structure that decomposes it into independent problems.

Specifically, early dark energy models that raise H₀ by injecting an additional energy component at z ≈ 3000–5000 (Poulin et al. 2019, Physical Review Letters 122, 221301) tend to increase the predicted σ₈, worsening the lensing tension (Hill et al. 2020, Physical Review D 102, 043507). Conversely, models that suppress structure growth to resolve σ₈ (such as massive neutrinos or decaying dark matter) tend to leave H₀ unchanged or shift it in the wrong direction (Di Valentino, Melchiorri, and Silk 2020, Physics Letters B 761, 242). The failure of any single modification to simultaneously resolve all three tensions after a decade of effort involving more than a thousand publications is itself evidence that the opacity is not localized to any single measurement but is structural to the boundary layer where the tensions converge.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

### The Measurement-Dependent Character of Cosmological Observables

In standard Void Framework applications, responsiveness denotes the capacity of a system to adapt its outputs contingent on observer behavior — the algorithmic feed that reshapes itself around a user's dwell time, the slot machine that modulates near-miss frequency in response to betting patterns. The responsiveness score (R: 0–3) captures how tightly the system's observable surface tracks and reacts to observer attention. In cosmological epistemics, no such intentional adaptation exists. The universe does not reshape its expansion rate because astronomers are measuring it. Yet the Void Framework's structural criterion for responsiveness is not intentionality but functional dependence: does the system's observable output change as a function of how, when, and with what assumptions the observer engages it? By this criterion, the cosmological parameter tension domain exhibits a distinctive and consequential form of responsiveness that we term **model-mediated observable elasticity** — the systematic dependence of extracted parameter values on the inferential apparatus through which raw photon counts are converted into cosmological quantities.

This section establishes that responsiveness in the Pe-Λ boundary layer operates through three specific mechanisms: prior-dependent parameter extraction, redshift-window sensitivity, and the reflexive interaction between tension-motivated theoretical proposals and subsequent measurement design. We assess cosmological responsiveness at R = 2 on the Void Framework's 0–3 scale, reflecting substantial but non-maximal observer-measurement coupling.

### 4.1 Prior-Dependent Parameter Extraction

No cosmological parameter is directly observed. H₀ is not read from a dial; σ₈ is not counted like galaxies in a field. Every reported cosmological parameter value is the output of a Bayesian inference chain in which raw data — photon counts, spectral line positions, shear ellipticities — are processed through likelihood functions that embed physical models, nuisance parameter priors, and calibration assumptions. The critical point for the Void Framework is that these inference chains are not inert conduits transmitting ground truth. They are responsive mediators whose outputs shift — sometimes dramatically — as modeling assumptions change.

The Hubble tension itself provides the clearest demonstration. The Planck Collaboration's reported value of H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 641, A6) is not a measurement of the expansion rate at the present epoch. It is a parameter of the best-fit ΛCDM model to the CMB temperature and polarization power spectra, which encode physics at redshift z ≈ 1100. The "measurement" of H₀ from the CMB is entirely contingent on the assumed cosmological model bridging 13.8 billion years of expansion history. When the model is altered — for instance, by allowing early dark energy (Poulin et al. 2019, Physical Review Letters 122, 221301), modified recombination physics (Jedamzik and Pogosian 2020, Physical Review Letters 125, 181302), or additional relativistic species (Anchordoqui et al. 2021, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics 32, 28) — the CMB-inferred H₀ shifts by several km/s/Mpc, sometimes resolving and sometimes exacerbating the tension. The observable surface of the CMB is, in this precise structural sense, responsive: the parameter it yields is a function of the theoretical framework the observer brings to the analysis.

This is not unique to the CMB. The local distance ladder measurement of H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al. 2022, Astrophysical Journal Letters 934, L7) depends on a chain of calibration choices — the period-luminosity relation for Cepheid variables, the treatment of metallicity dependence, the selection of Type Ia supernova samples, and the geometric anchors (masers, eclipsing binaries, parallaxes) that set the absolute distance scale. When Freedman (2021, Astrophysical Journal 919, 16) applied the Tip of the Red Giant Branch (TRGB) calibration instead of Cepheids, the resulting H₀ = 69.8 ± 1.7 km/s/Mpc fell between the two camps — a value responsive not to the universe's actual expansion rate changing but to the observer's choice of stellar population tracer. Subsequent JWST recalibration by Freedman et al. (2024, Astrophysical Journal 969, 56) further demonstrated that the extracted H₀ value moves depending on instrument, photometric reduction pipeline, and crowding correction methodology. The system's output — the number printed in the abstract — responds to the observer's methodological apparatus.

The σ₈ tension manifests identical structural responsiveness. Weak gravitational lensing surveys measure galaxy shape correlations and convert these into constraints on the matter power spectrum amplitude. But the extraction of S₈ depends on choices regarding intrinsic alignment modeling, photometric redshift calibration, baryonic feedback prescriptions applied to the matter power spectrum, and the angular scale range included in the analysis. Asgari et al. (2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 645, A104) demonstrated that KiDS-1000 S₈ constraints shift by approximately 0.5σ depending on which intrinsic alignment model is assumed. Amon et al. (2022, Physical Review D 105, 023514) showed that DES Y3 results are sensitive to baryonic feedback parameterizations at a level that could account for a significant fraction of the Planck discrepancy. The observable quantity S₈ is not a fixed feature of the cosmic matter distribution waiting to be uncovered; it is a parameter whose extracted value responds to the observer's modeling choices with a sensitivity that is commensurate with the tension itself.

### 4.2 Redshift-Window Sensitivity and the Pe-Λ Boundary Layer

The responsiveness of cosmological observables is not uniform across cosmic history. It concentrates precisely in the redshift range z ≈ 0.3–0.7 where the Pe-Λ boundary layer is located — the epoch at which dark energy transitions from subdominant to dominant, and the universe's expansion transitions from decelerating to accelerating. This localization is the key structural feature that motivates the unified boundary layer hypothesis.

Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) first-year data release (DESI Collaboration 2024, arXiv:2404.03002) revealed that the BAO scale measured in different redshift bins yields expansion history constraints that show mild tension with a cosmological constant when the bins spanning z ≈ 0.4–0.7 are included, but not when they are excluded. The observable output is responsive to which temporal window the observer examines. Efstathiou (2024, arXiv:2405.18168) demonstrated that the DESI preference for evolving dark energy is driven primarily by data in a narrow redshift range and is sensitive to the assumed dark energy parameterization (w₀–wₐ versus alternative functional forms), illustrating that the same photon data yield qualitatively different cosmological conclusions depending on the functional form the observer imposes on the dark energy equation of state.

This redshift-window sensitivity is precisely what the Pe-Λ framework predicts. At z > 1, matter dominates the energy budget and structure formation proceeds via well-understood gravitational instability — the "diffusive" regime where Pe-Λ < 1 and observables are relatively insensitive to dark energy modeling. At z < 0.2, dark energy dominates overwhelmingly and the expansion history is again relatively simple. But in the transition zone, the ratio of dark energy advective expansion to matter-dominated structure formation passes through unity, and small changes in modeling assumptions — the equation of state parameterization, the treatment of massive neutrinos, the assumed spatial curvature — propagate nonlinearly into extracted parameter values. The Pe-Λ boundary layer is where observables are maximally responsive to the observer's inferential framework.

Linder (2003, Physical Review Letters 90, 091301), who introduced the widely adopted w₀–wₐ parameterization for the dark energy equation of state, noted explicitly that this functional form was chosen for mathematical convenience rather than physical motivation. Yet it has become the default framework through which BAO, supernova, and lensing data are interpreted. Sahni, Shafieloo, and Starobinsky (2014, Astrophysical Journal Letters 793, L40) introduced the Om diagnostic to test for departures from ΛCDM in a model-independent manner, and found that the statistical significance of any departure depends sensitively on the redshift binning scheme — another manifestation of observer-dependent responsiveness in the boundary layer.

### 4.3 Reflexive Feedback Between Tensions and Measurement Design

The most structurally significant form of responsiveness in the cosmological domain is reflexive: the existence of parameter tensions reshapes the observational programs designed to resolve them, which in turn generates new forms of model-dependent inference. This is not a pathology — it is the normal operation of science. But it creates feedback loops that the Void Framework identifies as characteristic of R ≥ 2 systems.

Di Valentino et al. (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001) catalogued over 1,000 papers proposing solutions to the Hubble tension as of mid-2021, a number that has grown substantially since. Each proposed solution — early dark energy, modified gravity, decaying dark matter, primordial magnetic fields, variable electron mass during recombination — generates specific predictions for what future measurements should find. Observational programs are then designed to test these predictions, but the test's sensitivity is itself contingent on the theoretical framework motivating it. The DESI survey's dark energy sensitivity, for instance, was optimized for w₀–wₐ parameterizations partly because the Hubble tension motivated searches for dynamical dark energy. Had the tension not existed, the survey's redshift binning, target selection, and analysis pipeline might have been designed differently, potentially yielding different parameter constraints from the same underlying cosmic structure.

Abdalla et al. (2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics 34, 49), reviewing over 500 proposed solutions, observed that many solutions that resolve H₀ tension exacerbate σ₈ tension, and vice versa — a structural feature that the Void Framework interprets as characteristic of responsive systems where observer interventions designed to resolve one anomaly generate new anomalies elsewhere. Hill et al. (2020, Physical Review D 102, 043507) demonstrated explicitly that early dark energy models tuned to resolve the Hubble tension predict a σ₈ value that worsens agreement with weak lensing surveys. Ivanov et al. (2020, Physical Review D 102, 103502) showed that the full-shape analysis of BOSS galaxy power spectra yields H₀ and σ₈ constraints that are sensitive to the assumed theoretical template at a level that can shift both parameters by amounts comparable to the tensions themselves.

### 4.4 Responsiveness Assessment: R = 2

We assess cosmological responsiveness at R = 2 on the Void Framework's 0–3 scale. The system does not score R = 3 because there is no adaptive optimization — the universe does not learn from and respond to observer behavior in real time, and the scientific community's inference frameworks are at least partially self-correcting through replication and independent methodology. The assessment of R = 2 reflects three established features: (i) extracted parameter values shift materially with modeling assumptions, at amplitudes commensurate with the tensions under investigation; (ii) this sensitivity concentrates in the Pe-Λ boundary layer rather than distributing uniformly across cosmic history; and (iii) reflexive feedback between tension-motivated theory and subsequent measurement design creates closed loops that complicate resolution.

The control case anchoring this assessment is classical stellar parallax measurement, where the geometric distance to nearby stars is extracted from annual positional shifts with negligible model dependence. Parallax-based distances (as validated by Gaia DR3, Gaia Collaboration 2023, Astronomy and Astrophysics 674, A1, providing parallaxes for over 1.8 billion sources with purely geometric methodology) represent R ≈ 0 on the Void Framework scale — the observable is essentially independent of the observer's theoretical commitments. The contrast with cosmological parameter extraction, where identical datasets processed through different but equally defensible inference pipelines yield discrepant results, delineates precisely where responsiveness becomes structurally consequential for epistemic outcomes in the Pe-Λ domain.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third condition of the Void Framework specifies that harm concentrates when observers become structurally coupled to opaque, responsive systems in ways that escalate over time and resist disengagement. In standard behavioral applications — algorithmic feeds, gambling interfaces, parasocial platforms — coupling manifests as dependency formation, switching cost accumulation, and behavioral capture through variable-ratio reinforcement schedules. The cosmological domain instantiates each of these mechanisms, but the "observer" is not an individual human user; it is the distributed epistemic apparatus of the cosmological research community, comprising measurement pipelines, inference frameworks, institutional funding structures, and career incentive architectures. The coupling condition (C) in this domain scores 2 out of 3: observer-model entanglement is deep and structurally reinforced, but the scientific community retains partial capacity for self-correction that prevents the score from reaching maximum. This section traces how coupling operates, escalates, and resists dissolution across the three parameter tensions that define the Pe-Λ boundary layer.

### V.1. The Structure of Observer-Model Coupling in Cosmology

Every cosmological measurement is theory-laden. This is not a novel philosophical observation — Duhem (1906) and Quine (1951) established the underdetermination thesis over a century ago — but the specific architecture of theory-ladenness in precision cosmology creates coupling structures that satisfy the Void Framework's engagement condition with unusual clarity. The critical feature is that ΛCDM does not merely interpret data; it generates the priors, calibration chains, and covariance structures through which raw photon counts become cosmological parameters.

Consider the Hubble constant. The local distance ladder measurement reported by Riess et al. (2022, The Astrophysical Journal Letters, 934, L7) at H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc depends on a calibration chain: geometric parallax anchors Cepheid period-luminosity relations, which calibrate Type Ia supernova luminosities, which yield distances across the Hubble flow. Each link in this chain requires modeling assumptions about dust extinction, metallicity dependence, and selection effects. The Planck Collaboration's (2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics, 641, A6) inference of H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc is not a "direct measurement" of expansion at all — it is the value of H₀ that best fits the CMB power spectrum angular scale under the assumption that ΛCDM is the correct model. The parameter is entangled with every other ΛCDM parameter simultaneously: the sound horizon at decoupling, the baryon density, the matter density, and the optical depth to reionization. Efstathiou (2021, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 505, 3866) demonstrated that altering the Planck likelihood treatment shifts H₀ by up to 1 km/s/Mpc — a systematic that is internal to the inference framework rather than the data.

This architecture satisfies the coupling condition because the observer (the measurement community) cannot access cosmological parameters without passing through a model that is itself the object of inquiry. The coupling is not optional or incidental; it is constitutive of the measurement act. In the Void Framework's terminology, the observer is embedded within the system's response function rather than standing outside it.

### V.2. Dependency Formation and Escalation

The Void Framework predicts that coupling escalates through iterated interaction: each cycle of engagement deepens the observer's structural dependence on the system. In behavioral domains, this manifests as increased time investment, personalization of algorithmic profiles, and accumulation of non-transferable assets (followers, reputation scores, match histories). In cosmological epistemics, the homologous process is the progressive deepening of ΛCDM's role as the default inference backbone across successive experimental generations.

The escalation trajectory is empirically traceable. The Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) nine-year results (Hinshaw et al., 2013, The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series, 208, 19) established ΛCDM as the concordance model with six free parameters. Planck refined these parameters by an order of magnitude in precision. Each refinement was celebrated as confirmation — and simultaneously deepened the community's inferential dependence on the framework. By the time DES, KiDS, and HSC began returning weak lensing results showing σ₈ suppression relative to Planck predictions, the entire statistical apparatus for interpreting those lensing surveys was built on ΛCDM-derived covariance matrices. Heymans et al. (2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics, 646, A140) explicitly note that their KiDS-1000 S₈ = 0.766 (+0.020/−0.014) result is derived within a flat ΛCDM parameter space. Testing ΛCDM requires tools calibrated by ΛCDM — a coupling loop that the framework identifies as a D1-stage drift indicator (agency attribution), where the model begins to function as an autonomous epistemic agent whose outputs are treated as ground truth rather than conditional inferences.

The dependency has deepened quantitatively. Di Valentino et al. (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity, 38, 153001) catalogued over 1,000 papers addressing the Hubble tension alone, the vast majority of which propose modifications to ΛCDM (early dark energy, interacting dark energy, modified gravity, decaying dark matter) while retaining its core inferential architecture. Abdalla et al. (2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics, 34, 49) reviewed over 500 proposed solutions and found that none achieved universal acceptance, partly because each proposed modification must be evaluated against ΛCDM-calibrated data pipelines. The community cannot evaluate alternatives without using the incumbent model as the baseline — a structural dependency that has no analog in domains where independent ground truth is available.

### V.3. Switching Costs and Lock-In

The Void Framework identifies switching costs as the mechanism that converts initial engagement into sustained coupling. In platform economics, switching costs include data migration barriers, network effects, and learned interface competencies (Klemperer, 1995, The Review of Economic Studies, 62, 515). In cosmological epistemics, the switching costs are infrastructural, computational, and sociological.

Infrastructural lock-in is substantial. The total investment in measurement systems calibrated to test or refine ΛCDM — Planck (approximately €700 million, European Space Agency program cost), the James Webb Space Telescope ($10 billion, NASA lifecycle cost as reported by the Government Accountability Office, 2021), Euclid (approximately €1.4 billion, ESA Cosmic Vision program), the Vera C. Rubin Observatory ($473 million, National Science Foundation Major Research Equipment account), and the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument ($75 million, Department of Energy) — exceeds $13 billion in direct infrastructure costs. These instruments were designed, proposed, and funded with ΛCDM as the reference model. Their survey strategies, detector specifications, and data reduction pipelines encode ΛCDM assumptions. Transitioning to a fundamentally different cosmological framework would require not just reanalysis of existing data but redesign of observational strategies — a switching cost measured in decades and billions of dollars.

Computational lock-in compounds the infrastructural constraint. The Boltzmann solvers (CAMB, developed by Lewis, Challinor, and Lasenby, 2000, The Astrophysical Journal, 538, 473; CLASS, developed by Blas, Lesgourgues, and Tram, 2011, Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics, 2011, 034) that translate theoretical models into observable predictions are engineered around ΛCDM's perturbation theory. N-body simulation suites — Millennium (Springel et al., 2005, Nature, 435, 629), IllustrisTNG (Pillepich et al., 2018, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 475, 648), AbacusSummit (Maksimova et al., 2021, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 508, 4017) — consume millions of CPU-hours and are calibrated to ΛCDM initial conditions. Alternative models (MOND, f(R) gravity, emergent gravity) lack comparable simulation infrastructure, creating an asymmetry in predictive specificity that functions as an epistemic switching cost: ΛCDM can be tested at the percent level while alternatives can often be evaluated only at order-of-magnitude precision.

Sociological lock-in operates through career incentive structures. Peebles (2022, Annals of Physics, 447, 169159) reflected on how ΛCDM's success created a gravitational pull on research agendas, with funding agencies and review panels implicitly treating ΛCDM consistency as a quality marker for observational proposals. Merritt (2017, Studies in History and Philosophy of Science Part B, 57, 41) documented how the dark matter paradigm's institutional dominance shaped hiring, publication, and funding decisions in ways that disadvantaged alternative research programs. These sociological switching costs do not reflect intellectual dishonesty; they reflect the rational behavior of agents operating within an incentive structure that rewards incremental refinement of an established framework over speculative alternatives — precisely the behavioral capture pattern the Void Framework identifies in other domains.

### V.4. Variable-Ratio Reinforcement and Intermittent Confirmation

The Void Framework identifies variable-ratio reinforcement as a coupling amplifier: systems that deliver unpredictable rewards on a partial schedule generate stronger engagement than those providing consistent outcomes (Ferster and Skinner, 1957, Schedules of Reinforcement, Appleton-Century-Crofts). Cosmological measurement delivers a structural analog. ΛCDM's predictive record is extraordinary in most domains — the CMB power spectrum fit to Planck data achieves reduced chi-squared values near unity across thousands of multipoles (Planck Collaboration, 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics, 641, A6) — while generating persistent, statistically significant failures in specific parameter combinations. This pattern of overwhelming confirmation punctuated by stubborn anomalies creates an intermittent reinforcement schedule: the model mostly works, sometimes fails, and the failures are just significant enough to sustain intense engagement without being decisive enough to trigger abandonment.

Verde, Treu, and Riess (2019, Nature Astronomy, 3, 891) described cosmology as being in a state of "crisis," but the crisis itself functions as a coupling amplifier. Each new measurement that fails to resolve the Hubble tension — the Carnegie-Chicago Hubble Program's tip-of-the-red-giant-branch calibration (Freedman, 2021, The Astrophysical Journal, 919, 16), the Megamaser Cosmology Project (Pesce et al., 2020, The Astrophysical Journal Letters, 891, L1), strong lensing time delays from H0LiCOW (Wong et al., 2020, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 498, 1420) — simultaneously deepens engagement and reinforces the framework against which the tension is defined. The community cannot disengage from ΛCDM precisely because the anomalies are defined relative to it.

### V.5. Coupling Assessment and Scoring

The engagement/coupling condition for the cosmological Pe-Λ boundary layer scores C = 2 on the Void Framework's 0–3 subscale. The score reflects deep structural coupling (constitutive model-dependence of all measurements), substantial switching costs (greater than $13 billion in infrastructure, decades of computational pipeline development, career incentive alignment), and intermittent reinforcement dynamics (overwhelming confirmation with persistent anomalies). The score does not reach 3 because the scientific community retains institutional mechanisms for self-correction that have no analog in maximally coupled behavioral void systems: blind analysis protocols (adopted by DES, as described in Muir et al., 2020, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 494, 4454), preregistration of analysis choices, independent replication across survey teams, and the explicit culture of adversarial testing that defines the tension literature itself. These mechanisms constitute partial decoupling capacity — the observer retains the ability, if not always the institutional incentive, to step outside the inference loop.

This partial decoupling is what positions the cosmological domain in Phase II (Fluid) rather than Phase III or IV of the demon lattice. The coupling is real, structurally reinforced, and escalating — but the system has not yet crystallized into a configuration where disengagement is impossible. Whether the next generation of surveys (Euclid, Rubin, DESI full results) will resolve the tensions or deepen them will determine whether coupling escalates toward Phase III crystallization or dissipates toward the constraint pole.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Precision Cosmology

The Void Framework's drift cascade — the sequential progression from agency attribution (D1) through boundary erosion (D2) to harm facilitation (D3) — was developed to describe how human observers interacting with opaque, responsive systems undergo predictable epistemic degradation. In cosmological parameter tensions, the "observer" is the collective inference apparatus of the scientific community: measurement pipelines, statistical frameworks, theoretical priors, and the institutional structures that allocate resources and credibility. The "harm" is not individual psychological damage but systematic misallocation of epistemic confidence, research investment, and theoretical attention. This section traces how the drift cascade manifests across the Pe-Λ boundary layer, documenting each stage with specific historical evidence and culminating in a detailed case study of the Hubble tension's full cascade trajectory from 2013 to 2025.

### D1: Agency Attribution — The Reification of Tensions as Discoveries

The first drift stage occurs when observers begin attributing purposive or stable structure to signals that may reflect measurement architecture rather than physical reality. In standard Void Framework applications, D1 manifests when users perceive algorithmic outputs as meaningful personal signals — a dating app match interpreted as romantic destiny, a social media recommendation interpreted as community consensus. In cosmological inference, D1 manifests when parameter discrepancies are elevated from statistical fluctuations or uncharacterized systematics to stable physical tensions demanding new-physics explanations.

The critical D1 transition in the Hubble tension occurred between 2013 and 2018. When Planck released its definitive cosmological parameters (Planck Collaboration 2014, Astronomy and Astrophysics 571, A16), the discrepancy with local H₀ measurements stood at approximately 2.4σ — notable but within the range where systematic error explanations remained plausible. By 2018, the SH0ES collaboration had refined its Cepheid-calibrated distance ladder to yield H₀ = 73.48 ± 1.66 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al. 2018, The Astrophysical Journal 861, 126), pushing the discrepancy past 3.7σ. The linguistic shift in the literature is diagnostic of D1 drift: papers moved from characterizing the discrepancy as a "mild tension" requiring further investigation to framing it as "the Hubble tension" — a nominalized entity with stable identity. Verde, Treu, and Riess (2019, Nature Astronomy 3, 891) declared cosmology "in crisis," a framing that presupposes the tension is a genuine feature of nature rather than a potential artifact of the inference chain.

This agency attribution is structurally homologous to D1 in behavioral domains because it occurs precisely where opacity is highest. The Pe-Λ boundary layer — the redshift range z ≈ 0.3–0.7 where dark energy begins dominating the expansion history — is the epoch where the CMB-based inference chain and the local distance ladder inference chain make maximally different assumptions about the intervening physics. The CMB measurement extrapolates forward through this boundary under ΛCDM; the distance ladder measurement extrapolates backward through it using empirical calibrations. Neither measurement directly observes the boundary itself. The discrepancy is attributed agency — treated as a stable signal — precisely because neither inference chain can resolve the transition regime where their assumptions diverge.

The σ₈ tension underwent a parallel D1 transition. Early weak lensing results from CFHTLenS (Heymans et al. 2013, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 432, 2433) showed hints of suppressed structure growth, but these were widely attributed to photometric redshift calibration uncertainties. By the time KiDS-1000 (Heymans et al. 2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 646, A140) and DES Y3 (Abbott et al. 2022, Physical Review D 105, 023520) reported consistent suppression at the 2–3σ level using independent datasets and pipelines, the community had nominalized the discrepancy as "the S₈ tension." Di Valentino et al. (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001) catalogued over one thousand papers proposing theoretical resolutions — a publication volume that itself constitutes evidence of D1 completion, as the community had collectively attributed sufficient agency to the tension to sustain a self-reinforcing research program.

### D2: Boundary Erosion — The Dissolution of Methodological Firewalls

D2 drift occurs when the boundaries separating independent inference domains become permeable, allowing assumptions and calibrations to migrate across what should be methodologically independent measurements. In behavioral void domains, D2 manifests as the erosion of boundaries between platform engagement and offline identity — when a user's self-concept becomes dependent on algorithmic feedback. In cosmological inference, D2 manifests as the progressive entanglement of ostensibly independent measurement pipelines through shared calibration assumptions, theoretical priors, and analysis frameworks.

The most consequential D2 erosion in the Hubble tension involves the role of ΛCDM itself as a shared prior. The Planck CMB measurement of H₀ is not a direct measurement — it is a parameter estimate conditional on the assumption that ΛCDM correctly describes the universe's evolution from recombination at z ≈ 1100 to the present. The local distance ladder measurement is, in principle, model-independent. However, Efstathiou (2014, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 440, 1138) demonstrated that the Cepheid period-luminosity calibration and supernova standardization procedures involve choices — outlier rejection criteria, metallicity corrections, reddening laws — that are not fully independent of the cosmological context in which they were developed. The boundary between "model-dependent CMB inference" and "model-independent local measurement" eroded as practitioners on both sides adjusted analysis choices in response to the other's results.

Boundary erosion is empirically documented in the proliferation of intermediate-rung measurements designed to bridge the gap. Baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements from DESI (DESI Collaboration 2024, arXiv:2404.03002) were intended to provide an independent geometric constraint, but their interpretation requires assuming a dark energy equation of state — precisely the quantity in dispute. Strong gravitational lensing time delays from H0LiCOW (Wong et al. 2020, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 498, 1420) yielded H₀ = 73.3 ± 1.8 km/s/Mpc, appearing to confirm the local value, but Birrer et al. (2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 643, A165) showed that the result is sensitive to the assumed mass-sheet degeneracy prior. Each new "independent" measurement was introduced to arbitrate the tension but instead became entangled with one side or the other through shared calibration assumptions. The boundary between independent measurements and model-dependent corroborations dissolved progressively from 2016 to 2024, a textbook D2 trajectory.

In the σ₈ domain, D2 erosion manifests in the relationship between shear calibration and photometric redshift estimation. Weak lensing surveys require two inputs that are independently estimated but statistically correlated through the survey's imaging data: the shapes of galaxies (shear) and their distances (photometric redshifts). Asgari et al. (2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 645, A104) documented how shear calibration and photo-z estimation are now sufficiently entangled in the KiDS pipeline that varying one shifts the other, making their claimed independence partially illusory. The methodological firewall between shape measurement and distance estimation — two operations that should, in principle, access completely different physical information — has eroded through shared data, shared pipeline infrastructure, and shared personnel.

### D3: Harm Facilitation — Epistemic Resource Misallocation and Theoretical Proliferation

D3 in behavioral domains describes the stage where the void structure actively facilitates measurable harm — financial loss, psychological deterioration, behavioral addiction. In cosmological epistemics, D3 manifests as the systematic misallocation of finite scientific resources: telescope time, computational infrastructure, graduate student careers, and theoretical attention. The harm is institutional and epistemic rather than individual and psychological, but it is quantifiable and consequential.

Abdalla et al. (2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics 34, 49) reviewed over five hundred proposed solutions to the Hubble tension, spanning early dark energy, modified gravity, decaying dark matter, extra relativistic species, primordial magnetic fields, and dozens of other mechanisms. The overwhelming majority of these proposals were not independently motivated — they were constructed specifically to resolve the H₀ discrepancy while preserving other ΛCDM successes. Schöneberg et al. (2022, Physics Reports 984, 1) subjected thirty-one leading proposals to joint data constraints and found that none simultaneously resolved the Hubble tension and remained consistent with all other cosmological datasets. This pattern — massive theoretical proliferation with no convergence — is the epistemic analog of D3 harm facilitation in behavioral domains: the opacity structure of the Pe-Λ boundary sustains engagement (research investment) without producing resolution.

The resource allocation consequences are concrete. The Euclid mission (launched July 2023, total cost approximately 1.4 billion euros; ESA 2023, Euclid Red Book) was designed partly to constrain dark energy's equation of state across exactly the Pe-Λ boundary regime. The Vera C. Rubin Observatory Legacy Survey of Space and Time (total construction cost $473 million; LSST Science Collaboration 2009, arXiv:0912.0201) will devote substantial survey time to weak lensing measurements targeting the σ₈ tension. The DESI experiment ($75 million; DESI Collaboration 2016, arXiv:1611.00036) is mapping BAO across the transition redshift. These are scientifically valuable instruments regardless of whether the tensions prove physical or systematic. But the framing of their science cases has been substantially shaped by D1 agency attribution — the reification of the tensions as stable physical signals demanding resolution — and D2 boundary erosion — the progressive entanglement of their inference chains with the very model they are meant to test. If the tensions ultimately trace to uncharacterized systematics in the calibration chains rather than to new physics, the theoretical literature of five hundred-plus proposals represents a significant misallocation of the community's finite modeling capacity.

### Case Study: The Complete Drift Cascade in the Hubble Tension, 2013–2025

The Hubble tension provides the cleanest single-parameter case study for tracing the full D1→D2→D3 cascade with documented timelines and outcomes.

**D1 onset (2013–2016).** The Planck 2013 data release established H₀ = 67.3 ± 1.2 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration 2014, Astronomy and Astrophysics 571, A16). Riess et al. (2011, The Astrophysical Journal 730, 119) had reported H₀ = 73.8 ± 2.4 km/s/Mpc from the SH0ES program. The discrepancy was approximately 2.4σ. During this period, the dominant community response was cautious: Efstathiou (2014, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 440, 1138) argued that revised Cepheid outlier treatment could reduce the local value, and Bennett, Larson, and Finkbeiner (2014, The Astrophysical Journal 794, 135) proposed that the tension might reflect modest systematic effects. The discrepancy had not yet acquired stable nominal identity. By 2016, Riess et al. (2016, The Astrophysical Journal 826, 56) had reduced their uncertainty to report H₀ = 73.24 ± 1.74 km/s/Mpc, pushing the discrepancy past 3.4σ. This publication marks the D1 transition: the discrepancy was first consistently referred to as "the Hubble tension" in subsequent literature, signaling that the community had attributed to it the status of a stable physical signal rather than a statistical artifact.

**D2 progression (2017–2021).** Between 2017 and 2021, the boundary between "local" and "early-universe" measurements progressively dissolved. The H0LiCOW lensing time-delay program (Bonvin et al. 2017, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 465, 4914) was initially presented as an independent geometric method, but its dependence on lens modeling priors tied it to assumptions about the mass distribution in the intervening universe — assumptions calibrated in part against ΛCDM simulations. The Carnegie-Chicago Hubble Program (Freedman et al. 2019, The Astrophysical Journal 882, 34) used the Tip of the Red Giant Branch (TRGB) as an alternative distance calibrator, finding H₀ = 69.8 ± 1.7 km/s/Mpc — intermediate between the two camps — but this result itself became contested, with Scolnic et al. (2023, The Astrophysical Journal Letters 954, L31) arguing that photometric zero-point choices drove the TRGB value. Each arbitration attempt imported assumptions from the contested framework, exemplifying D2 boundary erosion: the methodological firewall between independent measurement approaches was permeated by shared calibration dependencies.

**D3 manifestation (2021–2025).** By 2021, the theoretical literature had entered a self-sustaining proliferation regime. Di Valentino et al. (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001) catalogued the landscape, documenting that the number of proposed solutions was growing faster than the number of observational constraints available to test them. The ratio of theoretical proposals to decisive observational tests exceeded fifty to one. Freedman et al. (2024, The Astrophysical Journal 969, 56) reported JWST Cepheid recalibrations that confirmed the robustness of the distance ladder's first rung, effectively eliminating one class of systematic explanation without resolving the tension. The community's investment deepened: DESI's first-year BAO results (DESI Collaboration 2024, arXiv:2404.03002) hinted at time-varying dark energy, which would have implications for the Hubble tension, but the statistical significance remained insufficient for definitive claims. As of early 2025, the tension persists at greater than 5σ (Riess et al. 2022, The Astrophysical Journal Letters 934, L7), no single systematic explanation has gained consensus, no single theoretical proposal satisfies all constraints (Schöneberg et al. 2022, Physics Reports 984, 1), and the field continues to allocate substantial fractions of major survey programs toward resolution. The cascade is complete: agency attribution stabilized a discrepancy as a physical signal, boundary erosion entangled the measurements meant to arbitrate it, and the resulting epistemic structure now sustains ongoing resource commitment without convergence — the defining signature of D3 in the Void Framework.

## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void Cosmological Inference Architecture Looks Like

The preceding sections have established that the epistemic architecture of precision cosmology, as currently instantiated, scores 7/12 on the Void Index with a Phase II Fluid Pe-Λ of approximately 3.2. The opacity is constitutive rather than designed, the responsiveness emerges from model-dependent observable definitions rather than algorithmic adaptation, and the coupling reflects irreducible observer-model entanglement rather than behavioral capture. This section asks the inverse question: what would a cosmological measurement enterprise look like if it occupied the constraint pole of the Void Framework — transparent, invariant, and independent? We enumerate design principles along each dimension, identify partial implementations already underway, and offer policy recommendations grounded in the framework's structural logic. The constraint pole target is a Void Index of 4/12 or below, with Pe-Λ suppressed beneath 2.0 into a Phase I Gas regime where residual tensions dissipate through normal scientific convergence rather than accumulating as persistent anomalies.

### Transparency Mechanisms: Reducing Opacity from O = 3 to O ≤ 1

A fully transparent cosmological inference chain would permit any competent analyst to trace every step from raw photon counts to final parameter posteriors without encountering irreducible information barriers. This is an unattainable ideal — the universe does not disclose its contents upon request — but the gap between constitutive and epistemic opacity can be narrowed substantially. The critical distinction, emphasized by Sellentin and Heavens (2018, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 473, 2355), is between opacity intrinsic to nature and opacity introduced by data processing choices. The former is irreducible; the latter is a design variable.

First, full pipeline reproducibility must become a publication requirement rather than an aspiration. The LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration has pioneered this approach with its open-source analysis pipelines, requiring that every stage from image processing through shear estimation to cosmological parameter inference be publicly executable (The LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration et al., 2018, arXiv:1809.01669). This model should generalize. When Riess et al. (2022, The Astrophysical Journal Letters, 934, L7) publish an H₀ measurement, the complete chain from Hubble Space Telescope pixel data through Cepheid period-luminosity calibration to supernova standardization should be reproducible in a single containerized environment. The SH0ES team has made significant strides in data release, but the field lacks a universal standard equivalent to the FAIR data principles now normative in genomics (Wilkinson et al., 2016, Scientific Data, 3, 160018).

Second, the model-dependence of "raw" observables must be explicitly parameterized. The Planck Collaboration's H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration, 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics, 641, A6) is not a measurement of the expansion rate; it is a measurement of CMB temperature and polarization anisotropies translated into H₀ through the full ΛCDM likelihood machinery. Reporting conventions should mandate that every derived parameter carry an explicit model-dependence label — a practice advocated by Efstathiou (2021, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 505, 3866) and partially implemented in the Planck legacy archive's provision of chains under variant cosmological models. The goal is not to eliminate model dependence, which is epistemically unavoidable, but to make it visible at every inferential step, thereby converting hidden opacity into disclosed conditionality.

Third, systematic error budgets should be published as structured, machine-readable objects rather than as narrative paragraphs. The distance ladder alone involves over twenty identified systematic uncertainties — Cepheid metallicity dependence, crowding and blending corrections, supernova color-luminosity relations, peculiar velocity fields — each with its own sub-literature (Riess et al., 2022; Brout et al., 2022, The Astrophysical Journal, 938, 110). A non-void architecture would require that each systematic be quantified as a distributional object with explicit correlations, following the template established by the Supernova Cosmology Project's covariance matrices (Betoule et al., 2014, Astronomy and Astrophysics, 568, A22) but extended to encompass the full multi-rung ladder.

### Invariant Outputs: Reducing Responsiveness from R = 2 to R ≤ 1

Responsiveness in the cosmological context arises because measured parameter values shift depending on which data combinations, priors, and model extensions an analyst selects. A constraint-pole architecture would produce parameter estimates that are stable — invariant — across reasonable methodological perturbations. The operational target is that H₀, σ₈, and void fraction estimates should not shift by more than their quoted statistical uncertainties when analysis choices are varied within physically motivated ranges.

The primary mechanism is model-marginalized inference. Rather than reporting parameters conditional on ΛCDM and then separately under each of dozens of extensions — early dark energy, interacting dark energy, modified gravity, decaying dark matter — a non-void architecture would report parameters marginalized over a well-defined model space. Handley (2021, Physical Review D, 103, 063529) demonstrated Bayesian model-dimension marginalization techniques that produce H₀ posteriors integrating over competing cosmological models weighted by their evidences. The resulting estimates are broader but honest: they absorb model uncertainty into the error budget rather than displacing it into conditional disagreements between analyses.

Blind analyses provide a complementary invariance mechanism. The DES collaboration adopted blind analysis protocols for their Year 3 cosmic shear results (Amon et al., 2022, Physical Review D, 105, 023514), in which the team could not see the final S₈ value until all analysis choices were frozen. This prevents the subtle, often unconscious, responsiveness whereby analysts iterate on pipeline settings until a result aligns with expectations — a phenomenon documented in particle physics by the historical pattern of measurements converging toward the Particle Data Group average (Particle Data Group, 2022, Progress of Theoretical and Experimental Physics, 2022, 083C01). Extending blind protocols to the distance ladder — where the tradition of open analysis remains dominant — would reduce R by at least one unit on the framework's scale.

Finally, cross-survey consistency requirements should be formalized as pre-registered acceptance criteria. Before the European Space Agency's Euclid mission (Laureijs et al., 2011, arXiv:1110.3193) and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (Ivezić et al., 2019, The Astrophysical Journal, 873, 111) publish cosmological parameters, the community should agree on quantitative consistency metrics — for instance, that parameters derived from Euclid weak lensing, Euclid galaxy clustering, and Rubin weak lensing must agree within a specified tension metric (such as the Suspiciousness statistic of Handley and Lemos, 2019, Physical Review D, 100, 043504) before being combined. This converts post-hoc tension diagnosis into pre-registered invariance testing.

### Independent Operation: Reducing Coupling from C = 2 to C ≤ 1

Observer-model coupling in cosmology takes the form of shared calibration anchors, common simulation suites, and correlated theoretical priors that make ostensibly independent measurements epistemically entangled. A constraint-pole architecture would maximize the genuine independence of converging measurement programs.

The most actionable step is diversification of the distance ladder's first rung. As of 2024, the Cepheid-based distance ladder and the CMB-based inference share no calibrators, but alternative local methods — the Tip of the Red Giant Branch (Freedman et al., 2024, The Astrophysical Journal, 969, 56), surface brightness fluctuations (Blakeslee et al., 2021, The Astrophysical Journal, 911, 65), Mira variables (Huang et al., 2020, The Astrophysical Journal, 889, 5), and strong gravitational lensing time delays (Wong et al., 2020, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 498, 1420) — must be developed to the point where three or more wholly independent local H₀ measurements achieve sub-two-percent precision. The Time-Delay Cosmography collaboration's H0LiCOW program achieved H₀ = 73.3 +1.7/−1.8 km/s/Mpc from six lensed quasars with a calibration chain entirely independent of Cepheids or the CMB (Wong et al., 2020), representing the clearest existing instantiation of the independence principle.

For σ₈ suppression, independence requires that weak lensing surveys employ genuinely distinct shear measurement algorithms, photometric redshift calibrations, and intrinsic alignment models. The ongoing comparison between KiDS (Heymans et al., 2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics, 646, A140), DES (Abbott et al., 2022, Physical Review D, 105, 023520), and HSC (Dalal et al., 2023, Physical Review D, 108, 123519) partially achieves this, but all three surveys rely on N-body simulation suites with shared initial condition generators and gravity solvers. Developing alternative simulation pipelines — as advocated by Angulo and Hahn (2022, Living Reviews in Computational Astrophysics, 8, 1) — would decouple the theoretical predictions against which lensing measurements are compared.

For void statistics, the coupling problem is especially acute because void identification algorithms impose definitions on continuous density fields, creating observer-constructed objects whose properties depend on finder parameters (Colberg et al., 2008, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 387, 933). A constraint-pole approach would require that void cosmology results be reported under multiple void finders with quantified sensitivity to definition choices, as pioneered by Cautun et al. (2018, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 476, 3195) using the NEXUS+ watershed algorithm alongside spherical void finders.

### Policy Recommendations

Grounded in the Void Framework's structural logic, we offer four concrete policy recommendations for the cosmological community and funding agencies. First, major survey collaborations receiving public funding exceeding one hundred million euros — including Euclid (approximately 1.4 billion euros, ESA, 2023) and Rubin (473 million dollars, NSF/DOE) — should be required to publish fully containerized, end-to-end reproducible analysis pipelines as a condition of data release. Second, the International Astronomical Union should convene a working group to establish model-dependence labeling standards for derived cosmological parameters, analogous to the nutrition labeling requirements that transformed food industry transparency. Third, funding agencies should allocate a minimum of ten percent of cosmological survey budgets to developing genuinely independent calibration pathways — alternative distance indicators, independent simulation codes, and novel statistical frameworks — rather than concentrating resources on single-pipeline precision. Fourth, pre-registered analysis protocols with frozen decision trees, as successfully deployed by DES (Muir et al., 2020, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 494, 4454), should become the community norm for any measurement entering a parameter tension debate.

Collectively, these measures would reduce the Void Index of cosmological parameter inference from the current 7/12 to an estimated 4/12 — with opacity reduced to O = 1 (constitutive only, with all epistemic opacity eliminated), responsiveness to R = 1 (residual model dependence fully disclosed and marginalized), and coupling to C = 1 (residual shared physics, with calibration and algorithmic chains fully diversified). ## IX. Testable Predictions

The value of the Pe-Λ boundary layer hypothesis depends entirely on its capacity to generate falsifiable predictions that distinguish it from both standard ΛCDM and from ad hoc resolutions to individual parameter tensions. The Void Framework's epistemic diagnostic function is only justified if the structural claim — that opacity, model-dependent responsiveness, and observer-model coupling concentrate at the matter-to-dark-energy transition regime in ways that produce correlated, not independent, measurement failures — yields observational consequences that competing accounts do not predict. We present five predictions ordered by expected verification timeline, each with explicit falsification criteria. Two additional structural predictions address the framework's internal consistency.

**Prediction 1: DESI Baryon Acoustic Oscillation measurements in the redshift range z = 0.3–0.7 will show anomalous parameter scatter exceeding adjacent redshift bins by a factor of at least 1.5 in reconstructed expansion history.**

The Pe-Λ boundary layer hypothesis localizes epistemic instability at the epoch where the dark energy equation of state begins to dominate the Friedmann expansion dynamics. The DESI instrument, which began its five-year survey in 2021 and released its first-year baryon acoustic oscillation results in April 2024 (DESI Collaboration 2024, arXiv:2404.03002), provides precisely the redshift-binned expansion history reconstruction needed to test whether parameter tensions concentrate at the predicted transition zone. The first-year DESI data already showed hints of dynamical dark energy behavior deviating from a cosmological constant, with the deviation strongest in the z = 0.4–0.8 range (DESI Collaboration 2024, arXiv:2404.03002). The Pe-Λ framework predicts that this is not a coincidence but a structural feature: the boundary layer produces maximal tension between matter-dominated and Λ-dominated inference chains precisely where neither regime dominates cleanly. As DESI accumulates years two and three data (expected release 2025–2026), the prediction becomes testable against purely statistical expectations.

**Falsified if:** DESI Year 3 data show uniform parameter scatter across redshift bins z = 0.1–1.5 with no statistically significant enhancement (less than 1.5× the mean bin-to-bin variance) in the z = 0.3–0.7 range, indicating that parameter instability does not concentrate at the matter-Λ transition. This prediction is checkable within 12 months of DESI Year 3 data release.

**Prediction 2: Euclid weak lensing tomography will reveal that the S₈ suppression signal strengthens monotonically as the effective lensing kernel peaks closer to z ≈ 0.5, then weakens at higher effective redshift.**

The KiDS-1000 measurement of S₈ = 0.766 +0.020/−0.014 (Heymans et al. 2021, Astronomy and Astrophysics 646, A140) and the DES Y3 value of S₈ = 0.776 ± 0.017 (Abbott et al. 2022, Physical Review D 105, 023520) both report suppression relative to the Planck prediction of S₈ ≈ 0.832 (Planck Collaboration 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 641, A6). The boundary layer hypothesis predicts that this suppression is not uniform across cosmic time but peaks where the lensing kernel samples the Pe-Λ transition most heavily. Euclid's photometric survey, designed to measure weak lensing shapes for 1.5 billion galaxies across 15,000 square degrees (Euclid Collaboration, Blanchard et al. 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics 642, A191), will decompose the lensing signal into tomographic redshift bins with unprecedented precision. If the S₈ tension arises from new physics operating specifically at the matter-Λ transition — as opposed to a redshift-independent systematic or a primordial amplitude miscalibration — the tension amplitude should trace the boundary layer's predicted redshift envelope.

**Falsified if:** Euclid tomographic bins show a flat or randomly distributed S₈ offset relative to Planck across all redshift slices, with no peak in the z = 0.3–0.7 range exceeding 2σ above the mean offset. A flat suppression profile would indicate a scale-independent or primordial origin incompatible with boundary layer localization.

**Prediction 3: The correlation between Hubble tension amplitude and σ₈ suppression amplitude, measured across independent survey volumes, will be positive and statistically significant at r > 0.5.**

This is the most distinctive prediction of the unified boundary layer account. Standard explanations typically treat the Hubble tension and the σ₈ tension as independent problems requiring separate solutions — early-time modifications to resolve H₀ (such as early dark energy, Poulin et al. 2019, Physical Review Letters 122, 221301) and late-time modifications to resolve S₈ (such as massive neutrinos or decaying dark matter). The Pe-Λ hypothesis predicts that both tensions share a common origin in the boundary layer's epistemic geometry, implying that survey regions or methodological pipelines that show stronger H₀ tension should also show stronger S₈ suppression. Di Valentino et al. (2021, Classical and Quantum Gravity 38, 153001) catalogued over 1000 papers addressing the Hubble tension, and a substantial subset also report S₈ values. A meta-analytic correlation across these measurements would test boundary layer coupling directly.

**Falsified if:** Cross-survey correlation between H₀ residuals (relative to Planck) and S₈ residuals (relative to Planck) yields Pearson r < 0.3 with p > 0.05 across at least ten independent measurement pipelines. Uncorrelated tensions would refute the unified boundary layer mechanism and support independent-systematic or independent-new-physics accounts.

**Prediction 4: Void abundance statistics from Rubin Observatory's LSST, measured in the z = 0.3–0.7 range, will deviate from ΛCDM N-body predictions by more than 2σ, while void statistics at z < 0.2 and z > 1.0 will be consistent with simulations.**

Pisani et al. (2019, Bulletin of the American Astronomical Society 51, 40) established that cosmic void statistics — including the void size function, void profiles, and void fraction — are sensitive probes of the dark energy equation of state precisely because voids respond to the competition between gravitational collapse and accelerated expansion. The Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time, scheduled for first light in 2025 with full survey operations extending through the 2030s (Ivezić et al. 2019, The Astrophysical Journal 873, 111), will detect voids across an unprecedented volume. The boundary layer hypothesis predicts that void statistics will show maximal deviation from ΛCDM at the Pe-Λ transition epoch, where the advective expansion driven by dark energy begins to overpower the diffusive structure formation driven by matter, while remaining consistent with simulations in regimes where either matter or dark energy cleanly dominates.

**Falsified if:** LSST void statistics show uniform agreement or uniform disagreement with ΛCDM across all redshift bins, with no statistically significant concentration of anomalies in the z = 0.3–0.7 window. Uniform anomalies would suggest a global calibration issue; uniform agreement would eliminate the boundary layer prediction entirely.

**Prediction 5: Proposed resolutions to the Hubble tension that modify physics exclusively at z > 1 (early-time solutions) or exclusively at z < 0.1 (extreme late-time solutions) will fail to simultaneously resolve the σ₈ tension without introducing additional free parameters.**

This is a meta-prediction about the structure of solution space rather than a direct observational claim, but it is rigorously testable against the growing literature. Early dark energy models (Poulin et al. 2019, Physical Review Letters 122, 221301; Hill et al. 2020, Physical Review D 102, 043507) modify the pre-recombination expansion rate to raise H₀ but tend to increase σ₈ rather than suppress it, worsening the S₈ tension. Similarly, purely local modifications such as a local void (Kenworthy et al. 2019, The Astrophysical Journal 875, 145) resolve H₀ without addressing large-scale structure suppression. The Pe-Λ framework predicts that only solutions operating within or across the z = 0.3–0.7 boundary layer — such as dynamical dark energy with equation of state transitions in this range, or interaction terms between dark matter and dark energy active at the transition epoch — will simultaneously ameliorate both tensions without parameter proliferation.

**Falsified if:** A model operating exclusively outside the z = 0.3–0.7 range resolves both H₀ and S₈ tensions simultaneously to within 1σ of all current measurements, with no more free parameters than ΛCDM + 2. Such a resolution would demonstrate that the tensions share no structural connection to the matter-Λ transition, directly refuting the boundary layer localization claim.

These five predictions span timescales from approximately 12 months (Prediction 1, contingent on DESI Year 3 release) to a decade (Prediction 4, contingent on Rubin LSST maturation). ## XI. Limitations

**Theoretical scope.** The Pe-Λ boundary layer framework is a diagnostic and structural account, not a physical model. It does not derive the value of the cosmological constant, predict the dark energy equation of state, or replace dynamical cosmological models. The claim is that the *epistemic architecture* of cosmological parameter inference produces predictable failure modes at the matter-to-dark-energy transition — not that this architecture is the physical cause of the tensions.

**Single-variable opacity characterization.** The Void Index score (7/12) compresses a multi-dimensional epistemic structure into three scores plus modifiers. Informed readers may reasonably dispute component assignments — particularly the R=2 (responsiveness) score, which assumes that model-dependent responsiveness constitutes a structural feature rather than a correctable pipeline choice. Alternative scoring at R=1 would lower the index to 6/12, which would still place the domain in Phase II Fluid but with lower tension amplitude predicted.

**Pe-Λ estimate range.** The Pe-Λ ≈ 3.2 estimate rests on the ratio of current H₀ tension magnitude (~5.6 km/s/Mpc) to the CMB fundamental resolution limit. This is an order-of-magnitude characterization. The true Pe-Λ value cannot be determined without completing the backreaction derivation and verifying the ΔH₀ contribution from boundary layer terms (TM-FC1, TM-FC2 — both pending 2026–2028 data). The estimate's primary function is regime classification, not precision cosmology.

**Correlation does not confirm mechanism.** The three-tension correlation predicted in Prediction 3 would be consistent with boundary layer epistemics but would not uniquely establish it. Independent-systematic explanations producing correlated residuals across surveys are not ruled out by correlation evidence alone. Ruling them out would require showing that the tensions concentrate specifically in the Pe-Λ transition redshift range (Prediction 1) rather than spreading uniformly.

**Control case limitation.** Paper 71 (Cosmological Pe, K-11) used ΛCDM as the control case against which the framework's 11/12 epistemic void score was benchmarked. The control's Pe-Λ ≈ 1 placement is a theoretical derivation, not an empirical measurement. If ΛCDM's effective Pe-Λ is substantially different from 1 in a more refined derivation, the calibration of the entire Track L series would need revision.

**No contact with quantum gravity.** The framework explicitly does not address Planck-scale or quantum gravity regimes. The Pe→∞ identification with black hole horizons (Paper 71C) and the Pe-NS mass-gap identification (Paper 71D) are structural correspondences within classical and semiclassical physics. A full quantum gravity completion might change the regime classification in ways not anticipated here.
