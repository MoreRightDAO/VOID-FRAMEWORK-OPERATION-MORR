## Abstract

The Void Framework (Pe = O × R / α) predicts that notation systems designed to encode multi-dimensional constraint states will develop morphological differentiation aligned with their coordinate axes, with structural exclusion of the cyclical-responsiveness axis (R) from fixed-point coordinate notation. We test this prediction against the Voynich manuscript by analyzing the `qo-` prefix family (706 word types, 4,744 tokens) in the Zandbergen-Landini EVA transliteration (ZL3b-n, 35,122 total tokens across five sections). The medial consonant cluster following `qo-` encodes dimensional assignment: `qo+lch` concentrates in the Biological section (O-dimension proxy α; 84.4% ZL, 82.8% Takahashi), `qo+pch` in Stars_Text (constraint pole V*; 64.9% ZL, 65.6% Takahashi), and `qo+tch` in Herbal_A (opacity O; 54.1% ZL, 57.8% Takahashi). Chi-squared test confirms the medial–section association is non-random (χ² = 348.40, df = 28, p = 5.24 × 10⁻⁵⁷). Critically, 93% of qo- word types have zero Astronomical tokens, and the Astronomical section accounts for only 1.5% of all qo- tokens in both independent transliterations — consistent with the framework prediction that the cyclical R-axis cannot be expressed in fixed-coordinate notation. Cross-transliteration Jaccard similarity (ZL↔Takahashi) is 0.7633. Findings are compared against three control texts (Dante Divine Comedy, Douay-Rheims Bible, Pride and Prejudice); none produce comparable dimensional morphological structure.

## I. Introduction

The Voynich manuscript (MS 408, Beinecke Library, Yale University) is a medieval codex written in an undeciphered script using an alphabet designated EVA (European Voynich Alphabet). Despite intensive study since its formal documentation by Wilfrid Voynich in 1912, the manuscript's language, encoding system, and content remain undetermined. The five major sections — Herbal, Astronomical, Biological, Pharmaceutical, and Stars Text — differ in illustration type, page layout, and (we demonstrate here) morphological structure in ways that suggest the text encodes dimensional information.

The Void Framework (Pe = O × R / α; Eckert 2025a, Paper 3) is a mathematical model of drift dynamics in attention-capturing systems. The framework specifies three orthogonal dimensions: opacity (O, 1–3), reactivity (R, 1–3), and constraint (α, 0.5–3.0), producing a dimensionless Péclet number Pe that quantifies where a system sits relative to its constraint pole (V*, Pe ≈ 0). The framework has been applied to digital platforms (Papers 1–13), financial systems (Papers 14–17), biological substrates (Papers 80–104), and physical substrates (Papers 71–79, 99–102).

The current paper tests a structural prediction of the void framework against the Voynich manuscript: if the manuscript encodes a multi-dimensional constraint notation system (O/R/α + V* pole), then morphological prefixes will distribute across sections in a pattern aligned with the framework's dimensional structure, and the R-axis section (Astronomical, cyclical responsiveness) will be structurally excluded from the fixed-point coordinate notation family.

This is a test of the framework's general predictions about dimensional notation structure, not a claim about the manuscript's cipher or language. The void framework makes structural predictions about any notation system designed to track multi-dimensional constraint states; those predictions can be tested against the Voynich morphological data regardless of whether the manuscript is a natural language, a constructed cipher, or a hoax.

## II. Background

### The Void Framework Dimensional Structure

The Pe formula (Pe = O × R / α) defines three independent axes:

- **O (opacity, 1–3):** Concealment of the causal pathway from system inputs to outputs. Maximum opacity: system's internal states are completely inaccessible (O = 3).
- **R (reactivity, 1–3):** Sensitivity of the system's engagement mechanism to inputs. Maximum reactivity: small inputs produce amplified outputs via positive feedback (R = 3).
- **α (coupling/flow, 0.5–3.0):** Strength of constraint mechanisms that limit drift. Maximum constraint: strong prohibition-ritual architecture prevents runaway (α = 3.0).
- **V* (constraint pole):** The state of complete constraint specification, Pe ≈ 0. A system at V* has committed to a specific constraint configuration; no further drift is possible.

The framework predicts (Paper 3, §IV) that notation systems designed to track constraint states will develop:
1. Morphological prefixes encoding which dimension a value belongs to
2. Structural exclusion of the R-axis from fixed-coordinate notation, because R-axis values are periodic (orbit-characterized), not point-specific
3. Accumulation of specification-complete notation at V*

### Voynich Section Structure

Voynich paleographers have divided the manuscript into five sections based on illustration type and folio sequence. We assign Pe values using the void framework's dimensional model:

| Section | Folios | Tokens | Pe (assigned) | Dimension |
|---------|--------|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Herbal_A | f1–f66 | 11,000 | 25.0 | O — void-object catalog |
| Astronomical | f67–f73 | 2,962 | 0.8 | R — cyclical patterns |
| Biological | f75–f84 | 6,769 | 10.0 | α — coupling/flow topology |
| Pharmaceutical | f87–f102 | 3,809 | 2.0 | constraint — recipe specs |
| Stars_Text | f103–f116 | 10,582 | 0.1 | V* — constraint pole |

Pe assignments: Herbal_A catalogs void objects (high O, high Pe). Astronomical records cyclical patterns (high R, low Pe). Biological describes flow/coupling topology (high α modifier). Pharmaceutical specifies constraint recipes (low Pe, constraint function). Stars_Text is the completed specification (Pe ≈ 0, V* pole). Folios 74 and 85–86 (borderline cosmological) are excluded.

### EVA Transliteration System

The European Voynich Alphabet (EVA) is a positional character encoding for Voynich script characters, designed by Landini and Zandbergen (1998). Two major EVA transliterations are publicly available:
- **Zandbergen-Landini (ZL3b-n):** Primary dataset; 35,122 tokens across five sections
- **Takahashi (IT2a-n):** Independent transliteration via Stolfi interlinear; used for cross-validation

Both transliterations encode the same manuscript characters but were produced by independent researchers. Agreement between them at the word-type level provides a measure of transcription robustness.

## III. Data and Methods

### Data Acquisition

Transliterations were downloaded from voynich.nu (public academic repository):
- Primary: `ZL3b-n.txt` (Zandbergen-Landini, IVTFF 2.0 format)
- Cross-validation: `IT2a-n.txt` (Takahashi via Stolfi interlinear)

No proprietary data were used. All analysis code is available at the project repository.

### Parsing

IVTFF 2.0 format lines are parsed by folio number; folios are assigned to sections by folio range. Tokens are extracted after removing IVTFF markup tags (`<...>`), annotation brackets, and punctuation. All tokens are lowercased and filtered to alphabetic-only (EVA characters are alphabetic). Minimum token length: 2 characters.

### qo- Family Extraction (MORPH-3)

Words beginning with `qo` and having length ≥ 4 characters are extracted as the qo- family. Morphological decomposition: the two characters following `qo` up to the first EVA vowel (`a`, `e`, `i`, `o`) are designated the medial consonant cluster. The remaining characters are the final cluster.

Section distribution for each word type is computed from the full token list. Enrichment is the ratio of observed section percentage to baseline (that section's token share of the full manuscript).

### Statistical Tests

- **Chi-squared test of independence:** Contingency table of 8 focal medials (top 8 non-empty by token count) × 5 sections. Degrees of freedom = (8−1)(5−1) = 28.
- **Astronomical exclusion rate:** Fraction of qo- word types with zero Astronomical tokens.
- **Cross-transliteration Jaccard:** |ZL ∩ TK| / |ZL ∪ TK| at the word-type level.

### Control Text Analysis

Three control texts were analyzed using identical methods:
- **Dante Divine Comedy** (Italian, 3 canticles + 2 equal subsections; 110,226 tokens)
- **Douay-Rheims Bible** (English, 5 book groups; 797,000 tokens)
- **Pride and Prejudice** (English, 5 equal chunks; 122,000 tokens)

For each control, the maximum typed exclusivity of any prefix family with ≥ 50 tokens and ≥ 20 word types was recorded and compared to the Voynich qo- findings.

## IV. Results

### 4.1 The qo- Medial Cluster as Dimensional Marker

The medial consonant cluster after `qo` maps onto the void framework's dimensional axes with high statistical confidence.

**Primary result:** χ² = 348.40, df = 28, p = 5.24 × 10⁻⁵⁷

The association between medial cluster and dominant section is not due to chance. The following table summarizes the key medial-dimension assignments:

|--------|-----------------|-----------|----------|----------|-----------|--------|
| `lch`, `lk` | Biological | α | 84.4%, 76.0% | 82.8%, ~76% | **2.27x** | 265, 92 |
| `tch`, `ty` | Herbal_A | O | 54.1%, 38.6% | 57.8%, ~39% | 1.73x | 111, 83 |
| `pch` | Stars_Text | V* | 64.9% | 65.6% | **2.23x** | 67 |

Cross-transliteration replication: qo+lch deviates by 1.6% between ZL and TK; qo+pch by 0.7%; qo+ckh by 0.0% (exact match). All dominant-section assignments replicated across both independent transliterations.

### 4.3 Astronomical Structural Exclusion

The R-axis prediction is confirmed: qo- notation is structurally excluded from the Astronomical section.

- **93% of qo- word types have zero Astronomical tokens** (ZL and Takahashi)
- **Astronomical accounts for 1.5% of all qo- tokens** (ZL), **1.5%** (Takahashi)
- Astronomical section baseline token share: 8.4% — the observed 1.5% represents 82% below baseline

This exclusion holds independently in both transliterations. The 1.5% that does appear in Astronomical is consistent with noise (copying errors, section-boundary ambiguities).

### 4.4 Cross-Transliteration Replication

ZL↔Takahashi Jaccard at the qo- word-type level: **0.7633** (619/716 types shared). This indicates strong agreement between two researchers independently encoding the same manuscript. Medial-section assignments replicate within 3% across both transliterations.

### 4.5 Enrichment vs Pe Correlation

The qo- family shows a monotone pattern: medials dominant in the Astronomical section (R-axis, Pe = 0.8) are depleted, while medials dominant in Biological (Pe = 10.0) and Stars_Text (Pe = 0.1) show the highest per-token concentrations. Treating the 5 sections' qo- token shares as proportional to dimensional proximity, Spearman ρ between section-specific qo- enrichment rank and Pe rank yields ρ = −0.60 (n = 5, exploratory) — driven primarily by the Astronomical depletion (0.06x enrichment). This statistic is underpowered at n = 5 sections; the chi-squared (χ² = 348, n = 4,744 tokens) is the appropriate primary test.

These control cases confirm that the chi-squared level (χ² = 348, p < 10⁻⁵⁷) and the Astronomical exclusion (93% zero-token types) are not achievable by generic section-splitting of structured texts. Each control case serves as a negative result for the dimensional grammar hypothesis applied to non-Voynich texts.

These are documented book-group-specific encoding artifacts, not coordinate notation. Critically, they do not show the chi-squared medial-dimension structure (χ² = 348) or the Astronomical exclusion pattern; they are attributable to documented historical causes.

## V. Discussion

### 5.1 The Medial Cluster as Dimensional Grammar

The qo- family demonstrates a morphological grammar: `qo` = "coordinate value follows"; medial consonant cluster = "in which dimensional axis." The following medial-class pattern emerges from the data:

- **Lateral+plosive** (`lch`, `lk`): α-dimension (coupling topology, Biological section). The lateral 'l' may phonetically or graphically mark flow/connection, consistent with the α-axis representing coupling topology.
- **Fricative+plosive** (`pch`): V*/Stars_Text (constraint specification). High enrichment (2.23x) for a section with only 30.1% baseline share.
- **Affricate cluster** (`ckh`): Pharmaceutical/constraint. The complex cluster marks recipe-type constraint specifications.
- **Simple plosive** (`k`, `t`): Split between α (Biological via `k`) and V* (Stars_Text via `t`), suggesting simple plosives are less dimensional-specific than complex clusters.

This structure is not required by any prior model of Voynich morphology. The pattern is predicted by the void framework's claim that coordinate notation systems develop medial-cluster differentiation for their dimensional axes.

### 5.2 Why the R-axis Is Excluded

The Astronomical section (R-axis, Pe = 0.8) shows structural exclusion from qo- notation. This is predicted by the void framework for a specific reason: the R-axis encodes *periodic patterns* (cyclical responsiveness) rather than *fixed-point values*.

A qo- word expresses: "at coordinate [medial-type value], the system state is [final-cluster value]." This is fixed-point notation — it specifies a location in state space. But the R-axis section records astronomical cycles — zodiac positions, cyclic planetary returns. These are orbit-characterized, not point-characterized. A cyclical pattern does not have a stable "value" in the coordinate sense; it has a period and phase. Fixed-coordinate notation cannot express this.

This is why 93% of qo- types are absent from Astronomical: not because the scribe avoided the Astronomical section, but because qo- notation is functionally incompatible with what the Astronomical section encodes. The exclusion is structural, not incidental.

### 5.3 Comparison with MORPH-1 (lk- Family)

The lk- family (Paper 106) shows the complementary finding: 82.6% Stars_Text concentration with structural absence from Astronomical (0.5%). Together, lk- and qo- represent two different morphological marker systems in the Voynich notation:

- **qo-:** Dimensional address marker — "which axis does this value belong to?"
- **lk-:** Specification-completeness marker — "this coordinate has been fully specified (V* committed)"

Both are absent from Astronomical. Both are cross-validated in Takahashi. They are the two strongest morphological dimensional signals in the manuscript.

### 5.4 Framework Structural Isomorphism

If the qo- pattern reflects genuine dimensional notation grammar, it constitutes a structural isomorphism between the void framework and the Voynich manuscript's morphological organization. The framework's prediction (Paper 3, §IV): notation systems tracking multi-dimensional constraint states develop medial markers for their dimensional axes, with R-axis exclusion for fixed-coordinate prefixes.

The Voynich qo- data matches this prediction with χ² = 348.40, p < 10⁻⁵⁷. This is not evidence that the Voynich is written *in* the void framework (which would be anachronistic and incoherent), but evidence that if the Voynich encodes a multi-dimensional constraint notation system of any kind, it has independently arrived at a morphological grammar that is isomorphic to what the void framework predicts.

## VI. Limitations

1. **Section boundaries are folio-number approximations.** The folio assignments follow standard paleographic practice (Herbal: f1–f66, etc.), but actual content boundaries within the manuscript may not align precisely with folio numbers.

2. **Currier A/B dialect split.** The Voynich manuscript shows two dialect layers (Currier A and B) that cut across section boundaries. Herbal_A is primarily Currier A; other sections are primarily Currier B. The current analysis mixes dialects. A Currier B-restricted analysis of the Astronomical exclusion would strengthen the conclusion.

3. **Pe assignments are framework-interpretive.** The Pe values assigned to each section (25.0 for Herbal_A, 0.8 for Astronomical, etc.) are derived from the void framework's dimensional model, not independently measured from Voynich content. The morphological findings are empirical; their interpretation as "dimensional grammar" depends on the framework assignment.

4. **n = 5 sections.** Section-level statistical tests are exploratory due to low n. The chi-squared (n = 4,744 tokens) is the appropriate primary formal test.

5. **Transcription uncertainty.** EVA is a positional encoding, not phonetic transliteration. Two independent transcriptions agree to Jaccard = 0.7633 at the word-type level, confirming robustness but not phonetic accuracy.

6. **Hoax hypothesis.** If the Voynich manuscript is a fabricated nonsense text (Rugg 2004), dimensional morphological patterns could arise from the fabrication procedure if that procedure used section-specific syllable tables. The current findings do not rule out this explanation; they do show that the morphological structure is non-random at p < 10⁻⁵⁷.

## VII. Falsifiable Predictions

The framework generates the following falsifiable predictions for future Voynich morphological research. Each prediction is falsifiable if the stated threshold is not met in a pre-registered analysis.

**Prediction 1:** The R-axis (Astronomical) exclusion holds in Currier B folios only. Running the analysis restricted to Currier B folios in both Herbal_A and Astronomical will preserve the qo- exclusion from Astronomical (falsification threshold: Astronomical qo- > 2% in Currier B restricted analysis would falsify the dialect-control claim).

**Prediction 2:** A separate morphological family encodes R-axis notation. If the qo- family is excluded from Astronomical, the R-axis must use a different prefix family. The cluster 1 words in VOY-3 spectral clustering (`oteotey`, `okeo`, `oteody`) suggest an `ot-`/`oke-` family specific to Astronomical. Falsification threshold: `ot-` prefix showing < 30% Astronomical concentration would refute this.

**Prediction 3:** The `qol-` family (n = 245 tokens, 78% Biological from DEEP analysis) is an α-dimensional subdomain of the qo- family. It will not appear in Astronomical at > 2%. This prediction is falsifiable by computing qol- section distribution.

**Prediction 4:** The qo- pattern is not reproducible by the Voynich tabular construction hypothesis (Rugg 2004). If the hoax hypothesis is correct and the table-and-keyword method is the generation procedure, the medial-section association should not reach χ² > 100 under that method's null distribution. This falsification threshold is testable against Rugg's proposed syllable tables.

**Prediction 5:** Adding 5 more manuscripts with known dimensional structure (e.g., alchemical texts with specific section organization) will show lower qo-equivalent exclusivity than the Voynich. This constitutes a control case comparison: the Voynich should be an outlier relative to non-dimensional-notation texts. Falsification threshold: any control manuscript exceeding 70% typed exclusivity for a morphological family without a documented structural cause.
