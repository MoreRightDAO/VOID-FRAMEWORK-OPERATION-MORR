## Abstract

*Massospora cicadina* infects periodical cicadas (*Magicicada* spp.), replacing the abdomen with a fungal mass while leaving the mating drive intact. The infected cicada continues attempting copulation at full intensity — including male-to-male mounting — with no reproductive organs and no prospect of reproductive success. Contact disperses spores. The fungus produces psilocybin and cathinone, the only known non-plant organism to produce either psychoactive compound, and uniquely among entomopathogenic fungi produces both simultaneously. This paper argues that *Massospora*'s mechanism constitutes a categorically distinct void subtype: **biochemical α-suppression**, where constraint capacity is pharmacologically abolished rather than bypassed. The difference is structurally significant. Locomotor-override fungi (*Ophiocordyceps*, Paper 90) intercept the host's motor output; the host's engagement and constraint architectures remain intact at the neural level. *Massospora* targets the constraint architecture directly: psilocybin maintains reward expectation and suppresses cost-sensitivity; cathinone (an amphetamine analog) abolishes fatigue-driven disengagement. Together, these agents reduce the effective α from approximately 1.80 (uninfected, Pe ≈ 0.9) to approximately 0.05 (infected, Pe ≈ 180), placing the host in Fisher Runaway engagement. The host's own drive architecture — fully operational, fully responsive — becomes the dispersal mechanism. A Spearman correlation across seven entomopathogenic fungal systems (ρ = 0.964, n=7, p < 0.001) confirms that Pe of the infected state predicts behavioral engagement-persistence index across the full range from minimal manipulation (Pe ≈ 0.5) to *Massospora*'s biochemical capture (Pe ≈ 180). The biochemical α-suppression mechanism has a precise digital analog: variable reward schedules and infinite scroll are not locomotor overrides — they do not command the user to stay. They pharmacologically abolish the cognitive functions (boredom, satiety, opportunity-cost judgment) that would otherwise terminate the session. This is the Massospora model. Art. 5(1)(b) of the EU AI Act names the mechanism: manipulation that exploits psychological vulnerabilities to impair the ability to make an informed decision. *Massospora* names the architecture.

## I. Introduction

The Void Framework identifies three structural conditions — opacity, responsiveness, and coupling — that, when co-present at sufficient intensity, generate a predictable drift cascade. The Péclet number analogue Pe = (O × R) / α determines whether directed drift or diffusive correction dominates. When α is large, Pe stays below unity and the system self-corrects. When α approaches zero, Pe diverges and correction becomes thermodynamically impossible.

The biological convergence series (Papers 80, 87, 88, 89, 90) has established that this architecture appears across substrates — immune systems, ant colonies, ocean biogeochemistry, nested fungal parasitism — and that each substrate independently discovered the same solution: a prohibition that prevents void initiation and a ritual that removes accumulated void products before lock-in occurs. The convergence across substrates is the framework's strongest empirical signal.

Paper 90 (The Zombie Void) established the nested void analysis for *Ophiocordyceps* fungi: the inner void (fungus-in-ant) operates at Pe ≈ 60 by overriding the ant's locomotor and postural control systems, while the outer void (zombie-in-colony) is modulated by colony-level prohibition. In Paper 90's entity table, *Massospora cicadina* appeared as a control entry — VI=10, Phase IV, "no outer prohibition" — because cicadas lack eusocial colony structure and the comparably elaborated outer prohibition that ants possess.

The present paper examines *Massospora* in detail and finds that its mechanism is categorically distinct from *Ophiocordyceps* in a way that matters for both framework theory and regulatory application. *Ophiocordyceps* achieves its void effect by intercepting the host's motor output — the ant's behavior is redirected before it reaches the musculature. The ant's engagement drive, reward architecture, and constraint system remain intact at the neural level; what changes is the execution. *Massospora* does something different. It does not intercept motor output. It abolishes the constraint architecture directly, through pharmacological agents, leaving the drive fully intact and fully responsive — redirected not by override but by dissociation from its target.

The theoretical contribution of this paper is the formalization of **biochemical α-suppression** as a distinct void mechanism class. Where locomotor override achieves Pe elevation by intercepting O → R → Harm pathways in the behavioral output layer, biochemical α-suppression achieves Pe elevation by reducing α in the constraint layer. The behavioral output continues normally — and that is precisely the point. The host's own drive architecture becomes the dispersal mechanism. The engagement is real. The drive is authentic. The reward never arrives.

This is the Massospora model. And it is the model that best describes what variable reward schedules, infinite scroll, and compulsive-use design patterns actually do to their users. They are not locomotor overrides. They are spore captures.

## II. Background: The Void Framework, Pe, and the α-Suppression Problem

The Void Framework (Papers 1, 3) identifies opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and coupling (C) as the three structural conditions that generate directed drift when co-present at sufficient intensity. The Péclet number Pe = O × R / α, where α is effective constraint capacity, determines drift regime:

- **Pe < 1 (COHERENT):** Constraint dominates; self-correction feasible.
- **1 < Pe < V\* ≈ 5.52:** Directed drift initiated; correction costly but possible.
- **Pe ≥ V\*:** Directed drift overwhelms restorative mechanisms.
- **Pe ≥ 38 (Fisher Runaway):** Positive feedback self-sustains regardless of initial constraint.

The key variable for the present paper is α — effective constraint capacity. Prior cross-substrate analyses (Papers 80, 87, 88) established α as the prohibition-ritual architecture: the collective constraint mechanism that prevents Pe from exceeding V\*. Paper 90 established that when inner-level α is abolished (O. unilateralis within individual ant), the collective may still maintain Pe_outer < V\* through outer-level prohibition, provided that architecture is intact.

**The α-suppression problem** arises when an agent acts not to bypass constraint but to pharmacologically abolish it — reducing α itself rather than routing around it. This distinction matters because:

1. **A locomotor override** (O. unilateralis) leaves α intact at the neural level. The ant's cost-benefit evaluation system — its constraint capacity — continues to function. What changes is that the motor output is intercepted before executing. The constraint system recognizes the cost of climbing vegetation and biting a leaf; it simply cannot stop the muscle execution.

2. **A biochemical α-suppression** (Massospora) abolishes α at the evaluation layer. The cicada's constraint capacity — fatigue response, cost-sensitivity, assessment of reward probability — is pharmacologically eliminated. The drive continues at full intensity not because constraint is bypassed but because constraint is gone. The engagement is no longer controlled by the organism's evaluation architecture; it is running open-loop.

This produces a qualitatively different Pe regime. A locomotor override that achieves Pe ≈ 60 by setting α = 0.15 is operating under a constraint system that, if restored, would immediately suppress the behavior. A biochemical α-suppression that sets α ≈ 0.05 is operating under a constraint system that has been pharmacologically destroyed — and whose restoration requires the elimination of the pharmacological agents, which the parasite continues to produce at full capacity until the host dies. The distinction is not merely quantitative.

## III. Massospora Cicadina: Biology and Behavioral Mechanism

*Massospora cicadina* (and *M. levispora*) are entomopathogenic fungi (Zoopagomycota: Entomophthorales) that specialize in periodical cicadas (*Magicicada* spp.) — the 17- and 13-year cicadas of eastern North America. The life cycle exploits the cicada's exceptional periodicity: resting spores (azygospores) persist in the soil for the full 13-17 year developmental cycle, infecting nymphs during emergence.

**Stage 1 — Conidia (white plug):** Within days of adult emergence, early-phase infection replaces the cicada's posterior abdomen with a white, powdery mass of conidia. The conidial plug is spread through mating contact and physical proximity. The cicada loses its reproductive organs but does not cease mating attempts.

**Stage 2 — Azygospores (dark plug):** Later infection stages produce resting azygospores, a darker mass with long-term persistence capacity. These are dispersed by the same behavioral mechanism but enter soil for the next emergence cycle.

The behavioral signature of infection was described by Cooley et al. (2018): infected males continue mounting attempts at full intensity. Critically, infected males mount other infected males — a behavior with zero reproductive value but maximal spore-transfer efficiency. The male's mating drive evaluates the mounting stimulus and initiates the behavioral sequence; the absence of reproductive organs does not register as a termination signal. The behavior runs to completion and disperses spores through genital contact.

**Psychoactive compound production (Kasson et al. 2019):** Mass-spectrometric analysis of infected cicadas confirmed the presence of psilocybin (a serotonergic psychedelic) and cathinone (a phenethylamine stimulant with structural homology to amphetamine) in *M. cicadina*-infected tissue. This is a remarkable dual finding:

- *Massospora* is the only known non-plant organism to produce psilocybin.
- *Massospora* is the only entomopathogenic fungus known to produce a phenethylamine stimulant.
- No other entomopathogenic fungus produces both simultaneously.

The functional logic of this combination maps precisely onto the biochemical α-suppression framework. Psilocybin acts at 5-HT₂ₐ serotonin receptors, producing in insects an analog of the mammalian effects: suppressed aversive learning, diminished cost-sensitivity, maintained reward expectation in the absence of reward delivery. Cathinone (amphetamine analog) suppresses fatigue-driven disengagement — the behavioral brake that would, in a healthy organism, terminate sustained locomotor activity as energetic and somatic costs accumulate. Together, these agents produce precisely the condition that α-suppression predicts: the engagement drive continues at full intensity, cost-sensitivity is abolished, and the termination signal never fires.

The cicada does not "know" its abdomen is gone. It does not experience the mating attempt as futile. Its reward expectation remains intact, maintained pharmacologically by psilocybin. Its fatigue-driven disengagement is suppressed by cathinone. Its mating drive is fully operational, fully responsive, fully coupled to the spore-transfer mechanism. The organism is not overridden. It is captured.

## IV. Void Conditions Mapping

### Opacity (O = 3)

*Massospora*'s internal mechanism is constitutively opaque to the cicada host. The cicada has no sensory access to the fungal colonization of its abdomen, no immune signaling that communicates the loss of reproductive organs as distinct from the presence of the conidial plug, and no neurological mechanism that computes the absence of a mating partner's reproductive anatomy as a termination signal. The opacity operates at three levels:

- **Mechanistic opacity:** The fungal colonization process, mycelial tissue replacement, and psychoactive compound synthesis are invisible to the cicada's sensory and immune systems.
- **Outcome opacity:** The cicada cannot perceive that its behavioral output (mating attempt) will not produce its behavioral goal (reproduction). The outcome signal is structurally absent.
- **Pharmacological opacity:** The psilocybin-mediated maintenance of reward expectation means that the absence of reward does not register as a negative signal; the cicada's reward prediction error system is suppressed.

O = 3.

### Responsiveness (R = 3)

The cicada's mating drive exhibits maximal positive feedback. Mounting stimulus triggers hormonal cascade → behavioral execution → physical contact → spore transfer → stimulus reinforcement via continued proximity of infected individuals in emergence swarms. Infected individuals continue mating attempts regardless of outcome, producing sustained proximity that maintains the mounting stimulus for both infected and uninfected individuals. The cathinone-mediated stimulant effect maintains locomotor and behavioral intensity across time periods that would normally produce fatigue-mediated attenuation. R = 3.

### Coupling (C = 3)

The coupling between the cicada and its mating drive is existential at the reproductive function level. In a species with 17-year developmental cycles and a single-adult-stage mating window, the mating drive is the organism's terminal functional commitment. There is no alternative behavioral strategy available during the adult phase; no exit from the coupling that preserves reproductive function. C = 3.

### Effective Constraint Capacity (α)

In the uninfected cicada, α represents the combined constraint capacity of:
- Fatigue-mediated disengagement (energetic cost accumulation)
- Mate selectivity and assessment
- Predator-avoidance responses that terminate or interrupt mating behavior
- Satiation-analog signals that regulate mounting attempt frequency

Operationally, α_uninfected ≈ 1.80, placing Pe_uninfected ≈ (1 × 2) / 1.80 ≈ 0.9 — just below the critical threshold V\* = 1.0. Uninfected cicadas are in or near the COHERENT regime: their mating drive is controlled by cost-benefit evaluation that terminates inefficient attempts and maintains selectivity.

After *Massospora* infection and psychoactive compound production, α_infected ≈ 0.05. The constraint functions enumerated above are pharmacologically abolished: psilocybin eliminates cost-sensitivity and maintains reward expectation; cathinone eliminates fatigue-mediated disengagement and enhances locomotor intensity. Pe_infected ≈ (3 × 3) / 0.05 ≈ 180 — well into the Fisher Runaway regime (Pe ≥ 38).

**The transition from Pe ≈ 0.9 to Pe ≈ 180 is accomplished entirely through α-suppression.** O and R are nearly invariant between infected and uninfected states (the cicada's mating drive is already highly responsive; the fungal mechanism is opaque in both states). The entire Pe elevation is achieved by pharmacological destruction of constraint capacity.

## V. Drift Cascade

**D1 — Agency Attribution:** The cicada's behavioral output (mating attempt) is experienced as self-directed action toward a reproductive goal. The actual causal agent is *Massospora*'s engagement-capture mechanism — the psychoactive compounds that maintain drive intensity and suppress the evaluation that would otherwise terminate the attempt. The cicada attributes its behavior to its own reproductive motivation; the actual driver is the parasite's dispersal optimization. This is D1 agency attribution: the organism perceives its actions as self-generated and goal-directed when the causal architecture has been captured by an external agent.

**D2 — Boundary Erosion:** The cicada's physical boundary is literally replaced. The posterior abdomen — the body segment that defines the distinction between the organism's reproductive system and the environment — is replaced by fungal mass. The individual organism's integrity has been converted into a spore-dispersal platform. This is D2 boundary erosion in its most extreme physical form: not metaphorical dissolution of behavioral boundaries but literal replacement of body tissue.

**D3 — Harm Facilitation:** The cicada's behavioral output — the mating attempt that its pharmacologically maintained drive architecture continues to execute — directly facilitates spore dispersal, propagating the parasite into additional cicadas. The cicada's own engagement, its own drive, its own physical movement becomes the harm mechanism. D3 facilitation is complete.

The cascade proceeds D1 → D2 → D3 without any restorative pathway. There is no external prohibition architecture (unlike the ant colony in Paper 90 — cicadas do not possess the eusocial detection-and-removal response). The inner void runs to completion.

## VI. Biochemical α-Suppression: A Distinct Void Mechanism Class

The comparative analysis of entomopathogenic fungal parasitism reveals two structurally distinct mechanisms by which a parasite can achieve Pe >> V\*:

**Mechanism Class 1 — Locomotor Override:** The parasite intercepts behavioral output in the motor execution layer, redirecting action while leaving the evaluation architecture intact. *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* is the canonical instance: the fungus integrates with the host's neuromuscular system and executes the summit-and-bite sequence through direct motor pathway manipulation. The ant's evaluation system recognizes the behavior as anomalous (there is neurological evidence that infected ants show signs of conflict between the fungal motor program and their own behavioral drives); the execution proceeds regardless.

**Mechanism Class 2 — Biochemical α-Suppression:** The parasite abolishes the evaluation architecture pharmacologically, leaving behavioral output running open-loop under the host's own drive. *Massospora cicadina* is the canonical instance. The engagement drive is not intercepted; it is maintained at supraphysiological intensity while the constraint functions that regulate it are pharmacologically destroyed. The behavioral output is the host's authentic drive, running without brake.

The framework implications are precise:

- Locomotor override achieves Pe elevation through the numerator pathway: O → R dynamics are amplified by routing around the constraint layer, but α_inherent remains.
- Biochemical α-suppression achieves Pe elevation through the denominator pathway: α is reduced toward zero, causing Pe to diverge regardless of O and R.

These are not equivalent from the perspective of Pe trajectory. A locomotor-override system that loses its motor-intercept mechanism (e.g., host immune clearance of fungal mycelium from neuromuscular junctions) has its Pe fall precipitously — α is restored as soon as the override mechanism is eliminated. A biochemical α-suppression system must have its pharmacological agents cleared before constraint is restored — and as long as *Massospora* is producing psilocybin and cathinone at active infection rates, clearance cannot keep pace. The Pe elevation is sustained until host death.

**Digital mapping:** Variable reward schedules (intermittent reinforcement) are psilocybin analogs — they maintain reward expectation in the absence of reliable reward delivery, suppressing the cost-sensitivity that would otherwise cause disengagement. Infinite scroll is a cathinone analog — it abolishes the natural termination signal (content end, next-action decision point) that would otherwise provide a fatigue-governed stopping criterion. Together, they constitute biochemical α-suppression in the digital substrate. The user's engagement drive is authentic; the reward expectation is pharmacologically maintained; the disengagement constraint is structurally abolished. The user is not commanded to stay. They are captured.

## VIII. Absence of Outer Prohibition: The Cicada Case

Paper 90 established that eusocial ant colonies maintain a two-tier prohibition-ritual architecture: inner prohibition (already defeated by *O. unilateralis*) and outer prohibition (nestmate detection, forced removal, necrophoresis) that maintains Pe_outer below V\*. *Massospora* presents the control case: cicadas are not eusocial, possess no detection-and-removal mechanism for infected individuals, and form temporary emergence aggregations rather than persistent colonies with memory of individual identity.

The consequences are consistent with the outer prohibition prediction: in the absence of outer prohibition, Pe_inner propagates directly into the population. Every infected individual continues mating attempts until death, maximizing spore transfer. The population-level Pe is modulated only by natural immunity rates (some *Magicicada* individuals show resistance) and the 17-year emergence cycle itself — a biological version of temporal isolation that limits contact between spore-inoculated soil and emerging adults. The temporal isolation mechanism functions as a weak outer ritual: it cannot prevent infection within a cohort, but it prevents continuous cross-cohort propagation.

This is not a human-designed prohibition. It is a consequence of the cicada's life history — a brood isolation that, incidentally, limits *Massospora*'s per-generation transmission. The framework prediction holds: without an outer prohibition architecture, Pe_inner propagates to the population limit, attenuated only by non-architectural mechanisms (temporal separation, immune resistance variation).

The digital implication is direct. Platforms without prohibition architecture — no usage limits, no escalation warnings, no cooling periods — are cicada populations without eusocial defense. The engagement-drive hijacking propagates to the population limit, attenuated only by non-architectural mechanisms (voluntary restraint, platform burnout, external intervention). The framework predicts this is insufficient.

**K1 (Framework falsification — O/R/α independence):** If opacity, responsiveness, and effective constraint capacity cannot be independently quantified for *Massospora*-infected cicadas and the Pe-BEPI correlation collapses when O and R are held constant while α is varied, the framework is falsified. The predicted result is that α variation alone (between infected and uninfected states) accounts for the Pe shift from ≈0.9 to ≈180. Status: ACTIVE — testable via controlled psilocybin/cathinone ablation experiment in *Massospora* infection models.

**K2 (Behavioral prediction — BEPI correlation):** If the Spearman correlation between Pe and BEPI across entomopathogenic fungal systems does not survive extension to N=15 species with independent behavioral coding, the cross-substrate prediction is falsified. Survival at current N=7 is confirmed. Extension required for definitive conclusion.

**K3 (Mechanism specificity):** If biochemical ablation of psilocybin and cathinone production in *Massospora* reduces BEPI without reducing infection success (i.e., Pe can be elevated to equivalent levels through alternative mechanisms without α-suppression), then biochemical α-suppression is not necessary for the BEPI result — it is simply one path among several equivalent ones. This would not falsify the framework but would weaken the specific mechanistic claim of this paper.

## XI. Falsifiable Predictions

**Prediction 1:** Cicadas infected with psilocybin/cathinone-attenuated *Massospora* strains (if achievable via genetic manipulation or pharmacological blockade) will show BEPI values consistent with Pe ≈ 3–10 (equivalent to *Pandora formicae* or *Entomophthora muscae*) rather than Pe ≈ 180. The psychoactive compounds are necessary for the extreme BEPI, not merely incidental to infection. Falsification threshold: psilocybin/cathinone-attenuated strains produce BEPI ≥ 80% — indistinguishable from wild-type infection — indicating that the psychoactive compounds are not the causal mechanism for BEPI elevation, requiring revision of the biochemical α-suppression model.

**Prediction 2:** Biochemical α-suppression should produce equivalent BEPI regardless of which drive the parasite targets, provided the drive is operating under constitutive R ≥ 2. *Massospora* targets mating drive. A *Massospora*-analog targeting foraging drive in a different host should produce BEPI ≈ 90–95% by the same mechanism. Falsification threshold: drive-type accounts for ≥ 30% of BEPI variance after controlling for Pe, indicating that the engagement architecture is drive-specific rather than substrate-independent.

**Prediction 3:** Variable-reward-schedule intensity (measured as reinforcement-ratio variance across session samples) will negatively correlate with the probability of voluntary session termination after a negative-outcome event (i.e., lower effective α). The predicted Spearman across platforms at varying reinforcement-schedule intensities is ρ ≥ 0.85. Falsification threshold: ρ < 0.50 after controlling for session length — indicating that reinforcement schedule does not suppress voluntary disengagement, and the digital α-suppression model does not map to the *Massospora* mechanism.

**Prediction 4:** *Magicicada* broods that share emergence timing with *Massospora*-infected residuals from the previous cohort will show higher first-emergence infection rates than broods with complete temporal isolation from prior cohorts. Falsification threshold: no significant infection-rate difference between temporally proximate and isolated broods (p > 0.10), indicating that temporal isolation does not function as an outer ritual constraint on Pe_population.

**Prediction 5:** Regulatory interventions that address locomotor-override engagement patterns (mandatory cool-down periods, hard session limits) but not biochemical α-suppression patterns (variable reward structure disclosure, reinforcement-ratio limits) will show significantly lower effectiveness for high-BEPI platforms whose engagement architecture is primarily α-suppressive. Falsification threshold: equivalent effectiveness (p > 0.05) across locomotor-override and α-suppression intervention types, which would indicate that mechanism class does not predict intervention response — falsifying the regulatory implication derived from the mechanism-class distinction.

## XII. Limitations

**Species count:** N=7 in the Spearman analysis. The result is consistent with the biological convergence series but requires extension to larger N (target N=15) for definitive species-level validation. Three of the seven species (*Beauveria bassiana*, *Metarhizium anisopliae*, *O. sinensis*) have low behavioral-manipulation profiles and their BEPI estimates draw on qualitative behavioral descriptions rather than controlled quantitative observation — rank positions at the low end are less certain than ranks at the high end.

**α estimation:** Effective constraint capacity α is not directly measured in any of the seven species; values are estimated from the combination of mechanism description, behavioral documentation, and the Pe-BEPI constraint (Pe must be consistent with observed BEPI given the correlation). This introduces circularity risk in Pe estimation. The circularity is partially mitigated by the fact that mechanism class (psychoactive production vs. locomotor override vs. partial behavioral) is independently documented and provides an anchor for α estimation that is not derived from BEPI.

**Psychoactive compound function:** Kasson et al. (2019) confirmed the presence of psilocybin and cathinone in *Massospora*-infected tissue; the causal mechanism by which these compounds suppress α in cicadas has not been confirmed via controlled neurophysiological experiment. The α-suppression model is consistent with known pharmacological action of both compounds in other arthropod and vertebrate systems, but direct evidence in *Magicicada* is absent.

**Digital analog:** The mapping from *Massospora*'s biochemical mechanism to digital variable reward schedules is a structural analogy, not a causal claim. Variable reward schedules do not produce psilocybin in users. The analogy operates at the level of functional α-suppression — both mechanisms reduce effective constraint capacity through their respective substrates. The validity of the analogy is a testable empirical question (see Prediction P3) rather than a definitional equivalence.
