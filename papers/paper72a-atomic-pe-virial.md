## I. Introduction

The virial theorem is one of the oldest, most thoroughly verified, and most universally applicable results in theoretical physics. First derived by Rudolf Clausius in 1870 as a mechanical theorem relating time-averaged kinetic and potential energies in bound systems governed by conservative forces, the result states, for any inverse-square-law interaction, that twice the time-averaged kinetic energy plus the time-averaged potential energy vanishes identically: 2⟨T⟩ + ⟨V⟩ = 0 (Clausius 1870, *Philosophical Magazine*, Series 4, 40(265), 122–127). Equivalently, the ratio of kinetic energy to the magnitude of potential energy in any such bound state is exactly one-half. This ratio holds for the ground state of hydrogen, for Keplerian binary star orbits, for self-gravitating stellar interiors, and for galaxy clusters containing thousands of member galaxies. Over the 155 years since Clausius published his proof, no exception has been discovered for systems satisfying its premises, no revision has been required, and the derivation itself has been reproduced in every major classical mechanics textbook in continuous print. ### III.A. Derivational Transparency

The virial theorem admits complete derivation from first principles accessible to any reader with single-variable calculus and introductory Newtonian mechanics. The classical proof, first published by Clausius in 1870 in the *Philosophical Magazine* (Series 4, 40(265), 122–127), proceeds from a quantity Clausius called the "virial" — the sum over all particles of the scalar product of each particle's position vector with the force acting upon it. Clausius demonstrated that for a bounded mechanical system in statistical equilibrium, the time average of the total kinetic energy stands in a fixed ratio to the time average of the virial. For inverse-square-law forces specifically — Newtonian gravity and the Coulomb interaction — the result takes the form 2⟨T⟩ + ⟨V⟩ = 0, or equivalently ⟨T⟩ = −½⟨V⟩, where T is total kinetic energy and V total potential energy.

This derivation has been independently reproduced in every major classical mechanics textbook of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Goldstein, Poole, and Safko (2002, *Classical Mechanics*, 3rd ed., Addison-Wesley, Chapter 3) present it in approximately one page of algebra. Landau and Lifshitz (1976, *Mechanics*, 3rd ed., Butterworth-Heinemann, §10) derive the same result by a distinct route employing homogeneity arguments and Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions, arriving at the generalized virial theorem for potentials of arbitrary power-law index n, with the Coulomb/gravitational case recovered by setting n = −1. Taylor (2005, *Classical Mechanics*, University Science Books, §7.2) provides yet another pedagogical derivation aimed at second-year undergraduates. Marion and Thornton (2004, *Classical Dynamics of Particles and Systems*, 5th ed., Brooks/Cole, §3.7) include the derivation with worked examples. The theorem appears in Symon (1971, *Mechanics*, 3rd ed., Addison-Wesley, §3.10), in José and Saletan (1998, *Classical Dynamics: A Contemporary Approach*, Cambridge University Press, §4.2), and in virtually every intermediate mechanics text in current pedagogical use.

The critical point for opacity scoring is not merely that the derivation exists in published form, but that the derivation is short, elementary, and self-contained. No step requires specialized knowledge beyond Newton's second law, the definition of kinetic and potential energy, and the properties of time averages over bounded orbits. A diligent undergraduate can reconstruct the proof from the statement of the theorem alone, without consulting any source. This is the opposite of opacity: the system's internal logic is not only accessible but compressible to a single page of universally taught mathematics.

### III.B. Quantum Mechanical Extension: Equally Transparent

The quantum virial theorem, which confirms that the classical result survives intact in the operator-algebraic framework of quantum mechanics, was established by Fock (1930, *Zeitschrift für Physik*, 63, 855–858) using scaling arguments applied to the Schrödinger equation. An independent derivation proceeds via the Hellmann-Feynman theorem: for the hydrogen atom Hamiltonian H = T + V with V = −e²/(4πε₀r), the expectation value of the commutator [H, A] for the generator of dilations A = (r · p + p · r)/2 vanishes in any energy eigenstate, yielding 2⟨T⟩ + ⟨V⟩ = 0 identically (Griffiths, 2018, *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics*, 3rd ed., Cambridge University Press, §4.2). This result requires no approximation, no perturbation expansion, and no numerical simulation. It is exact for every energy eigenstate of the hydrogen atom.

The derivation is taught in standard graduate quantum mechanics courses. Shankar (1994, *Principles of Quantum Mechanics*, 2nd ed., Springer, §13.3) derives it via the scaling method. Sakurai and Napolitano (2017, *Modern Quantum Mechanics*, 3rd ed., Cambridge University Press, §5.2) present it in the context of the hydrogen atom's fine structure corrections, where the virial theorem is used to evaluate radial expectation values. Messiah (1961, *Quantum Mechanics*, Vol. I, North-Holland, Ch. XI) derives the theorem for arbitrary power-law potentials. Cohen-Tannoudji, Diu, and Laloë (1977, *Quantum Mechanics*, Vol. I, Wiley, Complement CXII) provide a self-contained proof accessible to advanced undergraduates.

The quantum derivation introduces no new opacity relative to the classical case. The operator algebra is fully specified. The Hilbert space structure of the hydrogen atom is exactly solvable — one of only a handful of quantum systems for which this is true. The energy eigenvalues Eₙ = −13.6 eV/n² are derivable in closed form from the Coulomb Hamiltonian, without any free parameters beyond the electron mass, the electron charge, and Planck's constant (Bethe and Salpeter, 1957, *Quantum Mechanics of One- and Two-Electron Atoms*, Springer). ## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

The second condition of the Void Framework, responsiveness (R), measures the degree to which a system adapts its outputs, structure, or behavior in reaction to observer attention. ### A. Operational Definition of Responsiveness

Within the Void Framework, responsiveness is defined as the capacity of a system to modify its observable outputs as a function of detected observer attention, engagement, or interaction patterns. The critical features are threefold: the system must detect some feature of the observer's state; it must alter its subsequent behavior or outputs on the basis of that detection; and the alteration must create a feedback loop wherein the observer's modified behavior further modifies the system's outputs. This tripartite definition distinguishes responsiveness from mere interactivity. A calculator responds to keystrokes, but it does not detect the user's emotional state and modify its arithmetic accordingly. The distinction matters because conflating interactivity with adaptive responsiveness would inflate R scores across all technological domains, destroying the framework's discriminative power.

The paradigm cases of high responsiveness in the Void Framework literature involve systems such as TikTok's recommendation algorithm, which updates its content selection model after every user interaction with a convergence time on the order of 40 minutes of use, as documented by the Wall Street Journal's investigative analysis of bot accounts that received highly personalized feeds within two hours of account creation (Wells, Horwitz, and Seetharaman 2021, *Wall Street Journal*, "The Facebook Files"). Against this operational backdrop, the virial theorem serves as the null case. ## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third condition of the Void Framework—engagement or coupling—addresses the degree to which sustained interaction with a system produces progressive dependency, identity entanglement, or behavioral capture in the observer. ### V.1. The Structure of Observer-System Coupling in the Void Framework

The engagement/coupling condition (C) measures three interrelated phenomena. First, dependency formation: the degree to which continued interaction with a system makes disengagement progressively more costly, not because of accumulated knowledge but because of psychological, social, or economic lock-in. Second, behavioral capture: the degree to which the system modulates the observer's behavior through reinforcement schedules that exploit prediction-error signaling in dopaminergic circuits. Third, identity fusion: the degree to which the observer's self-concept becomes entangled with the system such that separation is experienced as self-loss rather than mere preference change.

The neuroscientific basis for these distinctions is well established. Schultz, Dayan, and Montague (1997, Science, 275, 1593–1599) demonstrated that midbrain dopamine neurons encode reward prediction errors rather than reward itself, establishing the neural substrate for variable-ratio reinforcement effects. Subsequent work by Knutson, Adams, Fong, and Hommer (2001, Journal of Neuroscience, 21, RC159) showed that anticipation of uncertain reward activates the nucleus accumbens more strongly than anticipation of certain reward, providing the mechanism by which unpredictable reinforcement schedules produce stronger behavioral capture than predictable ones. Robinson and Berridge (2003, Annual Review of Psychology, 54, 25–53) further distinguished "wanting" (incentive salience driven by mesolimbic dopamine) from "liking" (hedonic impact mediated by opioid and endocannabinoid systems), demonstrating that compulsive engagement can persist even when the subjective pleasure derived from the activity has diminished—a hallmark of coupling that has decoupled from rational utility.

These mechanisms are operative in domains that score high on the C condition: social media platforms deploying variable-ratio notification schedules (Alter 2017, Irresistible: The Rise of Addictive Technology and the Business of Keeping Us Hooked, Penguin Press), gambling systems exploiting near-miss effects (Clark, Lawrence, Astley-Jones, and Gray 2009, Neuropsychopharmacology, 34, 2688–2697), and parasocial relationship architectures in which audiences form attachment bonds with media entities that cannot reciprocate (Horton and Wohl 1956, Psychiatry, 19, 215–229). The question for the present analysis is whether any analogous mechanism operates when a physicist, student, or astronomer engages with the virial theorem.

The virial theorem exhibits none of these features. Compute the virial mass of a galaxy cluster and obtain a value determined entirely by the measured velocity dispersion and cluster radius, with no stochastic bonus, no near-miss tantalization, and no intermittent jackpot. ## XII. Limitations

**Derivation scope.** The virial theorem yields Pe = 1/2 exactly for inverse-square-law (1/r²) forces, encompassing Newtonian gravity and the Coulomb interaction. For other force laws — harmonic potentials (1/r), quartic confinement, hard-sphere potentials — the virial theorem yields different equilibrium ratios (2⟨T⟩ = −n⟨V⟩ for V ∝ rⁿ; Goldstein et al. 2002, §3.4). The Pe = 1/2 claim applies specifically and exclusively to 1/r potential systems. Extending this analysis to other potential types requires re-deriving the equilibrium ratio from the appropriate virial identity.

**Equilibrium assumption.** The virial theorem holds for systems in statistical equilibrium, specifically for time-averages over bounded trajectories in which the virial Clausius 1870 quantity ∑ pᵢ · rᵢ does not grow secularly. Strongly out-of-equilibrium systems — rapidly collapsing molecular clouds, violent mergers of galaxy clusters within a dynamical time, post-merger BH ringdown — do not satisfy the theorem's premises and may exhibit virial ratios that depart significantly from 1/2 during the transient. The control-case claim applies to the equilibrium state, not to transient approach.

**Pe notation disambiguation.** The physics-internal ratio ⟨T⟩/|⟨V⟩| = 1/2 should not be conflated with the Void Framework's Péclet-analogue void intensity, which is 0.0 for this domain. The coincidence of notation (Pe in both contexts) is pedagogically useful but requires explicit disambiguation in any cross-domain application. Future work comparing the virial energy ratio to void-intensity scoring in sociotechnical systems should state explicitly which Pe is being reported.

**Calibration dependence.** The constraint-pole status of this domain is load-bearing for the Void Framework's comparative assessments. If the scoring criteria were revised such that the virial theorem's derivational transparency, output invariance, or observer independence scored above zero, every calibrated score in the companion papers would require reinterpretation. This logical dependence is a feature — it makes the framework falsifiable through the calibration anchor — but it means that users of the framework must verify the anchor holds before applying comparative scores.
