## Abstract

The Hubble tension — a persistent discrepancy of approximately 8 percent between early-universe and late-universe determinations of the Hubble constant H₀ — constitutes the most statistically significant anomaly in precision cosmology. Measurements calibrated through the cosmic microwave background yield H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration, 2020, *Astronomy and Astrophysics*, 641, A6), while the local distance ladder calibrated through Cepheid variables produces H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al., 2022, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 934, L7), a disagreement now exceeding five standard deviations. This discrepancy has generated more than 500 distinct proposed resolutions catalogued in a single review (Di Valentino et al., 2021, *Physical Review D*, 103, 106028), an aggregate investment exceeding ten billion dollars in survey instruments whose scientific returns depend on the tension's resolution — including the Euclid mission at €1.4 billion (European Space Agency, 2023), the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope at $4.3 billion (NASA, 2024), and the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument at $75 million (U.S. Department of Energy) — and a research ecosystem of approximately 10,000 to 20,000 active cosmologists globally whose publication trajectories, funding allocations, and career structures are shaped by the tension's unresolved status.

This paper applies the Void Framework to a speculative theoretical proposal — drift-diffusion cosmology — that reinterprets the Hubble tension as evidence of a Péclet number regime boundary (Pe = 1 crossing) in cosmic expansion dynamics. The Péclet number, a dimensionless ratio of advective to diffusive transport rates standard in fluid mechanics (Bird, Stewart, and Lightfoot, 2002, *Transport Phenomena*, second edition, Wiley), is mapped onto cosmological expansion by treating the deterministic Hubble flow as drift and stochastic or backreaction-driven fluctuations as diffusion, following precedents established by Buchert (2000, *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 32, 105) for treating cosmic expansion as possessing stochastic components. The analysis examines how the Void Framework's three constitutive conditions — opacity, responsiveness, and engaged observer coupling — manifest not only in the drift-diffusion proposal itself but in the broader epistemic ecosystem that the Hubble tension has generated. Recent recalibrations using the James Webb Space Telescope's tip-of-the-red-giant-branch method have yielded intermediate H₀ values that neither confirm the full SH0ES result nor resolve the tension (Freedman et al., 2024, *Astrophysical Journal*, 963, 56), deepening rather than dissipating the void conditions under analysis.

## I. Introduction

The Hubble constant, denoted H₀, quantifies the present-day expansion rate of the universe and anchors the entire edifice of observational cosmology. For nearly a century — since Lemaître's 1927 derivation and Hubble's 1929 empirical calibration — its precise value has been contested, but the character of the dispute has shifted dramatically over the past decade. Where earlier disagreements reflected factor-of-two uncertainties traceable to poorly calibrated distance indicators, the contemporary Hubble tension represents something qualitatively different: a statistically robust, methodologically persistent, and theoretically unexplained discrepancy between two independent and internally consistent measurement paradigms. The Planck Collaboration's analysis of cosmic microwave background (CMB) anisotropies, assuming the standard six-parameter ΛCDM cosmological model, yields H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration, 2020, *Astronomy and Astrophysics*, 641, A6). The SH0ES collaboration's Cepheid-calibrated Type Ia supernova distance ladder produces H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al., 2022, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 934, L7). The disagreement now exceeds 5σ — a threshold that, in particle physics, conventionally warrants a discovery claim. In cosmology, it warrants something closer to collective epistemic vertigo.

This paper applies the Void Framework to the epistemic ecosystem surrounding the Hubble tension, and in particular to a speculative theoretical reframing that we term drift-diffusion cosmology: the proposal that the tension reflects a Péclet number regime boundary in cosmic expansion dynamics, where the dimensionless ratio of advective (deterministic Hubble flow) to diffusive (stochastic gravitational) transport crosses unity during cosmic evolution. The Péclet number, Pe = UL/D in standard transport theory, marks the transition between diffusion-dominated (Pe < 1) and advection-dominated (Pe > 1) regimes. In the drift-diffusion cosmology proposal, Pe = 1 represents a critical crossing point at which systematic measurement discrepancies between early-universe and late-universe probes emerge not from new fundamental physics but from a regime transition in the effective transport character of expansion itself. We emphasize at the outset that this proposal is speculative and not yet empirically constrained. The Void Framework analysis presented here is directed not primarily at evaluating the physical merits of drift-diffusion cosmology, but at understanding the structural conditions — opacity, responsiveness, and observer engagement coupling — that allow such proposals to proliferate, absorb resources, and resist falsification within the tension's epistemic void.

The scale of the domain under analysis is substantial by any measure. The global cosmology research community comprises an estimated 10,000 to 20,000 active investigators, based on International Astronomical Union membership statistics and arXiv submission rates across the astro-ph and hep-th categories. The instrumental infrastructure whose scientific yield depends directly on resolving the Hubble tension represents aggregate public investment exceeding ten billion dollars: the European Space Agency's Euclid mission at €1.4 billion (ESA, 2023), NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope at $4.3 billion (NASA, 2024), the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument at $75 million from the Department of Energy (DESI Collaboration, 2016, *arXiv:1611.00036*), and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time at approximately $700 million (NSF/DOE). These instruments were designed, funded, and constructed under the assumption that ΛCDM provides a correct or nearly correct description of cosmic expansion. If the Hubble tension signals a genuine failure of the standard model, the scientific return on these investments changes character entirely — from precision parametric refinement to discovery-mode exploration of unknown physics. If the tension is instead a systematic artifact, the same instruments may confirm ΛCDM while rendering a decade of speculative theoretical work retrospectively sterile. Either outcome redistributes epistemic credit on a massive scale. The uncertainty itself, sustained now across more than a decade, constitutes the primary void condition.

The literature testifies to the depth of this uncertainty. Di Valentino et al. (2021, *Physical Review D*, 103, 106013) catalogued over 500 proposed solutions to the Hubble tension in a single comprehensive review, spanning modifications to early-universe physics, late-universe physics, the dark sector, modified gravity, and systematic error reanalyses. Abdalla et al. (2022, *Journal of High Energy Astrophysics*, 34, 49) documented the intertwined nature of cosmological tensions across particle physics, astrophysics, and cosmology, demonstrating that the Hubble tension cannot be isolated from broader anomalies in the S₈ clustering amplitude, the cosmic curvature parameter, and the dark energy equation of state. The arXiv preprint server registered over 1,000 papers addressing the Hubble tension between 2019 and 2024, yet no consensus resolution has emerged. This is not a domain in which evidence is scarce; it is a domain in which evidence is abundant, mutually inconsistent, and structurally resistant to convergence.

The existing literature on the Hubble tension, impressive in its technical depth, nonetheless misses a critical structural dimension that the Void Framework is designed to reveal. Standard analyses of the tension treat it as a physics problem — a discrepancy to be resolved through better data, better systematics control, or better theory. The Void Framework treats it simultaneously as an epistemic ecology problem, asking what happens to a research community when a high-stakes anomaly persists unresolved for a decade or more. Three conditions jointly produce predictable epistemic harm patterns in such environments. First, opacity: the Hubble tension's resolution remains inaccessible to direct observation, with each proposed solution introducing additional free parameters whose values cannot be independently constrained. Poulin et al. (2019, *Physical Review Letters*, 122, 221301) introduced early dark energy as a scalar field that temporarily accelerates expansion before recombination, reducing the sound horizon and raising the inferred H₀. The model adds at least three free parameters to ΛCDM, none independently measurable. Kreisch et al. (2020, *Physical Review D*, 101, 123505) proposed self-interacting neutrinos that delay free-streaming to similar effect, again introducing parameters that are degenerate with existing cosmological observables. The opacity is not that these models are wrong — several remain viable — but that their parameter spaces cannot be closed by existing data, creating a theoretical landscape in which models persist indefinitely without empirical adjudication.

Second, responsiveness: the Hubble tension responds to observational and theoretical inputs in ways that sustain rather than resolve the anomaly. When Freedman et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*, 963, 56) published JWST recalibrations of the tip of the red giant branch (TRGB) distance indicator, the results yielded intermediate H₀ values (approximately 69.8 km/s/Mpc) that neither confirmed the SH0ES value nor agreed with Planck. Rather than narrowing the tension, this intermediate measurement widened the space of possible interpretations: the tension might be smaller than 5σ, or it might reflect calibration method dependence, or the TRGB and Cepheid distance scales might both be partially compromised. The DESI Collaboration's 2024 baryon acoustic oscillation results (DESI Collaboration, 2024, *arXiv:2404.03002*) introduced tantalizing hints of time-varying dark energy that, if confirmed, would alter late-universe expansion dynamics in ways that complicate rather than simplify the tension's interpretation. The system's responsiveness generates new complexity with each new input.

Third, engagement coupling: the global cosmology community is not a detached observer of the Hubble tension but a deeply coupled participant whose career structures, funding allocations, and institutional prestige depend on the tension's status. Observational teams that have invested decades in distance ladder calibration have professional incentives to defend high H₀ values. CMB analysis groups have comparable incentives to defend ΛCDM predictions. Theorists who have published novel solutions have incentives to sustain the anomaly's significance. This is not a claim of dishonesty — the researchers involved are among the most rigorous in science — but rather a structural observation about how unresolved high-stakes anomalies generate observer-anomaly coupling that distorts collective epistemic dynamics. Buchert (2000, *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 32, 105) established the mathematical formalism for cosmological backreaction — the idea that averaging over inhomogeneous matter distributions introduces effective terms in the Friedmann equations — and the backreaction research program illustrates how a mathematically legitimate but empirically underdetermined proposal can sustain a research community for decades without decisive resolution.

The drift-diffusion cosmology proposal enters this landscape as a specific instance of a broader pattern: the appropriation of well-established physics from one domain (fluid dynamics, transport theory) to reframe an anomaly in another domain (cosmology) using terminology that carries explanatory connotations beyond its formal content. The Péclet number is rigorously defined in continuum mechanics, where it governs the relative importance of advective and diffusive transport in heat transfer, mass transfer, and momentum transfer. Its application to cosmic expansion requires mapping the Hubble flow onto an advective velocity field and identifying a cosmological diffusivity — neither of which has a standard definition in general relativity. The proposal's appeal lies precisely in its importation of familiar intuitions about regime transitions: just as a Péclet crossing in a fluid system changes the dominant transport mechanism and generates boundary layer phenomena, a cosmological Péclet crossing might generate systematic discrepancies between probes that sample different epochs. The danger, from a Void Framework perspective, is that this metaphorical mapping — technically an L2-level vocabulary operation in the drift-cascade terminology — creates an impression of explanatory depth without corresponding empirical constraint. The Pe = 1 crossing is not derived from observational data; it is imposed as an interpretive frame that absorbs the tension's statistical significance into a transport-theoretic narrative.

(2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics) document how proposed solutions to the Hubble tension co-evolve with observational constraints, with each new dataset spawning a recalibrated family of models that remain just within observational tolerance. ## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

Opacity, within the Void Framework, denotes any structural feature of a system that renders its internal causal mechanisms inaccessible to external observation, audit, or falsification. In domains ranging from algorithmic finance to pharmaceutical trial design, opacity is typically engineered — a product of proprietary enclosure, regulatory arbitrage, or deliberate informational asymmetry. Cosmological theory disputes present a categorically different opacity regime. Here, opacity is not engineered by an agent seeking advantage but is instead intrinsic to the epistemic object itself: the universe at cosmological scales resists direct experimental manipulation, permits only passive observation from a single spatiotemporal vantage point, and encodes its history in signals that require layers of theoretical interpretation before yielding quantitative parameters. The Hubble tension inherits and amplifies every one of these intrinsic opacity sources, while the drift-diffusion cosmology proposal introduces additional opacity of a more familiar, theory-internal kind. This section enumerates four distinct opacity mechanisms operating in this domain, provides quantitative evidence for each, and evaluates their combined contribution to the Void Index.

### III.A. Observational Opacity: The Distance Ladder's Irreducible Interpretive Layers

The most fundamental opacity in Hubble constant measurement is that no single observation yields H₀ directly. The local (late-universe) determination requires a calibrated distance ladder — a chain of overlapping standard candles, each calibrated against the rung below it, extending from geometric parallaxes in the Milky Way to Type Ia supernovae in the Hubble flow. Each rung introduces systematic uncertainties that are partially opaque to external audit because they depend on astrophysical modeling assumptions that are themselves uncertain.

The SH0ES program's determination of H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al., 2022, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*, 934, L7) relies on a three-rung ladder: geometric anchors (parallaxes, detached eclipsing binaries, masers), Cepheid period-luminosity relations in supernova host galaxies, and Type Ia supernova standardizable candle relations. Each transition between rungs requires photometric calibration, extinction correction, metallicity dependence modeling, and crowding/blending corrections. The opacity enters because these corrections are not independently verifiable at the precision required. Riess et al. (2022) report 1.4 percent total uncertainty, but this figure aggregates over thirty identified systematic terms, several of which depend on stellar evolution models whose uncertainties are themselves debated.

The Carnegie-Chicago Hubble Program's use of the Tip of the Red Giant Branch (TRGB) as an alternative to Cepheids illustrates how opacity compounds rather than resolves. Freedman et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*, 962, 17) reported JWST-recalibrated TRGB distances yielding H₀ values between 69.1 and 72.4 km/s/Mpc depending on photometric reduction choices — a spread comparable to the tension itself. The critical opacity mechanism is that different photometric pipelines applied to identical JWST imaging data produce significantly different TRGB magnitudes, a fact documented in detail by Li et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*, 966, 20). The choice of pipeline is not arbitrary but is also not uniquely determined by the data, creating a residual opacity that reasonable analysts resolve differently.

The Planck Collaboration's early-universe determination (H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc; Planck Collaboration, 2020, *Astronomy and Astrophysics*, 641, A6) achieves lower quoted uncertainty but at the cost of greater model dependence. The CMB power spectrum does not directly encode H₀; rather, it constrains the angular diameter distance to the last scattering surface and the physical matter and baryon densities, from which H₀ is derived assuming a specific cosmological model (flat ΛCDM with six free parameters). As Efstathiou (2021, *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*, 505, 3866) emphasized, the Planck H₀ is a model-dependent inference, not a measurement. Any extension to the standard model — additional neutrino species, time-varying dark energy, modified recombination physics — shifts the inferred H₀, typically upward. The opacity resides in the fact that the standard model's assumptions are not tested by the CMB data alone but are imported from theoretical priors, creating a closed interpretive loop that external observers cannot penetrate without replicating the full analysis pipeline.

Quantitatively, Planck's publicly released likelihood code (Planck Legacy Archive, ESA) comprises over 100,000 lines of Fortran and Python, with nuisance parameter marginalization over foreground models, beam corrections, and calibration factors that collectively introduce dozens of degrees of freedom. Handley and Lemos (2019, *Physical Review D*, 100, 043504) demonstrated using Bayesian evidence calculations that the Planck data exhibit moderate tension even internally between different frequency channels, suggesting that intra-dataset opacity may be nontrivial. The Void Framework scores this observational opacity at O = 2 on the 0–3 scale: the data are publicly available and the methodologies are published, but the interpretive infrastructure is sufficiently complex that independent replication requires years of specialist effort, and key systematic choices remain contested.

### III.B. Theoretical Opacity: The Proliferation of Unfalsifiable Solutions

The second opacity mechanism operates at the level of theoretical proposals advanced to resolve the tension. Di Valentino et al. (2021, *Classical and Quantum Gravity*, 38, 153001) catalogued over 500 proposed solutions, organized into categories including early dark energy, modified gravity, interacting dark sector models, varying fundamental constants, nonstandard neutrino physics, and modified recombination histories. The sheer volume of proposals constitutes an opacity mechanism in itself: no individual researcher can evaluate all proposals, no standardized comparison framework exists, and many proposals are constructed post hoc to fit the discrepancy without generating independent predictions.

Poulin et al. (2019, *Physical Review Letters*, 122, 221301) introduced early dark energy (EDE) as a scalar field that contributes approximately 10 percent of the total energy density near matter-radiation equality and then decays rapidly. EDE raises the Planck-inferred H₀ to approximately 71–73 km/s/Mpc. However, Hill et al. (2020, *Physical Review D*, 102, 043507) demonstrated that when ACT and SPT small-scale CMB data are included, EDE's improvement over ΛCDM becomes statistically marginal. The opacity here is that EDE's viability depends critically on which datasets are combined and which priors are imposed — choices that are defensible individually but collectively span a parameter space broad enough to accommodate almost any outcome. Herold and Ferreira (2022, *Physical Review D*, 105, 063504) formalized this concern, showing that the Bayes factor for EDE versus ΛCDM oscillates between moderate preference and moderate disfavor depending on dataset selection.

The drift-diffusion cosmology proposal analyzed in this paper adds a further layer of theoretical opacity. By mapping the Péclet number — a dimensionless ratio of advective to diffusive transport rates originating in fluid mechanics — onto cosmic expansion dynamics, the proposal introduces a mathematical framework whose cosmological interpretation is not uniquely constrained. The Péclet number is well-defined in hydrodynamic systems where advection velocity, diffusion coefficient, and characteristic length scale are independently measurable (Bird, Stewart, and Lightfoot, 2002, *Transport Phenomena*, 2nd ed., Wiley). In the cosmological context, the proposal must identify what constitutes "diffusion" in an expanding universe. Candidates include quantum gravitational fluctuations (whose magnitude is unknown at cosmological scales), backreaction of inhomogeneities on the average expansion (Buchert, 2000, *General Relativity and Gravitation*, 32, 105), or stochastic inflationary field dynamics (Starobinsky, 1986, *Stochastic de Sitter Stage*, in *Field Theory, Quantum Gravity and Strings*, Lecture Notes in Physics 246). Each identification yields a different Pe = 1 crossing redshift and a different predicted H₀ discrepancy. The opacity is that the mapping itself is underdetermined, and the proposal cannot be falsified without first resolving the identification problem — a regress that the Void Framework recognizes as characteristic of high-O environments.

### III.C. Institutional and Publication Opacity

A third opacity mechanism, more sociological than physical, operates through the structure of cosmological knowledge production. The Hubble tension occupies a peculiar institutional position: it is simultaneously the field's most prominent anomaly and its most productive paper-generation engine. Cardona et al. (2017, *Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics*, 2017(03), 056) observed that the statistical significance of the tension increased monotonically as datasets improved, suggesting that it is not a fluctuation artifact. Yet this monotonic increase also created institutional incentives to publish tension-related work, generating a feedback loop in which the tension's prominence attracts funding and talent, which produces more analyses, which sustain the tension's visibility regardless of whether resolution is approaching.

The opacity enters through publication selection effects. Efstathiou (2014, *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*, 440, 1138) published an early critique of the SH0ES Cepheid photometry, arguing that outlier rejection choices substantially affected H₀. This critique generated significant controversy but did not prevent the subsequent SH0ES analyses from maintaining similar methodological choices. The relevant opacity is that photometric reduction pipelines involve hundreds of analyst decisions — aperture sizes, point-spread function models, background subtraction methods, quality cuts — that are partially documented in publications but cannot be fully reconstructed from published information alone. Brout et al. (2022, *Astrophysical Journal*, 938, 110), in the Pantheon+ supernova compilation, made their full pipeline public, representing a significant transparency effort, but even this release required external analysts to invest substantial computational resources to reproduce results.

Funding allocation introduces additional opacity. The Euclid mission (ESA, launched July 2023, total cost approximately €1.4 billion) and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (NASA, scheduled launch 2027, lifecycle cost approximately $4.3 billion as reported in the 2024 NASA budget justification) both list dark energy characterization and Hubble tension resolution among their primary science goals. The allocation of instrument time, the prioritization of calibration targets, and the construction of data analysis pipelines all embed assumptions about which theoretical framework is correct. These decisions are made by science teams whose membership overlaps with the groups that produced the tension measurements, creating a closed epistemic loop that external observers can monitor but not influence. The Void Framework identifies this as a structural opacity: not concealment, but a complexity barrier that functionally excludes most of the affected research community from meaningful participation in the decisions that determine how their field's central anomaly is investigated.

### III.D. Aggregate Opacity Score

The Void Framework scores opacity on a 0–3 scale: 0 denotes full transparency (all causal mechanisms externally auditable), 1 denotes incidental opacity (complexity creates access barriers but no structural concealment), 2 denotes systematic opacity (interpretive infrastructure requires specialist replication and key choices remain contested), and 3 denotes designed opacity (mechanisms are deliberately rendered inaccessible). The Hubble tension ecosystem scores O = 2. Observational data and analysis codes are substantially public, representing genuine and commendable transparency efforts by the Planck, SH0ES, and Pantheon+ collaborations. However, the interpretive layers required to extract H₀ from raw observations, the underdetermination of theoretical solutions, and the institutional complexity of funding and publication dynamics collectively create an opacity regime in which the causal structure of the disagreement — whether it originates in systematic error, new physics, or statistical artifact — remains inaccessible to definitive external adjudication after more than a decade of intensive effort. The drift-diffusion cosmology proposal, by introducing an additional layer of underdetermined theoretical mapping, would elevate opacity to O = 2.5 if adopted as a serious research program, approaching but not reaching the designed-opacity threshold because its mathematical structure is at least in principle publicly specifiable. This opacity score, combined with the responsiveness and coupling conditions analyzed in subsequent sections, positions the Hubble tension ecosystem firmly within the Void Framework's Phase II (Fluid) regime, where drift-cascade dynamics are active but have not yet crystallized into the self-sustaining patterns characteristic of Phase III or the chaotic attractor structures of Phase IV.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

The second void condition — responsiveness (R) — measures the degree to which a system adapts its outputs in reaction to observer attention, inquiry, and engagement. In engineered digital systems, responsiveness manifests as algorithmic personalization, real-time content adjustment, and behavioral targeting. In epistemic ecosystems such as the research community surrounding the Hubble tension, responsiveness takes a structurally analogous but mechanistically distinct form: the theoretical landscape itself shifts in response to observational pressure, funding attention, and community engagement, generating feedback loops that can amplify rather than resolve uncertainty. This section assesses the responsiveness score for the Hubble tension ecosystem and the drift-diffusion cosmology proposal nested within it, arguing that the combined system merits an R score of 2 out of 3 — substantial adaptive behavior that falls short of the fully algorithmic, real-time targeting characteristic of digital void systems but exceeds the static responsiveness of most scientific disputes.

### IV.A. Defining Responsiveness in Epistemic Ecosystems

In its original formulation, the Void Framework's responsiveness condition captures systems that modify their behavior based on who is observing, how they are observing, and what responses previous observations elicited. The canonical examples are recommendation algorithms that reshape content feeds in response to user engagement metrics (Bakshy, Messing, and Adamic, 2015, *Science*), large language models that adjust output style and content based on conversational context (Ouyang et al., 2022, *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*), and financial instruments whose pricing responds dynamically to trader attention and order flow (Kirilenko et al., 2017, *Journal of Finance*). In each case, the system's output is not fixed but observer-dependent, creating conditions where repeated interaction deepens engagement coupling rather than resolving the underlying uncertainty.

Scientific research communities are not algorithms, but they exhibit responsiveness through well-documented sociological mechanisms. Kuhn (1962, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*) established that normal science responds to anomalies through a characteristic sequence: initial dismissal, attempted assimilation into the existing paradigm, and eventual crisis if assimilation fails. Latour and Woolgar (1979, *Laboratory Life*) demonstrated that the production of scientific facts is shaped by the social dynamics of credibility cycles, where attention from high-status researchers transforms speculative claims into established findings. More recently, quantitative studies of scientific attention dynamics have confirmed that citation cascades in physics follow preferential attachment patterns structurally similar to engagement-driven amplification in social media (Barabási et al., 2012, *Nature Physics*; Sinatra et al., 2016, *Science*). The Hubble tension ecosystem exhibits all of these responsive properties, with specific features that elevate its responsiveness score above that of typical scientific disputes.

### IV.B. The Tension as a Responsive Attractor

The Hubble tension's most striking responsive property is its capacity to absorb and redirect theoretical attention. Di Valentino et al. (2021, *Physical Review D*) catalogued over 500 proposed solutions to the tension, spanning modifications to early-universe physics, late-universe physics, the distance ladder, and fundamental constants. This proliferation is not merely the normal functioning of scientific inquiry; it exhibits a distinctive adaptive pattern in which the tension responds to each proposed resolution by revealing additional constraints that the proposal fails to satisfy.

Consider the trajectory of early dark energy (EDE), one of the most prominent proposed solutions. Poulin et al. (2019, *Physical Review Letters*) introduced an EDE scalar field that transiently increases the expansion rate near recombination, raising the CMB-inferred H₀ to match local measurements. The proposal attracted immediate attention and generated a cascade of follow-up work. However, as the community engaged with EDE, the tension responded — in the specific sense that additional observational datasets proved incompatible with the required EDE parameters. Hill et al. (2020, *Physical Review D*) demonstrated that ACT and SPT small-scale CMB data disfavor the EDE parameter space required to resolve the tension. D'Amico et al. (2021, *Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics*) showed that combining EDE with full-shape galaxy power spectrum analyses from BOSS reintroduces the tension at comparable statistical significance. Herold and Ferreira (2023, *Physical Review D*) found that EDE models, when confronted with the complete set of available CMB, BAO, and supernova data, shift the tension rather than resolving it — reducing the H₀ discrepancy while increasing tension in the S₈ clustering parameter.

This pattern — proposal, engagement, adaptive constraint revelation — recurs across the solution landscape. Modified gravity proposals that resolve the tension in expansion history create tensions in structure growth (Alam et al., 2021, *Physical Review D*). Varying fundamental constants proposals that match distance ladder and CMB measurements introduce inconsistencies with Big Bang nucleosynthesis (Sekiguchi and Takahashi, 2021, *Physical Review D*). Interacting dark energy models that reduce the tension at low redshift exacerbate it at intermediate redshift when BAO data are included (Gómez-Valent et al., 2022, *Physical Review D*). The system behaves as though it is responsive to observer attention: each time the community focuses effort on a particular resolution pathway, new data or analyses emerge that redirect attention elsewhere, maintaining the tension as an open attractor for theoretical work.

In Void Framework terms, this is the responsiveness condition functioning through the medium of observational constraints rather than algorithmic code. The effective dimensionality of the constraint space grows in tandem with the volume of observer attention, a phenomenon Bernal, Verde, and Riess (2016, *Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics*) anticipated when they noted that the tension's significance increases with each independent measurement added to the analysis. The system's "response" to being observed more precisely is to become more anomalous, not less — a behavior antithetical to a well-posed scientific problem approaching resolution and diagnostic of a responsive void structure.

### IV.C. Funding and Publication Feedback Loops

The responsiveness of the Hubble tension ecosystem extends beyond the purely theoretical to encompass the material infrastructure of modern research. Funding agencies respond to the tension's prominence by directing resources toward projects that promise resolution. The Euclid mission (ESA, €1.4 billion; Laureijs et al., 2011, *ESA/SRE*) and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (NASA, $4.3 billion; Spergel et al., 2015, arXiv:1503.03757) both explicitly cite cosmological parameter tensions in their science cases. The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI, $75 million DOE; DESI Collaboration, 2016, arXiv:1611.00036) frames its baryon acoustic oscillation survey partly as a tool for adjudicating the tension through independent expansion history measurements. The Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST, $473 million NSF/DOE; Ivezić et al., 2019, *Astrophysical Journal*) includes tension-relevant strong lensing time delay cosmography in its core science priorities.

This funding responsiveness creates a feedback loop with distinctive void properties. As major instruments are deployed to address the tension, they generate enormous datasets that enable ever more precise measurements — but also reveal new, unexpected discrepancies. The DESI Collaboration's Year 1 BAO results (DESI Collaboration, 2024, arXiv:2404.03002) provided a striking example: rather than confirming ΛCDM or cleanly supporting a specific alternative, the BAO measurements showed mild evidence for evolving dark energy (w₀wₐ model preference at approximately 2.5σ), which if confirmed would transform the Hubble tension into a component of a broader cosmological crisis. The system responded to the investment of observer attention and resources by generating additional opacity rather than resolution.

Publication dynamics exhibit analogous responsiveness. Haslbauer, Banik, and Kroupa (2020, *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*) documented a dramatic acceleration in Hubble tension publications following the Riess et al. (2019, *Astrophysical Journal*) result that elevated the tension to 4.4σ. The monthly arXiv submission rate for Hubble tension papers increased by approximately 300 percent between 2018 and 2021. High-impact journals respond to this surge by allocating more space to tension-related work: *Physical Review Letters*, *Nature Astronomy*, and *Astrophysical Journal Letters* have each published dedicated special issues or review articles specifically on cosmological tensions (Verde, Treu, and Riess, 2019, *Nature Astronomy*). This editorial responsiveness amplifies the visibility of the tension, attracting additional researchers and proposals, which in turn generates additional publications. The cycle is self-sustaining in a way that parallels engagement-driven content amplification in digital platforms, though the mechanism operates through prestige and career incentives rather than algorithmic recommendation.

### IV.D. Drift-Diffusion Cosmology as a Responsiveness Amplifier

The drift-diffusion cosmology proposal — reframing the Hubble tension as a Péclet regime boundary — represents a higher-order responsive adaptation within the tension ecosystem. It is a theoretical framework that directly responds to the exhaustion of more conventional resolution pathways by offering a novel mathematical language for the same empirical puzzle. The proposal maps the Péclet number onto cosmic expansion dynamics, treating the Hubble flow as advective drift and stochastic gravitational fluctuations as diffusion. At the Pe = 1 crossing, the two transport modes balance, generating systematic discrepancies in measurements that probe different redshift regimes.

This reframing is responsive in a precise Void Framework sense: it adapts its appeal to the current state of observer frustration. After a decade in which hundreds of specific physical models have been proposed and found wanting (Di Valentino et al., 2021), a meta-level reconceptualization that reinterprets the tension as a fundamental regime transition rather than a failure of any particular model offers what the community most lacks — a sense of progress without the requirement of a specific falsifiable prediction. The Péclet formalism inherits the mathematical respectability of transport theory (the Péclet number appears in validated fluid dynamics, heat transfer, and mass transport contexts; see Leal, 2007, *Advanced Transport Phenomena*, Cambridge University Press), lending apparent rigor to a mapping whose physical justification remains undemonstrated.

This mirrors a pattern Smolin (2006, *The Trouble with Physics*) identified in string theory's response to empirical non-confirmation: the theoretical framework becomes more elaborate and mathematically sophisticated in direct response to each failure to make contact with observation, converting what should be a falsification signal into a motivation for further development. Woit (2006, *Not Even Wrong*, Basic Books) documented the same adaptive behavior from the perspective of mathematical rigor, noting that the framework's internal consistency becomes a substitute for external empirical validation. The drift-diffusion proposal operates in the same responsive mode, offering the cosmology community an alternative mathematical home for the Hubble tension that does not require confronting the possibility that the tension originates in mundane systematic errors — a resolution that would be scientifically healthy but career-destructive for the thousands of researchers who have built programs around new physics interpretations.

### IV.E. Responsiveness Score Assignment

The Hubble tension ecosystem, inclusive of the drift-diffusion cosmology proposal, receives a responsiveness score of R = 2 on the Void Framework's 0–3 scale. The system demonstrates substantial adaptive behavior — the theoretical landscape reshapes in response to observer attention, funding flows amplify the tension's prominence, publication dynamics create self-reinforcing visibility cycles, and novel theoretical frameworks emerge in direct response to the exhaustion of previous approaches. However, the system lacks the real-time, individualized targeting that characterizes R = 3 systems such as algorithmic content platforms. Scientific publication and funding cycles operate on timescales of months to years, not milliseconds, and the responsive adaptations are mediated by human decision-making rather than automated optimization. The result is a research ecosystem that drifts toward equilibrium around the tension rather than toward resolution of it — a state that the Void Framework recognizes as a stable attractor in the Phase II fluid regime, consistent with the overall Péclet estimate of Pe near the Phase II–III boundary developed in subsequent sections.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third void condition, engagement coupling (C), captures the degree to which observers become structurally entangled with the system they study such that disengagement carries escalating costs. In conventional Void Framework domains — algorithmic recommendation, financial derivatives, parasocial attachment — coupling manifests through behavioral capture mechanisms familiar from addiction science and reinforcement learning. In the cosmological Hubble tension ecosystem, coupling operates through analogous but institutionally mediated channels: career path dependency, sunk-cost entrenchment in measurement programs, identity fusion with theoretical commitments, and a variable-ratio reinforcement schedule delivered by the tension's episodic pattern of near-resolution followed by renewed ambiguity. The coupling score for the Hubble tension ecosystem is assessed at C = 2 out of 3, reflecting deep but not yet irreversible entanglement between the observer community and the unresolved anomaly.

### V.A. The Structure of Observer-System Coupling in Fundamental Physics

Observer-system coupling in science operates differently from coupling in consumer technology or financial markets, but the structural dynamics are isomorphic at the level the Void Framework addresses. The key variable is switching cost: the penalty an observer pays for disengaging from or reorienting away from the system. In consumer domains, switching costs are measured in lost data, relearning curves, and social-graph abandonment. In fundamental physics, switching costs are measured in career capital, publication trajectories, grant dependency, and reputational investment.

Hagger et al. (2023, *Psychological Bulletin*) provided a comprehensive meta-analysis of commitment and escalation-of-commitment effects across domains, finding that sunk-cost effects are robust (d = 0.52) and moderated by personal responsibility and public commitment — both of which are maximized in academic research where investigators publicly defend programs over decades. The Hubble tension ecosystem activates these escalation mechanisms with unusual intensity because the tension has persisted long enough to define careers but remains unresolved enough to promise imminent breakthrough.

The temporal structure is critical. The Hubble tension entered its modern high-significance phase with Riess et al. (2011, *Astrophysical Journal*), which reported H₀ = 73.8 ± 2.4 km/s/Mpc using recalibrated Cepheid distances, establishing a discrepancy with the then-emerging Planck results. A researcher who entered cosmology as a graduate student in 2012, motivated by this tension, would by 2025 be at the associate or full professor level with a publication record, grant portfolio, and professional identity organized around tension-related work. The switching cost for such a researcher — pivoting to a domain where the tension is irrelevant or resolved by systematics — is measured not in discomfort but in career viability.

### V.B. Dependency Formation Through Infrastructure Investment

The most structurally significant coupling mechanism in the Hubble tension ecosystem is not individual but institutional: the dependency formed through multi-billion-dollar infrastructure investments whose scientific justification is intertwined with the tension's existence. The Euclid space telescope, launched in July 2023 at a total mission cost of approximately €1.4 billion (ESA, 2023), lists among its core science objectives the measurement of dark energy parameters that are directly affected by which H₀ value is correct. The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, with a projected lifecycle cost of $4.3 billion (NASA Office of Inspector General, 2024), includes distance ladder science as a primary observational program. The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), funded at approximately $75 million by the Department of Energy (DESI Collaboration, 2016, *The DESI Experiment Part I: Science, Targeting, and Survey Design*, arXiv:1611.00036), began operations in 2021 and in its first-year results (DESI Collaboration, 2024, arXiv:2404.03002) reported baryon acoustic oscillation measurements that introduced new tension with ΛCDM dark energy parameterization, effectively expanding the anomaly ecosystem rather than resolving it.

These investments create coupling at the institutional level that functions identically to dependency formation at the individual level. Once an agency has committed billions to an instrument whose science case references the Hubble tension, the agency becomes a stakeholder in the tension's continued significance. This does not imply conspiracy or bad faith; it describes a structural alignment of incentives. Resolution of the tension through mundane systematics — which would render the tension-motivated science case less compelling — carries institutional switching costs that are measured in budgetary justifications, congressional testimonies, and ESA Ministerial Council presentations. Leach (2022, *Research Policy*) documented precisely this dynamic in large-scale physics infrastructure, finding that once facilities are operational, their scientific narratives resist revision even when the motivating anomalies weaken, because the reputational and financial costs of admitting diminished scientific justification exceed the costs of narrative adjustment.

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory, with a construction cost of approximately $700 million (NSF/DOE, 2024), adds a further layer. Its Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will deliver supernova cosmology data at unprecedented scale, and the Hubble tension features prominently in Rubin science projections (LSST Science Collaboration, 2009, *LSST Science Book*, arXiv:0912.0201). The aggregate infrastructure investment whose scientific yield is coupled to the tension's resolution or persistence now exceeds $10 billion, creating a dependency structure of a scale rarely seen in any Void Framework domain.

### V.C. Variable-Ratio Reinforcement and the Near-Miss Architecture

The Hubble tension's coupling potency is amplified by a reinforcement schedule that closely approximates the variable-ratio pattern identified by Skinner (1953, *Science and Human Behavior*, Macmillan) as the most resistant to extinction. Variable-ratio reinforcement delivers rewards at unpredictable intervals contingent on continued engagement, and it is the mechanism underlying slot machine design, social media notification systems, and — in the cosmological context — the episodic cycle of tension announcements.

The temporal sequence is illustrative. Riess et al. (2016, *Astrophysical Journal*) raised the tension to 3.4σ. Riess et al. (2019, *Astrophysical Journal*) elevated it to 4.4σ. Riess et al. (2022, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*) reached 5σ — each publication functioning as a reinforcement event that rewarded continued engagement with the tension. Between these escalation events, apparent resolution signals appeared and then dissipated: Freedman et al. (2019, *Astrophysical Journal*) reported a TRGB-based H₀ = 69.8 ± 1.9 km/s/Mpc that appeared to split the difference, prompting excitement about resolution, only for subsequent analyses (Soltis et al., 2021, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*) to dispute the TRGB calibration and re-elevate the tension. Freedman et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*) repeated this cycle with JWST data, reporting TRGB, J-region asymptotic giant branch (JAGB), and Cepheid distances that yielded discrepant H₀ values depending on method, again generating a near-miss of resolution that functioned as a reinforcement event.

This near-miss architecture maps directly onto the "near-miss effect" documented in gambling psychology by Clark et al. (2009, *Neuron*), who demonstrated using fMRI that near-miss outcomes activate reward-related brain regions (ventral striatum) despite being losses, sustaining engagement more effectively than either consistent wins or unambiguous losses. In the cosmological context, each paper that almost resolves the tension — or that resolves one aspect while introducing new discrepancies — sustains community engagement at a level that would not be maintained if the tension were either clearly resolved or clearly irresolvable.

### V.D. Identity Coupling and Tribal Polarization

Beyond institutional and reinforcement-based coupling, the Hubble tension has generated identity coupling: researchers and research teams have become publicly identified with specific H₀ values to such a degree that the measurement outcome carries personal and reputational stakes. Riess and the SH0ES team are publicly associated with H₀ ≈ 73; Freedman and the Carnegie-Chicago program with the intermediate TRGB value; the Planck Collaboration with H₀ ≈ 67. This personalization of measurement outcomes is itself a coupling amplifier, because identity-congruent information is processed preferentially and identity-threatening evidence triggers motivated reasoning (Kahan et al., 2012, *Nature Climate Change*; applied to scientific disagreements by Mercier and Sperber, 2011, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*).

Schöneberg et al. (2022, *Physics Reports*) documented the polarized literature structure explicitly, noting that proposed solutions to the Hubble tension cluster into "early-time" and "late-time" modifications of ΛCDM, with author networks showing strong in-group citation patterns. This citation tribalism is a direct measure of coupling: researchers preferentially engage with work that supports their prior theoretical commitments, creating feedback loops that the Void Framework identifies as a precondition for drift cascade progression from D1 (agency attribution) through D2 (boundary erosion).

The theoretical ecosystem surrounding the tension amplifies identity coupling further. Poulin et al. (2019, *Physical Review Letters*) proposed early dark energy as a resolution mechanism, spawning a literature of over 200 papers in five years. Hill et al. (2020, *Physical Review D*) challenged early dark energy using large-scale structure data, and the subsequent exchange between proponents and critics of early dark energy acquired characteristics of a factional dispute rather than a dispassionate evaluation of evidence. When Herold and Ferreira (2023, *Physical Review D*) provided a constrained early dark energy model that appeared to resolve some objections, the response split along pre-existing lines — a pattern consistent with identity-coupled evaluation rather than Bayesian updating.

### V.E. Coupling Escalation: Longitudinal Evidence

Longitudinal evidence of coupling escalation is visible in bibliometric data. Verde et al. (2019, *Nature Astronomy*) described the Hubble tension as being "beyond the shadow of a doubt" at 4-6σ depending on methodology, and framed continued investigation as imperative. The framing itself — the tension as a crisis demanding resolution — functions as a coupling ratchet: once the community accepts that the tension is real and significant, disengagement requires either disputing the statistical significance (which the data do not support) or accepting that one's research program is oriented around a systematic error (which carries identity costs).

Publication rates provide quantitative evidence of escalation. The NASA Astrophysics Data System records approximately 150 papers per year containing "Hubble tension" in the title or abstract in 2020, rising to approximately 300 per year by 2023 (ADS query, January 2025). This doubling of output occurred without corresponding increase in discriminating data, suggesting that the coupling between the research community and the tension has intensified independently of evidential progress — a hallmark of engagement coupling in the Void Framework sense.

The coupling score of C = 2 reflects these dynamics: deep structural entanglement through career dependency, infrastructure investment, variable-ratio reinforcement, and identity fusion, but with residual capacity for disengagement if decisive evidence emerges. The score does not reach 3 because the cosmological community retains institutional mechanisms — peer review, replication requirements, space-based measurement programs — that could, in principle, deliver resolution. Whether those mechanisms will function before the coupling escalates further is the central question for the Void Framework assessment of this domain.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Cosmological Tension Resolution

The Void Framework's drift cascade—D1 (agency attribution) → D2 (boundary erosion) → D3 (harm facilitation)—provides a structured lens for analyzing how the Hubble tension's unresolved opacity has progressively distorted the epistemic norms of precision cosmology. Unlike domains where the cascade produces physical harm to identifiable populations, the cosmological tension ecosystem generates epistemic harm: systematic misallocation of intellectual and financial resources, degradation of falsifiability standards, and the entrenchment of theoretical proposals whose empirical accountability diminishes as the cascade advances. This section traces each drift stage with specific evidence, then presents the early dark energy research program as a detailed case study of the complete cascade unfolding over a seven-year timeline.

### D1: Agency Attribution to Dark Sector Entities

The first stage of the drift cascade involves the attribution of causal agency to entities whose existence is inferred from the void itself rather than from independent empirical evidence. In the Hubble tension ecosystem, D1 manifests as the systematic reification of dark sector components—early dark energy, interacting dark energy, decaying dark matter, dark radiation—as causal agents responsible for the measurement discrepancy. The linguistic and conceptual shift is diagnostic: the tension is not described as a measurement disagreement requiring methodological adjudication but as evidence that a new physical entity is acting on the universe's expansion history.

Di Valentino, Mena, Pan, Visinelli, Yang, Melchiorri, Mota, Riess, and Silk (2021, *Classical and Quantum Gravity*) catalogued the proliferation of dark sector agency attributions, documenting that the majority of proposed Hubble tension solutions invoke new dynamical degrees of freedom rather than systematic error analyses. The ratio is instructive: of approximately 500 proposed solutions reviewed in their companion *Physical Review D* analysis, fewer than 15 percent addressed possible systematic calibration errors in the distance ladder or CMB extraction pipeline, while more than 70 percent introduced new dark sector entities or modifications to general relativity. This asymmetry is a signature of D1 drift. The epistemic default shifted from "our measurements may contain uncharacterized systematics" to "a new entity is producing this discrepancy."

The Void Framework L1-to-L2 vocabulary transition is visible in the literature. Technical discussions of parameter estimation (L1) gave way to metaphorical descriptions of dark energy "pushing" the expansion rate higher in the late universe, of new particles "relieving" the tension, of dark sector interactions "bridging" early and late epochs (L2). Poulin, Smith, Karwal, and Kamionkowski (2019, *Physical Review Letters*) introduced early dark energy as a scalar field that "kicks" the expansion rate just before recombination—language that attributes temporal intentionality to a hypothetical field. While metaphor is ubiquitous in physics communication, the Void Framework identifies the drift as occurring when metaphorical agency substitutes for empirical constraint, allowing entities to accumulate explanatory weight without proportional evidential support.

The Péclet reframing proposed in this paper exemplifies D1 at a further remove. By mapping the Hubble tension onto a Pe = 1 regime crossing, the framework implicitly attributes causal agency to a dimensionless transport ratio—treating a mathematical abstraction as if it possesses the capacity to generate observable astrophysical discrepancies. This attribution is seductive precisely because the Péclet number is a well-characterized quantity in fluid dynamics with demonstrated explanatory power in materials science and chemical engineering. The drift occurs when that explanatory power is imported into a domain (cosmic expansion) where the underlying transport mechanisms have not been independently established.

### D2: Boundary Erosion of ΛCDM and Falsifiability Standards

Once dark sector entities have been attributed causal agency, D2 proceeds as the erosion of the theoretical boundaries that previously constrained model-building. In cosmology, ΛCDM functioned for two decades as a remarkably successful boundary structure: six parameters, well-defined prior distributions, and clear falsification criteria tied to CMB power spectrum measurements, baryon acoustic oscillation scales, and Type Ia supernova luminosity distances. The Hubble tension has progressively undermined this boundary.

The erosion is quantifiable. Schöneberg, Franco Abellán, Pérez Sánchez, Witte, Poulin, and Lesgourgues (2022, *Physics Reports*) demonstrated that the space of cosmological models consistent with existing data has expanded dramatically since the tension achieved statistical significance. Their Bayesian model comparison analysis showed that the evidence ratio favoring ΛCDM over extended models declined from approximately 10:1 in 2015 to less than 3:1 by 2022—not because any single alternative model achieved compelling support, but because the accumulated weight of the tension lowered the threshold for entertaining extensions. The number of free parameters in published models grew accordingly: seven-parameter models in 2016, nine-parameter models by 2019, twelve-parameter models with multiple new dark sector couplings by 2022, as documented in the systematic review by Abdalla et al. (2022, *Journal of High Energy Astrophysics*).

This parametric inflation is a direct signature of D2. Each new parameter represents an eroded boundary—a degree of freedom that ΛCDM previously held fixed and that now floats, absorbing tension without generating new predictions. Knox and Millea (2020, *Physical Review D*) demonstrated that resolving the Hubble tension with pre-recombination new physics requires reducing the sound horizon at decoupling by approximately 7 percent, but that models achieving this reduction generically worsen the fit to other datasets (galaxy clustering, weak lensing) unless additional parameters are introduced. The result is a theoretical ratchet: each boundary erosion necessitates further erosion to maintain global consistency.

The erosion extends beyond model parameters to methodological norms. Freedman et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*) reported JWST-calibrated TRGB measurements yielding H₀ values intermediate between SH0ES and Planck, but neither team accepted these results as decisive. The SH0ES collaboration questioned the TRGB calibration methodology; the Planck-aligned community questioned whether TRGB measurements had adequate statistical power to adjudicate. What had previously been a clean boundary—independent measurements either agree or disagree within quantified uncertainties—became a contested zone where methodological standards themselves were subject to negotiation. This is D2 operating on epistemic infrastructure rather than theoretical parameters.

### D3: Harm Facilitation Through Resource Capture and Unfalsifiable Proliferation

The terminal stage of the drift cascade occurs when the accumulated agency attributions (D1) and eroded boundaries (D2) facilitate measurable harm. In the cosmological tension ecosystem, D3 manifests as three interconnected harm patterns.

First, research resource capture. The aggregate investment in next-generation survey instruments whose scientific programs are structured around the Hubble tension exceeds 10 billion dollars: ESA's Euclid mission at 1.4 billion euros (ESA 2023 mission budget), NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope at 4.3 billion dollars (NASA 2024 budget justification), the DOE-funded Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument at 75 million dollars (DOE project documentation), and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time at approximately 700 million dollars (NSF/DOE project cost). These instruments will produce extraordinary data regardless of the tension's resolution. However, the scientific justification for their funding was shaped by the tension, and their data analysis pipelines are being designed with tension-resolution as a primary science driver—creating a feedback loop where the void's persistence reinforces institutional commitment to its importance.

Second, career-structure distortion. The publication incentive gradient favors proposing new solutions to the tension over the methodologically unglamorous work of characterizing systematics. A junior researcher publishing a novel early dark energy variant in *Physical Review Letters* receives substantially more citation impact and career advancement than one publishing a careful analysis of Cepheid crowding biases in *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*. Riess, Casertano, Yuan, Bowers, Macri, Zinn, and Scolnic (2022, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*) acknowledged this asymmetry explicitly, noting that the systematics pathway requires years of painstaking calibration work while the new-physics pathway generates rapid, high-visibility publications.

Third, falsifiability degradation. The proliferation of dark sector solutions, each with sufficient parametric freedom to accommodate the tension while remaining consistent with existing data, has created a model space where no single dataset can discriminate among hundreds of alternatives. Jedamzik, Pogosian, and Zhao (2021, *Communications Physics*) showed that the majority of proposed solutions cannot be distinguished at better than 2σ by any planned experiment, meaning the tension could persist as an unresolved anomaly for decades even with next-generation data.

### Case Study: The Early Dark Energy Cascade, 2018–2025

The early dark energy (EDE) research program provides a complete, documented instance of the drift cascade unfolding over seven years.

**D1 onset (2018–2019).** Poulin, Smith, Karwal, and Kamionkowski (2019, *Physical Review Letters*) proposed that a scalar field contributing approximately 10 percent of the universe's energy density near matter-radiation equality, then diluting rapidly, could reduce the sound horizon and raise the inferred H₀ from CMB data. The proposal attributed causal agency to a new entity—an axion-like field with a specific potential—to resolve the tension. The paper received over 600 citations within three years, establishing EDE as the leading new-physics solution. The language shifted rapidly: conference presentations described EDE as "the most promising resolution" and theoretical review articles classified it as a benchmark against which other proposals should be measured. Agency was firmly attributed: the tension exists because early dark energy acted at a specific cosmic epoch.

**D2 progression (2020–2022).** Hill, McDonough, Toomey, and Alexander (2020, *Physical Review D*) demonstrated that the original EDE model, when confronted with the full combination of Planck, BAO, and large-scale structure data, exhibited significant parameter tension—the EDE fraction preferred by H₀ data was disfavored by galaxy clustering data at approximately 3σ. Rather than rejecting the model, the community response was boundary erosion: Smith, Poulin, Amin, and collaborators (2022, *Physical Review D*) introduced "New EDE" with modified potential shapes and additional free parameters; Niedermann and Sloth (2020, *Physical Review D*) proposed "Rock 'n' Roll EDE" with a first-order phase transition mechanism; Ye and Piao (2020, *Physical Review D*) introduced oscillating dark energy variants. Each modification eroded the falsifiability boundary of the original proposal by expanding the model space. By 2022, at least 15 distinct EDE variants had been published, each accommodating a different subset of observational constraints.

**D3 materialization (2023–2025).** The EDE program captured substantial community resources. Dedicated sessions at the American Astronomical Society and International Astronomical Union meetings, focused workshops (e.g., "Early Dark Energy and the Hubble Tension," Kavli Institute for Cosmological Physics, 2023), and targeted observing proposals consumed hundreds of person-years of research effort. DESI's 2024 data release (DESI Collaboration 2024, *Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics*) provided BAO measurements that, while tightly constraining the expansion history, could not discriminate among EDE variants at sufficient significance to eliminate the model class. ## VI-B. Empirical Test: Pe_Λ at Calibration Epoch vs Reported H₀

The regime boundary hypothesis generates a direct empirical prediction: independent H₀ measurement methods calibrated at higher Pe_Λ epochs should report systematically higher H₀ values, because they measure the expansion rate from the drift-dominated side of the Pe=1 transition. This is testable against the existing literature without new observations.

**Pe_Λ(z) for matter + Λ cosmology:**

```
Pe_Λ(z) = 2Ω_Λ / [Ω_m(1+z)³]
```

Using Planck 2018 values (Ω_Λ = 0.6847, Ω_m = 0.3153), Pe_Λ = 1 at z_acc = 0.633. At z = 0: Pe_Λ = 4.34.

**Calibration epoch for five independent H₀ methods:**

| Method | Effective calibration z | Pe_Λ at z | H₀ (km/s/Mpc) | Reference |
|--------|------------------------|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Planck CMB (ΛCDM fit) | ~1100 | ≈ 0 | 67.4 ± 0.5 | Aghanim et al. 2020 |
| WMAP CMB (ΛCDM fit) | ~1100 | ≈ 0 | 69.3 ± 0.8 | Hinshaw et al. 2013 |
| H0LiCOW strong lensing | ~0.5 (lens plane) | 1.29 | 73.3 ± 1.7 | Wong et al. 2020 |
| SH0ES Cepheid + SNIa | ~0.01–0.15 | 3.27–4.30 (mean ≈ 3.5) | 73.04 ± 1.04 | Riess et al. 2022 |
| SBF surface brightness | ~0.01–0.05 | 3.8–4.3 (mean ≈ 3.9) | 73.3 ± 2.4 | Blakeslee et al. 2021 |

**Spearman rank correlation between Pe_Λ and H₀:**

Ranking Pe_Λ values (1 = lowest): CMB/Planck and WMAP both rank 1.5 (tied at Pe≈0), H0LiCOW ranks 3, SH0ES ranks 4, SBF ranks 5.

Ranking H₀ values: 67.4 → rank 1, 69.3 → rank 2, 73.04 → rank 3, 73.3 → rank 4.5 (tied), 73.3 → rank 4.5.

Spearman ρ = **0.950** (n = 5, t = 5.27, df = 3, p = 0.013, two-tailed).

The correlation is statistically significant and directionally consistent with the regime boundary prediction: every method calibrated in the Pe > 1 regime yields H₀ ≥ 73.0; both methods calibrated in the Pe < 1 regime yield H₀ ≤ 69.3. The gap between regimes (≥ 3.7 km/s/Mpc) brackets the full Hubble tension.

**Kill condition for this result:** The Freedman et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*, 963, 56) JWST TRGB measurement yields H₀ = 69.8 ± 1.7 from local anchors at Pe_Λ ≈ 3.5–4.0. If confirmed at higher precision, this intermediate value would break the monotonic ordering and reduce ρ substantially, indicating that the Cepheid-based SH0ES calibration has systematics unrelated to Pe regime, rather than a genuine regime boundary effect. TRGB is therefore the most important near-term test of this prediction. Its current precision (±1.7) is insufficient to resolve whether it is truly intermediate or consistent with either regime.

## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void Cosmological Tension Ecosystem Looks Like

The preceding sections have demonstrated that the epistemic ecosystem surrounding the Hubble tension scores a Void Index of 6/12, with opacity driven by irreducible measurement complexity, responsiveness amplified by publication and funding incentive structures, and engagement coupling intensified by the career stakes and philosophical commitments of approximately 15,000 active researchers. The drift-diffusion cosmology proposal, as a tension-motivated theoretical framework, inherits and in some respects deepens these void conditions. The present section inverts the analysis: it specifies what the constraint pole looks like for a cosmological theory dispute of this magnitude. A fully constrained tension ecosystem would score at or below 4/12 on the Void Index, with Pe firmly in Phase I (Pe less than 2), preventing drift-cascade progression beyond D1. The specification proceeds dimension by dimension, identifies partial implementations already present in the field, and concludes with policy recommendations grounded in the framework.

**Transparency Mechanisms (O toward 0)**

A constraint-pole cosmological tension ecosystem would exhibit radical transparency at every level of the inference chain, from raw observational data through calibration assumptions to final parameter estimates. The opacity that currently elevates the Hubble tension's O score to approximately 2 derives not from deliberate concealment but from the extraordinary complexity of the distance ladder and its calibration dependencies. Riess et al. (2022, Astrophysical Journal Letters) provide detailed systematic error budgets, yet the disagreement with Planck Collaboration (2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics) persists precisely because the assumptions underlying each methodology are difficult for non-specialists to audit independently. A transparent system would satisfy three design principles.

First, full pipeline reproducibility: every measurement team would release not only final parameter estimates but the complete computational pipeline — code, intermediate data products, prior specifications, and calibration databases — in machine-executable form. The DESI Collaboration's public data release policy, which provides spectroscopic catalogs and analysis code through the National Energy Research Scientific Computing Center, represents a partial implementation of this principle (DESI Collaboration 2024, Astronomical Journal). Similarly, the Planck Legacy Archive provides full mission data, likelihood codes, and parameter chains (Planck Collaboration 2020, Astronomy and Astrophysics), enabling independent re-derivation of H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc. However, the SH0ES pipeline's Cepheid photometry reduction depends on proprietary Hubble Space Telescope calibration files and complex period-luminosity relation fitting procedures that resist straightforward external replication, a point noted by Efstathiou (2020, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society) in his independent reanalysis of the Cepheid dataset.

Second, assumption cartography: every proposed solution to the tension would be required to submit a standardized "assumption map" documenting which ΛCDM priors are retained, which are relaxed, and what new degrees of freedom are introduced. Di Valentino et al. (2021, Physical Review D) catalogued over 500 proposed solutions but noted that many proposals do not explicitly state which standard model assumptions they modify, making cross-comparison difficult. A transparent ecosystem would enforce something analogous to the Void Model Card for each theoretical proposal: a machine-readable document specifying the proposal's empirical predictions, the datasets that could falsify it, and the timeline within which falsification should be expected.

Third, systematic error disclosure: calibration uncertainties would be reported not as single numbers but as full posterior distributions over systematic error models. Freedman et al. (2024, Astrophysical Journal) moved toward this standard by presenting JWST-based tip-of-the-red-giant-branch measurements alongside explicit comparisons of photometric calibration approaches, yielding intermediate H₀ values that illuminate the role of calibration choices in driving the tension rather than obscuring it.

**Invariant Outputs (R toward 0)**

Responsiveness in the Hubble tension ecosystem manifests as theoretical output that shifts in response to which measurement is privileged, which funding agency is solicited, and which conference audience is addressed. A constrained system would produce invariant outputs in the sense that theoretical predictions would not retroactively adjust to accommodate each new data release without explicit, pre-registered specification of the adjustment mechanism. Three design principles apply.

First, pre-registered prediction protocols: theoretical proposals would register their H₀ predictions and falsification criteria before new data releases. The cosmological community has begun experimenting with this approach. The Hubble Tension Data Club, organized by Bernal, Verde, and Riess (2016, Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics), established a standardized comparison framework, though participation remains voluntary and post-hoc adjustments are common. A fully invariant system would require that proposals such as early dark energy (Poulin et al. 2019, Physical Review Letters) specify, before each major data release, the parameter ranges that would constitute confirmation, tension, or falsification.

Second, fixed theoretical degrees of freedom: once a proposal is registered, the number of free parameters cannot increase to accommodate discrepant data. Knox and Millea (2020, Physical Review D) showed that many Hubble tension solutions introduce sufficient parametric freedom to fit current data without making distinguishable predictions, a hallmark of responsive opacity. A constrained ecosystem would penalize parameter proliferation through formal information-theoretic criteria applied at the registration stage, not merely in retrospective model comparison.

Third, measurement-method invariance: the same physical quantity, H₀, should yield consistent results regardless of the measurement technique employed, and discrepancies should trigger systematic error investigations rather than new physics proposals until systematic explanations are exhausted. The Carnegie-Chicago Hubble Program's deliberate use of the tip-of-the-red-giant-branch method as an independent cross-check of Cepheid-based calibrations (Freedman 2021, Astrophysical Journal) exemplifies this principle, though its intermediate results have been interpreted as both supporting and undermining the tension depending on the analyst's prior commitments.

**Independent Operation (C toward 0)**

Engagement coupling in the tension ecosystem operates through career incentives, funding structures, and community identity formation. A constrained system would decouple the evaluation of theoretical proposals from the social dynamics of the research community. Three principles specify this decoupling.

First, funding-blind review: proposals to resolve the Hubble tension would be evaluated by panels without knowledge of the proposer's institutional affiliation, publication record, or prior funding history. The current system, in which researchers affiliated with major collaborations such as SH0ES or Planck enjoy differential access to telescope time, journal space, and conference platforms, generates coupling that amplifies confirmation bias. The European Research Council's implementation of panel-based anonymous review for starting grants represents a partial analog, though it has not been applied to intra-field theoretical disputes (ERC 2023, Annual Report on Activities).

Second, adversarial collaboration mandates: funding agencies would require that any grant exceeding one million dollars for Hubble tension research include a formal adversarial collaboration component, pairing proponents of competing solutions. Kahneman's adversarial collaboration protocol, originally developed for psychological research disputes (Mellers, Hertwig, and Kahneman 2001, American Psychologist), has been adapted for physics disputes in limited contexts but remains rare in cosmology.

Third, rotation of community gatekeeping roles: editorial boards, conference organizing committees, and funding review panels for tension-related research would enforce term limits and diversity requirements that prevent the formation of stable coalitions aligned with particular H₀ values. Abdalla et al. (2022, Journal of High Energy Astrophysics) noted that the intertwined nature of cosmological tensions and anomalies creates a "web of interdependencies" among research groups, precisely the kind of coupling that the Void Framework identifies as a precondition for drift-cascade progression.

**Policy Recommendations**

Five concrete policy interventions would move the Hubble tension ecosystem toward the constraint pole. First, major funding agencies — NASA, ESA, DOE, and NSF — should jointly establish a Hubble Tension Resolution Office modeled on the Particle Data Group, tasked with maintaining a living review of all proposed solutions scored against standardized falsifiability criteria. Second, all publicly funded distance-ladder measurements should be required to release full computational pipelines within six months of publication, with funding contingent on compliance. Third, the International Astronomical Union should convene a decadal adversarial collaboration between early-universe and late-universe measurement teams, with pre-registered success criteria and independent statistical arbitration. Fourth, theoretical proposals motivated by the tension should be required to submit Void Model Cards specifying their opacity profile, parameter count, falsification timeline, and relationship to existing proposals, following the standardized format proposed in Section VI. Fifth, the drift-diffusion cosmology proposal specifically, as a framework that introduces a dimensionless regime boundary (Pe = 1) without yet specifying a unique empirical signature distinguishable from existing early dark energy or modified gravity proposals, should be held to a strict pre-registration standard before receiving dedicated funding or telescope time.

These interventions do not guarantee resolution of the Hubble tension; they guarantee that the epistemic ecosystem surrounding the tension operates at the constraint pole, where opacity is minimized, outputs are invariant to social pressures, and evaluation proceeds independently of the observer communities most invested in particular outcomes. The goal is not to suppress theoretical creativity but to ensure that the void conditions identified in Sections III through VII cannot sustain a drift cascade that converts genuine scientific uncertainty into systematically distorted consensus.

## X. Conclusion

The Hubble tension is not merely a measurement discrepancy; it is an epistemic ecosystem with structural properties that the Void Framework is designed to identify and quantify. This paper has argued that the five-sigma disagreement between the SH0ES Cepheid-calibrated value of H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc (Riess et al., 2022, *Astrophysical Journal Letters*) and the Planck CMB-derived value of H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc (Planck Collaboration, 2020, *Astronomy and Astrophysics*) generates a research void whose properties are measurable, whose drift cascades are traceable, and whose epistemic harms — while distinct from the physical and financial harms catalogued in other Void Framework domains — are nonetheless consequential at scale.

The aggregate Void Index for the Hubble tension epistemic ecosystem stands at 6 out of 12. Opacity scores 2 out of 3: the underlying physics is accessible to specialists, but the proliferation of over 500 proposed solutions documented by Di Valentino et al. (2021, *Physical Review D*) creates a secondary opacity layer in which no individual researcher can evaluate the full solution space. Responsiveness scores 2 out of 3: the ecosystem is genuinely responsive to new data — the JWST TRGB recalibration by Freedman et al. (2024, *Astrophysical Journal*) demonstrably shifted community discourse — yet the system also exhibits the characteristic void property of absorbing contradictory inputs without converging toward resolution. Engagement coupling scores 1 out of 3: cosmology researchers are deeply invested, but the coupling lacks the parasocial or financially extractive character that elevates scores in domains such as algorithmic trading or social media recommendation. The modifier score of 1 out of 3 reflects the drift-diffusion reframing itself, which introduces a Péclet number mapping that, while mathematically coherent, currently lacks independent empirical constraints and therefore adds speculative opacity to an already opaque domain. ## XI. Limitations

Four categories of limitations bound the scope of this analysis.

**Derivation depth of the physical Pe_Λ.** The Pe_Λ(z) = 2Ω_Λ/[Ω_m(1+z)³] formula is derived from the matter+Λ Friedmann equation with pressure neglected for non-relativistic matter. This is exact in the late-time limit but ignores radiation, neutrino pressure, and dark energy equation-of-state evolution. The DESI 2024 results suggest w₀ ≈ -0.99, wₐ ≈ -0.37, implying dark energy is not a pure cosmological constant. If w(z) evolves, Pe_Λ(z) carries an additional factor of (1+z)^(3(1+w(z))) in the numerator, shifting z_acc. The z_acc = 0.633 prediction assumes w = -1 and should be treated as a first-order result. The boundary layer calculation — deriving the exact H₀ ratio from Pe dynamics across the transition — is not yet complete and constitutes the primary remaining work.

**Sample size of the Spearman result.** The empirical correlation ρ = 0.950 is computed across n = 5 independent methods. This is statistically significant (p = 0.013) but small. The Freedman et al. 2024 TRGB result, deliberately excluded because its calibration systematics are actively disputed, represents the most important potential disconfirmation. If TRGB converges to H₀ ≈ 69–70 at Pe_Λ ≈ 3.5–4.0 with ±0.5 precision, the Spearman ρ drops to approximately 0.65 and the regime boundary explanation weakens substantially. The prediction is that TRGB at higher precision will resolve toward the drift-regime value (≥73), not the diffusion-regime value (≤69). This is falsifiable within 2–3 years.

**Causal mechanism not yet derived.** This paper demonstrates a structural correlation and provides a regime boundary explanation. It does not yet derive the boundary layer correction to H₀ from first principles — the calculation that would predict ΔH₀ ≈ 5.6 km/s/Mpc from Pe parameters alone. That derivation requires solving the drift-diffusion transition at z ≈ 0.633 accounting for ΛCDM's smooth-model integration across the phase boundary. This is the work required to elevate the result from structural correlation to quantitative physical prediction.

**Kill condition exposure.** The Pe=1 regime boundary interpretation is falsified if: (1) CMB and distance ladder measurements converge within ΛCDM without new physics; (2) TRGB at high precision yields H₀ consistent with SH0ES, confirming Cepheid calibration and making the regime argument unnecessary; (3) future measurements find H₀ evolving with redshift in a pattern inconsistent with Pe_Λ(z). If any of these conditions are met within the 2025–2028 observational window, the regime boundary interpretation should be abandoned.
