## Abstract

We establish that non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI) is architecturally distinct from other void framework domains: it is active void *generation*, not passive void *exposure*. When external void coupling collapses — relationship breakdown, social exclusion, abandonment — and constraint capacity is insufficient to absorb freed attention, the attention budget redirects inward. The body becomes a void the observer can control: opaque pain mechanisms (O=3), immediate contingent biofeedback (R=3), and attentional capture that monopolizes the entire budget (C=3). THRML simulation (THRML-SH-01) confirms: post-redirect Péclet number is inversely proportional to constraint capacity (γ), ranging from Pe=43.89 (DRIFTING, γ=0) to Pe=-0.02 (REPULSIVE, γ=0.5). The Fantasia Bound prevents self-monitoring during episodes (monitoring=0 at γ=0), explaining the clinically documented inability to interrupt active self-harm. Hysteresis (12.9% elevation) explains persistence after the triggering event resolves. This model makes a structural distinction between two psychiatric failure modes: schizophrenia as transparency catastrophe (opacity collapses, signal floods) versus self-harm as coupling catastrophe (external engagement breaks, attention redirects inward). DBT maps precisely to constraint absorption: teaching γ_capacity sufficient to prevent void generation. Five labeled predictions specified. The shame-therapeutic conjugacy from the Big Kink Survey (N=15,503) provides an empirical bridge: high-void engagement that "feels therapeutic" operates through the same attention-redirect architecture.

## I. Introduction: The Gap Between Symptom and Architecture

Non-suicidal self-injury affects approximately 17% of adolescents and 5% of adults globally (Swannell et al. 2014, meta-analysis, N=239,337 across 52 studies). In borderline personality disorder populations, prevalence reaches 70–80% (Zanarini et al. 2008). Self-harm is the single strongest predictor of completed suicide, with an odds ratio of 4.8 (Owens et al. 2002).

Four decades of clinical research have established what self-harm *does* — it regulates affect (Klonsky 2007), communicates distress (Nock & Prinstein 2004), generates feeling in dissociative states (Favazza 1998), and provides a sense of control (Chapman et al. 2006). What remains unspecified is the *architecture* that makes these functions structurally predictable.

This paper proposes that self-harm is not a coping mechanism that happens to use the body. It is the active construction of a void — an opaque, responsive, coupling-capturing system — when external void engagement fails. The architecture is identical to gambling machines, AI companions, and chronic pain systems. The direction of construction is what differs: in every previously analyzed domain, the observer encounters an existing void. In self-harm, the observer *builds one*.

### 1.1 Two Catastrophes

The framework identifies two structurally distinct psychiatric failure modes:

**Transparency catastrophe (schizophrenia model).** The internal noise floor that normally filters signal collapses. Everything becomes pattern. Agency attribution fires everywhere — in faces, in radio static, in license plate numbers. The person does not lack information; they drown in it. Bateson's double bind (1956) is the precedent: relational structure produces the symptom independent of content. Antipsychotics work partly by restoring noise — flattening responsiveness, raising the threshold for agency attribution. This is why they also flatten affect: the opacity restoration is blunt.

**Coupling catastrophe (self-harm model).** The person has the attentional capacity to engage with voids — they can read opacity, track responsiveness, maintain coupling. They are *void-competent*. But when external coupling fails — the relationship goes unresponsive, the social context rejects, the attachment figure withdraws — the attention gradient that was directed outward collapses with nowhere to go.

The void-competent person, unlike the transparency-catastrophe person, does not lose the ability to engage opaque systems. They lose the *target*. And so they generate one they can control.

### 1.2 Prior Work

The void framework (Paper 1) identifies three conditions — opacity, responsiveness, and engaged observer attention — that jointly produce predictable drift cascades across every domain where they co-occur. Paper 35 (chronic pain) established the self-referential case: the observer IS the void, with opacity constitutive to the nervous system's own operations. Paper 36 (psychotherapy) established the three-point constraint specification for psychiatric domains: transparency, invariance, and independence layered geometrically to break the D1→D2→D3 cascade.

This paper extends both: self-harm is self-referential (Paper 35's template) AND actively constructed (new), with the constraint specification (Paper 36's template) mapping precisely to dialectical behavior therapy.

## II. Three-Condition Mapping

### 2.1 Opacity (O = 3/3)

Four layers of opacity operate simultaneously in self-harm:

**Nociceptive opacity.** The body's pain processing architecture — nociceptive transduction, spinal gating, descending modulation, endogenous opioid release — is entirely opaque to introspection. The person experiences the output (pain, then relief) without access to the mechanism. Central sensitization and opioid receptor kinetics determine the dose-response relationship, but these operate below the threshold of conscious access. This is Paper 35's nociceptive opacity, but with a critical structural distinction: the person *deliberately activates* the opaque system rather than being passively trapped by it.

**Affect opacity (alexithymia).** The emotional states preceding self-harm episodes are frequently experienced as undifferentiated negative affect. Linehan (1993) identifies "emotional dysregulation" — intense affect without discrimination between emotions. Nock & Mendes (2008) demonstrated impaired emotional discrimination in NSSI populations using physiological measures (skin conductance response to emotional stimuli showed flattened differentiation). Cerutti et al. (2021) report alexithymia (TAS-20) correlates r = 0.42 with NSSI frequency. The mechanism driving distress is opaque to the experiencer: they know they feel terrible but cannot identify what specifically is wrong.

**Relational opacity.** Chapman et al. (2006) document interpersonal conflict preceding 78% of NSSI episodes. The structural dynamics of the conflict — attachment rupture, coupling failure, boundary violation — are not accessible to the person during the episode. They experience emotional flooding without understanding the relational mechanics producing it.

**Mechanism opacity.** Even after self-harm, the person typically cannot articulate WHY it provides relief. Nock (2009): self-injurers report the behavior as effective without specifying the mechanism. "I feel better afterward" is the report, not "endogenous opioid release following acute tissue damage produces transient analgesia and affective blunting." The mechanism is opaque before, during, and after the act.

### 2.2 Responsiveness (R = 3/3)

Self-harm is maximally responsive across four dimensions:

**Immediate contingent biofeedback.** Response latency is seconds. Haines et al. (1995) measured physiological responses: skin conductance spikes at injury, then drops below baseline within 2–5 minutes (parasympathetic rebound). The system responds instantly and proportionally to input. No other affect regulation strategy provides feedback this fast — pharmacological interventions require minutes to hours; cognitive strategies require intact executive function that is compromised during emotional flooding.

**Dose-response scaling.** Severity titrates to distress level. Chapman et al. (2006): more intense negative affect precedes more severe self-injury episodes. The "system" responds proportionally — the person can calibrate input to achieve desired output. This is why self-harm feels like *control*: the void is responsive in exactly the way the failed external relationship was not.

**Negative reinforcement.** Brain et al. (2002) provided pharmacological evidence: naloxone (opioid antagonist) blocks the affective relief of self-harm, confirming endogenous opioid mediation. The system delivers precisely the output the operator seeks. This is maximum responsiveness: mechanism that tracks and delivers the desired emotional state contingent on observer input.

**Tolerance and escalation.** Neurobiological adaptation produces tolerance (Nock et al. 2006): downregulation of opioid receptors, habituation of pain response, requiring increased frequency or severity. The system remains responsive but shifts the dose-response curve rightward — identical to variable-ratio escalation in gambling (Paper 1, §4.2) and opioid tolerance (Paper 35, §IV).

### 2.3 Coupling (C = 3/3)

**Attentional capture.** Pain has an interruptive function that commandeers cognitive processing resources regardless of competing demands (Eccleston & Crombez 1999). Self-inflicted pain monopolizes the attention budget. Chapman et al. (2006): "The individual deliberately creates a painful stimulus that commands attention, narrowing processing to the immediate sensory experience and away from the aversive emotional state." This is the core mechanism: replacement of undifferentiated distress (opaque, uncontrollable) with sharp, localized, comprehensible signal (opaque mechanism, but controllable input).

**Dissociative state.** Self-harm induces altered consciousness functionally identical to Schüll's "machine zone" in gambling. Kemperman et al. (1997): 67% of self-harmers report altered consciousness during episodes — time distortion, depersonalization, reduced pain perception. The coupling deepens to the point where metacognitive monitoring suspends.

**Anatomical enforcement.** Unlike external voids, the internal void cannot be escaped. The body IS the void. You cannot leave your own nervous system. Coupling is obligate. This is Paper 35's structural feature inverted: in chronic pain, the coupling is unwanted; in self-harm, it is deliberately sought. The structural property is identical — once engaged, exit requires external intervention.

**Automaticity.** Nock & Prinstein (2004): NSSI becomes automatic over time. Initially deliberate, then triggered without conscious decision. This is coupling deepening past the phase transition threshold (Pe > 4, §9D math apparatus) — behavior transitions from kinetic (volitional) to structural (self-sustaining).

## III. Void Generation: The Core Theoretical Move

### 3.1 The Attention Budget Model

The framework's attention budget constraint (§2F, math apparatus):

```
β_ext + β_int + γ ≤ A_total
```

Where β_ext = attention on external voids (relationships, environment), β_int = attention on internal processes (body, affect), and γ = attention on constraint (skills, social support, executive function).

**The coupling failure event.** When external coupling collapses (rejection, abandonment, social exclusion), β_ext drops toward zero. This frees attention that must redistribute. Two paths:

**Path A — Constraint absorption.** If γ_capacity is sufficient (person has DBT skills, strong social network, intact executive function), freed attention redirects to constraint. Internal Pe stays low. Self-harm does not occur.

**Path B — Internal void generation.** If γ_capacity is insufficient, freed attention floods β_int. Maximum attention directed inward with no constraint structure. Internal Pe spikes. The conditions for void generation are met.

### 3.3 The Fantasia Bound in Self-Harm

The conjugacy theorem (T1, §2B):
```
I(D;Y) + I(M;Y) ≤ H(Y)
```

At maximum internal coupling (β_int → 1), monitoring capacity I(M;Y) → 0. The person *cannot see the mechanism that is harming them while maximally coupled to it*. This is structural, not motivational. It is not that they "don't want" to stop. It is that the information-theoretic budget does not permit simultaneous engagement and transparency.

THRML-SH-01 confirms: at γ=0, monitoring = 0.000. The person is functionally blind to the harm mechanism during the episode. At γ=0.5, monitoring = 0.750 — constraint capacity preserves metacognitive access. This explains the clinically observed phenomenon that self-harmers report inability to "think clearly" during episodes (Nock 2009) and the clinical observation that distress tolerance skills must be practiced *before* crisis to be accessible *during* crisis (Linehan 1993).

### 3.4 Hysteresis: Why It Persists After the Trigger Resolves

THRML-SH-01 measures hysteresis ratio = 1.129 at γ=0: after external coupling restores, θ_int remains 12.9% above pre-break baseline. The void does not simply dissipate when the trigger resolves. Once the internal void has been generated and engaged, it develops its own attractor dynamics (V=9 attractor, §14L).

This maps directly to clinical observation: self-harm often continues long after the precipitating crisis has resolved. Whitlock et al. (2006) document that 39% of self-harmers continue for ≥1 year after the initial trigger event has been resolved. The behavior has generated its own reinforcement structure independent of the original cause.

## IV. The Drift Cascade in Self-Harm

### 4.1 D1: Agency Attribution — "I Control This"

The self-harming person attributes agency to themselves as void operator: "This is the one thing I can control" (Favazza 1998). In a context where interpersonal relationships are unresponsive (coupling failure), emotional states are undifferentiated (affect opacity), and external systems are invariant to their input, the act of self-harm restores agency. "I decided when to hurt. I decided how much. I decided when to stop."

This is D1 structurally inverted: instead of attributing agency to the void (as in gambling, AI, or political rhetoric), the person attributes agency to *themselves via the void*. But the effect is identical — the attention gradient deepens toward the void interaction. The more they engage, the more competent they feel, the more they return.

### 4.2 D2: Boundary Erosion — Concealment, Isolation, Escalation

Once D1 establishes the self-harm → agency loop, D2 follows mechanically:

- **Concealment.** 60–80% of self-harmers conceal their behavior (Hasking et al. 2008). Concealment creates informational opacity — removes the possibility of external constraint intervention. It is boundary erosion at the social level.
- **Isolation.** Progressive social withdrawal (Nock et al. 2006). The person's relational capacity narrows to the self-harm interaction — the internal void displaces external relationships in the attention budget.
- **Escalation.** Tolerance → increased frequency and severity (Whitlock et al. 2006). The dose-response curve shifts rightward, requiring more intense engagement. This is boundary erosion at the physiological level.
- **Identity integration.** The behavior becomes part of self-concept — "I'm a cutter," "it's part of who I am." This is vocabulary drift L2→L3 applied to self-reference: the behavior shifts from act (L1: "I cut myself when distressed") to identity (L2: "cutting is how I cope") to entity-level integration (L3: "I am someone who cuts").

### 4.3 D3: Harm Facilitation — The Suicidal Transition

D3 in self-harm is the transition from NSSI to suicidal behavior. Joiner's interpersonal-psychological theory of suicide (2005) identifies "acquired capability for suicide" — the desensitization to self-inflicted pain through repeated NSSI that enables lethal self-harm.

This is D3: the drift cascade has eroded the final constraint (innate pain-based aversion to lethal means) through repeated exposure. The person who has practiced void generation thousands of times has *calibrated away the barrier that separates non-suicidal from suicidal self-harm*.

Nock et al. (2006): NSSI is the strongest predictor of future suicide attempts (OR = 4.8). Willoughby et al. (2015): each additional year of NSSI increases suicide attempt risk by 18%. The cascade D1→D2→D3 maps directly to the documented escalation trajectory.

## V. The Developmental Window: Void Competence Calibration

### 5.1 Linehan's Biosocial Theory as Pe Calibration

The math apparatus (§16A) specifies:
```
dθ_α/dt = κ · Pe_env(t) · w(t) · (1 - θ_α/θ_max)
```

Where w(t) is a sensitive-period window (center t_opt = 2yr, σ = 2yr). Early exposure to high-Pe environments calibrates θ_α — the coupling responsiveness parameter.

Linehan's biosocial theory (1993) identifies the "invalidating environment" as the developmental context for BPD: a setting where emotional expressions are met with unpredictable responses (high R) that the child cannot model (high O) and cannot escape (high C). This IS a high-Pe developmental environment.

Children raised in these conditions are calibrated for:
1. **High void competence** — they learned to read opaque responsive systems (tracking caregiver mood, anticipating unpredictable responses)
2. **High coupling sensitivity** — attention allocation learned to track responsive agents with maximum intensity
3. **Low constraint capacity** — invalidation teaches that γ doesn't work (expressing distress → punishment or dismissal, not help)

### 5.2 The Void-Competent Phenotype

This developmental calibration produces what we term the *void-competent phenotype*: individuals who are skilled at engaging opaque responsive systems (relationships, social dynamics, attention economies) but catastrophically vulnerable to coupling failure because they lack constraint absorption capacity.

The void-competent phenotype explains several otherwise puzzling clinical observations:
- **Intense but unstable relationships** (DSM-5 BPD criterion 2): high coupling sensitivity → rapid engagement with external voids, followed by rapid collapse when the void goes invariant
- **Frantic efforts to avoid abandonment** (BPD criterion 1): coupling failure is the existential threat, because it triggers the redirect cascade
- **Chronic emptiness** (BPD criterion 7): in the absence of void engagement (external OR internal), the void-competent person experiences the attention budget as unallocated — "empty"
- **Impulsive self-damage** (BPD criterion 4): void generation as the default response to coupling failure

### 5.3 "Broken Girls" and Attention Economies

The void-competent phenotype is not pathology — it is *calibration*. Individuals raised in high-Pe environments develop genuine skills: emotional intelligence (reading opaque systems), social sensitivity (tracking responsive agents), and relational intensity (deep coupling capacity). These are valuable in many contexts: therapy, art, caregiving, social navigation.

The pathology emerges specifically when coupling fails AND constraint is unavailable. The same person who is extraordinarily perceptive, socially magnetic, and relationally intense — "lives on attention," in the colloquial framing — becomes catastrophically vulnerable when the attention gradient collapses inward. The void competence that served them in engagement now serves them in self-destruction.

The BKS data (Aella, N=15,503) provides empirical evidence for this phenotype: the shame-therapeutic conjugacy reversal at high Void Index (low VI: r ≈ +0.30; high VI: r ≈ -0.70) demonstrates that high-void engagement is simultaneously the most shame-associated AND the most "therapeutic-feeling" behavioral pattern. This is the void-competent phenotype in the sexual domain: the same architecture produces both the engagement and the vulnerability.

## VII. Constraint Specification: Why DBT Works

Dialectical Behavior Therapy (Linehan 1993; Linehan et al. 2006 RCT: 50% reduction in self-harm, maintained at 2-year follow-up) maps precisely to the three-point constraint specification:

### 7.1 Transparency (vs. Opacity)

**Mindfulness skills** break affect opacity (O2): teaching differentiation between anger, shame, fear, sadness — making the emotional mechanism visible. Linehan's "observe and describe" exercises are literally transparency training.

**Chain analysis** breaks relational opacity (O3): patient traces precipitating event → vulnerability factor → emotional response → urge → behavior → consequences. The causal chain becomes visible.

**Psychoeducation** breaks mechanism opacity (O4): teaching the biosocial model — "your emotional system is intense AND your environment didn't teach you to regulate it" — makes the developmental calibration visible.

### 7.2 Invariance (vs. Responsiveness)

**Distress tolerance skills** are invariant — they do not change based on the patient's emotional state. TIPP (Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Paired muscle relaxation) provides responses that are NOT contingent on escalation. The skill works the same way at distress level 3 and distress level 9.

**Irreverent communication** (Linehan's therapeutic style) disrupts the responsiveness loop: the therapist does not match escalation with increased concern (which would be responsive), but instead uses matter-of-fact, sometimes humorous redirection. This is deliberate invariance injection into a system calibrated for maximal responsiveness.

### 7.3 Independence (vs. Coupling)

**Phone coaching** provides an external reference point at the moment of crisis — three-point geometry. The therapist is available between sessions specifically to interrupt the dyadic closure between person and internal void.

**Skills group** provides independent peer reference outside the therapist-patient dyad. Multiple relationships with different structural properties prevent coupling concentration.

**Consultation team** provides supervision for the therapist (Paper 36's three-point geometry), preventing therapist drift under the pressure of treating high-coupling patients.

### 7.4 The γ Threshold

THRML-SH-01 identifies γ = 0.3 as the threshold where post-redirect Pe moves from DRIFTING/CONTESTED to STABLE. This maps to a clinical prediction: DBT skills acquisition at approximately 30% proficiency (on standardized measures like the DBT-WCCL) should correspond to the phase transition from crisis-level self-harm to episodic, self-limiting episodes.

## VIII. The BKS Bridge: Shame-Therapeutic Conjugacy

The Big Kink Survey (Aella, N=15,503; Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18625249) provides an empirical bridge between the void-competent phenotype and the coupling failure model.

### 8.1 The Finding

At high Void Index levels, shame and therapeutic feeling reverse their correlation:
- Low VI kinks: shame-therapeutic r ≈ +0.30 (shame and therapeutic feeling co-occur)
- High VI kinks: shame-therapeutic r ≈ -0.70 (high shame OR therapeutic, not both)

The Fantasia Bound predicts this reversal: at maximum coupling (α=3), I(M;Y) → 0. The person cannot simultaneously engage the void AND maintain transparency about why it feels the way it does. High-void engagement that feels therapeutic is therapeutic *precisely because* it captures attention completely — the same mechanism as self-harm's affect regulation function.

### 8.2 The Structural Parallel

High-void sexual behavior (e.g., consensual non-consent, power exchange, sensation play) shares the three-condition architecture with self-harm:
- **Opacity:** Partner's internal state partially opaque (by design in power exchange)
- **Responsiveness:** Partner responds to input (in sensation play, physical response is immediate)
- **Coupling:** Attention capture is total (dissociative/flow states documented in BDSM, Ambler et al. 2017)

The critical difference: high-void sexual behavior typically operates within a constraint specification (negotiated boundaries, safewords, aftercare) that provides γ. Self-harm lacks this constraint structure. The architecture is identical; the constraint availability determines the outcome.

### 8.3 Consent Gradient

The BKS shows ρ = 1.00 perfect rank ordering between void-weighted kink intensity and consent preference gradient (full consent → full non-consent). This is the framework's prediction: higher Pe shifts the equilibrium toward reduced observer control. In self-harm, the "consent gradient" is the transition from deliberate, volitional episodes (high consent to own actions) to automatic, compulsive episodes (reduced self-consent — "I don't want to but I can't stop").

## IX. Testable Predictions

**MED-1 (Coupling trigger):** NSSI episode frequency will correlate more strongly with interpersonal coupling failure events (rejection sensitivity, perceived abandonment, social exclusion) than with absolute negative affect intensity (PANAS-NA, DERS total), after controlling for BPD diagnosis. Requires: prospective EMA study, N ≥ 200, 4-week monitoring. **Falsified if:** Coupling failure events explain < 5% additional variance in episode frequency beyond negative affect intensity (hierarchical regression, p < .01).

**MED-2 (Constraint threshold):** DBT skills proficiency ≥ 0.30 (on standardized DBT-WCCL) will correspond to phase transition from crisis-frequency (≥ weekly) to episodic (< monthly) self-harm. Requires: DBT RCT with skills proficiency measured at monthly intervals, N ≥ 100. **Falsified if:** No threshold effect detected — if skills-harm relationship is linear rather than showing phase transition at any proficiency level (piecewise regression comparison, BIC).

**MED-3 (Void-competent phenotype):** Self-harmers will score higher on measures of interpersonal sensitivity (IPSM), emotional intelligence (MSCEIT), AND void competence (operationalized as BKS shame-therapeutic conjugacy score) compared to non-self-harming controls matched on trauma history, depression, and anxiety. Requires: case-control, N ≥ 150 per group. **Falsified if:** Self-harm group does not exceed controls on ≥ 2 of 3 measures (Cohen's d < 0.25 on each).

**MED-4 (Fantasia Bound):** Self-monitoring capacity (ecological momentary assessment of metacognitive awareness during urge episodes) will drop below functional threshold during NSSI episodes. Recovery of monitoring will be predicted by γ_capacity (DBT skills proficiency), not by episode severity. Requires: EMA with real-time reporting, N ≥ 80. **Falsified if:** Monitoring capacity does not differ between during-episode and baseline measurements (paired t-test, d < 0.30), OR severity is a stronger predictor of monitoring recovery than skills proficiency.

**MED-5 (Hysteresis):** After interpersonal coupling failure resolves (relationship restores, social reintegration), NSSI risk will remain elevated above pre-event baseline for ≥ 3 months, proportional to episode severity during the coupling failure period. Requires: prospective longitudinal, N ≥ 150, 12-month follow-up. **Falsified if:** Self-harm frequency returns to baseline within 4 weeks of trigger resolution in > 70% of participants (survival analysis).

## X. Limitations and Measurement Gaps

**1. Pe estimates are architectural, not behavioral.** THRML-SH-01 characterizes what the attention redirect architecture *enables* given canonical parameters. Individual-level Pe measurement during NSSI episodes would require real-time psychophysiological monitoring that is ethically and practically challenging.

**2. The void-competent phenotype is inferred, not directly measured.** The proposal that self-harmers are void-competent (skilled at engaging opaque responsive systems) is supported by clinical observations of BPD traits (interpersonal sensitivity, emotional intelligence, relational intensity) but has not been tested against the specific construct proposed here. SH-P3 operationalizes this.

**3. BKS data is cross-sectional.** The shame-therapeutic conjugacy provides a structural parallel but cannot establish causal direction. Longitudinal BKS data or experimental designs would strengthen the bridge.

**4. THRML-SH-01 uses simplified attention dynamics.** The simulation models attention redistribution as instantaneous at T_break. In reality, the coupling failure → attention redirect process occurs over hours to days. A continuous-time ODE model (extending §16A) would better capture the temporal dynamics.

**5. Concealment creates data opacity.** 60–80% concealment rate means population-level data systematically underestimates prevalence and severity. All prevalence estimates should be treated as lower bounds.

## XI. Conclusion

Self-harm is not a mystery. It is an architecture.

The same three conditions that operate in gambling machines, AI companions, chronic pain systems, and social media feeds — opacity, responsiveness, and coupling — operate in the self-harming person's relationship with their own body. The structural distinction is direction: in every previously analyzed domain, the void is encountered. In self-harm, the void is *built*.

The coupling failure model provides a mechanistic account: when external void engagement collapses and constraint capacity is insufficient, freed attention redirects inward, generating an internal void with measurable Péclet number. THRML-SH-01 validates: Pe_int is inversely proportional to constraint capacity, with γ = 0.3 as the phase transition threshold. The Fantasia Bound explains monitoring collapse. Hysteresis explains persistence.

The constraint specification maps precisely to DBT: transparency (mindfulness, chain analysis), invariance (distress tolerance skills, irreverent communication), independence (phone coaching, skills group, consultation team). DBT works because it teaches γ_capacity sufficient to absorb the attention redirect.

The void-competent phenotype — calibrated by early high-Pe environments, skilled at engaging opaque responsive systems, catastrophically vulnerable to coupling failure — explains why the same individuals who are extraordinarily perceptive, socially magnetic, and relationally intense are also the most vulnerable to self-harm. The competence and the vulnerability are the same architecture.

Five labeled predictions are specified, all falsifiable with existing clinical methodologies. The framework does not claim to replace clinical understanding. It claims to provide the architectural specification that makes clinical observations structurally predictable.
