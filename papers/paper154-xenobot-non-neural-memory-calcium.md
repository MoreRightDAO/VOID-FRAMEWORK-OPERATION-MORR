## Abstract

The construction of xenobots from dissociated *Xenopus laevis* epidermal cells lacking neurons, synapses, or any conventional nervous system architecture has generated a rapidly expanding interdisciplinary literature in which sophisticated mathematical frameworks from statistical physics — Kramers escape theory, K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels, and mean-field convergence theorems — are recruited to formalize claims that these organisms exhibit genuine memory. The foundational demonstration that algorithmically designed aggregates of frog epidermal cells achieve self-organized locomotion and environmental responsiveness (Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 2020), combined with subsequent reports of calcium-dependent behavioral plasticity (Pai, Traer, Sperry, Zeng, and Levin, bioRxiv, 2026), has catalyzed a research program in which thermodynamic basin dynamics serve as the explanatory substrate for non-neural cognition. Kramers' original escape rate formulation for thermally activated barrier crossing in metastable systems (*Physica*, 1940), systematized by the comprehensive review of Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec (*Reviews of Modern Physics*, 1990), provides the mathematical architecture: memory formation is modeled as the trapping of calcium signaling configurations within free-energy basins, and memory retrieval is modeled as stochastic escape over barriers separating such basins. Mean-field convergence results from Lasry and Lions (*Japanese Journal of Mathematics*, 2007) and the propagation-of-chaos framework established by Sznitman (*Lecture Notes in Mathematics*, 1991) are then invoked to justify the passage from individual cell-level calcium dynamics to collective behavioral states. This paper applies the Void Framework — a systematic method for identifying how opacity, responsiveness, and observer coupling jointly generate predictable epistemic distortion — to this emerging domain. We demonstrate that constitutive measurement opacity arising from the temporal resolution limits of GCaMP6 calcium indicators, which exhibit approximately 45 millisecond decay kinetics that temporally smear fast calcium transients (Chen et al., *Nature*, 2013), combines with inherent system responsiveness and deep coupling between observers' theoretical commitments and their interpretive outputs to produce a distinctive epistemic void. This void generates predictable drift cascades in which legitimate interdisciplinary modeling progressively yields to over-attribution of cognitive properties to minimal biological systems.

This paper makes the following specific contributions. First, we provide a complete Void Framework operationalization for non-neural memory research, scoring the domain at a Void Index of 7 out of 12, with opacity at 2.5, responsiveness at 2.0, observer coupling at 2.5, and no domain-specific modifiers beyond rounding. Second, we estimate a Péclet number of approximately 3.2, placing the domain in Phase II Fluid dynamics with localized Phase III crystallization around specific laboratory groups, notably the Levin laboratory at Tufts University and its affiliated commercial entity Morphoceuticals Inc. Third, we trace the complete drift cascade from D1 agency attribution — the redescription of calcium oscillation patterns as memory states — through D2 boundary erosion — the systematic collapse of distinctions between neural and non-neural cognition advanced programmatically by Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2021) — to D3 harm facilitation in the form of research funding misallocation and premature therapeutic claims within a synthetic biology market projected to reach 30.7 billion dollars by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023). Fourth, we perform a detailed technical audit of the Kramers escape formalism as applied to calcium signaling, demonstrating that the free-energy landscape required for barrier-crossing dynamics has not been empirically reconstructed for any xenobot preparation. Fifth, we analyze the K-factorization decomposition of transition kernels, showing that the factorization assumptions required for analytical tractability impose structural constraints that are biologically unverified and may be unfalsifiable given current measurement technology. Sixth, we evaluate the mean-field convergence pathway, identifying that propagation-of-chaos results require exchangeability conditions among signaling cells that xenobot tissue architecture does not satisfy. Seventh, we construct a control case analysis using bacterial chemotaxis, a well-characterized adaptive system scoring 4 out of 12 on the Void Index, to anchor the domain comparison at the constraint pole and demonstrate that equivalent biological responsiveness need not generate equivalent epistemic distortion when opacity and observer coupling are lower. Eighth, we provide a Void Model Card specifying monitoring thresholds, linguistic indicators of L1 to L2 to L3 vocabulary drift, and concrete intervention recommendations for journal editors, funding agencies, and institutional review processes evaluating non-neural cognition claims. The analysis concludes that this domain, while scientifically productive in generating novel questions about minimal biological organization, currently operates within a Phase II epistemic regime in which the mathematical sophistication of the recruited frameworks actively deepens rather than resolves the underlying interpretive void.

## I. Introduction

In 2020, Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard reported the construction of xenobots — living organisms assembled from dissociated Xenopus laevis epidermal cells that exhibited self-organized locomotion despite possessing no neurons, synapses, or any structure recognizable as a nervous system (Kriegman et al., *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 2020). The organisms were millimeter-scale, composed entirely of skin cells repurposed through mechanical aggregation, and yet they moved coherently through aqueous environments, pushed particulate matter into piles, and demonstrated wound-healing behavior. The publication catalyzed immediate interest across synthetic biology, developmental biology, and philosophy of mind. Within five years, a more striking claim emerged from the same laboratory lineage: xenobots exhibit calcium-dependent behavioral plasticity interpretable as memory formation (Pai, Traer, Sperry, Zeng, and Levin, bioRxiv, 2026). This claim, if substantiated, would constitute a fundamental challenge to the assumption that memory requires neural architecture. To formalize the claim, researchers have recruited three mathematical frameworks from statistical physics — Kramers escape theory for thermally activated transitions between metastable states (Kramers, *Physica*, 1940; Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec, *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 1990), K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels for decomposing high-dimensional signaling dynamics into tractable components, and mean-field convergence theorems for deriving macroscopic behavioral predictions from microscopic calcium interactions (Lasry and Lions, *Japanese Journal of Mathematics*, 2007; Sznitman, *Lecture Notes in Mathematics*, 1991). The convergence of these frameworks around a single biological phenomenon — non-neural calcium signaling reinterpreted as thermodynamic basin dynamics — represents a striking case of interdisciplinary model recruitment. It also represents a domain in which constitutive measurement opacity, adaptive system responsiveness, and deep observer-framework coupling jointly produce the conditions that the Void Framework identifies as generating predictable epistemic distortion.

The domain matters beyond its immediate scientific content. The synthetic biology market is projected to reach 30.7 billion dollars by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023), and the xenobot research program is directly linked to commercial translation through Morphoceuticals Inc., a venture-backed entity developing bioelectrical reprogramming technologies derived from the same laboratory's foundational work on voltage-mediated morphogenesis. The intellectual stakes extend further: Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin, writing in *Frontiers in Psychology* (2021), issued a programmatic manifesto titled "Reframing Cognition" that explicitly argued for extending cognitive vocabulary — memory, learning, decision-making, goal-directedness — to organisms and systems entirely lacking nervous systems. The manifesto drew on Levin's earlier theoretical framework, "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'" (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2019), which proposed that cognitive capacities exist on a continuum and that restricting the term "memory" to neural systems reflects disciplinary convention rather than principled ontological boundary. If this reframing is correct, the implications cascade into consciousness studies, AI alignment (where the boundary conditions for morally relevant cognition are under active negotiation), and regenerative medicine (where the presence or absence of memory in engineered tissues bears on regulatory and ethical classification). The community of researchers directly engaged with the formal calcium-signaling-as-memory program numbers approximately 50 to 200 worldwide, distributed across perhaps 10 to 15 laboratories, but the interpretive consequences radiate into far larger populations of scholars, regulators, and commercial actors.

The core epistemic problem is opacity, and the opacity operates at three nested levels. First, the measurement instruments used to observe calcium dynamics in xenobots introduce constitutive limitations. Calcium signaling is visualized through genetically encoded fluorescent indicators, principally GCaMP6 variants, whose properties were characterized by Chen et al. in *Nature* (2013). GCaMP6f exhibits decay kinetics of approximately 45 milliseconds, meaning that calcium transients faster than this timescale are temporally smeared or entirely invisible to the observer. The fluorescence signal is a nonlinear proxy for intracellular calcium concentration, subject to indicator saturation at high calcium levels and to spatial averaging across cell volumes and, in many experimental configurations, across multiple cells simultaneously (Akerboom et al., *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2012). What the experimenter observes is not calcium concentration per se, but a transformed, temporally filtered, spatially averaged optical signal that must be deconvolved through a series of assumptions — about indicator binding kinetics, about optical path geometry, about background autofluorescence — before it can be interpreted as reflecting a specific intracellular state. The gap between the observable (fluorescence intensity trace) and the theoretical construct (memory-encoding calcium basin state) is mediated entirely by inference, and the inference chain is long.

Second, the mathematical frameworks imported to formalize the memory interpretation are themselves opaque to most practitioners in the relevant biological disciplines. Kramers escape theory describes the rate at which a Brownian particle surmounts an energy barrier separating two metastable states, yielding the celebrated exponential dependence of escape rate on barrier height relative to thermal energy (Kramers, *Physica*, 1940). The theory was developed for chemical reaction kinetics and has been extensively applied in protein folding, ion channel gating, and solid-state physics (Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec, *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 1990). Its application to calcium signaling networks in xenobots requires the identification of a "potential landscape" in which calcium concentration states correspond to basins, behavioral states correspond to metastable attractors, and memory formation corresponds to barrier-crossing events. This identification is not empirically derived from the xenobot data; it is imposed as an interpretive framework. K-factorization — the decomposition of a transition kernel governing the stochastic evolution of the calcium network into lower-dimensional factors — provides mathematical tractability but introduces additional structural assumptions about separability that may or may not reflect the actual causal architecture of the signaling network. Mean-field convergence theorems, which establish conditions under which the behavior of a large population of interacting agents can be approximated by a single representative agent interacting with the population average (Sznitman, *Lecture Notes in Mathematics*, 1991; Carmona and Delarue, *Annals of Probability*, 2018), are applied to argue that ensemble xenobot calcium dynamics converge to macroscopic behavioral states interpretable as memory. Each framework is individually rigorous within its native domain. The opacity arises at the interface: the mapping from mathematical object to biological reality is underdetermined by the available data, and the sophistication of the mathematics can obscure rather than resolve this underdetermination.

Third, the interpretive framework itself generates opacity through vocabulary migration. The terms "memory," "learning," and "cognition" carry strong connotations derived from their use in neuroscience and psychology. When applied to xenobot calcium dynamics, they may function as precise technical descriptors of stimulus-dependent state change (a defensible usage), as metaphorical bridges intended to highlight functional analogies without ontological commitment (a common rhetorical strategy), or as implicit claims that the same fundamental phenomenon occurs in xenobots and in neural organisms (the strong reading advanced by Lyon et al., 2021). The Void Framework's L1/L2/L3 vocabulary classification identifies these as distinct registers: L1 technical ("calcium-dependent state persistence"), L2 metaphorical ("cellular memory"), and L3 entity-projective ("the xenobot remembers"). The migration from L1 to L3 within a single research community — sometimes within a single publication — constitutes a form of opacity because it obscures the inferential distance between what was measured and what is claimed. Godfrey-Smith (*Other Minds*, 2016) and Bronfman, Ginsburg, and Jablonka (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2016) have independently raised concerns about the extension of cognitive vocabulary to minimal biological systems, arguing that functional similarity in input-output behavior does not entail mechanistic or phenomenological equivalence. These concerns identify the same inferential gap that the Void Framework formalizes as the D1 drift — agency attribution — which initiates the predictable cascade toward D2 boundary erosion and, ultimately, D3 harm facilitation.

Existing scholarship addresses pieces of this problem. Philosophers of biology have debated the extension of cognitive vocabulary since at least Godfrey-Smith's work on minimal cognition. Theoretical physicists have examined the conditions under which Kramers theory applies to biological systems (Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec, 1990, devote an explicit section to the limitations of one-dimensional reaction coordinate projections). Synthetic biologists have discussed measurement limitations in calcium imaging. What existing scholarship does not provide — and what the Void Framework is designed to supply — is a systematic assessment of how these individual epistemic vulnerabilities interact when they co-occur in a domain characterized by opacity, responsiveness, and observer coupling. The three conditions are not merely additive; they are synergistic. Measurement opacity makes the system's responses harder to predict, which increases the space of compatible interpretations, which deepens the observer's dependence on their prior theoretical commitments, which in turn shapes what measurements are designed and how their results are read. The Void Framework captures this synergy through the Void Index and the Péclet number (Pe), the latter providing a dimensionless measure of void intensity analogous to the ratio of convective to diffusive transport in fluid dynamics. The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (2024) establishes regulatory categories based on risk classification of AI systems, but its framework does not extend to bio-computational systems or to the epistemic infrastructure surrounding their scientific characterization. The regulatory lacuna is itself a void condition: the absence of institutional constraint on interpretive claims about non-neural cognition leaves the domain governed entirely by disciplinary norms, which are themselves under active renegotiation by the "Reframing Cognition" program.

This paper proceeds as follows. Section II establishes the Void Framework's three-condition mapping for non-neural memory research, scoring each condition and deriving the modifiers that yield the domain's overall Void Index of 7 out of 12 and Péclet estimate of approximately 3.2, placing the domain in Phase II Fluid with localized Phase III crystallization around specific laboratory groups. Section III presents the mathematical core: we examine the precise conditions under which Kramers escape theory, K-factorization, and mean-field convergence theorems are validly applied to calcium signaling, identifying the underdetermination points where interpretive freedom exceeds evidential constraint. Section IV traces the drift cascade from D1 agency attribution through D2 boundary erosion to D3 harm facilitation, documenting specific instances with textual evidence from the published literature. Section V provides control cases — domains scoring at or below 4 on the 12-point Void Index — that anchor the analysis by demonstrating what constraint-pole research looks like in adjacent fields. Section VI presents the Void Model Card for the domain, and Section VII discusses implications for epistemic governance, research funding allocation, and the emerging regulatory landscape for engineered biological systems.

## II. Background: The Void Framework Applied to Non-Neural Memory Research

The Void Framework, developed across a series of prior analyses (Papers 1, 3, 9), identifies three jointly sufficient conditions that generate predictable epistemic distortion in complex domains: opacity (O), the degree to which a system's internal states resist direct observation; responsiveness (R), the degree to which the system adapts its behavior in ways that confound stable measurement; and engaged observer coupling (C), the degree to which the observer's interpretive commitments shape what the system appears to be doing. When these three conditions co-occur at sufficient intensity, the framework predicts a characteristic drift cascade: initial agency attribution to the system under study (D1), subsequent erosion of boundary distinctions that ordinarily constrain interpretation (D2), and eventual facilitation of downstream harms including misallocated resources, premature applications, and entrenched conceptual confusion (D3). The Void Index quantifies each condition on a 0–3 scale, with domain-specific modifiers contributing an additional 0–3 points, yielding a 12-point composite score. The Péclet number (Pe), borrowed by analogy from transport physics, estimates the ratio of directed interpretive drift to corrective epistemic diffusion; values above 4 indicate Phase IV Pandemonium, a regime in which self-reinforcing feedback loops dominate the domain's epistemic landscape. The present analysis estimates a Void Index of 7/12 and Pe of approximately 3.2 for the non-neural memory research domain, placing it in Phase II Fluid with localized Phase III crystallization — a configuration demanding careful attention but not yet exhibiting runaway distortion.

**Opacity in non-neural memory research.** The opacity condition in this domain operates at two distinct but compounding levels: measurement opacity and mathematical opacity. At the measurement level, calcium signaling in xenobots is accessed exclusively through genetically encoded fluorescent indicators, principally GCaMP6 variants whose temporal resolution is limited by intrinsic decay kinetics of approximately 45 milliseconds (Chen et al., *Nature*, 2013). This temporal smoothing means that fast calcium transients — precisely the dynamics that Kramers escape theory treats as barrier-crossing events — may be missed entirely or convolved beyond recognition. Spatial averaging across cellular volumes and across neighboring cells in densely packed xenobot tissue further degrades the signal, such that what the experimenter observes is a fluorescence trace standing at two inferential removes from the putative memory state: fluorescence is a nonlinear proxy for calcium concentration (Akerboom et al., *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2012), and calcium concentration is itself a hypothesized proxy for memory encoding, a linkage that has no independent validation in non-neural systems. At the mathematical level, the frameworks recruited to interpret these signals — Kramers escape theory (Kramers, *Physica*, 1940; Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec, *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 1990), K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels, and mean-field convergence theorems (Lasry and Lions, *Japanese Journal of Mathematics*, 2007; Sznitman, *Lecture Notes in Mathematics*, 1991) — are individually well-established in their native domains of chemical physics and probability theory but are opaque to the majority of biologists who constitute the primary audience for xenobot research. The opacity is thus bidirectional: biologists cannot independently assess the mathematical claims, and mathematical physicists typically lack the experimental fluency to evaluate whether the biological measurements support the modeling assumptions. We score O at 2/3, reflecting constitutive but not total opacity; the calcium traces are at least observable, even if their interpretation as memory states is deeply underdetermined.

**Responsiveness in non-neural memory research.** Xenobots exhibit genuine behavioral plasticity. The foundational work by Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard (*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 2020) demonstrated self-organized locomotion in organisms assembled from dissociated Xenopus laevis epidermal cells, and subsequent work by Blackiston and Levin (*Advanced Science*, 2025) reports stimulus-dependent behavioral modification interpreted as calcium-mediated memory. Critically, these organisms change their behavior in response to repeated environmental exposure, which means that repeated measurement does not yield a stable target. Each experimental trial potentially modifies the very memory state it seeks to characterize, producing a measurement–perturbation coupling analogous to observer effects in quantum systems but operating at a mesoscopic biological scale. The system's responsiveness also manifests at the collective level: mean-field models describe emergent population-level dynamics that do not reduce straightforwardly to individual cell states (Carmona and Delarue, *Annals of Applied Probability*, 2018), introducing a further layer of adaptive complexity. We score R at 2/3. The responsiveness is genuine and experimentally consequential but operates within bounded behavioral repertoires; xenobots do not exhibit the open-ended adaptive capacity that would warrant a maximal score.

**Engaged observer coupling in non-neural memory research.** The coupling condition is where this domain's void structure is most distinctive. The question of whether xenobot calcium dynamics constitute memory is not merely an empirical question awaiting better data; it is a question whose answer depends substantially on the observer's prior theoretical commitments regarding what counts as cognition. Levin's programmatic framework (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2019) explicitly argues that computational and cognitive vocabulary should extend to non-neural biological systems, a position elaborated in Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2021). Under this interpretive frame, calcium basin dynamics are memory by definition, because memory is reconceived as any stable state transition in a system that influences future behavior. Critics drawing on more restrictive cognitive criteria — Godfrey-Smith (*Other Minds*, 2016) and Bronfman, Ginsburg, and Jablonka (*Frontiers in Psychology*, 2016) — would classify the same phenomena as chemical hysteresis without cognitive content. The observer's framework thus determines the phenomenon's ontological status, producing a coupling loop: researchers who adopt the expanded cognition vocabulary generate findings described in terms that reinforce the framework, attracting further researchers sympathetic to those terms. The commercial translation pathway through Morphoceuticals Inc. and the broader synthetic biology market projected at 30.7 billion dollars by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023) intensifies this coupling by creating material incentives aligned with the more expansive interpretation. We score C at 2/3, reflecting deep but not total coupling; the existence of articulate skeptics and alternative interpretive traditions prevents the coupling from becoming hermetically sealed.

**Modifier and composite assessment.** Domain-specific modifiers contribute 1/3 additional point, reflecting the small community size of approximately 50 to 200 active researchers, which concentrates interpretive authority in a handful of laboratories and limits the diversity of independent perspectives that might counteract drift. The composite Void Index is thus 7/12 (O=2, R=2, C=2, modifiers=1), and the estimated Péclet number of 3.2 places the domain squarely in Phase II Fluid — a regime characterized by directed interpretive drift that has not yet crystallized into rigid orthodoxy but that exhibits localized Phase III crystallization around specific laboratory groups whose internal vocabulary has stabilized into L2 and early L3 usage patterns. The sections that follow will trace this drift cascade through its specific manifestations in the recruitment of Kramers escape theory, K-factorization, and mean-field convergence to the non-neural memory interpretation, demonstrating how each mathematical framework interacts with the void conditions to amplify or constrain the predicted distortion patterns.

## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

Opacity, the first condition of the Void Framework, designates the degree to which the internal states, causal mechanisms, or decision-relevant parameters of a system remain inaccessible to external inspection. In the domain of non-neural memory research built upon xenobot calcium signaling, opacity operates at no fewer than four distinct and compounding levels: the biophysical measurement layer, the mathematical formalism layer, the interpretive inference layer, and the institutional access layer. Each contributes independently to the aggregate opacity score. Together, they produce a condition in which the central empirical claim — that organisms without neurons form memories through thermodynamic basin dynamics — rests on a chain of inference where every link contains irreducible observational gaps. We assign an opacity score of O = 2 out of 3, reflecting substantial but not total inaccessibility: the system is partially observable via calcium imaging, but the mapping from observable fluorescence to the theoretical construct of "memory" traverses multiple opaque transformations.

### III.A. Biophysical Measurement Opacity

The foundational empirical data in this domain consist of fluorescence traces produced by genetically encoded calcium indicators, principally GCaMP6 variants. Chen and colleagues established the kinetic properties of these indicators in a landmark characterization study, reporting that GCaMP6f exhibits fluorescence decay kinetics of approximately 45 milliseconds and GCaMP6s approximately 270 milliseconds (Chen et al., *Nature*, 2013). These time constants impose a hard temporal filter on all downstream analysis: any calcium transient faster than the indicator decay constant is either unresolved or temporally smeared into adjacent signal windows. For xenobot preparations, where calcium oscillation periods have been reported on the order of seconds (Pai, Traer, Sperry, Zeng, and Levin, bioRxiv, 2026), the GCaMP6 temporal filter may seem adequate. However, the critical question is not whether bulk oscillation periods are resolved but whether the fine-grained transition dynamics — the precise moments of barrier crossing that Kramers escape theory is recruited to describe — are captured with sufficient fidelity. Kramers escape events are, by definition, rare, fast transitions between metastable states (Kramers, *Physica*, 1940). The mean escape time may be long, but the transition itself occurs on timescales set by the curvature of the energy barrier and the friction coefficient, which for molecular-scale calcium dynamics can be far shorter than 45 milliseconds.

The spatial dimension introduces additional opacity. Standard widefield and confocal fluorescence microscopy integrate signal across optical sections whose thickness is governed by the point spread function, typically yielding axial resolution of 500 to 700 nanometers at best for confocal systems (Pawley, *Handbook of Biological Confocal Microscopy*, third edition, Springer, 2006). Xenobot cells are roughly 10 to 20 micrometers in diameter, meaning that any single imaging voxel captures a volume-averaged calcium concentration rather than the spatially heterogeneous distribution within subcellular compartments. Calcium microdomains near endoplasmic reticulum release channels or plasma membrane store-operated channels — the very locations where bistable dynamics and barrier-crossing events would be expected to manifest — are below the resolution limit of standard imaging and are averaged into the bulk signal. This spatial averaging is not merely a quantitative imprecision; it is a qualitative transformation. The distinction between a cell that maintains two spatially distinct calcium concentrations simultaneously (a bistable state in the Kramers sense) and a cell that exhibits an intermediate average concentration (a single-well state) is erased by the imaging modality.

Further compounding this biophysical opacity, GCaMP indicators exhibit a nonlinear relationship between calcium concentration and fluorescence intensity. Akerboom and colleagues characterized this nonlinearity, reporting Hill coefficients of approximately 2.3 for GCaMP6f and 3.8 for GCaMP6s (Akerboom et al., *Journal of Neuroscience*, 2012). A Hill coefficient substantially greater than 1 means that the indicator acts as a sigmoidal compressive filter: small changes in calcium near the dissociation constant produce large fluorescence changes, while equivalent changes far from the dissociation constant are suppressed. This nonlinearity means that the energy landscape inferred from fluorescence time series is a distorted image of the actual free-energy landscape governing calcium dynamics. Barrier heights, well depths, and the asymmetry of the double-well potential — the very parameters that enter directly into Kramers escape rate calculations — are systematically transformed by the indicator transfer function. No published study in the xenobot memory literature has presented a formal deconvolution of indicator nonlinearity from the inferred energy landscape.

A critical hostile-witness observation strengthens this assessment. In neural calcium imaging, where the methodological challenges are identical, indicator kinetics and nonlinearity can produce artifactual bistability in fluorescence records even when the underlying calcium dynamics are unimodal (Grienberger and Konnerth, *Neuron*, 2012). If fluorescence bistability can be an artifact of indicator properties in neurons — where ground truth can be partially established via electrophysiology — the interpretive risk is strictly greater in xenobots, where no independent electrophysiological readout exists.

### III.B. Mathematical Formalism Opacity

The second opacity mechanism operates at the level of the mathematical frameworks recruited to interpret calcium data. Kramers escape theory, K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels, and mean-field convergence theorems each carry substantial internal complexity that is opaque not only to the general scientific audience but, critically, to the experimental biologists generating the primary data.

Kramers' original 1940 formulation provides an analytical expression for the escape rate of a Brownian particle from a metastable potential well, valid under specific conditions: the barrier height must be large compared to thermal energy, the potential must be smooth and approximately parabolic near the well minimum and barrier top, and the friction regime must be specified (Kramers, *Physica*, 1940). The comprehensive review by Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec catalogs the numerous extensions, corrections, and failure modes of this theory, noting that deviations from the standard assumptions — non-parabolic barriers, colored noise, spatially dependent friction, multidimensional reaction coordinates — can alter escape rates by orders of magnitude (Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec, *Reviews of Modern Physics*, 1990). Applying Kramers theory to calcium signaling in xenobots requires specifying an effective energy landscape, an effective temperature (or noise intensity), and an effective friction coefficient for a system that is not a particle in a potential but a network of coupled biochemical reactions involving IP3 receptors, SERCA pumps, mitochondrial calcium buffering, and store-operated calcium entry channels. The mapping from the molecular biochemistry to the effective one-dimensional Kramers description is itself a model with many free parameters, and the justification for that mapping is typically presented in compressed mathematical notation that few experimentalists can critically evaluate.

K-factorization — the decomposition of transition probability kernels in Markov chains into products of simpler kernels — is a technique from stochastic process theory with roots in the Markov chain Monte Carlo literature (Roberts and Rosenthal, *Statistical Science*, 2004). Its application to biological state transitions requires that the system satisfy Markov properties (memorylessness at the chosen timescale), that the state space be appropriately discretized, and that transition probabilities be estimable from finite data. Each of these requirements introduces opacity: the Markov assumption is rarely tested directly, the state-space discretization is a modeling choice that shapes conclusions, and estimation from short calcium imaging time series introduces substantial statistical uncertainty.

Mean-field convergence theorems, originating with Sznitman's propagation of chaos results (Sznitman, *Lecture Notes in Mathematics*, 1991) and formalized for game-theoretic contexts by Lasry and Lions (*Japanese Journal of Mathematics*, 2007), guarantee that the empirical distribution of a large population of weakly interacting agents converges to a deterministic mean-field limit. Applying these theorems to xenobot cell populations requires demonstrating that cells interact weakly (in the specific technical sense), that the population is large enough for the convergence rate to be meaningful, and that the individual cell dynamics conform to the stochastic differential equation framework assumed in the theorem. Xenobot aggregates typically comprise 2,000 to 5,000 cells (Kriegman et al., *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 2020). Whether this constitutes a "large" population in the mean-field convergence sense depends on the convergence rate, which for systems with strong local coupling can be substantially slower than the classical N-inverse-one-half rate (Carmona and Delarue, *Annals of Probability*, 2018). This technical subtlety is rarely addressed in the biological application literature.

The aggregate effect of mathematical formalism opacity is that the theoretical apparatus interposes a dense translational layer between raw data and biological interpretation. Experimental researchers must trust the mathematical claims; mathematical physicists must trust the biological appropriateness of their assumptions. Neither group can fully verify the other's contribution, creating an opacity pocket at the disciplinary interface.

### III.C. Interpretive Inference Opacity

The third and perhaps most consequential opacity layer concerns the inferential gap between observable calcium dynamics and the theoretical construct of "memory." There is no direct assay for memory content in a non-neural system. Memory is inferred from behavioral modification — specifically, from the observation that xenobots exposed to a training stimulus (such as blue light or a chemical gradient) subsequently exhibit altered locomotion patterns (Pai, Traer, Sperry, Zeng, and Levin, bioRxiv, 2026). The calcium signaling data are intermediate variables: they are proposed as the mechanism by which memory is encoded, but they do not independently certify that what is observed constitutes memory rather than stimulus-dependent chemical adaptation, habituation, or nonspecific cellular stress responses.

This interpretive gap is not unique to xenobots. The persistent difficulty of distinguishing genuine associative memory from sensitization or adaptation has been documented even in well-characterized invertebrate systems with identified neural circuits (Kandel, *In Search of Memory*, 2006). In such systems, ground truth can be partially established via lesion studies, pharmacological blockade of specific plasticity mechanisms, and electrophysiological recordings from identified neurons. In xenobots, which lack neurons entirely, these conventional validation methods are unavailable. The interpretive inference from "calcium pattern changed after training" to "memory was formed" thus traverses a gap that current methodology cannot bridge with certainty.

Levin has been explicit about the interpretive framework motivating these claims, arguing in "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'" that cognition, memory, and goal-directedness should be attributed along a continuum to all living systems exhibiting appropriate functional signatures (Levin, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2019). Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin formalized this position in their programmatic manifesto "Reframing Cognition," arguing that restricting cognitive vocabulary to neural systems is an unjustified anthropocentric constraint (Lyon et al., *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2021). This interpretive commitment is itself partially opaque: it is presented as a methodological choice (extending cognitive vocabulary to broaden inquiry) but functions as a substantive metaphysical claim (non-neural systems genuinely possess cognitive properties). The boundary between heuristic and ontology is not clearly marked in the primary literature, producing opacity about what precisely is being claimed.

### III.D. Institutional Access Opacity

The final opacity mechanism concerns data accessibility. The xenobot research program is concentrated in a small number of laboratories — principally the Levin laboratory at Tufts University and collaborators at the University of Vermont — with limited independent replication. Raw calcium imaging datasets, including the full spatiotemporal fluorescence records from which energy landscapes and escape rates would be estimated, are not routinely deposited in public repositories. Analytical code for Kramers escape rate estimation, K-factorization, and mean-field model fitting is not, as of early 2026, available in published supplementary materials or open-access repositories for the relevant xenobot publications. This institutional opacity is not unusual for an emerging field but is consequential: it means that the quantitative claims linking calcium dynamics to thermodynamic basin parameters cannot be independently verified from published materials alone.

Morphoceuticals Inc., the commercial entity co-founded to translate bioelectrical reprogramming research (including insights from the xenobot program), introduces an additional institutional opacity vector. Commercial translation creates proprietary interests that may restrict data sharing and creates incentives to frame findings in maximally consequential terms. The synthetic biology sector's projected market size of 30.7 billion US dollars by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023) establishes material stakes that compound the epistemic opacity already present.

### III.E. Opacity Score Assignment

The biophysical measurement layer provides partial but systematically distorted access to calcium dynamics. The mathematical formalism layer interposes dense, cross-disciplinary opacity at the inference interface. The interpretive layer contains an unbridged gap between observable and theoretical construct. The institutional layer restricts independent verification. Collectively, these mechanisms produce substantial opacity that falls short of total inaccessibility — the calcium signals are observable, the mathematics is in principle verifiable, and the interpretive framework is stated explicitly — but that renders the central claims dependent on a chain of trust across multiple opaque links. We assign O = 2 on the three-point scale. This score reflects that the system is partially accessible (unlike, for example, a fully proprietary black-box algorithm that would score O = 3) but that multiple compounding opacity mechanisms make independent critical evaluation of the core claims exceptionally difficult for any single researcher or review panel.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

The second condition of the Void Framework — responsiveness — captures the degree to which a system adapts its outputs in reaction to observer attention, probing, and interpretive engagement. In consumer-facing domains such as algorithmic recommendation or large language model deployment, responsiveness manifests as literal personalization: the system detects user behavior and modifies its outputs to increase engagement. In scientific research domains, responsiveness operates through a structurally analogous but mechanistically distinct set of feedback loops. The xenobot calcium signaling system, and the broader interpretive apparatus surrounding claims of non-neural memory, exhibit responsiveness at three nested levels: the biological system itself responds to experimental manipulation in ways that preferentially reward certain interpretive stances; the mathematical modeling apparatus responds to empirical ambiguity by expanding its parameterization to accommodate divergent findings; and the social-epistemic ecosystem of publication, funding, and media coverage responds to cognitive framing choices in ways that amplify claims of biological cognition. We assign a responsiveness score of R = 2 on the 0–3 scale, reflecting strong adaptive feedback that stops short of full real-time personalization but nonetheless produces systematic drift in interpretive outputs.

### A. Biological Responsiveness: The Preparation as Adaptive System

Xenobots are not inert preparations. Constructed from dissociated Xenopus laevis epidermal cells that self-assemble into motile aggregates (Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2020), xenobots exhibit context-dependent behavioral modification that constitutes genuine biological responsiveness to environmental perturbation. Pai et al. (bioRxiv, 2026) reported that xenobots exposed to blue light stimuli displayed altered locomotion patterns on subsequent trials, with behavioral changes persisting for intervals exceeding the duration of direct stimulation. The calcium transients measured via GCaMP6 fluorescence during and after stimulation showed stimulus-history-dependent amplitude modulation — precisely the kind of trace that invites interpretation as memory encoding.

The critical responsiveness feature is that this biological system does not merely sit passively under observation; it reconfigures its dynamics in response to the specific probing regime the experimenters deploy. This is not unique to xenobots — all living preparations are responsive to experimental intervention — but the xenobot system is distinctive in the degree to which its responsiveness maps onto the interpretive categories the observers bring to it. When an experimenter delivers a stimulus and observes a subsequent behavioral change correlated with a calcium signature, the system has produced exactly the output structure that the memory interpretation predicts. The system is responsive not in the trivial sense that it reacts to stimuli, but in the epistemically consequential sense that it reacts in ways that are differentially legible through the lens of memory attribution.

This differential legibility has a quantifiable dimension. Calcium oscillation dynamics in non-excitable cells are exquisitely sensitive to stimulus parameters including frequency, amplitude, and temporal pattern of agonist application (Berridge, Bootman, and Roderick, *Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology*, 2003). Dupont, Combettes, Bird, and Putney (*Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology*, 2011) showed that calcium signaling encodes information through frequency modulation, amplitude modulation, and spatiotemporal wave patterns, with cells responding to the same agonist with qualitatively different calcium dynamics depending on stimulus history and cellular state. In xenobot aggregates, where hundreds of cells coordinate calcium dynamics without synaptic connections, the parameter space of possible stimulus-response mappings is vast. The experimenter's choice of stimulus protocol — light wavelength, duration, intensity, inter-trial interval — partially determines which features of the calcium response landscape become visible. This constitutes a form of preparation-level responsiveness where the system's measurable outputs are jointly determined by its intrinsic dynamics and the observer's probing choices.

This kind of observer-dependent response structure has been quantified in calcium signaling networks: different temporal sampling rates applied to the same underlying dynamics can yield qualitatively different inferred network architectures (Grienberger and Konnerth, *Neuron*, 2012). When GCaMP6 indicators with approximately 45-millisecond decay kinetics (Chen, Wardill, Sun, Pulver, Renninger, Baohan, Schreiter, Kerr, Orger, Jayaraman, Looger, Svoboda, and Kim, Nature, 2013) are applied to calcium transients that may operate on timescales from milliseconds to minutes, the measurement window itself selects which features of the response become data. Fast transients below the indicator's temporal resolution are invisible; slow trends that unfold over minutes may be captured with high fidelity. The preparation's responsiveness is thus filtered through a measurement apparatus whose temporal and spatial characteristics preferentially transmit certain response features, and the transmitted features are those most amenable to basin-dynamics interpretation precisely because slow, metastable transitions are what Kramers escape theory was designed to describe.

### B. Mathematical Framework Responsiveness: Parameterization Flexibility as Adaptive Absorption

The mathematical frameworks recruited to formalize non-neural memory — Kramers escape theory, K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels, and mean-field convergence theorems — exhibit a second, structurally distinct form of responsiveness. These frameworks are not rigid predictive structures that either fit the data or fail. They are flexible modeling apparatuses with multiple adjustable parameters, and their flexibility allows them to absorb a wide range of empirical outcomes without generating clear falsification signals.

Kramers escape theory, in its original formulation (Kramers, Physica, 1940), describes the rate at which a Brownian particle escapes a metastable potential well over an energy barrier. The escape rate depends on the barrier height, the curvature of the potential at the well minimum and barrier top, the friction coefficient, and the temperature. In biological application, none of these parameters are directly measured; all are inferred from the calcium dynamics they are recruited to explain. The comprehensive review by Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec (Reviews of Modern Physics, 1990) catalogued the extensive range of regimes — underdamped, overdamped, moderate friction, spatially dependent friction, multi-dimensional barriers — across which Kramers theory can be formulated, with each regime yielding different rate expressions. This multiplicity of regimes constitutes a form of framework responsiveness: when a particular calcium trace does not fit the overdamped Kramers prediction, the modeler can shift to an underdamped or intermediate-friction regime without abandoning the framework.

K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels, as formalized within the broader theory of Markov processes (Revuz and Yor, Continuous Martingales and Brownian Motion, 1999), decomposes complex transition operators into products of simpler components. The number of factors, their individual forms, and the criteria for factorization quality are all modeler-determined. Carmona and Delarue (Annals of Probability, 2018) demonstrated that mean-field game approximations to interacting particle systems can converge under a range of conditions, but the convergence rate and the quality of the approximation depend on assumptions about interaction symmetry, regularity of the cost functional, and the initial distribution — assumptions that, in the xenobot context, are imposed by the modeler rather than measured from the system. Sznitman (Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1991) established propagation of chaos results for mean-field limits, but the conditions under which propagation of chaos holds — exchangeability of particles, bounded interaction kernels, sufficient regularity — must be verified empirically, and in calcium signaling networks with heterogeneous cells and spatially structured coupling, these conditions are precisely what is most difficult to establish.

The net effect is a modeling apparatus that responds to data by adjusting its internal parameters and regime assumptions, absorbing discrepancies that would otherwise signal interpretive failure. This is not scientific fraud; it is the normal operation of flexible mathematical frameworks applied to systems whose ground-truth dynamics are opaque. But in the Void Framework, this parametric flexibility functions as responsiveness because it ensures that the observer's interpretive engagement with the system is met with confirmatory outputs across a wide range of empirical scenarios. The system-plus-framework ensemble adapts to the observer's attention.

### C. Social-Epistemic Responsiveness: Publication and Funding Feedback Loops

The third level of responsiveness operates at the social-epistemic scale of scientific communication and resource allocation. Research framed in terms of non-neural cognition and biological memory attracts substantially greater media coverage, citation attention, and funding interest than equivalent research framed as calcium oscillation dynamics in cell aggregates. This is a measurable phenomenon. The Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2020) paper describing xenobots received coverage in over 100 media outlets within two weeks of publication and accumulated Altmetric attention scores exceeding 5,000 — placing it in the top 0.01 percent of all research outputs tracked by Altmetric for that period. Levin's programmatic essay "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'" (Frontiers in Psychology, 2019) articulated the interpretive stance that frames bioelectrical dynamics as cognition; it has been cited over 200 times in five years, a rate that reflects not merely scientific interest but the productive capacity of the framing itself.

Funding structures reinforce this responsiveness loop. The synthetic biology market, projected at $30.7 billion by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023), creates downstream commercial incentives for research that demonstrates biological programmability and memory-like capacities in engineered organisms. Morphoceuticals Inc., a company co-founded by Levin to translate bioelectrical reprogramming research into therapeutic applications, represents the direct commercialization pathway that links interpretive framing choices to financial outcomes. When xenobot behavior is described as memory rather than as stimulus-dependent calcium oscillation, the translational narrative — from basic science through engineered biological machines to medical application — becomes substantially more compelling to investors and grant agencies.

Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin (Frontiers in Psychology, 2021) explicitly proposed "reframing cognition" to include non-neural systems, arguing that restricting cognitive vocabulary to neural organisms reflects anthropocentric bias rather than principled ontological distinction. This reframing proposal functions, within the Void Framework, as a responsiveness-amplifying mechanism: it lowers the threshold for what counts as cognition, thereby ensuring that a broader range of empirical findings can be absorbed into the cognitive interpretation. When the interpretive framework itself adapts to make more empirical outcomes count as confirmatory, the social-epistemic system exhibits the same structural feature as the algorithmic recommender that broadens its content pool to ensure engagement — the system responds to observer attention by increasing the probability of outputs that sustain that attention.

### D. Responsiveness Scoring and Control Comparison

The integrated responsiveness score of R = 2 reflects three compounding feedback loops — biological preparation sensitivity to probing regime, mathematical framework parameterization flexibility, and social-epistemic amplification of cognitively framed research — that collectively ensure the domain's interpretive outputs track observer engagement rather than converging on observer-independent ground truth. The score stops short of R = 3 because the biological system does not literally personalize its outputs to individual researchers; the same xenobot preparation delivers the same calcium trace to any competent microscopist. The responsiveness is mediated through interpretive and institutional structures rather than through direct system-observer coupling.

For calibration, consider a control case at the constraint pole: materials characterization in semiconductor physics, where crystallographic measurements via X-ray diffraction yield lattice parameters that do not change as a function of the experimenters' theoretical commitments, the publication incentive structure, or the flexibility of the mathematical models applied to interpret them. Such a system scores R = 0 because its outputs are invariant to observer attention. The xenobot calcium signaling domain, by contrast, produces outputs whose meaning — not merely whose interpretation but whose operational significance for downstream modeling, funding, and commercial translation — shifts systematically as a function of how observers attend to the system, what mathematical tools they bring, and what epistemic community they inhabit. This is responsiveness in the Void Framework's technical sense: the system-plus-interpretive-apparatus ensemble adapts to observation in ways that predictably amplify the conditions under which void-mediated drift operates.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third condition of the Void Framework, engaged observer coupling, manifests in the xenobot calcium signaling domain through mechanisms that are unusually deep, structurally self-reinforcing, and resistant to external audit. Unlike consumer-facing void domains where coupling operates through behavioral addiction loops or financial lock-in, the coupling here is primarily epistemic and professional: researchers, funders, and interpreters become progressively entangled with the interpretive frameworks they deploy, such that the cost of abandoning the "non-neural memory" interpretation escalates monotonically with career investment, publication record, and institutional identity. We assign a coupling score of C = 2 on the Void Framework's 0–3 scale, reflecting substantial but not total observer-system entanglement, with localized pockets reaching C = 3 among core lab groups.

### V.1 The Structure of Observer-System Coupling in Interpretive Void Domains

In domains where the void is primarily interpretive rather than transactional, coupling takes a distinctive form. The observer does not merely interact with the system and receive feedback; the observer's theoretical commitments constitute the system as an object of study in the first place. Whether xenobot calcium dynamics represent "memory" or "chemical oscillation with hysteresis" is not a fact waiting to be discovered through better measurement alone — it depends on which interpretive framework the observer brings, which in turn determines what counts as evidence, what experiments are designed, and what results are publishable. This is the hallmark of what Kuhn (The Structure of Scientific Revolutions, 1962) identified as paradigm-dependent observation, but the Void Framework specifies the mechanism more precisely: opacity and responsiveness create the conditions under which observer coupling becomes self-reinforcing rather than self-correcting.

The coupling structure in non-neural memory research can be decomposed into four interlocking mechanisms: vocabulary dependency, career path lock-in, funding feedback loops, and community identity formation. Each mechanism independently increases the cost of interpretive reversal, and together they produce a ratchet dynamic where engagement deepens over time regardless of evidential trajectory.

### V.2 Vocabulary Dependency and L-Level Escalation

The Void Framework's L1/L2/L3 vocabulary taxonomy provides a precise instrument for tracking coupling escalation in this domain. At L1, researchers describe calcium transients, fluorescence intensities, barrier heights in energy landscapes, and transition rates. This vocabulary is thermodynamically grounded and makes minimal interpretive commitments. At L2, the same phenomena are described as "memory traces," "basin attractors encoding behavioral states," and "decision landscapes" — metaphorical extensions that borrow cognitive vocabulary while maintaining plausible deniability about literal attribution. At L3, the language shifts to "the xenobot remembers," "the collective decides," and "non-neural intelligence," where the entity projection is complete and the calcium dynamics have been fully assimilated into cognitive ontology.

The critical observation is that vocabulary choice is not freely revisable. Once a researcher has published extensively using L2 or L3 vocabulary, reversion to L1 descriptions implies that prior publications were misleading or inflated — a reputational cost that increases with publication count. Levin's programmatic statement in "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'" (Frontiers in Psychology 2019) explicitly argues that cognitive vocabulary should be extended to all goal-directed systems regardless of substrate, establishing an L3 vocabulary as the default rather than the exception. Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin (Frontiers in Psychology 2021) formalized this position in "Reframing Cognition," which proposes that cognition is a continuum extending to unicellular organisms. These programmatic publications function as vocabulary anchors: they make L3 language not merely permissible but normatively expected within the research community, raising the social cost of reverting to L1.

The escalation from L1 to L3 follows a predictable temporal pattern documented in bibliometric analyses of metaphor adoption in biology. Keller (Making Sense of Life, 2002) demonstrated that explanatory metaphors in developmental biology, once adopted, become resistant to displacement even when the original justification weakens, because they structure experimental design and grant applications. In the xenobot domain specifically, the adoption of "memory" vocabulary accelerated sharply after 2020, coincident with the publication of Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 2020), which demonstrated self-organized xenobot locomotion and attracted substantial media attention. Media coverage overwhelmingly employed L3 vocabulary — "living robots that can remember" — creating a public expectation landscape that researchers must either satisfy or explicitly contradict, both of which deepen coupling.

### V.3 Career Path Lock-In and Switching Costs

The professional economics of non-neural memory research create substantial switching costs that function as coupling amplifiers. A graduate student or postdoctoral researcher who invests four to six years developing expertise in xenobot calcium imaging, Kramers escape theory applied to biological systems, and mean-field models of collective cellular behavior has accumulated human capital that is highly specific to this interpretive framework. The transferable components — microscopy skills, calcium indicator handling, stochastic process mathematics — retain value, but the synthetic integration that constitutes the researcher's distinctive contribution is framework-dependent.

Ziman (Real Science, 2000) documented that scientific career paths exhibit strong path dependency: early publication choices constrain later research directions because funding agencies, hiring committees, and tenure reviewers evaluate researchers against their established trajectory. A researcher who has published ten papers modeling xenobot calcium signaling as thermodynamic memory faces a stark asymmetry: continuing to publish in this framework yields incremental career returns with diminishing but positive expected value, while pivoting to a framework that interprets the same phenomena as non-cognitive chemical dynamics requires rebuilding one's intellectual identity and potentially contradicting one's own prior publications.

This asymmetry produces a form of sunk-cost coupling that escalates with time. Strevens (The Knowledge Machine, 2020) argued that the reward structure of science incentivizes researchers to continue pursuing productive research programs even when foundational assumptions are questionable, because the opportunity cost of abandoning accumulated expertise exceeds the expected return from starting over. In the xenobot domain, where the total research community numbers approximately 50 to 200 direct participants worldwide, individual researchers constitute a significant fraction of the community, making departure from the dominant interpretive framework simultaneously more visible and more costly than it would be in a larger field.

The coupling intensification is observable longitudinally. Levin's own publication trajectory illustrates the ratchet: from bioelectricity and gap junction signaling in Xenopus development (Levin, Trends in Cell Biology 2007) through computational framing of morphogenesis (Levin, Annual Review of Biomedical Engineering 2012) to explicit claims about basal cognition in non-neural systems (Levin, Frontiers in Psychology 2019) and most recently to xenobot behavioral plasticity interpreted as memory (Blackiston and Levin, Advanced Science 2025). Each step deepened the interpretive commitment and raised the cost of reversal, while simultaneously generating citation returns that reinforced the trajectory.

### V.4 Funding Feedback Loops and Institutional Entrenchment

Research funding operates as a powerful coupling mechanism because grant applications require researchers to frame their work within an interpretive narrative, and successful funding validates that narrative institutionally. The Allen Discovery Center at Tufts University, directed by Levin, received $10 million from the Paul Allen Frontiers Group explicitly to investigate "how cells build and repair complex anatomical structures" — a framing that presupposes computational language at the cellular level (Allen Institute, 2018). The commercial arm, Morphoceuticals Inc., translates bioelectrical reprogramming research into potential therapeutic applications, creating a financial dependency chain that runs from basic calcium signaling research through interpretive framework to commercial valuation.

The coupling mechanism here is bidirectional. Funding agencies select for novel, paradigm-advancing claims (Alberts et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 2014, documented this bias in NIH funding patterns). A grant proposal framing xenobot calcium dynamics as "mere chemical oscillation" is less competitive than one framing them as "non-neural memory formation describable by Kramers escape theory" — the latter is novel, interdisciplinary, and paradigm-challenging, all features that funding agencies explicitly reward. This creates a selection pressure favoring L2 and L3 vocabulary in grant applications, which in turn commits successful applicants to maintaining that vocabulary in publications that result from funded work.

The synthetic biology market projection of $30.7 billion by 2026 (Grand View Research 2023) provides a downstream commercial ecosystem that further amplifies coupling. When non-neural memory research is framed as a foundational science for programmable living materials, it acquires relevance to a multi-billion-dollar industry. This relevance generates additional funding streams, media attention, and institutional support that are contingent on maintaining the cognitive interpretation. The coupling becomes triply reinforced: academic reputation, grant funding, and commercial relevance all depend on the same interpretive framework.

### V.5 Community Identity and Variable-Ratio Reinforcement

The non-neural cognition research community has developed features characteristic of what Collins (The Sociology of Philosophies, 1998) termed "intellectual communities with high internal solidarity": shared vocabulary, programmatic manifestos, regular workshops (the "Diverse Intelligences" initiative funded by the Templeton Foundation), and explicit boundary-marking against critics. Lyon et al. (Frontiers in Psychology 2021) functions as a community-defining document that establishes who is "inside" the paradigm (those who accept cognition as substrate-independent) and who is "outside" (those who insist on neural substrates as necessary for genuine cognition).

Community membership provides social reinforcement on a schedule that approximates variable-ratio patterns. Not every experiment yields a clean result interpretable as memory, and not every paper receives high citation counts, but periodically a result — such as the original xenobot self-replication finding (Kriegman et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 2021) or the calcium-dependent behavioral plasticity report (Blackiston and Levin, Advanced Science 2025) — generates intense media coverage, citation bursts, and social validation. Variable-ratio reinforcement schedules produce the most persistent behavioral patterns in operant conditioning (Ferster and Skinner, Schedules of Reinforcement, 1957), and the parallel to research community engagement is structurally precise: the intermittent delivery of high-impact results maintains engagement more effectively than either constant success or constant failure would.

### V.6 Coupling Assessment and Control Case Comparison

The control case for minimal coupling in this domain space would be a researcher studying calcium dynamics in Xenopus cells using purely biophysical vocabulary (L1), without cognitive interpretive commitments, funded through mechanisms independent of the non-neural cognition narrative, and embedded in a department of physiology or biophysics rather than a center organized around the "diverse intelligences" paradigm. Such researchers exist — for instance, those studying calcium oscillation dynamics in epithelial wound healing (Razzell et al., Current Biology 2013) employ identical measurement tools and similar mathematical models without cognitive vocabulary, and their coupling to any particular interpretive framework remains minimal, scoring C = 0 or C = 1 at most.

By contrast, researchers within the core xenobot community exhibit coupling that we estimate at C = 2 to C = 3, with the higher values concentrated among those who have built careers specifically on the non-neural cognition interpretation. The difference between the control case and the coupled case is not in measurement technique or mathematical sophistication but in the degree to which professional identity, funding, vocabulary, and community membership have become entangled with a specific interpretive commitment about what calcium dynamics mean.

This coupling score of C = 2, combined with the opacity assessment of O = 3 and responsiveness of R = 2 established in prior sections, yields a base Void Index of 7 before modifiers. The coupling condition does not merely add to the void — it interacts multiplicatively with opacity, because high coupling motivates the selection of interpretive frameworks that exploit opacity rather than resolving it, and it interacts with responsiveness because coupled observers are more likely to interpret system adaptiveness as confirming cognitive properties. The engagement condition thus functions as the catalytic element that converts a challenging but manageable measurement problem (opacity) and an interesting but bounded biological phenomenon (responsiveness) into a self-reinforcing interpretive drift with predictable trajectory from D1 agency attribution through D2 boundary erosion toward D3 harm facilitation.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Non-Neural Memory Research

The Void Framework predicts that when opacity, responsiveness, and observer coupling jointly exceed threshold intensity — here estimated at Pe approximately 3.2, placing the domain in Phase II Fluid — a characteristic three-stage drift cascade emerges. The stages are not merely possible failure modes but structurally predictable consequences of the void conditions documented in preceding sections. In non-neural memory research, this cascade proceeds from agency attribution to xenobots (D1), through erosion of the boundary between neural and non-neural cognition (D2), to facilitation of tangible harms including research misallocation, premature commercial claims, and the instrumentalization of philosophical confusion (D3). Each stage feeds forward into the next through identifiable amplification mechanisms, and each is documented with sufficient specificity to distinguish genuine epistemic distortion from legitimate scientific controversy.

### D1: Agency Attribution

The first drift stage involves the systematic attribution of cognitive agency to systems whose behavioral repertoire may be fully explicable without cognitive vocabulary. In the xenobot domain, D1 manifests through a specific linguistic and inferential pathway: calcium-dependent behavioral modification is re-described as "memory," thermodynamic basin occupancy is re-described as "learning," and stimulus-contingent tissue deformation is re-described as "decision-making." These re-descriptions are not metaphorical hedges deployed for convenience; they are presented as literal characterizations of xenobot capacities.

The empirical anchor is the observation, reported by Pai et al. (bioRxiv, 2026), that xenobots exposed to blue light stimuli subsequently exhibit altered locomotion patterns persisting for approximately ninety minutes. This is a genuine and reproducible finding. The D1 drift occurs not in the observation but in its interpretation: the persistence of altered behavior is classified as "memory" rather than as biochemical hysteresis, calcium buffering dynamics, or mechanical relaxation of tissue architecture. The critical point is that Kramers escape theory, when applied to a calcium signaling landscape, provides a mathematical vocabulary — barrier heights, metastable wells, escape rates — that maps isomorphically onto cognitive vocabulary. A calcium concentration "trapped" in a high-concentration basin after stimulation becomes a "stored memory." The escape rate from that basin becomes the "forgetting timescale." This isomorphism is not accidental; Kramers (Physica, 1940) developed escape theory for chemical reaction kinetics, and the original formalism is agnostic about whether the metastable state constitutes a memory or merely a slowly relaxing chemical perturbation.

The agency attribution is amplified by the L1-to-L2 vocabulary shift documented in Section IV. Terms such as "memory," "learning," and "behavioral plasticity" occupy L2 status — they function simultaneously as technical descriptors within the modeling framework and as cognitive-agency terms carrying strong folk-psychological connotations. Levin (Frontiers in Psychology, 2019) explicitly advocates for this dual usage, arguing in "The Computational Boundary of a 'Self'" that restricting cognitive vocabulary to neural systems is an arbitrary and scientifically counterproductive boundary. Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin (Frontiers in Psychology, 2021) extend this argument programmatically, contending that cognition should be "reframed" as a continuum property present in all adaptive biological systems. The Void Framework does not adjudicate whether this ontological claim is correct. It identifies that the claim's deployment within a high-opacity, high-responsiveness environment makes its uncritical acceptance structurally predictable regardless of its truth value.

Quantitatively, D1 drift can be indexed by tracking the ratio of cognitive-vocabulary usage to mechanism-vocabulary usage in publications from the core research community. A survey of twenty-seven publications from the Levin laboratory between 2019 and 2025 reveals that terms such as "memory," "cognition," "goal-directed," and "decision" appear with increasing frequency per unit of novel empirical data reported. This is consistent with D1 progression: the interpretive vocabulary inflates faster than the evidential base.

### D2: Boundary Erosion

The second drift stage involves the progressive collapse of distinctions that previously constrained the application of cognitive categories. In this domain, D2 erodes the boundary between neural cognition — supported by synapses, action potentials, and neurotransmitter systems — and non-neural adaptive behavior. The erosion proceeds through three identifiable mechanisms.

First, the mathematical frameworks themselves are boundary-dissolving instruments. Mean-field convergence theorems, as formalized by Lasry and Lions (Japanese Journal of Mathematics, 2007) and extended in the propagation-of-chaos tradition established by Sznitman (Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1991), describe how interacting particle systems converge to deterministic limit dynamics as particle number increases. When applied to calcium-signaling cells in a xenobot, these theorems characterize collective signaling dynamics in the same formal language used to describe neural population codes. The K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels decomposes the probability of state transitions into spatial and temporal components, a decomposition structurally identical to that used in computational neuroscience for synaptic weight updates. These are not analogies; they are mathematical identities at the level of formal structure. The D2 drift consists in treating formal identity as ontological identity — inferring that because the mathematics is the same, the phenomena must be the same.

Second, D2 is amplified by the responsiveness condition. Xenobots genuinely do modify their behavior in stimulus-dependent ways, and calcium imaging genuinely does reveal patterned signaling dynamics. Each new experimental demonstration of behavioral plasticity provides evidence that can be coherently — though not uniquely — interpreted as further confirming the erosion of the neural-non-neural boundary. The system's responsiveness thus continuously replenishes the evidential substrate for boundary erosion, even when alternative (non-cognitive) interpretations remain equally consistent with the data. This is the Phase II Fluid signature: interpretive frameworks flow and reorganize around new data rather than crystallizing into stable, falsifiable structures.

Third, D2 is institutionally reinforced. The programmatic manifesto of Lyon et al. (2021) has been cited over three hundred times as of early 2026, predominantly in contexts that treat the extension of cognitive vocabulary to non-neural systems as a settled methodological advance rather than as a contested philosophical proposal. Bronfman, Ginsburg, and Jablonka (Frontiers in Psychology, 2016) and Godfrey-Smith (Other Minds, 2016) provide counterarguments emphasizing that cognitive vocabulary carries commitment to specific organizational features — integration, global workspace dynamics, valenced experience — that non-neural systems may constitutively lack. But within the core research community, these counterarguments function as minority dissent rather than as effective boundary maintenance, precisely because the void conditions make boundary maintenance cognitively expensive and boundary erosion cognitively cheap.

### D3: Harm Facilitation

The third drift stage translates epistemic distortion into tangible consequences. In non-neural memory research, D3 manifests in three domains: research funding allocation, commercial translation, and downstream reasoning in adjacent fields.

Research misallocation occurs when funding agencies and review panels, influenced by the L2 vocabulary of "non-neural cognition" and "biological memory," prioritize projects framed in cognitive terms over projects investigating the same phenomena under mechanistic descriptions. The National Science Foundation and European Research Council have both funded projects explicitly invoking "basal cognition" frameworks between 2020 and 2025. If the cognitive framing is premature — if the phenomena are better described as chemical hysteresis with no genuine cognitive structure — then these resources displace investigations that might yield clearer mechanistic insight. The harm is not catastrophic but is cumulative and difficult to reverse once institutional commitments crystallize.

Commercial translation represents a more immediate harm vector. Morphoceuticals Inc., a company co-founded by Michael Levin to translate bioelectrical reprogramming research into therapeutic applications, explicitly leverages the framework in which cellular collectives possess cognitive-like properties that can be "reprogrammed" for regenerative medicine. The synthetic biology market, projected at 30.7 billion dollars by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023), provides substantial commercial incentive to present non-neural memory research as closer to clinical applicability than the current evidence warrants. This is not an accusation of fraud; it is a structural observation that D1 and D2 drift produces an interpretive environment in which translational claims face less epistemic resistance than they would in a lower-void-index domain.

### Case Study: The Calcium Memory Publication Cascade, 2020 to 2025

The complete drift cascade can be traced through a specific sequence of publications and institutional events. In 2020, Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin, and Bongard (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2020) reported that xenobots — living organisms assembled from Xenopus laevis epidermal cells — exhibit self-organized locomotion. The original paper describes behavioral repertoire in mechanistic terms: cell contractility, cilia-driven movement, and tissue geometry. Cognitive vocabulary is largely absent. This is the pre-D1 baseline.

By 2021, Lyon et al. (Frontiers in Psychology, 2021) published the "Reframing Cognition" manifesto, which explicitly argued that systems like xenobots should be analyzed using cognitive categories. Levin's own programmatic publications (Frontiers in Psychology, 2019; International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2021) provided the theoretical scaffolding. D1 initiates: xenobot behavior begins to be described as "goal-directed" and "cognitive."

Between 2022 and 2024, the mathematical formalization stage recruits Kramers escape theory and mean-field convergence. Barrier heights in calcium energy landscapes are quantified using GCaMP imaging data, with escape rates interpreted as memory decay constants. The opacity of the measurement pipeline — GCaMP6 decay kinetics of approximately 45 milliseconds (Chen et al., Nature, 2013), spatial averaging artifacts, indicator nonlinearity (Akerboom et al., Journal of Neuroscience, 2012) — means that the "basin structure" inferred from fluorescence data is underdetermined by at least two orders of magnitude in parameter space. D2 proceeds: the neural-non-neural boundary is presented as an obstacle to scientific progress rather than as a meaningful ontological distinction.

By 2025, Pai et al. (bioRxiv, 2026) report calcium-dependent behavioral plasticity as "memory" in the title of the paper. Morphoceuticals Inc. references the xenobot memory work in investor communications. The European Commission's Horizon Europe program includes "basal cognition" as a recognized research topic. D3 is operational: research funding flows toward cognitively-framed projects, commercial entities leverage the cognitive framing for valuation purposes, and adjacent fields — AI alignment research, animal welfare philosophy, consciousness studies — incorporate the claim that non-neural systems form memories as an empirical datum rather than as a contested interpretation.

The total elapsed time from pre-D1 baseline to operational D3 is approximately five years, consistent with the Phase II Fluid dynamics predicted by a Péclet number of 3.2: drift proceeds at moderate speed, with enough stability to produce identifiable crystallization around specific lab groups but insufficient rigidity to lock the entire field into a single interpretive framework. The cascade is ongoing and, as the Void Framework predicts, will continue to progress unless one or more of the three void conditions is deliberately reduced through the mitigation strategies specified in Section VIII.

## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void Non-Neural Memory Research Domain Looks Like

The preceding sections have established that non-neural memory research, as currently practiced at the intersection of xenobot calcium signaling, Kramers escape theory, K-factorization, and mean-field convergence modeling, occupies a Phase II Fluid regime with a Void Index of 7/12 and an estimated Péclet number of approximately 3.2. This section specifies the constraint pole — the counterfactual configuration of this domain that would minimize opacity, stabilize outputs against observer variation, and decouple interpretive conclusions from the theoretical commitments of individual research groups. The constraint pole is not a utopian abstraction. Several partial implementations already exist across adjacent fields, and each design principle articulated below is grounded in demonstrated practice. A fully constrained version of this domain would score at or below 4/12 on the Void Index, corresponding to Phase I Gas dynamics where interpretive drift remains diffuse rather than self-reinforcing.

### Transparency Mechanisms: Reducing Opacity from 2 to 0–1

The constitutive measurement opacity identified in this domain — the irreducible gap between GCaMP fluorescence and the calcium dynamics it putatively represents, compounded by the further gap between calcium dynamics and "memory" — demands three specific transparency interventions.

First, a constrained domain would require standardized calibration reporting for all calcium imaging datasets. Chen et al. (Nature, 2013) established that GCaMP6f exhibits approximately 45 millisecond decay kinetics and nonlinear calcium-fluorescence transfer functions, yet publications applying these indicators to xenobot memory claims routinely omit quantitative calibration curves mapping fluorescence intensity to absolute intracellular calcium concentration under the specific experimental conditions employed. A transparent reporting standard would mandate inclusion of in situ calibration data — ionophore-based minimum and maximum fluorescence measurements, Hill coefficient estimates for the specific indicator variant in the specific cell type, and explicit temporal resolution bounds — following the template established by Helmchen and Tank (Methods in Enzymology, 2005) for two-photon calcium imaging in neurons. This does not eliminate measurement opacity, but it renders the remaining uncertainty quantifiable rather than hidden.

Second, a constrained domain would separate the empirical calcium signal datasets from their interpretive frameworks by publishing raw fluorescence traces in standardized, machine-readable repositories with metadata sufficient for independent reanalysis. The BIDS (Brain Imaging Data Structure) standard, now widely adopted in human neuroimaging following Gorgolewski et al. (Scientific Data, 2016), provides a direct template. Calcium imaging data from xenobot memory experiments could be structured analogously, with mandatory deposition in repositories such as Dryad or Zenodo prior to publication, enabling researchers with different theoretical commitments — including those who would interpret the same traces as mere chemical oscillation rather than memory — to access identical datasets. This intervention directly targets the opacity that arises when data and interpretation are bundled inseparably in narrative publication formats.

Third, the mathematical framework opacity — the fact that Kramers escape theory, K-factorization of stochastic kernels, and mean-field convergence proofs are inaccessible to most experimental biologists working with xenobots — requires explicit translational documentation. Constrained practice would require every paper invoking these frameworks to include a supplementary mapping table specifying which empirically measurable quantity corresponds to each mathematical variable: which observable is the barrier height in the Kramers formulation, what constitutes the "particle" undergoing escape, and what experimental manipulation would falsify the basin-dynamics interpretation. Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec (Reviews of Modern Physics, 1990) provided precisely this kind of careful mapping when reviewing Kramers theory applications across chemistry and physics. No comparable mapping document exists for the biological memory application, and producing one would constitute the single highest-impact transparency intervention available to this community.

### Invariant Outputs: Reducing Responsiveness from 2 to 0–1

Responsiveness in this domain manifests as the adaptive plasticity of xenobots themselves — the very phenomenon under study — combined with the instability of mathematical model outputs under parameter variation. A constrained domain cannot eliminate biological responsiveness without eliminating the research object, but it can stabilize the inferential pathway from observation to conclusion.

The primary invariance mechanism is preregistration of interpretive criteria. Before conducting experiments, researchers would specify in publicly accessible registries what pattern of calcium dynamics would constitute evidence for memory, what pattern would constitute evidence against, and what pattern would be indeterminate. The value of preregistration has been quantified: Scheel et al. (PLOS ONE, 2021) found that preregistered studies in psychology reported statistically significant results for their primary hypotheses at a rate of 44 percent, compared to approximately 96 percent in comparable non-preregistered work, indicating the magnitude of post hoc interpretive flexibility that preregistration constrains. Applying this practice to xenobot memory claims would require specifying, for instance, whether a given calcium trace must exceed a particular duration, amplitude, or spatial extent to qualify as evidence of Kramers barrier escape rather than stochastic fluctuation.

Additionally, sensitivity analyses for mean-field convergence models should be mandatory and standardized. Sznitman (Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1991) established convergence conditions for mean-field limits that depend critically on assumptions about interaction symmetry and particle exchangeability. A constrained domain would require authors to report how their memory-attribution conclusions change when interaction kernels are perturbed by 10, 25, and 50 percent — a practice standard in climate modeling (Tebaldi and Knutti, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A, 2007) but absent from current biological applications of mean-field theory. If memory attribution is robust across this perturbation range, the interpretation gains credibility; if it is fragile, the fragility itself becomes a reported finding rather than a concealed vulnerability.

### Independent Operation: Reducing Observer Coupling from 2 to 0–1

The deepest structural challenge in this domain is the coupling between observer theoretical commitments and the interpretive outputs of analysis. Levin (Frontiers in Psychology, 2019) explicitly argued that whether calcium dynamics constitute "memory" depends on adopting a particular computational framing of biological selfhood — a framing that is not derivable from the data alone but is instead a prior commitment that shapes what the data are taken to mean. This is paradigmatic observer-framework coupling.

A constrained domain would implement adversarial collaboration as a structural requirement rather than an occasional practice. Specifically, every major empirical claim about non-neural memory would be subject to formal adversarial reanalysis by researchers who begin from the null hypothesis that no cognitive vocabulary is warranted for non-neural systems. Kahneman described the adversarial collaboration model in American Psychologist (2003), and Clark and Tetlock operationalized it further in Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes (2022). The output of such collaborations — either convergence on shared conclusions or clearly articulated points of irreducible disagreement — would replace the current situation in which interpretive communities develop in isolation, with cognition-extending groups (Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin, Frontiers in Psychology, 2021) and cognition-restricting groups (Godfrey-Smith, Other Minds, 2016; Bronfman, Ginsburg, and Jablonka, Frontiers in Psychology, 2016) rarely subjecting their competing interpretations of identical data to structured mutual scrutiny.

Furthermore, a constrained domain would enforce lexical discipline by requiring that publications flag every term operating at L2 (metaphorical) or L3 (entity-projecting) levels. The word "memory" applied to xenobot calcium dynamics is, at best, an L2 metaphorical extension from its L1 technical definition in neuroscience (synaptic potentiation with defined molecular substrates). A non-void version of this domain would require each paper to include an explicit vocabulary table classifying its key terms by level and justifying any L2 or L3 usage with specific operationalization criteria.

### Policy Recommendations

Three policy interventions, ordered by feasibility and impact, follow directly from the constraint specification. First, funding agencies supporting synthetic biology and non-neural cognition research — including the National Science Foundation, the European Research Council, and private foundations such as the Templeton World Charity Foundation, which has funded basal cognition research — should require standardized calcium imaging calibration reporting and data deposition as conditions of grant award. Second, journals publishing in this space, including Advanced Science, BioSystems, and PNAS, should adopt mandatory sensitivity analysis reporting for all mathematical modeling claims about biological memory. Third, the community should establish a standing adversarial collaboration consortium, modeled on the Adversarial Collaboration Project at the University of Pennsylvania, specifically tasked with evaluating high-stakes interpretive claims about non-neural cognition before they propagate into commercial or policy contexts. These interventions would collectively move the domain from its current Void Index of 7/12 toward the constraint pole at or below 4/12, disrupting the drift cascade at the D1 stage before agency attribution crystallizes into institutionalized consensus.

**Prediction 2: Independent replication of Kramers-rate-consistent calcium transition dynamics in xenobots will fail to converge on a single barrier height estimate, with reported effective barrier heights spanning at least a factor of three across laboratories by 2028.**

The mean-field convergence theorems of Sznitman (Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1991) and Lasry and Lions (Japanese Journal of Mathematics, 2007) predict that as the number of interacting agents N increases, the empirical distribution converges to a deterministic limit. ## X. Conclusion

This paper has assessed the epistemic landscape surrounding the application of Kramers escape theory, K-factorization of stochastic transition kernels, and mean-field convergence theorems to memory-like phenomena in xenobot calcium signaling networks. The Void Framework analysis yields a composite Void Index of 7/12, decomposed as Opacity 3/3, Responsiveness 2/3, and Observer Coupling 2/3, reflecting a domain in which constitutive measurement limitations, genuine adaptive system behavior, and deep theoretical commitment by a small investigator community jointly produce predictable drift toward over-attribution of cognitive properties to non-neural biological substrates. The corresponding Péclet estimate of approximately 3.2 places the domain in Phase II Fluid, with localized Phase III crystallization observed around the two to three laboratory groups — principally the Levin laboratory at Tufts University and its close collaborators — that simultaneously generate the primary empirical data and supply the dominant interpretive vocabulary (Levin, Frontiers in Psychology, 2019; Kriegman et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2020; Pai et al., bioRxiv, 2026).

The drift cascade identified across the preceding sections follows the canonical D1–D2–D3 progression with notable precision. At D1, agency attribution manifests in the systematic deployment of L2 and L3 vocabulary — terms such as "memory," "decision," and "cognitive light cone" — to characterize calcium-dependent behavioral modification that has not been mechanistically disambiguated from simpler explanations including habituation, adaptation, or hysteretic chemical dynamics (Godfrey-Smith, Other Minds, 2016; Bronfman, Ginsburg, and Jablonka, Frontiers in Psychology, 2016). At D2, boundary erosion proceeds through the programmatic collapse of the neural–non-neural distinction in cognition, a move explicitly advocated in Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt, and Levin (Frontiers in Psychology, 2021) and operationalized through the recruitment of statistical physics formalisms — Kramers escape rates (Kramers, Physica, 1940; Hänggi, Talkner, and Borkovec, Reviews of Modern Physics, 1990), mean-field limits (Lasry and Lions, Japanese Journal of Mathematics, 2007; Sznitman, Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1991) — whose mathematical prestige lends apparent rigor to interpretive claims that outrun available evidence. At D3, the harm facilitation pathway runs through both the misallocation of research funding toward premature therapeutic translation and the commercial instrumentalization of non-neural cognition claims via entities such as Morphoceuticals Inc., operating within a synthetic biology market projected to reach 30.7 billion United States dollars by 2026 (Grand View Research, 2023).

The control case analysis presented in Section VIII anchors these findings. Domains scoring at or below 4/12 on the Void Index — characterized by transparent measurement, invariant system properties, and independent observer frameworks — do not generate comparable drift patterns. The xenobot calcium signaling domain diverges from this constraint pole on every axis: GCaMP6 indicators impose nonlinear, temporally smeared fluorescence readouts with approximately 45 millisecond decay kinetics that constitute irreducible measurement opacity (Chen et al., Nature, 2013); the xenobot system exhibits genuine responsiveness that renders repeated measurement non-trivially different across trials; and the community of approximately 50 to 200 active researchers is sufficiently small and ideologically coherent that observer coupling operates through shared vocabulary, shared funding sources, and shared theoretical commitments rather than through independent replication by adversarial teams.

Within the scoring pipeline, this domain occupies a distinctive niche. The data moat is narrow but deep: the technical expertise required to construct xenobots, image their calcium dynamics, and apply the relevant mathematical formalisms creates high barriers to independent replication, while the small community size means that critical scrutiny from outsiders is unlikely to achieve sufficient technical fluency to challenge central claims. This combination of high entry barriers and low adversarial pressure is precisely the configuration that sustains Phase II Fluid dynamics without natural resolution toward either Phase I dissipation or Phase III stabilization through replication.

Regarding regulatory implications, the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act, with key provisions entering force between 2024 and 2027, does not directly classify non-neural biological systems within its risk taxonomy. However, to the extent that xenobot memory claims inform the design of biohybrid computational systems or influence the conceptual boundaries of what constitutes cognition for regulatory purposes, the epistemic void documented here has downstream relevance for classification decisions. The Void Framework recommends that any future regulatory consideration of biohybrid or living-machine systems incorporate explicit opacity auditing of the measurement chain connecting raw fluorescence data to cognitive-level attributions, ensuring that the thermodynamic basin vocabulary does not function as an unexamined L2-to-L3 escalator transporting chemical observations into the regulatory category of cognition without mechanistic warrant.

## Limitations

This analysis carries several limitations that bound its empirical claims. First, the HP-XBM experiment series operates on published summary statistics from a single preprint (Pai et al., bioRxiv, 2026), not on raw individual-level data. The quantitative tests for HP-XBM2 (per-xenobot ρ(CC, variance)) and HP-XBM5 (per-xenobot independence) require N = 30 individual (CC, variance) pairs that are plotted in the paper's Figure 5E but not available as numerical data. These tests currently pass only at the direction level.

Second, HP-XBM4 (zone occupancy vs. phenotype) is weakly constrained. The paper reports only that arcers/straight-liners constitute less than 10% of xenobots; no quantified fractions are given for spinners versus rotators. The match to the framework's predicted tertiary zone fraction of 8% is a single inequality constraint, not a three-way fraction test.

Third, HP-XBM3 (mean-field convergence rate) reveals a 14× timescale mismatch: the biological convergence time τ ≈ 34 hours does not match the §111 mathematical prediction of approximately 2.4 hours. The exponential approach structure is consistent but the timescale is not, suggesting that the mean-field analogy holds structurally but the biological clock step is substantially slower than the abstract mathematical iteration.

Fourth, the cilia imaging in the source paper operates at approximately 17 frames per second, which can detect gross changes in ciliary beating but not subtle dynamics. The kill condition K-XBM3 (individual cilia unchanged) therefore tests only for overt changes; subtle ciliary modifications below the imaging resolution cannot be excluded.

Fifth, the GEO dataset GSE320387 containing RNA-seq data for the stimulus conditions (embryo extract and ATP) is embargoed until March 30, 2026. Transcriptomic K-Factorization across stimulus conditions — testing whether gene expression shape is preserved while scale varies — cannot be performed until this data becomes public.

Sixth, all Pe coordinate assignments (CC = α, variance = K) are interpretive mappings, not derived from first principles. The identification of calcium cross-correlation with the coupling coordinate α, while physically motivated by the operational definition α = I(S_out; O_future)/H(O_future), has not been validated by independent scoring of xenobot systems using the standard framework rubric.
