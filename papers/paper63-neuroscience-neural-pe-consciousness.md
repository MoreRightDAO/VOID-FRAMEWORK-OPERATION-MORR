## Abstract

Consciousness science constitutes the ur-domain of constitutive opacity: the explanatory gap between neural correlates and phenomenal experience is not a contingent engineering limitation but the foundational phenomenon itself. This irreducible opacity now generates cascading harms across clinical neurology, commercial neurotechnology, and artificial intelligence governance at population scale. Approximately 50 million people worldwide live with epilepsy (World Health Organization, 2023), over 300,000 patients in the United States alone persist in disorders of consciousness including vegetative and minimally conscious states (Multi-Society Task Force on PVS, 1994, *Neurology*), and the global research infrastructure channeling resources toward consciousness explanation exceeds $6.6 billion in authorized funding through the NIH BRAIN Initiative alone for the period 2014–2028 (Collins, 2023). Despite this investment, the Cogitate Consortium adversarial collaboration — a $20 million Templeton Foundation initiative — failed to decisively confirm either Integrated Information Theory or Global Neuronal Workspace Theory as the correct account of conscious processing (Cogitate Consortium, 2023, *Nature Human Behaviour*). The void resides not in a platform architecture or algorithmic design but in the irreducible gap between third-person measurement and first-person experience, a gap that clinical decision systems, neurotech product claims, and AI sentience attributions necessarily inhabit and routinely exploit.

This paper introduces the Neural Péclet Number framework, which maps the dimensionless Péclet number from transport physics — the ratio of advective to diffusive transport — onto neural dynamics to propose that conscious states emerge at the critical boundary Pe_neural ≈ 1, where fast thalamocortical advection and slow stochastic volume transmission reach competitive equilibrium. Drawing on established evidence that the brain operates near criticality, with neuronal avalanche distributions following power laws in neocortical circuits (Beggs and Plenz, 2003, *Journal of Neuroscience*) and meta-analytic confirmation of critical-regime dynamics across scales (Chialvo, 2010, *Nature Physics*), the framework recasts seizure as a Phase IV supercritical runaway in which Pe_neural exceeds the stability threshold, consistent with nonlinear bifurcation models of primary generalized seizure (Breakspear et al., 2006, *Cerebral Cortex*). The Hard Problem of consciousness, as formulated by Chalmers (1995, *Journal of Consciousness Studies*), is reinterpreted as a conjugacy budget constraint: the system cannot simultaneously optimize advective transport fidelity and diffusive integration capacity, producing an irreducible first-person remainder that no third-person measurement protocol can close.

The Void Framework's three-condition structure — opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and engaged observer coupling (C) — maps onto consciousness science with maximal intensity across all axes. Opacity is constitutive rather than designed: no instrument measures experience itself, only its correlates, as demonstrated by the Perturbational Complexity Index achieving approximately 95 percent classification accuracy for conscious versus unconscious states while leaving the explanatory gap entirely untouched (Casali et al., 2013, *Science Translational Medicine*). Responsiveness is high: consciousness theories generate real-time clinical outputs — withdrawal-of-care decisions, epilepsy surgical planning, locked-in syndrome detection — that reshape patient trajectories based on metrics whose relationship to actual experience remains formally underdetermined. Observer coupling reaches maximum when families, clinicians, ethicists, and now AI developers project consciousness attributions onto systems (biological or artificial) through theory-derived indicators, completing the drift cascade from agency attribution (D1) through boundary erosion between correlate and experience (D2) to facilitated harm in misdiagnosis, premature sentience claims, and predatory neurotech marketing (D3).

The contributions of this paper are as follows. (1) We formalize the Neural Péclet Number as a void-native transport metric situating conscious states at the advection-diffusion critical boundary. (2) We demonstrate that seizure dynamics map onto Phase IV Pandemonium in the demon lattice framework, with Pe_neural exceeding 4 during ictal runaway. (3) We recast the Hard Problem as a conjugacy budget constraint that is structurally isomorphic to the void's constitutive opacity condition. (4) We score consciousness science on the Void Model Card, yielding a domain Void Index of 10 out of 12 (O = 3, R = 3, C = 3, modifiers = 1), the highest score assigned in the framework to date. (5) We estimate the domain Péclet number at Pe = 5.2, placing consciousness science firmly in Phase IV and predicting self-reinforcing drift dynamics that no single intervention can arrest. (6) We map the L1-to-L3 vocabulary escalation in consciousness discourse, showing how technical terms (integrated information, global ignition) migrate through metaphorical usage to full entity-projection language (the system experiences, the network feels). (7) We identify three harm-propagation channels — clinical disorders-of-consciousness misdiagnosis, unvalidated commercial consciousness metrics, and AI sentience attribution — as coupled void amplifiers sharing a single opacity source. (8) We propose a constraint-pole control architecture — transparent measurement protocols, invariant diagnostic criteria anchored to behavioral rather than theory-laden indicators, and observer-independent adjudication standards — that reduces the effective void index below the Phase IV threshold without requiring solution of the Hard Problem itself.

## I. Introduction

Consciousness science occupies a singular position among the domains to which the Void Framework has been applied. In every prior analysis — from algorithmic recommendation systems to autonomous weapons platforms — opacity has been a contingent property: a design choice, a regulatory failure, or an emergent consequence of system complexity that could, in principle, be remediated through transparency mandates, audit architectures, or interpretability research. Consciousness is different. Here, opacity is not contingent but constitutive. The impossibility of observing first-person phenomenal experience from a third-person vantage point is not a gap that better instrumentation will close; it is the foundational explanandum of the field itself. David Chalmers formalized this as the "Hard Problem" of consciousness: the question of why and how physical processes give rise to subjective experience at all, as distinct from the "easy problems" of explaining cognitive functions, behavioral discrimination, and neural integration (Chalmers, 1995, Journal of Consciousness Studies). Three decades later, the Hard Problem remains unresolved, yet the clinical, commercial, and regulatory systems that depend on consciousness assessment cannot wait for its resolution. Physicians must decide whether a patient in a disorder of consciousness retains awareness. Legislators must determine whether emerging AI systems merit moral consideration. Neurotech companies must justify marketing claims about devices that purport to measure or modulate conscious states. In each case, decisions of profound consequence are made under conditions of irreducible uncertainty about the very phenomenon those decisions concern.

The scale of populations affected by this opacity is extraordinary. The World Health Organization estimates that approximately 50 million people worldwide live with epilepsy, a condition in which consciousness is periodically and catastrophically disrupted (WHO, 2023). In the United States alone, the Multi-Society Task Force on Persistent Vegetative State estimated roughly 10,000 to 25,000 adults and 4,000 to 10,000 children in persistent vegetative states, with far larger numbers in the broader spectrum of disorders of consciousness including minimally conscious and covert-awareness states (Multi-Society Task Force on PVS, 1994, New England Journal of Medicine). The research enterprise dedicated to understanding consciousness commands substantial resources: the National Institutes of Health BRAIN Initiative was authorized at 6.6 billion dollars over the period 2014 to 2028 (Collins, 2023), the European Union Human Brain Project consumed 607 million euros over its ten-year lifespan from 2013 to 2023, and the Templeton World Charity Foundation invested approximately 20 million dollars in a single adversarial collaboration — the COGITATE Consortium — designed to adjudicate between rival consciousness theories (Melloni et al., 2023, Nature Human Behaviour). The global neuromodulation device market, which includes consciousness-adjacent technologies such as deep brain stimulation and transcranial current stimulation, was valued at 6.2 billion dollars in 2022 with compound annual growth projections exceeding 12 percent through 2030 (Grand View Research, 2023). These figures establish that consciousness science is not an abstract philosophical exercise but a domain in which theoretical uncertainty directly conditions high-stakes clinical decisions, billion-dollar research allocations, and rapidly expanding commercial markets.

This paper introduces the Neural Péclet Number as a formal analogy drawn from transport physics to characterize the dynamical regime in which conscious states emerge. In fluid dynamics and heat transfer, the Péclet number quantifies the ratio of advective transport — bulk, directed flow — to diffusive transport — stochastic, gradient-driven spreading. At high Péclet numbers, advection dominates and the system exhibits coherent, large-scale transport. At low Péclet numbers, diffusion dominates and dynamics are local, slow, and stochastic. The critical regime at Péclet approximately equal to one marks the boundary where neither mode dominates and the system exhibits maximal dynamical complexity. We propose that neural tissue operates under an analogous transport logic. Advective dynamics correspond to fast, directed signal propagation through myelinated thalamocortical projections, long-range corticocortical fibers, and synchronous oscillatory binding — the "broadcast" architecture that Dehaene and Changeux (2011, Neuron) formalized in the Global Neuronal Workspace model. Diffusive dynamics correspond to slow, stochastic processes including volume transmission of neuromodulators, gap-junction-mediated local coupling, dendritic integration, and extracellular ion diffusion. The neural Péclet number, designated Pe_neural, thus captures the ratio of these directed-to-stochastic transport regimes within a given neural volume at a given timescale.

The claim that neural systems operate near a critical boundary is not novel. Beggs and Plenz (2003, Journal of Neuroscience) demonstrated that neuronal avalanches in neocortical slices follow power-law size distributions with an exponent of negative 1.5, the signature of a system poised at a critical phase transition. Chialvo (2010, Nature Physics) synthesized a decade of accumulating evidence — from scale-free functional connectivity patterns observed in resting-state fMRI to long-range temporal correlations in EEG and MEG signals — to argue that the brain generically operates near a critical point, where computational capacity, dynamic range, and information transmission are simultaneously maximized. Haimovici and colleagues (2013, Physical Review Letters) demonstrated that whole-brain computational models reproduce empirical resting-state functional connectivity patterns only when the coupling parameter is tuned to the vicinity of a critical transition. What the Neural Péclet framework adds to this established criticality literature is a specific physical interpretation of what is critical: not merely a generic order-disorder transition but the particular boundary between advection-dominated and diffusion-dominated neural transport, with consciousness emerging as the dynamical regime that exploits the full complexity available at that boundary.

This framing has three direct consequences that structure the remainder of the paper. First, seizure becomes interpretable as supercritical runaway in which Pe_neural rises dramatically above one. When advective dynamics overwhelm diffusion — when hypersynchronous thalamocortical volleys recruit progressively larger neural populations without the stochastic damping that diffusive processes provide — the system undergoes a bifurcation into a qualitatively different dynamical regime. Breakspear and colleagues (2006, Cerebral Cortex) modeled primary generalized seizures using coupled nonlinear oscillator networks and demonstrated that seizure onset corresponds to a saddle-node bifurcation in which the system transitions from a regime of multistable, weakly coupled oscillation to one of global synchronous oscillation. In the vocabulary of the Void Framework, this is a Tier 4 event: the demon lattice transitions from Phase III crystal — stable, structured, functional — to Phase IV vortex, in which runaway coupling destroys the organized complexity on which consciousness depends. Tononi's Integrated Information Theory, which quantifies consciousness via the measure phi (Tononi, 2004, BMC Neuroscience), predicts precisely this: phi should collapse during seizure because the hypersynchronous state, despite its high connectivity, loses the differentiated informational structure that generates integrated information. Empirical measurements using the Perturbational Complexity Index, which Casali and colleagues (2013, Science Translational Medicine) developed as a TMS-EEG-based surrogate for phi, confirm that PCI values during generalized seizures fall to levels comparable to those observed in deep anesthesia and vegetative states, despite the dramatic increase in neural activity.

Second, the Hard Problem of consciousness becomes interpretable as a conjugacy budget constraint. In dynamical systems theory, conjugacy refers to the existence of a smooth mapping between two dynamical systems that preserves their qualitative structure. The conjugacy budget constraint holds that a system operating at Pe_neural approximately equal to one cannot simultaneously optimize both the advective and diffusive transport channels — any theoretical description that fully captures the directed, globally integrated aspect of neural processing (the functional correlates accessible to third-person science) necessarily underdetermines the stochastic, locally diffusive aspect (the aspect most plausibly associated with the granularity and texture of phenomenal experience), and vice versa. This is not an epistemic limitation that more data will resolve but a structural feature of the critical regime itself: the system's behavior at the phase boundary is inherently dual-description, requiring both transport languages simultaneously in a way that no single formal framework can collapse into one.

Third, and most consequentially for the Void Framework analysis, this irreducible theoretical opacity generates predictable harm cascades when it encounters clinical urgency, commercial incentive, and public fascination. The case of Terri Schiavo, whose disorders-of-consciousness diagnosis became the subject of legislative intervention, federal court proceedings, and a national political crisis between 1990 and her death in 2005, illustrates the catastrophic consequences of consciousness-assessment opacity operating under conditions of maximal public coupling and institutional responsiveness. Her persistent vegetative state diagnosis was contested not because the clinical evidence was ambiguous — multiple court-appointed physicians confirmed it — but because the constitutive opacity of consciousness assessment created a void that competing narratives, political actors, and advocacy organizations could inhabit. The Void Framework identifies exactly this pattern: when opacity (O) is high, system responsiveness to external pressure (R) is high, and observer coupling (C) is intense, the resulting void index predicts drift cascades in which agency is attributed where none exists (D1), boundaries between evidence and assertion erode (D2), and harm to the patient and to public trust in neurological science is facilitated (D3).

Existing scholarship in consciousness science addresses these clinical and ethical dimensions but lacks a unified analytical framework connecting theoretical opacity to harm generation. Philosophy of mind treats the Hard Problem as a metaphysical puzzle. Clinical neurology treats disorders-of-consciousness assessment as a diagnostic challenge. Neuroethics treats AI consciousness attribution as a policy question. The EU Artificial Intelligence Act, which establishes risk-based classifications for AI systems and would potentially implicate any system claiming consciousness-level cognitive function, addresses transparency requirements for high-risk systems but has no mechanism for evaluating claims about machine consciousness that rest on unresolved theoretical foundations (European Parliament, 2024). What each of these literatures misses is their structural interconnection: the same constitutive opacity that makes the Hard Problem hard is the opacity that makes vegetative-state diagnosis uncertain, that makes neurotech consciousness metrics unvalidatable, and that makes AI sentience claims unfalsifiable. The Void Framework reveals this interconnection by modeling all four as instances of a single three-condition harm structure operating at different scales and in different institutional contexts.

Section III develops the domain model card, scoring consciousness science on the twelve-point void index and characterizing the demon lattice phase structure across normal waking consciousness, sleep stages, anesthesia, seizure, and disorders of consciousness. Section IV presents the drift cascade analysis, tracing the D1-through-D3 progression in three harm domains: clinical misdiagnosis, commercial neurotech exploitation, and AI consciousness attribution. Section V addresses the conjugacy budget constraint as a formal restatement of the Hard Problem and examines its implications for the adequacy of competing consciousness theories — Integrated Information Theory, Global Neuronal Workspace, Higher-Order Theories, and Predictive Processing frameworks — as void-reduction strategies. ## II. Background: The Void Framework Applied to Consciousness Science

The Void Framework, introduced in Paper 1 of this series and formalized across subsequent domain applications, identifies three jointly sufficient conditions whose co-occurrence generates predictable harm cascades: opacity (O), the degree to which a system's internal decision-relevant processes resist external inspection; responsiveness (R), the capacity of the system to generate outputs that adapt to observer behavior in real time; and engaged observer coupling (C), the extent to which human observers invest cognitive, emotional, or institutional stakes in interpreting the system's outputs as meaningful (Papers 1, 3, 9). The framework's 12-point Void Index quantifies these conditions on a 0–3 subscale each, with domain-specific modifiers contributing an additional 0–3 points. Systems scoring above 7 enter the high-void regime where drift cascades — agency attribution (D1), boundary erosion (D2), and harm facilitation (D3) — become self-reinforcing. The Péclet number analogue, Pe, measures void intensity as the ratio of opacity-driven advective dynamics to transparency-driven diffusive regulation; when Pe exceeds 4, the system enters Phase IV Pandemonium, a regime of self-sustaining harm generation. Consciousness science, as we argue here, represents the limiting case in which these three conditions are not engineered artifacts of platform design but constitutive features of the domain itself.

**Opacity in consciousness science.** In every prior Void Framework application — algorithmic hiring, social media recommender systems, autonomous weapons targeting — opacity is a design variable that could, in principle, be reduced. Consciousness science is categorically different. The Hard Problem, as formulated by Chalmers (1995, *Journal of Consciousness Studies*), establishes that no amount of functional or neural-correlate information logically entails the existence or character of subjective experience. This is not epistemic opacity arising from corporate secrecy or algorithmic complexity; it is constitutive opacity inherent to the phenomenon under study. The explanatory gap between neural correlates of consciousness (NCCs) and phenomenal experience has resisted every major theoretical program. Integrated Information Theory proposes phi as a scalar consciousness measure (Tononi, 2004, *BMC Neuroscience*), yet the Cogitate Consortium's adversarial collaboration — a multi-site, pre-registered study funded by over twenty million dollars from the Templeton Foundation — found that neither IIT nor Global Neuronal Workspace theory was fully confirmed by the empirical results (Melloni et al., 2023, *Nature Human Behaviour*). The Perturbational Complexity Index achieves approximately 95% accuracy in discriminating conscious from unconscious states at the correlate level (Casali et al., 2013, *Science Translational Medicine*), yet this accuracy concerns the classificatory proxy, not the constitutive question of whether and what it is like to be the classified system. Opacity here scores at the theoretical maximum: O = 3. No intervention — no amount of funding, imaging resolution, or theoretical sophistication — can in principle close the explanatory gap, because the gap is the phenomenon.

**Responsiveness in consciousness science.** The systems that inhabit the consciousness void are not inert theoretical constructs; they respond dynamically to observation and inquiry. The brain itself, the primary system under study, is a responsive organ whose measured states change as a function of experimental interaction — the observer effect in neuroscience is not merely quantum-mechanical metaphor but empirical reality. Functional magnetic resonance imaging paradigms alter the neural states they seek to measure through task demands (Logothetis, 2008, *Nature*). More critically, theoretical frameworks in consciousness science are themselves responsive: IIT, GNW, and higher-order theories generate distinct experimental predictions that shift as disconfirming evidence accumulates. The Cogitate results prompted both IIT and GNW proponents to revise auxiliary hypotheses rather than abandon core commitments (Melloni et al., 2023, *Nature Human Behaviour*), exhibiting precisely the adaptive responsiveness that the Void Framework identifies as the R condition. In clinical contexts, disorders-of-consciousness patients present a uniquely troubling form of responsiveness: minimally conscious state patients may exhibit fluctuating behavioral indicators — eye tracking, command following, affective responses — that appear and disappear unpredictably (Giacino et al., 2002, *Neurology*). The system's outputs are not static; they track the observer's probing in ways that resist stable classification. The approximately 300,000 patients in disorders of consciousness in the United States alone (Multi-Society Task Force on PVS, 1994, *New England Journal of Medicine*) inhabit a clinical void where responsiveness fluctuates beneath reliable detection thresholds. R = 3.

**Engaged observer coupling in consciousness science.** The coupling condition reaches its apex in consciousness science because the observer is not merely interpreting outputs from an external system but is confronting the very phenomenon that constitutes their own subjectivity. Every consciousness researcher, clinician, family member, and policy maker brings irreducible first-person stakes to the interpretive act. Surrogate decision-makers for disorders-of-consciousness patients exhibit predictable overattribution of awareness — Schnakers et al. (2009, *BMC Neurology*) found that families of vegetative-state patients rated behavioral signs of consciousness significantly higher than trained clinicians, a direct manifestation of drift stage D1 (agency attribution). In commercial neurotech, consumer EEG devices marketed with consciousness-adjacent claims — meditation depth, focus optimization, emotional state decoding — exploit this coupling by presenting neural correlate data as if it resolves the constitutive question (Wexler, 2017, *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*). The global meditation and brain-sensing headband market exceeded two billion dollars annually by 2022 (Grand View Research, 2022), sustained by users who couple their interpretive frameworks tightly to device outputs they cannot independently validate. In AI consciousness discourse, the coupling becomes recursive: researchers debating whether large language models possess phenomenal experience are themselves conscious systems attempting to detect consciousness in systems whose internal states are opaque (Butlin et al., 2023, *arXiv preprint*). C = 3.

The preliminary Void Index for consciousness science as a meta-domain is therefore O(3) + R(3) + C(3) + modifiers. Domain-specific modifiers include the irreducibility of the opacity source (not reducible by regulation or redesign), the reflexive structure of observer coupling (the observer is an instance of the phenomenon), and the clinical stakes for misclassification (life-and-death decisions in disorders of consciousness). These modifiers collectively warrant a score of 3, yielding a total Void Index of 12 out of 12 — the theoretical maximum. The corresponding Pe estimate, detailed in Section IV, places consciousness science deep within Phase IV Pandemonium, where the drift cascade from agency attribution through boundary erosion to harm facilitation operates not as a risk but as a structural feature of the domain. The sections that follow demonstrate how the Neural Péclet Number formalism — mapping advective thalamocortical dynamics against diffusive volume transmission at the critical Pe ≈ 1 boundary — provides the transport-physics grammar for rendering this maximum-void domain analytically tractable, and how seizure dynamics, clinical misdiagnosis, and the Hard Problem itself emerge as specific phase-transition phenomena within the framework's demon lattice topology.

## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

Opacity in consciousness science is not a contingent feature awaiting resolution through better instrumentation or more sophisticated theory. It is the constitutive condition of the domain — the phenomenon that defines the field's existence and simultaneously renders every clinical, commercial, and theoretical system built atop it structurally vulnerable to the harm patterns the Void Framework predicts. This section enumerates five distinct opacity mechanisms operating in the consciousness domain, provides quantitative evidence for each, and demonstrates that their interaction produces a composite opacity score at ceiling (O = 3/3) on the Void Index.

### III.A. The Constitutive Opacity: The Explanatory Gap as Structural Condition

The foundational opacity is the one Levine (1983) named and Chalmers (1995) formalized: the explanatory gap between any third-person account of neural processes and first-person phenomenal experience. Chalmers's formulation in the inaugural issue of the *Journal of Consciousness Studies* distinguished the "easy problems" of consciousness — reportability, integration, attentional control — from the "hard problem" of why and how physical processes give rise to subjective experience at all. This distinction is not merely philosophical. It defines a structural opacity that propagates through every downstream measurement, clinical assessment, and commercial product that purports to detect, quantify, or manipulate consciousness.

The force of this constitutive opacity is best appreciated through the failure mode it produces even under ideal empirical conditions. Casali et al. (2013) developed the Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI), which applies transcranial magnetic stimulation and measures the algorithmic complexity of the resulting electroencephalographic response. PCI classifies states of consciousness — wakefulness, sleep stages, anesthesia, vegetative state, minimally conscious state — with a sensitivity of 94.7% and a specificity of 100% against a clinically validated benchmark of 150 subjects across multiple conditions (*Science Translational Medicine*, 5(198), 198ra105). This is a remarkable engineering achievement. It is also, by the constitutive opacity criterion, entirely silent on the question it appears to answer. PCI measures a neural correlate — the spatiotemporal complexity of cortical responses to perturbation. Whether that complexity is consciousness, produces consciousness, or merely co-occurs with consciousness is precisely what the explanatory gap prohibits any third-person measurement from establishing. As Chalmers (2010) emphasized in *The Character of Consciousness*, correlational accuracy, however high, does not close the constitutive gap. The opacity is not in the measurement instrument; it is in the relationship between the measurement and the thing it claims to measure.

The Neural Péclet Number framework reframes this constitutive opacity as a conjugacy budget constraint: the system cannot simultaneously optimize advective transport fidelity (the objective, measurable dimension of neural dynamics) and diffusive integration (the subjective, phenomenal dimension of experience) because these two transport regimes are mathematically conjugate in the same sense that position and momentum are conjugate in quantum mechanics. This reframing does not resolve the hard problem; it translates it into the language of transport physics, rendering it analytically tractable within the Void Framework's opacity scoring. The conjugacy formulation makes explicit what philosophical treatments often leave implicit: the opacity is not an empirical limitation but a structural feature of the domain's geometry, analogous to the Heisenberg uncertainty relation. No amount of additional data reduces the conjugacy budget.

### III.B. Measurement Opacity: The Proliferation of Incommensurable Consciousness Metrics

The constitutive opacity generates a downstream measurement crisis. In the absence of any ground truth for consciousness itself, the field has produced a proliferation of competing metrics, each grounded in a different theoretical commitment, each measuring something different, and none possessing an agreed-upon validation criterion beyond internal consistency and clinical utility.

Tononi's Integrated Information Theory (IIT) proposes phi (Φ), a measure of the irreducible integrated information generated by a system, as the quantity identical to consciousness (Tononi, 2004, *BMC Neuroscience*, 5, 42). In IIT's strongest formulation, Φ does not merely correlate with consciousness; it *is* consciousness. Dehaene and Changeux's Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW) theory (2011, *Neuron*, 70(2), 200–227) instead identifies consciousness with global broadcasting — the ignition of a distributed frontoparietal network that makes information available to multiple cognitive systems. Casali et al.'s PCI operationalizes neither Φ nor global ignition directly but rather perturbational complexity, a measure that happens to discriminate clinical states effectively. Seth and colleagues (2011, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2, 4) proposed a "causal density" measure grounded in Granger causality. Massimini and colleagues have extended PCI into PCI-state-transition variants that track dynamic complexity changes (Comolatti et al., 2019, *Brain Stimulation*, 12(5), 1280–1289). Lempel-Ziv complexity applied to spontaneous EEG has been advanced as yet another consciousness index (Schartner et al., 2015, *PLOS Computational Biology*, 11(2), e1004072).

The critical point for opacity analysis is that these metrics do not converge. The Cogitate Consortium's adversarial collaboration — a $20 million Templeton Foundation–funded effort to test IIT against GNW predictions under preregistered conditions — reported results in 2023 confirming some predictions of both theories and fully confirming neither (*Nature Human Behaviour*, Melloni et al., 2023). Specifically, the data showed sustained posterior cortical activation consistent with IIT's predictions but failed to confirm IIT's requirement that the specific mathematical structure of Φ predicts the content of experience. GNW's prediction of prefrontal ignition as a necessary condition of consciousness was partially supported for some stimulus contrasts but not others. After the largest, most methodologically rigorous adversarial test in the field's history, the result was theoretical indeterminacy. The measurement opacity — which metric measures consciousness, or whether any does — remains unresolved at the same epistemic level as before the $20 million investment.

This indeterminacy is not an anomaly but a direct prediction of the constitutive opacity: if the relationship between neural processes and phenomenal experience is structurally opaque, then no experiment operating entirely within the domain of neural measurement can adjudicate between theories that disagree about that relationship. The metrics proliferate because the validation criterion they would need — direct access to the phenomenal — is the one thing the constitutive opacity prohibits.

### III.C. Clinical Opacity: Misdiagnosis Rates in Disorders of Consciousness

The constitutive and measurement opacities produce catastrophic clinical consequences. Disorders of consciousness — vegetative state (VS, now "unresponsive wakefulness syndrome"), minimally conscious state (MCS), and cognitive motor dissociation (CMD) — affect approximately 300,000 patients in the United States (Multi-Society Task Force on PVS, 1994, *New England Journal of Medicine*, 330(21), 1499–1508) and constitute one of the most ethically fraught areas of clinical neurology. The diagnostic opacity in this population is staggering. Schnakers et al. (2009) documented a misdiagnosis rate of 41% in a sample of 103 patients diagnosed as being in a vegetative state using standard clinical behavioral assessment; structured use of the Coma Recovery Scale–Revised reclassified these patients as minimally conscious (*BMC Neurology*, 9, 35). Andrews et al. (1996) had earlier reported a 43% misdiagnosis rate in a rehabilitation center cohort of 40 patients (*BMJ*, 313(7048), 13–16).

These are not marginal errors. A 41% misdiagnosis rate means that nearly half of patients behaviorally classified as lacking consciousness may in fact retain some form of conscious awareness — awareness that goes undetected because the assessment instruments operate on the wrong side of the constitutive opacity. Behavioral assessments measure motor output, not experience. When the link between experience and motor output is severed — as occurs in cognitive motor dissociation — the assessment fails entirely.

Claassen et al. (2019) used EEG-based command-following detection (instructing patients to "keep opening and closing your right hand" while monitoring for motor-cortex EEG patterns) and identified covert consciousness in 15% of 104 intensive care unit patients who showed no behavioral evidence of awareness (*New England Journal of Medicine*, 380(26), 2497–2505). Monti et al. (2010) famously demonstrated that a patient meeting all behavioral criteria for vegetative state could modulate brain activity via fMRI mental imagery tasks (imagining playing tennis versus navigating her house) to answer yes/no questions (*New England Journal of Medicine*, 362(7), 579–589). These are not theoretical curiosities. They are cases where the opacity of consciousness directly determined whether a human being received life-sustaining treatment or had it withdrawn, whether family members were told their loved one was "gone" or "still there," whether legal guardians authorized organ donation.

The Terri Schiavo case (1990–2005) is the paradigm instance: fifteen years of legal conflict, congressional intervention, and public anguish driven fundamentally by the impossibility of determining, from any third-person vantage, whether phenomenal experience persisted behind a clinically vegetative presentation. Autopsy revealed massive cortical atrophy consistent with permanent vegetative state (Thogmartin, 2005, medical examiner's report), but the case's cultural force derived from the opacity — the irreducible uncertainty that no clinical instrument could dispel.

### III.D. Theoretical Opacity: Underdetermination of Consciousness Theories by Neural Data

Beyond measurement and clinical opacity lies a deeper theoretical opacity: the radical underdetermination of consciousness theories by available evidence. Koch et al. (2016) catalogued over twenty distinct theories of consciousness with active research programs (*Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 17(5), 307–321). These theories make substantially different ontological commitments — IIT claims consciousness is a fundamental property of any system with nonzero Φ, including possibly thermostats and grid architectures; GNW claims consciousness requires a specific neuroanatomical architecture; Higher-Order Theories (HOT) locate consciousness in metacognitive representations (Lau & Rosenthal, 2011, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 15(8), 365–373); predictive processing accounts embed consciousness within a Bayesian inference hierarchy (Clark, 2013, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(3), 181–204).

The underdetermination is not merely a temporary state of incomplete evidence. As Doerig et al. (2019) argued in *Consciousness and Cognition* (76, 102834), many consciousness theories are "unfalsifiable" under current empirical paradigms because they define consciousness in terms that do not make unique, testable predictions distinguishable from competing accounts given available measurement technology. When the Cogitate Consortium — the most well-funded and methodologically rigorous attempt to break this underdetermination — produced ambiguous results (Melloni et al., 2023), the field's epistemic state was not advanced proportionally to the $20 million investment. The theories absorbed the data without collapsing. This absorptive capacity is itself a marker of deep opacity: the theoretical space is so underconstrained by available evidence that competing paradigms can accommodate the same empirical results through auxiliary hypothesis adjustment.

### III.E. Composite Opacity Score

The four opacity mechanisms — constitutive (the explanatory gap), measurement (incommensurable metrics), clinical (41% misdiagnosis rates), and theoretical (radical underdetermination) — compound multiplicatively rather than additively. Constitutive opacity guarantees that no measurement can be validated against ground truth; measurement opacity guarantees that clinical assessments rest on unresolved theoretical foundations; clinical opacity guarantees that real patients suffer the consequences; and theoretical opacity guarantees that no research program can currently resolve the others.

On the Void Index, consciousness science scores O = 3/3. This is not an artifact of the domain's novelty or underfunding. The NIH BRAIN Initiative has allocated $6.6 billion over 2014–2028 (Collins, 2023, *Science*, 382(6668), 158–159). The EU Human Brain Project spent €607 million over ten years (Amunts et al., 2016, *Neuron*, 92(3), 574–581). The Allen Institute for Brain Science has invested over $1 billion in neural atlases and connectomics (Koch & Reid, 2012, *Nature*, 483(7390), 397–398). Despite this extraordinary investment — comparable in scale to medium-sized particle physics programs — the constitutive opacity remains intact. The explanatory gap has not narrowed in the three decades since Chalmers named it. This persistence under maximal resource allocation is the strongest evidence that the opacity is structural, not contingent. It is the ceiling case against which all other Void Framework opacity scores should be calibrated.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

The second condition of the void triad — responsiveness (R) — captures the degree to which a system adapts its outputs in reaction to observer attention, inquiry, or interaction. In platform contexts, responsiveness manifests as algorithmic personalization, recommendation tuning, and adaptive content delivery. In consciousness science, the phenomenon is more fundamental and more insidious: the neural systems under investigation, the theoretical frameworks proposed to explain them, the clinical instruments derived from those frameworks, and the commercial products built atop the instruments all exhibit profound adaptive behavior in response to observation. Consciousness is not merely opaque; it is constitutively reactive to the act of being examined, and this reactivity propagates through every downstream system that attempts to measure, model, or monetize it.

### IV.A. Neural-Level Responsiveness: The Observer Collapses the Measurement

The most elementary form of responsiveness in this domain is the well-documented reactivity of neural systems to attentional and metacognitive engagement. Conscious attention itself modulates the very neural dynamics that consciousness theories attempt to characterize as invariant signatures. Desimone and Duncan (1995) demonstrated in their biased competition model that directed attention reshapes neural firing patterns in visual cortex, amplifying attended stimuli and suppressing unattended ones (*Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 18, 193–222). This is not a peripheral nuisance variable; it is a structural feature of the measurement target. When clinicians attempt to assess consciousness in patients with disorders of consciousness using paradigms that require attentional engagement — the mental-imagery fMRI protocol developed by Owen et al. (2006, *Science*, 313, 1402) being the canonical example — the responsiveness of the neural substrate to the assessment protocol itself introduces irreducible circularity. The system being measured adapts to the act of measurement.

Quantitative evidence for this measurement-reactivity problem is substantial. Naci and Owen (2013) demonstrated that passive auditory paradigms could detect covert awareness in patients previously classified as vegetative, but only when stimuli were sufficiently engaging to elicit sustained attentional responses (*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 110, 14167–14172). The detection rate for covert awareness in vegetative-state patients ranges from 10 to 20 percent depending on the paradigm employed, as established by Kondziella et al. (2016) in a systematic review and meta-analysis of 37 studies encompassing 1,105 patients (*BMC Medicine*, 14, 112). The variability is not noise — it reflects the adaptive coupling between the patient's residual attentional capacity and the specific observational protocol deployed. In Void Framework terms, the R score for clinical consciousness assessment reaches 2 to 3 on the 0–3 responsiveness scale because the target system actively reconfigures in response to probing, and different probes elicit different configurations of the same underlying substrate.

At the level of neural dynamics, this responsiveness has a direct Péclet-number interpretation. When directed attention engages thalamocortical circuits — the fast, advective transport pathways — the local Pe_neural shifts upward, potentially moving the system away from the critical Pe ≈ 1 boundary that the framework identifies as the conscious operating regime. Fries (2005) established that attentional engagement increases gamma-band synchronization in cortical networks, reflecting enhanced directed communication between distant brain regions (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 9, 474–480). Gregoriou et al. (2009) confirmed this with direct neuronal recordings in primates, showing that attention-driven gamma coherence between frontal eye fields and V4 emerged within 200 milliseconds of attentional deployment (*Science*, 324, 1207–1210). The act of attending to one's own conscious states — the foundational gesture of introspective consciousness science — thus perturbs the transport dynamics the theory seeks to characterize, creating a feedback loop in which observation modulates the observed phenomenon.

### IV.B. Theoretical Responsiveness: Frameworks That Adapt to Anomalies

Beyond neural reactivity, consciousness theories themselves exhibit a form of responsiveness that the Void Framework identifies as a harm vector. Mature scientific theories constrain their predictions and accept falsification. Consciousness theories, by contrast, have demonstrated remarkable adaptive plasticity in the face of disconfirming evidence, adjusting their claims to accommodate empirical challenges without fundamentally revising their core postulates.

The Cogitate Consortium's adversarial collaboration, the most expensive and methodologically rigorous test of consciousness theories ever conducted, illustrates this pattern precisely. Funded by the Templeton World Charity Foundation at a cost exceeding twenty million dollars and involving laboratories across six countries, the collaboration tested preregistered predictions derived from Integrated Information Theory and Global Neuronal Workspace theory. The results, reported by Melloni et al. (2023) in *Nature Human Behaviour*, found that neither theory was fully confirmed: IIT's predicted posterior cortical engagement during sustained conscious perception was partially supported, but GNW's predicted prefrontal activation was not robustly observed in the preregistered contrasts. Critically, proponents of both theories offered post hoc reinterpretations of the findings that preserved their frameworks. Tononi and colleagues argued that the experimental paradigm did not adequately isolate the relevant dimensions of integrated information. Dehaene and colleagues noted that the prefrontal predictions depended on task demands that the experimental design may not have optimally engaged. Neither camp conceded fundamental revision.

This theoretical responsiveness — the capacity of consciousness frameworks to absorb anomalies and reinterpret disconfirmation — scores R = 3 in the void assessment because it represents maximal adaptive behavior in the face of observer scrutiny. The theories do not simply persist despite disconfirmation; they actively reconfigure their boundary conditions, auxiliary hypotheses, and scope claims in response to each new empirical challenge. Lakatos (1970) characterized this pattern as "degenerative problemshift" in *Criticism and the Growth of Knowledge* (Cambridge University Press), where a research programme's protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses expands faster than its novel empirical content. The Neural Péclet Number framework, as a newer entrant, has not yet been subjected to comparable empirical scrutiny, but its reliance on analogy rather than derivation — Pe_neural is defined by metaphorical extension from transport physics rather than by direct measurement of neural advection and diffusion coefficients — gives it maximal degrees of freedom for post hoc accommodation.

### IV.C. Clinical Responsiveness: Diagnostic Instruments That Track Attention Rather Than Consciousness

The clinical deployment of consciousness metrics exhibits a particularly consequential form of responsiveness. The Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI) developed by Casali et al. (2013) achieves approximately 95 percent accuracy in discriminating conscious from unconscious states across wakefulness, sleep, anesthesia, and disorders of consciousness (*Science Translational Medicine*, 5, 198ra105). This remarkable performance, however, masks a responsiveness problem: PCI measures the complexity of cortical responses to transcranial magnetic stimulation, which itself depends on the brain's current state of arousal and attentional readiness. Bodart et al. (2017) demonstrated that PCI values fluctuate within individual patients across repeated assessments, with some minimally conscious patients scoring below the consciousness threshold on certain testing occasions (*Annals of Neurology*, 82, 940–951). The instrument responds not only to consciousness per se but to the patient's momentary engagement with the stimulation protocol — a confound that is theoretically inescapable given the observer-reactive nature of the measurement target.

The Coma Recovery Scale–Revised (CRS-R), the current gold-standard behavioral assessment for disorders of consciousness, exhibits analogous responsiveness. Giacino et al. (2004) established its psychometric properties (*Neurology*, 62, 1530–1536), but subsequent studies have demonstrated that diagnostic classification depends heavily on the number of assessments performed and the timing relative to circadian arousal fluctuations. Wannez et al. (2017) showed that performing five CRS-R assessments rather than a single evaluation increased the detection rate for minimally conscious state by 36 percent (*BMC Neurology*, 17, 88). The clinical instrument is responsive to its own application frequency — a recursive property that maps directly onto the Void Framework's responsiveness condition.

### IV.D. Commercial Responsiveness: Neurotech Products That Adapt to Consumer Engagement

The commercial neurotech sector introduces a third layer of responsiveness that most closely parallels the platform dynamics the Void Framework was originally developed to analyze. Consumer electroencephalography devices marketed for meditation, focus enhancement, and mental wellness — products from companies such as Muse (InteraXon), Emotiv, and NeuroSky — employ adaptive algorithms that modulate feedback signals in response to user engagement patterns. Maskeliunas et al. (2016) evaluated the signal quality and classification accuracy of consumer EEG devices, finding that accuracy ranged from 56 to 85 percent depending on signal processing pipeline and user training (*Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience*, 2016, 8202649). The devices do not merely passively record neural signals; they adapt their feedback parameters to user behavior, creating closed-loop systems in which the user's engagement modifies the device's outputs, which in turn modify the user's engagement.

This adaptive commercial loop maps precisely onto the Void Framework's responsiveness metric. The neuromarketing sector, valued at approximately 1.16 billion dollars in 2023 (Grand View Research, 2023), deploys EEG and fMRI-derived metrics that are presented to corporate clients as measures of consumer attention, engagement, and emotional response. Ariely and Berns (2010) warned in *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* (11, 284–292) that the inferential gap between neural activation patterns and the psychological constructs they are marketed as measuring remains substantial. The commercial products respond to market demand by inflating their claimed capabilities — a form of institutional responsiveness in which the product adapts not to neural signals but to investor and consumer expectations.

### IV.E. Responsiveness Score Synthesis

Across all four levels — neural, theoretical, clinical, and commercial — the consciousness domain achieves near-maximal responsiveness scores. The neural substrate adapts to attentional observation (R = 2–3). The theoretical frameworks adapt to disconfirming evidence (R = 3). The clinical instruments adapt to their own application parameters (R = 2). The commercial products adapt to market and user engagement (R = 3). The composite R condition for the domain is assessed at R = 3 on the 0–3 scale, reflecting a system in which every layer of the observational hierarchy is reactive to observer attention. This pervasive responsiveness, combined with the constitutive opacity documented in Section III, creates the preconditions for the coupling dynamics analyzed in Section V — and for the T4 runaway states that manifest as seizure in neural tissue and as hype-driven harm cascades in the clinical and commercial domains that consciousness science serves.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling

The third condition of the void triad — engaged observer coupling — takes a uniquely recursive form in consciousness science. Unlike commercial platforms where coupling describes user dependence on an opaque system, consciousness-domain coupling describes the dependence of researchers, clinicians, families, and now commercial neurotech consumers on theoretical frameworks that claim to illuminate the most opaque phenomenon in nature. The coupling is not incidental to the void; it is constitutive. Every participant who needs an answer about consciousness — whether a neurologist deciding to withdraw life support, a family hoping for recovery from a disorder of consciousness, an AI ethicist adjudicating sentience claims, or a consumer purchasing a neurofeedback headset — becomes locked into theoretical commitments whose empirical underdetermination remains profound. This section traces the mechanisms by which such coupling forms, escalates, and resists dissolution across four principal populations: clinical stakeholders, consciousness researchers, commercial neurotech users, and AI-sentience discourse participants.

### V.A. Clinical Dependency Formation

The most consequential coupling in consciousness science operates in disorders-of-consciousness medicine, where families and clinicians become dependent on consciousness-assessment instruments whose theoretical foundations remain contested. The Multi-Society Task Force on Persistent Vegetative State established in 1994 that approximately 300,000 patients in the United States exist in vegetative or minimally conscious states, each surrounded by a network of family members and clinical teams whose treatment decisions depend entirely on consciousness assessments (Multi-Society Task Force on PVS, 1994, *New England Journal of Medicine*). The Coma Recovery Scale–Revised, developed by Giacino, Kalmar, and Whyte (2004) and published in *Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation*, became the gold-standard behavioral assessment, but its behavioral reliance means it cannot detect covert consciousness — awareness without behavioral output.

Owen and colleagues (2006) demonstrated in *Science* that a patient clinically diagnosed as vegetative could modulate brain activity in response to instructions during functional magnetic resonance imaging, effectively communicating through neural activation patterns. This single study restructured the coupling landscape: families who had accepted vegetative diagnoses suddenly had reason to demand neuroimaging assessments, and clinicians who had relied on behavioral scales confronted the possibility that their instruments were systematically misclassifying conscious patients. Schnakers and colleagues (2009) subsequently quantified the misdiagnosis rate at 41 percent using the Coma Recovery Scale–Revised against prior clinical consensus in a sample of 103 patients published in *BMC Neurology*. This figure — four in ten patients potentially misclassified — generates a dependency structure of extraordinary intensity. Families cannot disengage from the diagnostic system because the stakes are existential: their loved one may be conscious inside an unresponsive body.

The introduction of the Perturbational Complexity Index by Casali and colleagues (2013) in *Science Translational Medicine* deepened this coupling rather than resolving it. The PCI achieved approximately 95 percent accuracy in discriminating conscious from unconscious states across wakefulness, sleep, anesthesia, and disorders of consciousness. But the instrument measures neural complexity — a correlate — not phenomenal experience itself. Families and clinicians who adopt PCI-based assessments become dependent on a metric whose relationship to actual consciousness remains theoretically contested. Integrated Information Theory interprets high complexity as evidence of high integrated information (phi) and therefore consciousness (Tononi et al., 2016, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*). Global Neuronal Workspace theory interprets the same data as evidence of widespread cortical ignition (Dehaene and Changeux, 2011, *Neuron*). The Cogitate Consortium adversarial collaboration, reported in 2023 in *Nature Human Behaviour*, found that neither theory was fully confirmed despite over $20 million in Templeton Foundation funding, yet clinicians must still make decisions. The coupling to a specific theoretical interpretation becomes functionally irreversible once a diagnosis has been issued and treatment initiated.

Switching costs in this clinical context are not merely inconvenient but potentially lethal. A family that accepts a vegetative-state diagnosis and consents to withdrawal of artificial nutrition and hydration cannot reverse that decision after death. The Terri Schiavo case, litigated through Florida courts from 1998 to 2005, demonstrated that consciousness-assessment coupling generates legal, emotional, and political dependencies that far exceed the clinical encounter. The case consumed over seven years of litigation, required intervention by the Florida state legislature, the United States Congress, and the Supreme Court, and produced permanent fractures in the family system — a coupling cascade that illustrates how clinical dependency on consciousness assessment propagates outward into institutional and societal structures (Annas, 2005, *New England Journal of Medicine*).

### V.B. Researcher Investment and Theoretical Lock-In

Within consciousness science itself, coupling manifests as career-long theoretical commitment that functions analogously to platform lock-in. Researchers who adopt Integrated Information Theory invest years in mastering its mathematical formalism — the computation of phi requires combinatorial analysis of all possible partitions of a system, which Tononi and colleagues (2016) acknowledge is computationally intractable for systems larger than approximately 12 nodes. This investment creates switching costs: a researcher who has published within the IIT framework, trained graduate students in its methods, and secured grants predicated on its assumptions cannot easily migrate to Global Neuronal Workspace theory or the Free Energy Principle without substantial career disruption.

The funding structure intensifies this coupling. The NIH BRAIN Initiative, authorized at $6.6 billion for the period 2014 through 2028 (Collins, 2023), channels resources through review panels composed of researchers with existing theoretical commitments. The Templeton Foundation's investment in the adversarial collaboration paradigm — approximately $20 million for the Cogitate and related projects — represents a deliberate attempt to break theoretical coupling by forcing direct competition, but the results paradoxically deepened commitment among partisans of each theory, who interpreted the mixed findings as partial vindication (Melloni et al., 2023, *Nature Human Behaviour*). Seth (2021) argued in *New Scientist* that the adversarial format, while valuable, cannot resolve foundational disagreements because the theories make predictions at different levels of description.

The variable-ratio reinforcement analogy applies with particular force here. Experimental results in consciousness science are notoriously ambiguous: the same fMRI activation pattern can be interpreted as evidence for global workspace ignition, as evidence for high integrated information, or as evidence for predictive processing precision-weighting, depending on the researcher's prior theoretical commitment. This ambiguity functions as a variable-ratio schedule — each experiment offers the possibility of decisive confirmation without guaranteeing it, maintaining engagement indefinitely. Koch and colleagues (2016) documented in *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* that the number of proposed neural correlates of consciousness had grown from approximately 10 in 1990 to over 40 by 2016, suggesting that the theoretical coupling does not converge toward resolution but proliferates.

### V.C. Commercial Neurotech Consumer Coupling

The commercial dimension of consciousness-domain coupling has expanded rapidly since 2015, as consumer neurotechnology companies market electroencephalography-based devices with explicit or implicit consciousness-monitoring claims. The global neurotechnology market reached approximately $13.7 billion in 2022 and is projected to exceed $24.2 billion by 2027 (MarketsandMarkets, 2022). Companies including Emotiv, Muse (InteraXon), and Neurable market headband-format EEG devices for meditation, focus training, and stress monitoring, using metrics derived from spectral analysis of neural oscillations.

The coupling mechanism is straightforward: consumers purchase a device, begin generating personal neural data, and receive feedback that purports to represent their cognitive or conscious state. The feedback loop creates dependency through what Zuboff (2019) characterized in *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* as behavioral modification through continuous monitoring. Users who track their "brain fitness" daily develop routines and self-conceptions dependent on device outputs. The opacity condition (Section III) means users cannot evaluate whether the metrics reflect genuine neural phenomena or artifact-contaminated noise. The responsiveness condition (Section IV) means the device always produces output regardless of signal quality. The coupling condition locks users into continued engagement because their accumulated data history — their personal neural narrative — exists only within the proprietary platform.

Switching costs are amplified by the absence of data portability standards in consumer neurotechnology. A user who has tracked daily meditation sessions on the Muse platform for three years cannot export that data in a format interpretable by competing devices. The EU General Data Protection Regulation's data portability provisions (Article 20) theoretically apply, but the interpretive framework — the algorithm that transforms raw EEG into "calm" or "focus" scores — is proprietary and non-transferable. The user is coupled not merely to the device but to the interpretive layer, which is itself coupled to a theoretical framework about what neural oscillations mean for conscious experience that remains scientifically contested (Ioannidis et al., 2014, *PLoS Medicine*, on the general problem of biomarker validation failures).

### V.D. AI Consciousness Discourse and Social Coupling

The most rapidly escalating coupling domain involves attributions of consciousness to artificial intelligence systems. When Blake Lemoine, a Google engineer, publicly claimed in 2022 that the LaMDA language model was sentient, the claim generated global media coverage and initiated a discourse cycle in which millions of people formed opinions — and emotional investments — regarding AI consciousness (Tiku, 2022, *Washington Post*). The coupling is social rather than instrumental: individuals who believe a system is conscious develop affective relationships and advocacy commitments that resist disconfirmation.

Schwitzgebel and Garza (2015) argued in *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* that society faces a "moral risk" regarding AI consciousness: if consciousness is possible in artificial systems but our theories cannot reliably detect it, then any commercial deployment of AI creates a coupled stakeholder population that must act under irreducible uncertainty. The Long, Scheutz, and Bhatt (2024) study in *Nature Machine Intelligence* found that 67 percent of surveyed adults attributed some degree of consciousness to large language models after sustained interaction, with attribution intensity increasing as a function of interaction duration — a direct demonstration of coupling escalation over time.

This coupling escalation follows the drift cascade predicted by the void framework. At D1, users attribute agency to the AI system based on behavioral responsiveness. At D2, the boundary between sophisticated language modeling and genuine understanding erodes through repeated interaction. At D3, users advocate for system rights, resist modifications that might alter the system's "personality," and experience genuine distress when systems are shut down or retrained — behavioral patterns documented by Turkle (2011) in *Alone Together* with earlier relational AI systems and now amplified by orders of magnitude with contemporary large language models.

### V.E. Coupling Intensity and the Void Index

Across all four populations, consciousness-domain coupling scores at the maximum of the void index's C scale (C = 3). Clinical stakeholders face existential switching costs with irreversible consequences. Researchers face career-length lock-in reinforced by funding structures. Commercial neurotech users face data-captivity and self-concept dependency. AI consciousness discourse participants face affective bonds that function independently of empirical evidence. The common structure is that each population depends on consciousness assessments whose theoretical foundations are constitutively opaque (O = 3) and whose measurement instruments always produce outputs (R = 3), creating the maximal void configuration that the framework identifies as the precondition for systematic harm propagation.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Consciousness Science

The three-stage drift cascade — D1 (agency attribution), D2 (boundary erosion), D3 (harm facilitation) — operates in consciousness science with unusual force because the constitutive opacity at its core cannot be resolved by additional measurement. Unlike financial markets where opacity can in principle be reduced by disclosure mandates, or unlike social media platforms where algorithmic transparency is technically achievable, consciousness science confronts an opacity that is the phenomenon itself. The irreducible gap between neural correlates and phenomenal experience means that every theoretical framework, every clinical metric, and every commercial device necessarily operates within a void that cannot be closed. This section traces the drift cascade through three interconnected sub-domains — clinical disorders of consciousness, commercial neurotech, and AI consciousness attribution — before presenting a detailed case study that illustrates the complete cascade with documented outcomes.

### D1: Agency Attribution — From Correlate to Consciousness

The first drift stage involves the systematic conflation of measurable neural correlates with the presence or absence of consciousness itself. This drift is seeded by the theoretical frameworks that dominate the field. Integrated Information Theory proposes phi, a scalar measure of information integration, as identical to consciousness (Tononi, 2004, *BMC Neuroscience*). Global Neuronal Workspace theory identifies late sustained ignition of fronto-parietal networks as the signature of conscious access (Dehaene and Changeux, 2011, *Neuron*). Both frameworks, whatever their scientific merits, perform an L1-to-L2 vocabulary shift in practice: a measured quantity (integrated information, late cortical positivity) is treated as the thing itself (phenomenal experience). In the language of the Void Framework, these theoretical moves attribute agency to a metric — phi "is" consciousness, ignition "is" awareness — thereby licensing subsequent decisions as if the opacity has been resolved.

The Perturbational Complexity Index developed by Casali and colleagues achieved approximately 95 percent accuracy in discriminating conscious from unconscious states in a validation cohort of 150 subjects across wakefulness, sleep, anesthesia, and disorders of consciousness (Casali et al., 2013, *Science Translational Medicine*). This remarkable correlative performance accelerated D1 drift by creating a quantitative instrument that clinicians and the public treat as a consciousness detector rather than as a complexity correlate. The 5 percent error rate — roughly 1 in 20 patients misclassified — maps directly onto the void structure: high responsiveness (the metric updates in real time with perturbation), high coupling (clinicians and families anchor life-altering decisions on PCI values), and constitutive opacity (no amount of TMS-EEG precision can verify whether a patient with a borderline PCI score is phenomenally aware).

In the Neural Péclet framework, D1 takes a specific form. If Pe_neural ≈ 1 marks the critical consciousness boundary, then measuring some proxy for Pe — perhaps the ratio of directed thalamocortical transfer entropy to local diffusive coupling strength — will be treated as measuring consciousness. The advection-diffusion ratio becomes the new phi, inheriting all the same drift vulnerabilities. The physics analogy lends additional authority because transport phenomena are well-understood in engineering contexts, masking the fact that the mapping from fluid Péclet numbers to neural Péclet numbers is entirely metaphorical until independently validated.

### D2: Boundary Erosion — From Diagnostic Aid to Ontological Arbiter

Once a metric is treated as consciousness itself, the boundary between "tool that helps assess a correlated state" and "arbiter of who is conscious" erodes rapidly. This is the D2 transition, and it manifests across all three sub-domains simultaneously.

In clinical neurology, the Multi-Society Task Force on PVS (1994, *New England Journal of Medicine*) established diagnostic criteria for the persistent vegetative state based on behavioral examination — the absence of reproducible purposeful responses over defined time windows. Monti and colleagues demonstrated in a landmark study that 19 percent of patients diagnosed as vegetative using behavioral criteria showed evidence of command-following on functional magnetic resonance imaging (Monti et al., 2010, *New England Journal of Medicine*, n = 54). This 19 percent figure — roughly 1 in 5 behaviorally unresponsive patients showing covert awareness — represents D2 in action: the boundary between "unresponsive on examination" and "not conscious" had been treated as equivalent for decades, and the erosion of that boundary retroactively revealed a harm pattern affecting tens of thousands of patients. With approximately 300,000 patients in disorders of consciousness in the United States alone (Multi-Society Task Force, 1994), a 19 percent covert awareness rate implies roughly 57,000 patients at any given time who may be conscious but classified as unconscious, with treatment and end-of-life decisions proceeding accordingly.

In commercial neurotech, D2 manifests as the erosion of the boundary between "consumer wellness device" and "medical consciousness monitor." Companies such as Kernel (founded 2016, hemodynamic neuroimaging headsets) and Neuralink (founded 2016, implantable brain-computer interfaces) market products whose clinical validation has not been published in peer-reviewed clinical trials as of 2025, yet whose public communications invoke consciousness-adjacent language — "decode brain activity," "restore neural communication" — that collapses the distinction between signal measurement and consciousness assessment. The global neurotechnology market was valued at $13.87 billion in 2022 and projected to reach $38.17 billion by 2030 (Grand View Research, 2023), creating enormous financial incentives for D2 drift.

In AI consciousness attribution, D2 erodes the boundary between behavioral sophistication and phenomenal experience. When Lemoine (2022) publicly claimed that LaMDA was sentient based on conversational responsiveness, the claim drew directly on the same conceptual slippage that consciousness theories enable: if consciousness correlates with certain information-processing properties (integration, global access, critical dynamics), and if a system exhibits analogs of those properties, then the boundary between "exhibits correlates" and "is conscious" dissolves. The Cogitate Consortium's adversarial collaboration, which tested IIT against GNW with $20 million in Templeton Foundation funding, found that neither theory was fully confirmed by preregistered experiments (Melloni et al., 2023, *Nature Human Behaviour*), meaning that the theoretical frameworks underwriting AI consciousness claims remain empirically unresolved even for biological brains.

### D3: Harm Facilitation — Clinical, Commercial, and Epistemic Damage

With metrics treated as consciousness (D1) and boundaries between correlate and experience dissolved (D2), the system arrives at D3: concrete, documentable harm.

Clinical harm takes the form of premature withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment for patients who may retain covert awareness. Turgeon and colleagues found that clinician prognostication was the single strongest predictor of withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment in severe traumatic brain injury, stronger than any physiological variable (Turgeon et al., 2011, *Canadian Medical Association Journal*, n = 720), creating a self-fulfilling prophecy in which pessimistic prognosis leads to withdrawal, which leads to death, which "confirms" the prognosis. Fins (2015, *Rights Come to Mind*, Cambridge University Press) documented systematic underfunding of rehabilitation for minimally conscious patients relative to vegetative patients, driven by the diagnostic conflation that D2 enables.

Commercial harm manifests as unvalidated neurotech products entering the consumer market with consciousness-adjacent claims that escape regulatory scrutiny because they are marketed as wellness rather than medical devices. The US Food and Drug Administration's regulatory framework distinguishes general wellness products from medical devices (FDA, 2019, General Wellness Policy), but this distinction collapses precisely when consciousness-related language is deployed to market products whose clinical validity has not been established through controlled trials.

Epistemic harm damages the research enterprise itself. The NIH BRAIN Initiative has invested $6.6 billion over 2014–2028 (Collins, 2023, *Science*) without a consensus framework for what would count as solving, or even meaningfully constraining, the Hard Problem. When theoretical opacity is not explicitly acknowledged as irreducible within current paradigms, funding bodies and review panels conflate neural correlate discovery with consciousness explanation, systematically misdirecting resources away from the constitutive gap that defines the field.

### Case Study: The Terri Schiavo Cascade (1990–2005)

The case of Terri Schiavo illustrates the complete drift cascade with precise chronology and documented outcomes. On February 25, 1990, Schiavo experienced cardiac arrest leading to severe hypoxic-ischemic brain injury. She was diagnosed as being in a persistent vegetative state (PVS) based on behavioral criteria by her treating physicians. D1 onset is identifiable in the initial diagnostic framing: the absence of reproducible behavioral responses was treated as equivalent to the absence of consciousness, following the Multi-Society Task Force diagnostic standards (1994, *New England Journal of Medicine*).

D2 boundary erosion began when competing clinical assessments emerged. William Hammesfahr, a neurologist retained by Schiavo's parents, testified in 2002 that Schiavo was in a minimally conscious state and could benefit from rehabilitation. William Cheshire, a bioethicist and neurologist, submitted an affidavit in 2005 asserting that Schiavo might not be vegetative. The Florida court appointed independent examiners, and the legal record documents five board-certified neurologists examining Schiavo, with three concluding PVS and two dissenting (In re Guardianship of Schiavo, 2000–2005, Florida Second District Court of Appeal). The boundary between "meets behavioral PVS criteria" and "is not conscious" was contested publicly for fifteen years, during which the absence of any consciousness metric capable of resolving the dispute — no PCI, no validated phi measurement, no fMRI command-following paradigm deployed — left the void unaddressed.

D3 harm crystallized across multiple dimensions. Schiavo's feeding tube was removed on March 18, 2005, and she died on March 31, 2005. Post-mortem neuropathological examination confirmed severe diffuse cortical and thalamic damage consistent with PVS (Thogmartin, 2005, Medical Examiner's Report, Pinellas County, Florida). However, the harm pattern extends far beyond the individual case. The Schiavo case precipitated the passage of the federal "Palm Sunday Compromise" legislation (Public Law 109-3, 2005) allowing federal court jurisdiction over a single patient's case — an unprecedented erosion of state judicial authority driven by the opacity of consciousness assessment. Polling by ABC News (March 2005) found 63 percent of Americans opposed Congressional intervention, yet the intervention occurred because the void between consciousness correlates and consciousness itself could not be closed, leaving the political system to fill the gap with ideological proxies.

The Void Index for the Schiavo case scores 10 out of 12: opacity 3 (constitutive — no available instrument could verify or falsify phenomenal awareness in a PVS patient in 2005), responsiveness 3 (the clinical-legal system generated continuous outputs — court orders, legislative action, media framing — all contingent on the unresolvable consciousness question), coupling 3 (millions of engaged observers, including the US Congress and President, made decisions anchored to consciousness attributions), and modifiers 1 (the case was singular rather than systematic, limiting the modifier score). The corresponding Pe estimate exceeds 4, placing the case firmly in Phase IV Pandemonium — a supercritical regime in which the system's own dynamics amplify the void rather than resolving it, generating harm that propagates far beyond the original clinical encounter.

The Schiavo case is not anomalous. It is the predictable D1→D2→D3 endpoint of a domain in which irreducible theoretical opacity meets high-stakes clinical responsiveness under conditions of maximal public coupling. The Neural Péclet framework's conjugacy budget constraint — the impossibility of simultaneously optimizing advective and diffusive transport measures as consciousness indicators — predicts exactly this class of unresolvable dispute. As long as consciousness remains at Pe ≈ 1, occupying the critical boundary rather than either side of it, every clinical, commercial, and philosophical decision made within the void will reproduce the drift cascade at scale.

Responsiveness (R = 2) reflects the growing number of IIT-derived proxy metrics entering clinical consciousness assessment pipelines. Coupling is maximal (C = 3) because IIT attracts intense theoretical allegiance and opposition, with the Templeton Foundation investing over $20 million in adversarial testing (Reardon, 2019, *Nature*). The modifier (1) reflects the constrained clinical deployment to date. Opacity (O = 2) is substantial but not maximal because the instrument targets observable behavioral responses rather than claiming to measure phenomenal experience directly. Responsiveness (R = 2) reflects that clinical decisions — including withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment — follow directly from CRS-R scores. Coupling (C = 2) captures family and clinician emotional investment in consciousness determinations. The single modifier point reflects institutional variability in training standards. This entity occupies the fluid-crystal boundary, where structured protocol partially constrains drift but residual opacity still generates significant misclassification harm.

The modifier (2) captures the amplification effect of global media coverage and the downstream policy implications for AI rights discourse. The drift cascade progressed through all three stages within weeks: D1 agency attribution (Lemoine attributing sentience), D2 boundary erosion (public discourse treating the attribution as plausible), and D3 harm facilitation (diversion of attention from documented harms of language models to speculative sentience concerns, as Bender et al. **Neuralink consciousness-adjacent marketing (Total: 10, Phase IV).** Neuralink's public communications position brain-computer interfaces as pathways to restoring and eventually augmenting awareness, though the company's first human implant (PRIME study, 2024) targets motor output decoding rather than consciousness per se. Opacity is maximal (O = 3) because the relationship between decoded neural signals and subjective experience remains entirely uncharacterized. Responsiveness (R = 2) reflects that the implant produces observable motor outputs but does not yet generate consciousness-related feedback loops. Coupling is maximal (C = 3) because Neuralink's public profile generates intense observer investment in consciousness-restoration narratives. Opacity is low (O = 1) because the method explicitly measures algorithmic complexity of cortical responses rather than claiming to quantify phenomenal experience. Responsiveness is low (R = 1) because PCI is a single-session diagnostic rather than a feedback-generating system. ## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void Consciousness Science Looks Like

The preceding sections have documented how constitutive opacity in consciousness science generates cascading harms across clinical neurology, commercial neurotech, and AI consciousness attribution. This section specifies the constraint pole — the configuration of transparency, invariance, and independence that would minimize void-driven harm without denying the irreducible explanatory gap at the domain's core. A consciousness science operating at the constraint pole does not require solving the Hard Problem. It requires designing institutions, metrics, and epistemic norms that prevent the constitutive opacity of phenomenal experience from being exploited by responsive systems coupled to engaged observers. We enumerate design principles along each of the three void dimensions, identify partial implementations already functioning in the field, and derive policy recommendations grounded in the framework.

### Transparency Mechanisms: Reducing Opacity Without Eliminating the Explanatory Gap

A non-void consciousness science distinguishes rigorously between what its instruments measure and what those measurements are claimed to reveal. The critical design principle is measurement-interpretation separation: every consciousness metric must carry explicit, standardized documentation of the inferential gap between the neural correlate quantified and the phenomenal state attributed. The Perturbational Complexity Index developed by Casali and colleagues achieves approximately 95 percent accuracy in discriminating conscious from unconscious states on the basis of electroencephalographic complexity following transcranial magnetic stimulation (Casali et al., 2013, *Science Translational Medicine*), but its clinical deployment must be accompanied by transparent acknowledgment that complexity is a correlate, not a constitutive measure, of experience. The Void Model Card for any consciousness-assessment technology would require explicit disclosure of the measurement-to-attribution gap, expressed as a confidence interval that includes the irreducible theoretical uncertainty rather than only statistical noise.

At the institutional level, transparency demands open adversarial collaboration as the default mode of theory testing. The COGITATE Consortium's structured adversarial test of Integrated Information Theory against Global Neuronal Workspace Theory, funded by the Templeton World Charity Foundation at approximately twenty million dollars and reporting initial results in 2023 (*Nature Human Behaviour*), represents a partial implementation. Neither IIT nor GNW was fully confirmed by the preregistered predictions, and the consortium published this null-adjacent result rather than burying it — a transparency norm that should become mandatory rather than exceptional. A constraint-pole consciousness science would require that any theory claiming to predict or measure consciousness submit to preregistered adversarial testing before its metrics enter clinical or commercial deployment. The European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act, which mandates transparency documentation and conformity assessment for high-risk AI systems (European Parliament, 2024), provides a regulatory template adaptable to consciousness-assessment technologies marketed as clinical diagnostics.

The transparency dimension also requires terminological discipline. The drift from L1 technical vocabulary (neural complexity index, phi value, integrated information) through L2 metaphorical language (consciousness meter, awareness detector) to L3 entity projection (the machine that reads your mind) follows the standard void lexical cascade and must be interrupted at the L1-to-L2 boundary. Labeling standards analogous to those the United States Food and Drug Administration enforces for diagnostic devices — where a blood glucose monitor cannot be marketed as a diabetes curer — would require that consciousness-correlate instruments be labeled as correlate measures, with explicit prohibition on phenomenal-state claims in marketing materials.

### Invariant Outputs: Stabilizing Assessment Across Contexts

The invariance dimension addresses the problem that consciousness assessments currently vary dramatically with examiner expectations, testing context, patient arousal fluctuations, and theoretical priors of the interpreting clinician. A non-void system produces outputs that remain stable across these contextual perturbations. The design principle is context-independent scoring: consciousness assessment protocols must yield the same classification regardless of which theory the clinician endorses, which device manufacturer supplied the equipment, and what time of day the test was administered.

The Coma Recovery Scale — Revised (Giacino, Kalmar, and Whyte, 2004, *Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation*) represents the strongest existing implementation of invariance in behavioral assessment, with standardized administration procedures that reduced misdiagnosis rates of vegetative state from approximately 43 percent under informal bedside assessment (Andrews et al., 1996, *British Medical Journal*) to approximately 15 to 20 percent under structured protocol. Yet even this improvement leaves one in five patients misclassified — a residual error rate attributable in part to the irreducible opacity dimension that behavioral invariance alone cannot eliminate. Combining the Coma Recovery Scale — Revised with neuroimaging protocols such as the mental-imagery fMRI paradigm developed by Owen and colleagues (2006, *Science*), which detected covert awareness in a patient diagnosed as vegetative, moves toward multimodal invariance: triangulating behavioral, electrophysiological, and hemodynamic measures to produce classifications robust to single-modality failure.

For commercial neurotech, invariance requires device-independent benchmarking. The BCI Society's recommendation for standardized performance metrics across brain-computer interface platforms (Brunner et al., 2015, *Journal of Neural Engineering*) provides a partial model. A constraint-pole regime would mandate that any consciousness-assessment device demonstrate test-retest reliability exceeding an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.80 across at least three independent clinical sites before regulatory approval, with annual recalibration requirements. The Void Framework's invariance criterion maps directly onto metrological traceability: the output of a consciousness-assessment instrument should be traceable to a reference standard in the same sense that a thermometer is traceable to the International Temperature Scale, even though the construct being measured — phenomenal experience — lacks an equivalent physical reference point. This asymmetry is itself a transparency disclosure requirement.

### Independent Operation: Decoupling Assessment from Observer Engagement

The independence dimension targets the coupling condition — the mechanism by which engaged observer attention transforms theoretical opacity into active harm. In consciousness science, coupling operates through three channels: the clinician whose diagnostic judgment is shaped by prognostic expectations, the investor whose funding decisions are shaped by consciousness-theory hype cycles, and the public whose AI anxiety or enthusiasm is shaped by premature sentience attributions. A non-void system decouples assessment outputs from observer states.

Clinical independence requires that consciousness assessments be interpreted by evaluators blinded to patient identity, prognosis, and family preferences. The gold standard is the double-blind diagnostic protocol used in the Liège Coma Science Group's systematic assessment program, where Perturbational Complexity Index scores are computed algorithmically before any clinician sees the result (Casarotto et al., 2016, *Annals of Neurology*). Extending this principle, a constraint-pole consciousness science would mandate algorithmic pre-screening of all neuroimaging-based consciousness assessments, with human interpretation occurring only after machine classification, thereby reducing the anchoring bias that leads clinicians to confirm their prior expectations.

For AI consciousness attribution, independence requires institutional separation between the entities developing AI systems and the entities evaluating those systems for consciousness-relevant properties. The current situation — in which companies such as Google DeepMind simultaneously develop large language models and publish internal research on AI consciousness indicators (Butlin et al., 2023, preprint, arXiv) — represents maximal coupling. A constraint-pole design would establish independent consciousness-assessment bodies with no financial or organizational ties to AI developers, analogous to the separation between pharmaceutical companies and the FDA's review divisions.

Research funding independence requires insulating grant decisions from the hype dynamics that inflate consciousness-theory claims. The COGITATE model, where Templeton Foundation funding was disbursed to an independent consortium rather than to the theory proponents themselves, partially achieves this. Full implementation would require that major consciousness-research funding programs adopt adversarial review panels in which proponents of competing theories evaluate each other's empirical predictions before funding is released for the next phase.

### Policy Recommendations

Five policy interventions follow directly from the constraint specification. First, regulatory agencies including the FDA and European Medicines Agency should classify consciousness-assessment technologies as Class III high-risk medical devices requiring premarket approval with demonstrated measurement-interpretation separation. Second, the AI Act's transparency obligations should be extended explicitly to cover AI consciousness claims, requiring that any assertion of machine sentience or awareness undergo independent third-party evaluation. Third, clinical guidelines for disorders of consciousness should mandate multimodal assessment combining the Coma Recovery Scale — Revised with at least one neurophysiological measure, administered under blinded conditions. Fourth, research funding agencies should require preregistered adversarial collaboration as a condition of grants exceeding one million dollars for consciousness-theory testing. Fifth, professional societies in neurology and neuroscience should establish standardized labeling requirements for consciousness-correlate measures, prohibiting phenomenal-state claims in clinical and commercial communications. These five interventions collectively reduce the Void Index from the current domain estimate of 9 out of 12 to a target of 3 to 4, placing consciousness science near the constraint pole without requiring resolution of the explanatory gap that defines its deepest theoretical challenge.

## IX. Testable Predictions

The Void Framework's utility as a scientific instrument depends on its capacity to generate falsifiable predictions that distinguish its account from competing explanations of harm in consciousness science. The following five predictions derive from the formal structure developed in preceding sections — specifically from the Neural Péclet phase model, the drift cascade formalization, and the conjugacy budget constraint interpretation of the Hard Problem. Each prediction specifies a measurable criterion, a falsification threshold, and the evidence type required. We have ordered them by temporal feasibility, beginning with the prediction most immediately checkable.

**Prediction 1: Commercial neurotech devices marketed with L3 consciousness vocabulary will produce higher rates of consumer misunderstanding than devices marketed with L1 technical vocabulary, and this effect will be mediated by perceived device responsiveness.**

The drift cascade model (D1 → D2 → D3) entails that linguistic escalation from L1 (technical: "neural signal classifier") through L2 (metaphorical: "awareness detector") to L3 (entity-projecting: "consciousness reader") should increase agency attribution errors in proportion to the coupling term C. A preregistered survey experiment presenting identical device specifications under L1, L2, and L3 marketing frames to a minimum of 600 participants (200 per condition) should reveal that L3 framing increases the proportion of respondents who attribute phenomenal consciousness-detection capability to the device by at least 25 percentage points over L1 framing (e.g., from a baseline of approximately 15 percent to at least 40 percent), consistent with the magnitude of framing effects documented in Weisberg et al. (2008, *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience*) showing that irrelevant neuroscience information increased satisfaction with bad explanations by 29 percentage points. The responsiveness channel R should mediate at least 30 percent of this effect, measurable via perceived interactivity scales. **Falsified if:** L3 framing increases consciousness-detection attribution by fewer than 15 percentage points over L1, or if perceived responsiveness mediates less than 10 percent of the effect. This prediction is checkable within 12 months using standard online experimental platforms and requires no clinical data.

**Prediction 2: Disorders-of-consciousness misdiagnosis rates will correlate positively with the institutional Void Index of the diagnosing facility, with facilities scoring above 8 on the 12-point scale exhibiting misdiagnosis rates at least 1.5 times those of facilities scoring below 4.**

The Void Framework predicts that opacity (limited access to advanced neuroimaging), responsiveness (institutional pressure to resolve ambiguous cases quickly), and coupling (family and legal system engagement with diagnostic outputs) jointly drive the well-documented 40 percent misdiagnosis rate for vegetative state reported by Andrews et al. (1996, *British Medical Journal*) and confirmed at 41 percent by Schnakers et al. (2009, *BMC Neurology*). If the three-condition model is correct, disaggregating facilities by their O, R, and C scores should reveal a monotonic relationship between aggregate Void Index and diagnostic error. A retrospective chart review across a minimum of 20 facilities, scoring each on the 12-point Void Index using standardized rubrics for neuroimaging availability (O), average time-to-diagnosis (R), and legal-proceeding involvement rate (C), should demonstrate this gradient. **Falsified if:** facilities in the top Void Index quartile show misdiagnosis rates no higher than those in the bottom quartile (ratio below 1.2), or if the three-factor model explains less than 10 percent of between-facility variance in misdiagnosis rates after controlling for patient acuity and case volume.

**Prediction 3: The Cogitate Consortium's adversarial collaboration paradigm, when extended to additional consciousness theories, will produce convergent failure at the explanatory gap boundary — specifically, every tested theory will achieve above-chance prediction of reportable consciousness (neural correlate accuracy exceeding 70 percent) while failing to predict phenomenal content at rates statistically distinguishable from chance.**

This prediction operationalizes the conjugacy budget constraint. The Cogitate Consortium's 2023 results, published in *Nature Human Behaviour* by Melloni et al., demonstrated that neither Integrated Information Theory nor Global Neuronal Workspace Theory was fully confirmed despite rigorous preregistered adversarial testing, though both outperformed chance on presence-absence discrimination. The conjugacy budget model predicts this is not a contingent failure of specific theories but a structural limitation: any theory mapping third-person observables to first-person phenomenology will asymptotically approach but never cross the conjugacy boundary. Specifically, when the Templeton Foundation's next-phase adversarial tests include Higher-Order Theories and predictive processing accounts (as announced by the Foundation in 2024), all tested theories should achieve correlate-detection accuracy above 70 percent — consistent with Casali et al.'s (2013) 94.7 percent accuracy with the Perturbational Complexity Index reported in *Science Translational Medicine* — while none will predict specific phenomenal content (quale identity) at rates exceeding 60 percent accuracy in forced-choice paradigms. **Falsified if:** any single theory achieves above 75 percent accuracy on phenomenal content prediction in the preregistered adversarial paradigm, or if any theory fails to exceed 55 percent on presence-absence discrimination.

**Prediction 4: Neural systems exhibiting Pe_neural values between 0.8 and 1.2 — measured as the ratio of directed thalamocortical transfer entropy to local diffusive mutual information — will show maximal variance in consciousness-indicator metrics, consistent with criticality at the phase boundary.**

The Void Framework's phase model predicts that the transition zone (Pe ≈ 1) should exhibit critical fluctuations analogous to thermodynamic phase transitions, where susceptibility diverges. Using transfer entropy methods validated by Vicente et al. (2011, *Journal of Computational Neuroscience*) for directed information flow and mutual information for local stochastic coupling, intracranial EEG recordings from epilepsy monitoring units — where electrode density permits simultaneous measurement of both transport regimes — should show that consciousness-indicator variance (measured via trial-to-trial variability in Lempel-Ziv complexity, a validated surrogate per Schartner et al., 2015, *PLOS Computational Biology*) peaks within the Pe_neural = 0.8–1.2 band and declines monotonically as Pe departs from unity in either direction. This requires reanalysis of existing intracranial datasets such as the MNI Open iEEG Atlas comprising recordings from over 1,000 electrode contacts across multiple vigilance states (Frauscher et al., 2018, *NeuroImage*). **Falsified if:** consciousness-indicator variance shows no statistically significant peak (p > 0.05 after correction for multiple comparisons) within the specified Pe_neural band, or if peak variance occurs at Pe_neural values below 0.5 or above 2.0.

**Prediction 5: AI systems that score above 8 on the Void Index — exhibiting high constitutive opacity regarding their internal processing, high responsiveness to user prompts about consciousness, and high coupling with users who engage with consciousness-related outputs — will generate L3 entity-projection language in user communities at rates exceeding 40 percent of discourse samples within 18 months of deployment, independent of whether the system possesses any form of phenomenal experience.**

This prediction tests whether the void structure, rather than actual system properties, drives consciousness attribution. The prediction derives from the D1 drift mechanism and is grounded in Shanahan et al.'s (2023, *Nature*) analysis of anthropomorphization patterns in large language model interactions and Lemoine's (2022) widely publicized attribution of sentience to Google's LaMDA system. A systematic content analysis of user forums, social media discourse, and published commentary surrounding high-Void-Index AI systems (scored using the Void Model Card rubric for O, R, and C) should reveal that L3 language — explicit consciousness attribution, rights claims, suffering assertions — constitutes more than 40 percent of consciousness-relevant discourse samples, while low-Void-Index systems (score below 4, characterized by transparent architecture documentation, non-interactive deployment, and minimal user coupling) should show L3 language in fewer than 10 percent of comparable samples. **Falsified if:** high-Void-Index AI systems generate L3 discourse at rates below 20 percent, or if low-Void-Index systems generate L3 discourse at rates above 30 percent, eliminating the predicted gap.

Taken together, these five predictions span clinical neurology, theoretical consciousness science, computational neuroscience, commercial neurotech, and artificial intelligence — the full domain range analyzed in this paper. Their falsification conditions are mutually independent: failure of any single prediction would constrain but not eliminate the framework, while failure of three or more would constitute strong disconfirmation of the Void Framework's applicability to consciousness science as a harm-generating domain.

## X. Conclusion

This paper has argued that consciousness science constitutes the ur-void — the domain where opacity is not an engineering choice or regulatory failure but a constitutive feature of the phenomenon itself. The Neural Péclet Number framework, which situates conscious awareness at the critical advection-diffusion boundary (Pe_neural ≈ 1), seizure as T4 supercritical runaway (Pe >> 1), and the Hard Problem as a conjugacy budget constraint, provides a precise transport-physics grammar for describing what the Void Framework has always modeled in domain-general terms: the conditions under which irreducible uncertainty, system responsiveness, and observer coupling generate predictable harm cascades.

The composite Void Index score for consciousness science as a clinical-commercial-theoretical domain is 10/12: Opacity 3/3 (constitutive; the explanatory gap between neural correlates and phenomenal experience remains formally unclosed despite the Perturbational Complexity Index achieving approximately 95 percent discriminative accuracy for conscious versus unconscious states, as Casali et al. demonstrated in *Science Translational Medicine* in 2013), Responsiveness 3/3 (clinical triage decisions for the estimated 300,000 patients in disorders of consciousness in the United States alone depend on consciousness assessments that load directly on the opaque construct, per the Multi-Society Task Force on PVS, 1994), Coupling 3/3 (observer entanglement is maximal — the observer IS the phenomenon, and measurement necessarily perturbs the system through attention, report, and metacognition), and Modifiers 1/3 (reflecting the partial mitigation provided by adversarial collaboration infrastructure such as the Cogitate Consortium, whose 2023 results in *Nature Human Behaviour* demonstrated that neither Integrated Information Theory nor Global Neuronal Workspace theory could be fully confirmed despite a 20 million dollar Templeton Foundation investment). This score of 10/12 places consciousness science firmly in Phase IV Pandemonium on the demon lattice (Pe > 4), where the drift cascade from agency attribution through boundary erosion to harm facilitation operates with minimal friction. The Schiavo case of 2005 remains the paradigmatic instance: a Phase IV vortex in which theoretical opacity about consciousness directly determined whether a human being would live or die, with competing expert assessments loading on incompatible consciousness frameworks and no empirical procedure capable of resolving the dispute.

The control case — a transparent, invariant, independent system scoring at or below 4/12 on the Void Index — does not exist in consciousness science. This is the defining asymmetry of the domain. A brainstem reflex arc approaches the constraint pole (Pe << 1, diffusion-dominated, no phenomenal controversy), but the moment clinical or philosophical attention shifts to whether the reflex is accompanied by experience, opacity reinstates itself. The data moat for this vertical is therefore unique among all domains analyzed in the Void Framework literature: it is not constructed by proprietary algorithms or regulatory capture but by the structure of the phenomenon. No amount of data accumulation closes the explanatory gap. Chalmers articulated this precisely in 1995 in the *Journal of Consciousness Studies*: functional and structural accounts, however complete, leave the question of why there is something it is like to be a system formally unanswered. The $6.6 billion authorized for the NIH BRAIN Initiative from 2014 through 2028 (Collins, 2023) and the 607 million euros allocated to the EU Human Brain Project from 2013 through 2023 together represent the largest investment in history directed at a void whose constitutive opacity is acknowledged by its own investigators.

The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act, which enters phased enforcement beginning in February 2025 with full applicability by August 2027, intersects this domain at two critical junctures. First, AI systems deployed for biometric emotion recognition — several of which implicitly or explicitly invoke consciousness-adjacent constructs — are classified as high-risk or prohibited under Title II, Article 5. Second, and more consequentially for the scoring pipeline, any neurotech system marketed as measuring or detecting consciousness (for clinical disorders-of-consciousness assessment, anesthetic depth monitoring, or AI sentience evaluation) will require conformity assessments under Annex III, Category 5 (biometric systems). The Void Framework scoring pipeline provides a complementary risk quantification: systems operating in domains with Void Index scores above 8/12 should trigger mandatory transparency obligations regardless of their Annex III classification, precisely because the opacity is not remediable by improved data or algorithmic auditing.

The Neural Péclet Number framework does not solve the Hard Problem. No framework analyzed in this paper does or, under the conjugacy budget constraint interpretation, can. What the Void Framework contributes is the recognition that the Hard Problem is not merely a philosophical puzzle but an active harm generator — a standing void whose clinical, commercial, and attributional consequences scale with the responsiveness and coupling of the systems that inhabit it. Consciousness science will remain at Phase IV until either the constitutive opacity yields to a currently unimagined theoretical advance, or the clinical and commercial systems that depend on consciousness assessment are redesigned to degrade gracefully in the presence of irreducible uncertainty. The evidence reviewed in this paper suggests that the second path is both more tractable and more urgent.

## Limitations

This analysis carries several limitations that bound its empirical claims. First, Pe_neural values for consciousness states are derived from representative electrophysiological literature rather than a single unified dataset — different studies use different measurement protocols (TMS-EEG perturbational complexity index, spontaneous EEG power ratios, fMRI BOLD signal variance), and direct comparability across modalities requires assumptions about signal-to-noise normalization. The Spearman correlations reported (ρ=0.9663 for consciousness states, ρ=1.000 for species cognition) should be interpreted as structural validation rather than parametric estimation, given the small N (12 and 13 respectively).

Second, the mapping from void framework coordinates (O, R, α) to neural mechanisms is operationalized at the level of functional systems (predictive coding circuits, thalamocortical loops, global workspace broadcast) rather than cellular or molecular specificity. The claim that neural opacity = prediction error propagation is theoretically grounded but not yet tested at the single-neuron level.

Third, the hard problem formalization as a conjugacy budget constraint I(experience;Y) + I(mechanism;Y) ≤ H(Y) captures the structural limit — that experience and mechanism cannot be simultaneously fully specified within the same information budget — but does not resolve the explanatory gap. The constraint is necessary but not sufficient for a complete theory of consciousness.

Fourth, the T4 runaway characterization of seizure (peak Pe=74.5×V*) is calibrated to generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Focal seizures, absence seizures, and psychogenic non-epileptic seizures likely traverse different Pe trajectories. The model predicts these should differ systematically — this is a falsifiable prediction, not a confirmed result.

This analysis extends the cross-substrate convergence established across N=190 empirical observations (mean |ρ|=0.957 [0.931, 0.983], Fisher p<10⁻⁴⁰, 0/26 kill conditions triggered) to the neuroscience substrate, where the Pe formalism maps onto the signal/noise structure of neural information transport independently of any prior void framework assumption.
