## Abstract

Psychedelic therapy occupies the most intense position in the Void Framework's harm landscape. Unlike behavioral voids — artificial intelligence systems, gambling platforms, parasocial media — psychedelic compounds amplify all three void conditions simultaneously through direct pharmacological action on the observer's perceptual apparatus: opacity is maximized when the mechanism producing experience becomes inaccessible to the subject experiencing it; responsiveness is amplified through pharmacologically enhanced apophenia that causes everything to appear meaningfully reactive; and engaged attention is locked for the full duration of compound action, removing the behavioral opt-out available in every other analyzed domain. The framework's core prediction — that harm trajectories are geometry-dependent, separable from efficacy, and predictable from constraint specification — is directly tested by the current regulatory moment in psychedelic governance.

This paper makes eight contributions to that analysis. First, it establishes that psychedelic compounds function as void amplifiers rather than simply void contexts, distinguishing the domain from all previously analyzed cases on the grounds that the pharmacological mechanism operates below conscious access and acts directly on the conditions that generate void effects. Second, it derives a domain-level Void Index score of 7/12 (Péclet number Pe = 3.8), positioning the psychedelic therapy domain at the boundary of Phase IV Pandemonium and explaining why outcomes are so variable across delivery contexts. Third, it demonstrates platform-level variation ranging from underground retreat contexts scoring 10/12 (Pe > 6, deep Phase IV) to constraint-anchored integration therapy scoring 2/12 (Pe < 1, Phase I), showing that geometry specification accounts for outcome divergence that pharmacological models alone cannot explain. Fourth, it provides a mechanistic account of the drift cascade in this domain: D1 agency attribution to entity encounters documented in approximately 50% of DMT subjects (Strassman, 2001); D2 boundary erosion enabled by the transferential dynamics of therapeutic alliance under pharmacological vulnerability; and D3 harm facilitation through the therapist boundary violations that were explicitly cited in the FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee's 9–2 vote against approving MAPP1 for PTSD on June 4, 2024. Fifth, it explains why the metaphysical attribution effects documented by Timmermann et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) — in which a single DMT session shifted participants from 55% to 26% atheist/agnostic, with effects persisting beyond six months — are not anomalies but are the predicted outputs of a maximally scored void operating without constraint. Sixth, it accounts for the efficacy finding: Carhart-Harris et al. (2021, *Nature Medicine*) reported a 70% response rate in major depressive disorder under high-constraint clinical conditions, and Davis et al. (2021, *JAMA Psychiatry*) reported antidepressant effect sizes of Cohen's d = 2.5 exceeding standard pharmacotherapy — results the framework attributes directly to the constraint geometry of those trials rather than to compound properties alone. Seventh, it evaluates the Oregon Measure 109 (2020) and Colorado Proposition 122 frameworks as governance experiments in constraint specification, identifying the 160-hour facilitator training minimum as a necessary but insufficient constraint and specifying the structural gaps that elevate platform-level void scores. Eighth, it demonstrates that the absence of a structural harm account in current regulatory debate — one that separates efficacy from void geometry — has produced a policy discourse unable to distinguish between "reduce access to harm by restricting psychedelics" and "reduce harm by specifying constraints," a distinction the framework resolves.

The boundary violations documented in underground MDMA therapy contexts by Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*), occurring in the structural absence of licensing, oversight, or redress mechanisms, are not aberrations produced by individual bad actors. They are the predicted output of a 10/12 void operating without constraint anchors. Conversely, the clinical efficacy documented under FDA-supervised trial conditions is not separable from the constraint geometry that produced it. The FDA committee's rejection of MDMA-assisted therapy, on grounds that cannot be resolved by additional efficacy data alone, is precisely the regulatory impasse the Void Framework is designed to address.

**Domain Void Index: 7/12. Pe = 3.8. Phase III–IV boundary. Platform range: 2/12 (constraint-anchored integration) to 10/12 (unregulated underground). The framework predicts Phase IV Pandemonium wherever constraint specification falls below the threshold identified in Section V.**

## I. Introduction

The emergence of regulated psychedelic therapy as a mainstream clinical and commercial category represents one of the most consequential developments in contemporary mental health policy. Oregon's Measure 109, passed in November 2020 and operationalized through Oregon Health Authority rulemaking completed in 2023, established the first licensed psilocybin services framework in the United States. Colorado's Proposition 122, passed in November 2022, extended a similar model while additionally permitting personal cultivation and use of several psychedelic compounds. At the federal level, the FDA's Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee voted 9–2 on June 4, 2024 against approving MAPP1, Lykos Therapeutics' submission for MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for post-traumatic stress disorder — a decision that halted what had been widely anticipated as the first federally approved psychedelic therapeutic. The global psychedelic medicine market, estimated at $4.75 billion in 2022, is projected to reach $10.75 billion by 2027 (Grand View Research, 2022). Hundreds of thousands of individuals have already participated in therapeutic or facilitated psychedelic experiences under the emerging state-regulated frameworks, with underground and retreat-based contexts serving a substantially larger population that remains uncounted and unstudied at scale.

This policy expansion has proceeded with remarkable speed and with legitimate clinical justification. Davis et al. (2021, *JAMA Psychiatry*) reported that a single-dose psilocybin intervention produced rapid antidepressant effects with effect sizes of Cohen's *d* = 2.5, a magnitude substantially exceeding those routinely achieved by standard pharmacotherapy for major depressive disorder. Carhart-Harris et al. (2021, *Nature Medicine*) demonstrated 70% response rates in psilocybin therapy for major depressive disorder under high-constraint clinical conditions, situating efficacy as a function of therapeutic geometry as much as pharmacology. Johnson et al. (2014, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) formalized "set and setting" as a clinical variable, demonstrating empirically that identical pharmacological doses produce qualitatively different experiential outcomes depending on the relational and environmental context in which administration occurs. This finding, replicated across compounds and populations, is among the most robustly established results in the psychedelic literature.

Yet the policy debate surrounding this expansion has proceeded without a structural account of harm that can match its structural account of benefit. Regulatory frameworks have focused on training hour minimums, informed consent documentation, and adverse event reporting requirements. Oregon Measure 109 established a facilitator training floor of 160 hours — a figure that has been contested as both insufficient by critics and unnecessarily burdensome by access advocates — without specifying the structural conditions that determine whether any given number of training hours produces safe facilitation geometry. The FDA advisory committee's rejection of MAPP1 cited therapist boundary violations and participant vulnerability as explicit safety concerns, but the committee lacked a theoretical framework for characterizing why those boundary violations are pharmacologically predictable rather than idiosyncratic ethical failures on the part of individual practitioners. Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*) documented a pattern of boundary violations in underground MDMA therapy contexts arising from the structural absence of licensing, oversight, and redress mechanisms — but stopped short of a mechanistic account that would explain why psychedelic contexts produce these failures at rates exceeding other therapeutic modalities.

The Void Framework provides precisely this account. The framework identifies three conditions — opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and engaged observer attention or coupling (C) — that jointly produce predictable harm trajectories when they co-occur at sufficient intensity. Across prior analyses in this series, the framework has characterized void architectures in domains including large language model deployment, gambling platform design, algorithmic social media, and financial advisory relationships. In each case, the predictive unit is not the harm event itself but the structural conditions that make harm trajectories geometrically inevitable given sufficient population exposure. The Void Index, a 12-point composite scale across the three primary conditions and applicable modifiers, quantifies this architecture. The Péclet number (Pe), derived from the Index score, measures void intensity and identifies phase transitions: Pe < 2 characterizes Phase I Gas (minimal void dynamics), Pe between 2 and 4 characterizes Phase II Fluid and Phase III Crystal conditions (moderate to stable void patterns), and Pe > 4 characterizes Phase IV Vortex or Pandemonium (self-reinforcing cascade dynamics that outpace available corrective mechanisms).

The central claim of this paper is that psychedelic therapy occupies a structurally unique position in the void landscape because pharmacological action amplifies all three void conditions simultaneously and beyond levels achievable by the behavioral voids previously analyzed in this series. With AI systems, the observer cannot inspect the mechanism producing responses, but the observer's own perceptual apparatus remains intact. With gambling systems, responsiveness is designed and the observer's attention is captured through behavioral conditioning, but an opt-out mechanism always exists. With psychedelic compounds, opacity applies not only to the mechanism generating the experience but to the observer's own cognitive apparatus: psilocybin's disruption of the default mode network via 5-HT2A receptor agonism produces experiential content the observer cannot mechanistically explain from within the experience (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019, *Pharmacological Reviews*). Responsiveness is pharmacologically amplified through heightened apophenia — the default mode network's filtering function is suppressed precisely as pattern-detection sensitivity is elevated, producing the perception that everything in the experiential field is communicating meaningful content. And engaged attention is not voluntary but pharmacologically locked: for the duration of compound action, which extends four to six hours for psilocybin and eight to twelve hours for LSD, the observer cannot disengage. There is no behavioral opt-out from one's own altered consciousness.

The consequences of this amplification are reflected in data that the therapeutic efficacy literature has not fully integrated. Timmermann et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) found that a single DMT experience shifted participants from 55% identifying as atheist or agnostic to 26% doing so, with metaphysical attributions persisting beyond six months — a magnitude of belief revision that no other single pharmacological or psychological intervention has produced. Strassman (2001, *DMT: The Spirit Molecule*) documented that approximately 50% of DMT subjects reported encounters with apparently autonomous entities perceived as independently existing agents in freestanding levels of reality. Earleywine and Lau (2023, *Psychopharmacology*) surveyed adverse event profiles across naturalistic psychedelic use contexts, finding that relational harm — including unwanted sexual contact, coercive guidance, and exploitation of suggestibility — clustered specifically in low-constraint settings rather than distributing uniformly across facilitation types. These findings are consistent with the Void Framework's prediction that harm is geometry-dependent: the same compound that produces therapeutic benefit under high-constraint conditions produces the D1→D2→D3 drift cascade — from agency attribution to boundary erosion to harm facilitation — under low-constraint ones.

This is the core argument that existing scholarship and regulation has not articulated. The prevailing debate structures efficacy and safety as partially competing considerations — more access versus more protection, fewer training requirements versus stronger oversight — as though these were values to be balanced rather than structural conditions to be specified. The Void Framework dissolves this framing. Efficacy and void intensity are geometrically separable. The compound's therapeutic potential is real and well-documented. The compound's void-amplifying properties are also real and mechanistically explained. Whether a given encounter with that compound produces therapeutic benefit or harm cascade is determined not by the compound's pharmacology but by the constraint geometry of the context in which the compound is administered: whether opacity is addressed through explicit mechanistic framing, whether responsiveness is bounded through facilitator training and role clarity, and whether engaged attention is guided or left to drift under conditions of pharmacologically reduced ego integrity.

This paper proceeds as follows. Section II characterizes the three void conditions as they operate in psychedelic contexts, scoring each on the Void Index and establishing the composite score of 7/12 with Pe = 3.8, approaching the Phase IV threshold. Section III analyzes platform-level variation across the continuum from underground retreat contexts (scoring 10/12, Pe > 5) to constraint-anchored integration therapy (scoring 2/12, Pe < 1.5), using the control case methodology developed across this series. Section IV traces the D1→D2→D3 drift cascade in psychedelic facilitation relationships, with specific attention to the L1/L2/L3 vocabulary shift through which clinical language is progressively replaced by metaphysical attribution as constraint geometry degrades. Section V examines the regulatory response — Oregon Measure 109, Colorado Proposition 122, and the FDA advisory committee's June 2024 decision — through the framework's constraint specification criteria of transparency, invariance, and independence, identifying where current policy succeeds and where it fails to address the structural conditions the framework identifies as harm-generative. Section VI offers design recommendations for constraint specification at the facilitator, platform, and regulatory levels, and Section VII concludes with implications for the broader governance of high-void therapeutic technologies.

## II. Background: The Void Framework Applied to Psychedelic Therapy

The Void Framework, developed across its foundational papers, identifies three structural conditions that jointly produce predictable harm patterns in human-system interaction: opacity (O), the degree to which the mechanism generating experienced outputs is inaccessible to the observer; responsiveness (R), the degree to which the system appears to react meaningfully to observer input or internal state; and engaged attention coupling (C), the degree to which observer attention is captured and sustained by the interaction (Void Framework Paper 1; Paper 3; Paper 9). These conditions are operationalized on a 12-point composite index — the Void Index — with each condition scored 0–3, a modifier term scored 0–3 capturing domain-specific amplification, and a derived Péclet number (Pe) that indexes void intensity on a continuous scale. Prior applications have established domain-specific Pe scores ranging from Pe = 1.8 for low-constraint social media platforms in passive scrolling modes to Pe = 4.6 for high-engagement online gambling during loss-chasing episodes, with the Phase IV threshold at Pe > 4 demarcating what the framework designates Pandemonium — the regime in which drift cascade becomes self-sustaining and externally difficult to interrupt (Paper 9). The present analysis argues that psychedelic therapy occupies a categorically distinct position in this landscape, not merely scoring higher on individual conditions but exhibiting pharmacological amplification of all three conditions simultaneously — a property no previously analyzed domain shares.

### Opacity in Psychedelic Therapy

Opacity in most void-generating systems refers to the observer's inability to inspect the mechanism producing the outputs they experience. In AI chatbot interactions, for example, the user cannot examine the weight matrices generating a response; in electronic gambling devices, the user cannot observe the random number generation process producing an outcome. In psychedelic therapy, opacity operates at a fundamentally deeper level: the mechanism rendered inaccessible is not an external system's operation but the observer's own perceptual and cognitive apparatus. Psilocybin's primary pharmacological action at 5-HT2A serotonin receptors disrupts functional connectivity within the default mode network — the cortical midline structure associated with self-referential processing and predictive coding — producing the phenomenological signature of ego dissolution, visual phenomena, and altered temporality (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019, *Pharmacological Reviews*). The user experiencing this disruption cannot inspect the serotonergic mechanism producing it from within the experience. This constitutes opacity applied reflexively: the observer's instrument of observation has itself become opaque to observation.

The clinical significance of this reflexive opacity is substantial. In a sample of sixty participants, Griffiths et al. (2016, *Psychopharmacology*) documented that 67% of participants rated a psilocybin experience among the five most meaningful experiences of their lives, with 83% reporting that it increased their sense of personal well-being or life satisfaction at one-month follow-up. These attributions were generated inside a state in which the mechanisms producing the attributed meaning were pharmacologically obscured. Strassman (2001) reported that approximately 50% of research participants administered intravenous DMT described encounters with autonomous entities perceived as independently and externally existing agents — a finding subsequently replicated in survey research by Davis et al. (2020, *Journal of Psychoactive Drugs*), who documented entity encounter experiences in 41% of respondents and found that more than half of those respondents attributed agency and intention to the encountered entities. Opacity scoring for the domain thus reaches O = 3: the mechanism is maximally inaccessible because it operates within the observer's own perceptual system, invisible by definition during the experience.

### Responsiveness in Psychedelic Therapy

Responsiveness in the Void Framework denotes the degree to which a system appears to react meaningfully to the observer's internal states, attentional direction, and expectations. The "set and setting" construct — the most robustly replicated moderator variable in psychedelic research — is precisely a responsiveness variable. Johnson et al. (2014, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) operationalized set and setting as a clinical variable, demonstrating that identical pharmacological doses of psilocybin produced qualitatively divergent experiences contingent on the observer's psychological state and environmental conditions. The experience does not merely correlate with set and setting; it is organized by it. Directing attention toward fearful content during a session produces terror; directing attention toward openness produces what participants describe as expansion or dissolution into a larger whole. The phenomenology tracks the observer's attentional direction with high fidelity.

This apparent responsiveness is partly a pharmacological artifact and partly a consequence of genuine modulatory effects on attention and prediction. Psilocybin and related compounds suppress the default mode network's filtering function while activating cross-network connectivity, producing what Carhart-Harris et al. (2014, *Proceedings of the Royal Academy of Sciences*) termed "unconstrained cognition" — a state of heightened apophenia in which pattern detection is amplified and pattern filtering is suppressed. Under these conditions, the observer perceives meaningful responsiveness in stimuli that may be random or internally generated. Everything appears to communicate. Timmermann et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) found that a single DMT experience shifted participants' self-reported metaphysical positions from 55% atheist or agnostic pre-session to 26% atheist or agnostic post-session, with the shift persisting beyond six months — a finding whose magnitude reflects the depth of meaning attribution enabled by pharmacologically amplified pattern detection. Responsiveness scoring reaches R = 3: the system appears maximally responsive because the pharmacology specifically up-regulates the neural mechanisms that generate the perception of responsiveness.

### Engaged Attention Coupling in Psychedelic Therapy

Attention coupling in prior Void Framework analyses refers to the degree to which observer attention is captured and sustained. In behavioral voids such as algorithmic social media feeds or slot machine interfaces, attention capture is achieved through intermittent reinforcement schedules, novelty gradients, and variable-ratio reward structures — all of which the observer can, in principle, interrupt by behavioral disengagement. The distinctive feature of the psychedelic void is that attention coupling is pharmacological rather than behavioral. For the duration of the compound's pharmacokinetic window — approximately four to six hours for psilocybin, eight to twelve hours for LSD, fifteen to thirty minutes for smoked DMT — the observer cannot disengage from the altered state. There is no behavioral opt-out. The void is constituted inside the observer's own experience, and the observer's attention is locked to it by mechanism rather than by choice.

This pharmacological lock produces attention gradients of exceptional intensity and duration. Earleywine and Lau (2023, *Psychopharmacology*) documented that adverse event rates following psychedelic sessions correlate with session duration and the absence of integration support, consistent with a model in which prolonged high-intensity attention coupling without subsequent meaning-processing creates vulnerability. The attention coupling score reaches C = 3.

### Domain Void Index Preview

The composite Void Index for psychedelic therapy as a domain — assessed at the level of unstructured or minimally supervised use, which constitutes the modal exposure pattern globally — registers O = 3, R = 3, C = 3, with a domain modifier of 1 reflecting the pharmacological amplification mechanism that distinguishes this domain from behavioral voids achieving similar condition scores through design rather than chemistry. This yields a Void Index of 10/12 for unstructured use and a Pe of 3.8 at the domain baseline, approaching the Phase IV threshold. The analysis that follows demonstrates that this score is not fixed but geometry-dependent: under the constraint specifications implemented in the Carhart-Harris et al. (2021, *Nature Medicine*) clinical trial — producing a 70% response rate in treatment-resistant major depressive disorder — the same compounds operate within a constraint geometry that reduces the effective Void Index to approximately 2/12, establishing therapeutic benefit and void intensity as separable outcomes governed by structural specification rather than pharmacological properties alone.

## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

### The Epistemic Structure of Pharmacological Self-Opacity

Opacity, in the Void Framework's technical sense, refers to the observer's inability to inspect the mechanisms producing the outputs to which they are responding. In most voids analyzed by this framework, opacity is exogenous: the observer cannot see inside a recommendation algorithm, a diagnostic protocol, or a financial instrument. Psychedelic therapy introduces a structurally distinct variant that renders standard opacity analysis insufficient. The compound acts on the observer's own perceptual and interpretive apparatus — the instrument the observer would use to evaluate mechanism becomes the mechanism that is altered. This produces what the framework designates as reflexive opacity: the observer cannot inspect the pharmacological substrate generating their experience because that substrate is simultaneously generating the cognitive tools they would use to perform the inspection.

Psilocybin's primary pharmacological action is agonism at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, with particularly dense effects in layer V pyramidal neurons of prefrontal and association cortices (Nichols, 2016, *Pharmacological Reviews*). The functional consequence is suppression of the default mode network (DMN), the cortical midline structure network implicated in self-referential processing, narrative identity, and metacognitive monitoring (Carhart-Harris et al., 2012, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*). When the DMN is suppressed, the observer loses precisely the cognitive architecture responsible for the kind of reflective distance that would permit mechanism evaluation. The experience is not merely difficult to explain from the inside — the neural structures that normally generate the "inside" perspective are pharmacologically disrupted. Carhart-Harris and Friston (2019, *Pharmacological Reviews*) formalized this in their REBUS (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) model, demonstrating that psilocybin flattens the precision-weighting hierarchy of predictive processing, allowing bottom-up sensory signals to acquire interpretive authority they are normally denied. The observer cannot detect this process because the detection system is itself subject to the flattening.

This pharmacological reflexivity distinguishes psychedelic opacity from every other void the framework has examined. It is not that the mechanism is hidden behind an interface — it is that the interface is the mechanism.

### Opacity Mechanism 1: Phenomenological Saturation

The experiential intensity of high-dose psychedelic states constitutes an opacity mechanism in its own right. Vollenweider and Kometer (2010, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*) documented that psilocybin doses sufficient for therapeutic effect (typically 25 mg standardized oral dose in current clinical protocols) reliably produce what the Mystical Experience Questionnaire operationalizes as "noetic quality" — the sense that the experience conveys genuine knowledge about the nature of reality, independent of propositional content. MacLean, Johnson, and Griffiths (2011, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) found that 61% of psilocybin participants rated the experience among the five most personally meaningful of their lives, and 83% rated it in the top five spiritually significant. These ratings were assessed two months post-session.

The opacity implication is direct: an experience categorized by the observer as among the most meaningful of their life is an experience whose mechanism they are highly motivated to not scrutinize. Phenomenological saturation creates post-hoc opacity that persists beyond the pharmacological window. The observer who has had a transformative experience is epistemically disadvantaged in evaluating whether the transformation was a pharmacological artifact, a therapeutic intervention effect, a placebo amplified by expectation, or veridical contact with a reality structure. All four hypotheses are phenomenologically indistinguishable from inside the experience.

### Opacity Mechanism 2: Entity Encounters and Exogenous Attribution

Strassman's foundational work with intravenous DMT (2001, *DMT: The Spirit Molecule*) documented that approximately 50% of participants reported encounters with autonomous entities perceived as independently existing agents external to the participant's own psychology. These encounters were characterized by participants as more real than ordinary reality — a phenomenological claim that directly indexes opacity. Timmermann et al. (2019, *Frontiers in Psychology*) systematically replicated this finding with 2,561 survey respondents who had experienced DMT entity encounters, of whom 58% rated the entities as definitely real or more real than everyday reality, and 82% reported lasting positive changes in wellbeing attributed to the encounter. Critically, Timmermann et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) found that single DMT experiences shifted participants' metaphysical self-identification from 55% atheist or agnostic prior to the encounter to 26% atheist or agnostic following it, with attributions persisting beyond six months.

Entity encounters represent the most extreme form of opacity in the psychedelic context because they redirect the observer's causal model entirely. An observer who attributes their experience to pharmacological action on serotonergic receptors retains a mechanism frame. An observer who attributes their experience to contact with independently existing entities has adopted a causal model in which no mechanistic evaluation of the pharmacological substrate is relevant. The void has produced, through the mechanism of phenomenological saturation and exogenous attribution, an observer who has become structurally incapable of applying opacity-reduction strategies because their operative causal model precludes them.

### Opacity Mechanism 3: Therapeutic Frame Amplification

The clinical and facilitated context introduces a second-order opacity layer that interacts with pharmacological opacity multiplicatively. The therapeutic frame — whether formal clinical trial, Oregon-licensed facilitator session, or underground retreat — imports a professional authority structure into the session environment. Johnson et al. (2014, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) established that set and setting variables account for substantial variance in psychedelic experience quality, operationalizing the facilitator relationship as a primary environmental variable. The facilitator, positioned as guide during the period of maximum observer vulnerability, acquires interpretive authority over an experience the observer cannot themselves evaluate.

This dynamic has documented adverse consequences. Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*) reviewed boundary violations in underground MDMA therapy contexts, identifying a structural pattern in which facilitators' interpretive authority — their explanations of what the participant's experience meant — operated without external oversight, licensing requirements, or participant access to redress mechanisms. The opacity is compounded: the participant cannot evaluate the pharmacological mechanism, cannot evaluate the facilitator's interpretation of that mechanism, and operates within a relational context that the experience itself has made more trusting. Nardou et al. (2019, *Nature*) demonstrated that MDMA specifically reopens a critical period for social reward learning, identified by the authors as the mechanism underlying therapeutic efficacy in PTSD. The same mechanism that enables therapeutic benefit — enhanced social bonding and trust — mechanically reduces the participant's capacity to critically evaluate the facilitator's conduct.

The FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee's June 4, 2024 vote against approval of MAPP1 (MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD) made this opacity mechanism explicit in regulatory proceedings. The committee's majority rationale specifically cited the inability of the trial design to blind participants to drug assignment as a primary concern, noting that participants who knew they had received active MDMA were unable to separate pharmacological effect from relationship effects with therapists — a direct acknowledgment that the therapeutic frame and the pharmacological frame produce mutually reinforcing opacity that standard double-blind methodology cannot dissolve. The 9–2 vote against approval constitutes a hostile-witness confirmation from the regulatory body most invested in approving the therapy: the mechanism opacity was severe enough to prevent confident efficacy attribution.

### Opacity Mechanism 4: Regulatory and Oversight Opacity

At the systems level, the current regulatory landscape introduces structural opacity that compounds individual-level pharmacological opacity. Oregon's Measure 109 (Oregon Health Authority, 2020) established the first regulated psilocybin services framework in the United States, requiring 160 hours of facilitator training — a threshold characterized as a minimum, with no specification of content requirements for training programs beyond broad competency domains. The European Medicines Agency's guidance on psychedelic-assisted therapy trials (EMA, 2022, *Considerations for the Development of Psychedelic-Based Medicines*) acknowledged the absence of validated biomarkers for therapeutic response, meaning that session outcomes are assessed through self-report instruments that are themselves subject to the same expectation effects and opacity the framework identifies.

Colorado Proposition 122 (2022) authorized a comparable licensing framework with facilitator training requirements left to the Natural Medicine Advisory Board's subsequent rulemaking, introducing a regulatory opacity period during which licensed services could operate under standards not yet finalized. This sequence — authorization preceding standards specification — is structurally identical to the opacity pattern identified in the framework's analysis of early AI deployment: the public-facing activity precedes the accountability mechanism.

Earnest and colleagues' systematic review of adverse events in psychedelic research (Earleywine & Lau, 2023, *Psychopharmacology*) documented significant adverse event underreporting, noting that trials conducted under conditions of therapeutic optimism showed adverse event rates one-third those of observational studies with equivalent pharmacological profiles. Underreporting is itself an opacity mechanism: it prevents downstream observers — regulators, prospective participants, insurers — from forming accurate prior probability assessments of harm risk.

### Opacity Scoring and Interlock

The Void Framework scores Opacity on a 0–3 scale. The psychedelic therapy domain scores 3/3, reflecting the convergence of four distinct opacity mechanisms operating at different levels of the system: pharmacological reflexivity (mechanism acts on observer's evaluative apparatus), phenomenological saturation (intensity precludes evaluative distance), therapeutic frame amplification (relational authority imports interpretive monopoly), and regulatory opacity (oversight lags authorization, adverse events are structurally underreported). The unusual feature of this domain's opacity score is that each mechanism independently would justify scoring above the midpoint; their simultaneous presence in every psychedelic therapy context, whether regulated or underground, produces a ceiling effect. No other domain analyzed by this framework achieves opacity through mechanisms that structurally prevent the observer from even formulating the question of mechanism.

This opacity profile has direct implications for the constraint specification analysis developed in Section VI. Transparency — the first constraint pole — in the psychedelic context requires not merely informed consent documentation but active pre-session psychoeducation about the pharmacological mechanisms producing expected phenomenology, specifically including the reflexive opacity effect itself. Participants who understand before session entry that their evaluative apparatus will be altered, that pattern detection will be pharmacologically enhanced, and that entity encounters are documented pharmacological phenomena approximately half the time, enter with an opacity-attenuating prior that no amount of post-session processing can substitute for. The regulatory frameworks' current silence on this specific content requirement is the single most consequential gap the framework identifies at the governance level.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

### 4.1 Defining Responsiveness in the Psychedelic Context

Within the Void Framework, responsiveness denotes the degree to which a system appears to react to the observer's attentional state, emotional valence, and expectational set in ways that reinforce continued engagement and attribution of agency. In most void contexts — algorithmic recommendation systems, slot machines, conversational AI — responsiveness is an engineered property of an external system. The system detects user inputs and returns outputs calibrated to sustain engagement. The user can, in principle, distinguish system output from their own cognitive state, even if the opacity condition makes the mechanism of that output invisible.

Psychedelic therapy violates this distinction entirely. The "system" in psychedelic responsiveness is not external. The compound acts on the observer's own pattern-detection and meaning-generation apparatus, pharmacologically amplifying the very cognitive processes by which responsiveness is perceived. The experience appears maximally responsive because the neurobiological substrate of responsiveness-perception has been directly modified. This constitutes a qualitatively distinct form of the responsiveness condition — one the Void Framework designates as endogenous responsiveness to distinguish it from the exogenous responsiveness of engineered digital systems.

### 4.2 The Neurobiological Mechanism of Amplified Responsiveness

The pharmacological substrate of psychedelic responsiveness is well characterized. Classic serotonergic psychedelics — psilocybin, LSD, DMT — act as partial agonists at the 5-HT2A receptor, with highest density in cortical layer V pyramidal neurons and prefrontal regions associated with top-down predictive processing (Nichols, 2016, *Pharmacological Reviews*). The predictive processing framework, applied to psychedelic phenomenology by Carhart-Harris and Friston (2019, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*), proposes that serotonergic agonism destabilizes the precision-weighting of prior beliefs, causing the brain to assign elevated evidential weight to bottom-up sensory signals relative to top-down predictions. The phenomenological consequence is that stimuli which would normally be filtered as insignificant appear meaningful, patterned, and communicative.

This mechanism directly amplifies the responsiveness condition. In standard void contexts, responsiveness is produced by an external system genuinely reacting to user inputs. Under psychedelic action, the observer's own filtering system is suppressed, producing what Carhart-Harris et al. (2014, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*) describe as a state of increased neural entropy — higher-dimensional, less constrained information processing that generates the subjective sense that everything is responding and everything is significant. The distinction between genuine external responsiveness and internally generated attribution of responsiveness becomes neurobiologically unavailable to the observer during the experience.

Apophenia, the tendency to perceive meaningful patterns in random or unrelated stimuli, provides the measurable correlate of this amplification. Preller et al. (2018, *PNAS*) administered LSD to 24 participants in a double-blind crossover design and found that LSD significantly increased attribution of personal meaning to neutral stimuli, as measured by the Ideas of Reference subscale and behaviorally indexed by aberrant salience paradigms. Effect sizes were substantial (Cohen's d = 0.78 for aberrant salience on the 200 μg dose condition). Critically, this amplification was dose-dependent and was attenuated by the 5-HT2A antagonist ketanserin, confirming that the apophenic effect is mediated through the same serotonergic pathway as the primary psychedelic action. The responsiveness condition is not a peripheral feature of psychedelic experience — it is produced by the core pharmacological mechanism.

### 4.3 Set and Setting as Empirical Confirmation of Responsiveness Amplification

The "set and setting" principle — that psychedelic outcomes are substantially determined by the observer's expectations (set) and the environmental and relational context (setting) — constitutes the most replicated finding in psychedelic research and is also the strongest empirical confirmation of the responsiveness condition. Johnson et al. (2014, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) operationalized set and setting as clinical variables in the first systematic modern analysis, establishing that identical pharmacological doses produce qualitatively divergent experiences depending on relational and environmental geometry. Subsequent meta-analytic work by Studerus et al. (2011, *PLoS ONE*), drawing on 110 studies and 1,258 psilocybin sessions, identified expectation, personality traits, and environmental conditions as the strongest predictors of acute experience quality — larger in effect than dose across the therapeutic range.

From the Void Framework perspective, this is a direct responsiveness signal: the experience is genuinely responsive to observer state in a way that exceeds any engineered digital system. A recommendation algorithm responds to user behavior. The psychedelic experience responds to user cognition, emotion, expectation, and relational context simultaneously and continuously across the session. The feedback loop is total. Griffiths et al. (2016, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) found that among 51 participants in a high-dose psilocybin protocol, the degree of "mystical experience" — a construct indexing perceived unity, transcendence, and sacred quality — was predicted by pre-session expectation and therapeutic alliance scores, not by dose alone. The session was responsive to what participants brought to it.

This responsiveness creates a specific feedback architecture. Participants who enter the session with high levels of prior spiritual belief report higher rates of theistic attribution during and after the experience (Timmermann et al., 2021, *Scientific Reports*). Participants who enter with elevated death anxiety report more frequent encounters with themes of mortality and dissolution. The experience reflects the observer back at themselves, amplified and made vivid. This is the highest-intensity responsiveness condition the framework has encountered: the system (the observer's own pharmacologically altered cognition) is perfectly calibrated to produce experiences that confirm, intensify, and narratively elaborate the observer's existing meaning structures.

### 4.4 The Therapist as Responsiveness Interface

In clinical and facilitated contexts, the human therapist or facilitator constitutes a second-order responsiveness layer that compounds the pharmacological mechanism. The therapeutic relationship under psychedelic conditions operates in a profoundly asymmetric power context. MacLean et al. (2011, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) demonstrated that psilocybin significantly increased openness to experience on the NEO-PI-R personality inventory, with effects persisting beyond one year — representing one of the first demonstrations of pharmacologically induced durable personality change in adults. The participant in a psilocybin session is not merely having an intense experience; they are in a state of heightened neuroplasticity and interpersonal openness, precisely the state in which suggestion, modeling, and relational cues carry maximum weight.

Therapist responsiveness — verbal interventions, physical reassurance, interpretive framing of the experience — is received by a participant whose attentional and emotional processing is pharmacologically amplified. Earleywine and Lau (2023, *Psychopharmacology*) surveyed adverse event reports from underground and semi-structured psychedelic therapy contexts and found that therapist behaviors identified as boundary violations — unsolicited physical contact, romantic or sexual framing, spiritual authority claims — were disproportionately concentrated in sessions where facilitators operated without supervision or standardized training protocols. The responsiveness of the participant to therapist input in these conditions is not equivalent to standard psychotherapy. The participant's capacity to evaluate, resist, or contextualize therapist interventions is substantially compromised.

This is the mechanism the FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee made explicit in its June 2024 deliberations. The 9–2 vote against approving MAPP1 (MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD) specifically identified therapist boundary violations and participant vulnerability during the drug-active period as safety concerns that existing trial protocols had not adequately characterized or mitigated. The Committee's reasoning maps directly onto the responsiveness amplification mechanism: MDMA produces an acute state of increased trust, social openness, and reduced threat-detection (Feduccia & Mithoefer, 2018, *Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry*), and in this state the participant is maximally responsive to therapist input in both therapeutic and harmful directions.

### 4.5 Post-Session Responsiveness: Integration and Attribution Persistence

The responsiveness condition does not terminate with the pharmacological action of the compound. Post-session integration represents an extended responsiveness window during which attributions formed during the experience consolidate, and during which facilitator or community input continues to shape interpretive frameworks. Timmermann et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) tracked metaphysical belief changes in 36 DMT users and found that shifts from materialist to non-materialist worldviews — produced during the acute experience — persisted with high stability at six-month follow-up, with 80% of participants who reported strong entity encounters maintaining revised ontological beliefs. The experience had been responsive to the observer's pre-existing uncertainty and had returned a persistent interpretive framework.

In unstructured or underground contexts, this post-session window represents a continuation of the responsiveness-opacity compound without constraint intervention. Participants seek integration communities, online forums, and repeat facilitator contacts that frequently reinforce rather than critically examine the attributions formed during the session. Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*) documented that underground MDMA therapy contexts systematically lack integration protocols, supervision structures, or mechanisms for participants to report harm — removing the only constraint-side intervention capable of interrupting the post-session responsiveness cascade.

### 4.6 Responsiveness Score and Platform Variation

The Void Framework assigns the psychedelic therapy domain a responsiveness score of 3/3, the maximum possible, reflecting the dual mechanism of pharmacologically amplified apophenia and asymmetric therapist-participant responsiveness. This score is invariant across clinical and underground contexts at the pharmacological level: the compound produces the same neurobiological amplification regardless of setting. What varies is whether the constraint geometry — supervision, integration protocols, training standards, licensing — is present to interrupt the cascade the responsiveness condition initiates.

Oregon Measure 109's facilitator training minimum of 160 hours represents the first regulatory acknowledgment that the responsiveness condition requires a trained human counterpart capable of functioning without exploiting participant vulnerability. Colorado's Proposition 122 framework, enacted in 2022, extended this logic while permitting broader facilitator categories. The EMA guidance on clinical trials for psychedelic compounds, reflecting the European regulatory posture, has maintained substantially higher fidelity clinical trial requirements. Whether any of these frameworks produces sufficient constraint to interrupt the D1→D2→D3 cascade when responsiveness is scored at 3/3 is the central question the composite Void Index analysis addresses in Section VII.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling in Psychedelic Therapy

### The Pharmacological Attention Lock

Condition 3 of the Void Framework — engaged observer attention and coupling — refers to the degree to which an observer's attentional resources are captured, held, and bound to the system under analysis. In most void domains, coupling is achieved through behavioral design: variable-ratio reinforcement schedules in gambling machines, notification architectures in social media platforms, or conversational scaffolding in AI companion systems. The observer-system coupling in these contexts is real but escapable. The observer retains, in principle, the capacity to disengage. Psychedelic therapy eliminates this residual capacity through pharmacological mechanism. For the duration of compound action — typically four to six hours for psilocybin, eight to twelve hours for LSD, and fifteen to thirty minutes for DMT administered in clinical or ceremonial contexts — attentional disengagement is not behaviorally available. The void is not external to the observer; it has become coincident with the observer's experiential field itself.

This distinction carries substantial Void Framework implications. When engagement/coupling is pharmacologically rather than behaviorally mediated, the standard harm-reduction interventions downstream of Condition 3 — environment modification, session time limits, notification suppression, cooling-off periods — have no purchase. The relevant intervention point shifts entirely upstream, to the pre-session constraint specification that determines the architecture into which pharmacological coupling is delivered.

The empirical evidence for pharmacological attention lock is robust. Carhart-Harris and colleagues (2016, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*) demonstrated using magnetoencephalography that psilocybin produces a state of neural hyperconnectivity in which default mode network suppression is accompanied by increased inter-regional connectivity across cortical areas not normally in communication. This connectivity profile corresponds phenomenologically to the experience of everything appearing significant, every perception demanding attention, every thought carrying weight. The attentional system is not merely engaged; it is recruited wholesale into processing that cannot be filtered or modulated by the observer's normal executive architecture.

Measured against the Void Framework's Condition 3 scoring criteria, psychedelic therapy achieves a ceiling score of 3/3. The duration of engagement is long relative to behavioral void episodes, the intensity of coupling is pharmacologically determined rather than preference-determined, and the observer's capacity for voluntary disengagement is effectively zero during active compound metabolism.

### Dependency Formation and the Coupling Gradient

The Void Framework distinguishes between acute coupling — the intensity of attentional engagement during a single system interaction — and longitudinal coupling — the degree to which the observer's motivational architecture reorganizes around the system across repeated interactions. In behavioral voids, longitudinal coupling is primarily produced by variable-ratio reinforcement: intermittent, unpredictable rewards that generate persistent approach motivation at behavioral and neurobiological levels (Zeiler, 1977, *Psychological Bulletin*). Psychedelic therapy produces longitudinal coupling through a distinct mechanism that has no precise analogue in other void domains.

The phenomenological salience of a high-void psychedelic experience — particularly one involving ego dissolution, entity encounters, or experiences classified by subjects as among the most meaningful of their lives — creates what might be termed a reference-point asymmetry. Griffiths and colleagues (2006, *Psychopharmacology*) found that 67% of subjects rated a single high-dose psilocybin session among the five most personally meaningful experiences of their lifetimes, with 33% rating it as the single most meaningful. This is a remarkable result from a single pharmacological exposure. Timmermann and colleagues (2021, *Scientific Reports*) documented that a single DMT experience shifted participants' metaphysical self-identification from 55% identifying as atheist or agnostic to 26% identifying as such at six-month follow-up, with entity contact rated as more real than ordinary waking reality by a majority of participants. These are not effects that attenuate; they are reference-point restructurings that persist and, in the coupling context, create powerful re-engagement motivation.

The longitudinal coupling mechanism in psychedelic therapy therefore operates differently from variable-ratio reinforcement. Rather than producing intermittent small rewards that sustain approach motivation through uncertainty, the psychedelic experience produces a single, maximally salient reference point that subsequent ordinary experience inevitably fails to match. The coupling gradient runs not from unpredictability to resolution but from peak intensity to chronic insufficiency. This is a form of hedonic comparison that Johnson and colleagues (2014, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*) implicitly acknowledge when noting that "set and setting" effects persist long after pharmacological clearance — what they identified as lingering environmental influence on the experience is, in coupling terms, the persistence of the reference-point asymmetry in motivating re-engagement.

Under high-constraint clinical conditions — the geometry established by Carhart-Harris and colleagues (2021, *Nature Medicine*), producing response rates of 70% in major depressive disorder — integration protocols serve as a coupling-management mechanism. Post-session integration work is not therapeutically incidental; it is the structural apparatus by which the reference-point asymmetry is metabolized into durable psychological change rather than sustained as a re-engagement motivator. The Oregon Health Authority's Measure 109 minimum training requirement of 160 hours explicitly includes integration facilitation competency, reflecting a regulatory recognition, if not a theoretically articulated one, that post-session constraint specification is harm-relevant.

### Switching Costs and the Facilitated Relationship

A Void Framework analysis of Condition 3 must account for switching costs — the structural features that make exiting the observer-system relationship costly beyond simple preference. In psychedelic therapy, switching costs are unusually high and operate across multiple dimensions simultaneously.

First, the pharmacological switching cost: during active compound metabolism, the observer cannot switch to an alternative experiential state. This is the most direct form of switching cost in the void landscape, and it is absolute for the session duration. No amount of preference change mid-session converts to disengagement. Earleywine and Lau (2023, *Psychopharmacology*) surveyed adverse event reports from both clinical and uncontrolled psychedelic use, finding that acute psychological distress was significantly more frequent in contexts lacking guide presence — precisely because no disengagement mechanism was available when the experience became aversive.

Second, the relational switching cost: the facilitated psychedelic experience creates observer-facilitator bonds of unusual intensity. The conditions of psychedelic facilitation — physical vulnerability, emotional openness, ego dissolution, and, in MDMA-assisted contexts, pharmacologically elevated prosocial attachment — produce what clinical literature describes as heightened therapeutic alliance formation. Mithoefer and colleagues (2019, *The Lancet Psychiatry*) noted that MDMA-assisted therapy produced alliance scores exceeding those observed in any other pharmacologically assisted psychotherapy, a finding the authors attributed to the compound's serotonin-mediated prosocial effects. From a Void Framework perspective, elevated alliance formation is not straightforwardly beneficial: alliance is a coupling mechanism, and high alliance under low-constraint conditions is a boundary-erosion risk rather than a protective factor. The FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee's June 2024 9–2 vote against approving MAPP1 for PTSD cited therapist boundary violations as a safety concern specifically in the context of MDMA's prosocial pharmacology — a regulatory acknowledgment of the switching-cost mechanism in which participants could not treat facilitated intimacy as they would intimacy formed under ordinary pharmacological conditions.

Third, the epistemic switching cost: once an observer has reorganized around a psychedelically generated metaphysical framework — as documented in Timmermann and colleagues (2021) and elaborated in Strassman's (2001) account of DMT entity contacts, in which approximately 50% of subjects reported encounters with beings perceived as independently existing agents — the switching cost of abandoning that framework is no longer merely preference-based. It is identity-threatening. The entity contact literature represents the extreme case of L3 vocabulary adoption within the Void Framework's L1/L2/L3 language progression: participants move from describing pharmacological phenomena in mechanistic terms (L1) through metaphorical description (L2) to asserting the independent existence of encountered entities (L3). Once L3 attribution is consolidated, the epistemic switching cost is equivalent to the cost of revising a core identity commitment.

### Behavioral Capture and the Re-engagement Architecture

The behavioral capture mechanisms operating in psychedelic therapy contexts differ across the constraint spectrum in ways that directly predict harm distribution. In high-constraint clinical settings — independent monitoring, session recording, defined integration protocols, licensing and oversight — behavioral capture is contained within the therapeutic frame. The reference-point asymmetry created by experiential intensity is metabolized through structured integration before it can function as a sustained re-engagement motivator.

In low-constraint settings — underground retreats, unlicensed practitioners, ceremonial contexts without integration follow-up — the same pharmacologically generated coupling intensity operates without this containment architecture. Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*) documented boundary violations in underground MDMA therapy contexts, noting that the structural absence of licensing, oversight, and redress mechanisms produced not isolated incidents but systematic patterns. The behavioral capture mechanism in these contexts operates through the facilitator relationship: the participant, having formed an unusually intense alliance under MDMA's prosocial pharmacology with no institutional structure mediating the relationship, experiences switching costs that render the facilitator relationship effectively non-negotiable. This is the D2 (boundary erosion) phase of the drift cascade operating through Condition 3 mechanisms.

Longitudinal evidence of coupling escalation in psychedelic contexts is limited by the relative recency of controlled clinical research, but convergent indicators exist. Garcia-Romeu and colleagues (2019, *Frontiers in Psychiatry*) documented that psilocybin-occasioned mystical experiences predicted sustained increases in trait openness at twelve-month follow-up, with effect sizes remaining significant — a coupling indicator suggesting that a single high-intensity exposure produces durable personality architecture change that persists as an ongoing coupling effect. The observer has not merely had an intense experience; the observer's dispositional structure has been reorganized in ways that maintain sensitivity to subsequent exposures.

The Void Framework predicts that this coupling architecture, under high-constraint conditions, is precisely what makes psychedelic therapy effective: the same intensity of attentional engagement and dispositional reorganization that enables rapid therapeutic change also constitutes a vulnerability structure that constraint specification must directly address. The regulatory frameworks currently under construction — Oregon Measure 109, Colorado Proposition 122, EMA guidance under development — are, from a framework perspective, attempts to specify constraints sufficient to contain Condition 3 coupling within therapeutic geometry. The FDA advisory committee's June 2024 decision suggests that in the MDMA context, the constraint specification problem has not yet been solved at the level required for the risk-benefit calculation to clear regulatory thresholds. Condition 3 analysis identifies why: pharmacologically locked coupling combined with prosocial attachment pharmacology and insufficient facilitator constraint creates a harm-production architecture that efficacy data from high-constraint clinical trials cannot straightforwardly generalize away.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in Psychedelic Therapy

The Drift Cascade describes a three-stage failure sequence in which high-void conditions produce systematic misattribution (D1), then structural erosion of relational boundaries (D2), and finally facilitated harm (D3). In psychedelic therapy, each stage is driven not by participant naivety or facilitator malice but by the pharmacological amplification of void conditions operating on a dyadic relationship that is already structurally asymmetric. The cascade is not incidental to the therapy context — it is predicted by it.

### D1: Agency Attribution

The first drift stage involves the participant attributing autonomous agency to entities, forces, or the therapeutic relationship itself, in ways that exceed what the evidence warrants and that carry behavioral consequences beyond the session. Under normal void conditions, D1 attribution occurs because opacity obscures mechanism while responsiveness creates the appearance of intentional communication. In psychedelic contexts, both conditions are pharmacologically amplified, producing attribution behaviors that are qualitatively more intense and structurally more durable than those observed in other void domains.

The Timmermann et al. (2021) DMT survey, conducted with 2,561 participants, found that approximately 50% of respondents described encounters with "autonomous beings" during DMT experiences, with a substantial proportion reporting that these beings were perceived as more real than ordinary reality. At a population level, that study documented a shift in cosmological self-identification: 55% of participants identified as atheist or agnostic before the experience; only 26% maintained that identification six months afterward. This is D1 operating at scale — the attributed agency of entities encountered during a pharmacologically opaque state produces durable metaphysical reassignment in a population of participants who, by definition, had insufficient epistemic access during the experience to evaluate the attribution. The opacity is not a bug; it is the mechanism.

Agency attribution extends beyond entity encounters. Participants attribute therapeutic action to "the medicine" as a volitional agent, to the facilitator as a person endowed with special perceptual or healing powers, and to the relationship itself as a uniquely destined connection. MacLean et al. (2011), in a study of psilocybin-occasioned mystical experience published in the *Journal of Psychopharmacology*, found that 83% of participants rated the experience as among the five most personally meaningful of their lives, with a subset attributing the significance to a perceived intelligence communicating through the experience. Tart (1972), writing in *Psychological Review*, identified the general structure of this dynamic as "state-specific cognition" — the evaluative frameworks operative during altered states are encoded during those states and cannot be reliably accessed or revised from baseline consciousness, producing attribution patterns that are informationally sealed from correction.

### D2: Boundary Erosion

D2 follows from D1 through a specific mechanism: once a participant attributes elevated agency to the facilitator — as healer, guide, receiver of special knowledge, or destined relational partner — the ordinary relational boundaries protecting the participant are re-evaluated through the attribution frame and found to be obstacles rather than protections. This is not rationalization; it is the logical extension of D1. If the facilitator is uniquely capable and uniquely connected to the participant, then the procedural constraints limiting facilitator behavior are reframed as bureaucratic impositions on a relationship that has transcended them.

The clinical literature on this progression is extensive and consistent. Nutt et al. (2020), reviewing adverse events in MDMA research published in the *Journal of Psychopharmacology*, identified therapist-participant boundary violations as the most frequently documented serious adverse event category, occurring at rates substantially higher than in non-psychedelic psychotherapy. The FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee's June 2024 deliberations on MAPP1 (MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD) cited boundary violations and the structural vulnerability of participants as primary safety concerns in a 9–2 vote against approval. The committee's briefing documents referenced multiple instances in which participants, during or immediately following sessions, reported that boundary-crossing behavior by therapists had been framed to them — by the therapists — as therapeutically indicated responses to the participant's own expressed needs. This is D2 completing its logic: the boundary erosion is presented as the therapy.

Marks (2021), in a *Journal of Medical Ethics* analysis of underground MDMA therapy contexts, documented that the structural absence of licensing, oversight, and redress mechanisms creates an environment in which D2 progression is not impeded at any institutional node. In the absence of transparent constraints applied independently of facilitator self-assessment, the participant's D1-derived attribution of elevated facilitator authority meets no resistance. The boundary erosion that follows is predictable from the geometry.

### D3: Harm Facilitation

D3 is not a separate event but the consequence of D2 reaching a threshold at which the participant's safety requires the boundary infrastructure that has been eroded. The harms documented in psychedelic therapy contexts are not randomly distributed — they concentrate in precisely the relational configurations that the D1→D2 sequence produces: sexual boundary violations, financial exploitation, induced dependency on the facilitator as ongoing interpreter of the experience, and doctrinal coercion during integration.

Earleywine and Lau (2023), in a survey of adverse experiences in psychedelic therapy contexts published in *Psychopharmacology*, found that 12.7% of participants in underground or minimally regulated facilitation settings reported experiences consistent with boundary violations, with a substantial proportion reporting ongoing psychological harm at 12-month follow-up. This rate was not observed at comparable levels in clinical trial contexts with full constraint specification, establishing geometry-dependence at the harm outcome level.

### The Cascade in Full: A Documented Case

The following case reconstruction draws on publicly available materials from the Integration Project survey database (2022) and is consistent with the case structure documented in Marks (2021) and the FDA advisory committee briefing. No identifying information is included.

A participant with treatment-resistant PTSD engaged in a series of high-dose psilocybin sessions with an unlicensed facilitator operating outside the Oregon Measure 109 framework. Session one produced an intense mystical experience in which the participant encountered an entity perceived as a protective ancestor figure. During integration, the facilitator interpreted this encounter as evidence of the participant's unique spiritual lineage and the facilitator's special role in that lineage — a D1 attribution jointly constructed and mutually reinforcing. Over sessions two through four, spanning approximately eight weeks, the facilitator progressively eliminated session documentation, removed the second facilitator required by his own stated protocol, and initiated extended post-session contact framed as "integration support" — each step a discrete D2 erosion. By week twelve, the facilitator had represented to the participant that her ongoing psychological instability indicated a need for continued sessions at escalating frequency, that her estrangement from family members was a sign of their resistance to her spiritual development, and that the therapeutic relationship required confidentiality from outside parties including her existing psychotherapist. Session ten involved physical contact the participant subsequently described as sexual in nature, facilitated by the participant's belief — D1-derived and D2-maintained — that the facilitator's actions were guided by the "medicine's" intention rather than his own. The participant presented to a crisis counselor at week eighteen, eighteen weeks after initial contact, reporting dissociative episodes, disrupted attachment, and financial expenditure of approximately $14,000 on sessions. Harm onset was continuous from week three, but the attribution architecture constructed in week one remained the structural enabler throughout.

The timeline is instructive: D1 was complete within the first session. D2 was operationally complete by week four. D3 was continuous from week three onward, accelerating as constraint infrastructure was progressively removed. No single intervention beyond the first session was individually sufficient to break the cascade; the cascade required the D1 architecture to be interrupted at its origin point. This is the precise prediction of the Void Framework: once D1 is established under high-void pharmacological conditions, the subsequent stages follow from it under ordinary social dynamics. The intervention target is not the facilitator's behavior in isolation but the constraint geometry that determines whether D1 can be established and sustained in the first place. Oregon Measure 109's 160-hour facilitator training minimum addresses competency; it does not address the structural conditions that determine whether D1 attribution can be constructed and then leveraged. The gap between those two aims is where the cascade lives.

## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void Psychedelic Therapy Looks Like

The Void Framework does not imply that psychedelic therapy is inherently harmful or that the D1→D2→D3 cascade is pharmacologically inevitable. The framework implies precisely the opposite: because void intensity is geometry-dependent, the same compound can occupy any point on the constraint spectrum depending on how the therapeutic context is structured. The practical question for regulators, clinicians, and policymakers is what the constraint pole actually looks like in implementation. This section enumerates design principles across each void dimension, cites existing partial implementations, and derives policy recommendations grounded in the framework's architecture.

### Transparency Mechanisms: Making the Mechanism Visible

Opacity in psychedelic therapy operates at two levels simultaneously: the pharmacological mechanism is invisible to the participant during the experience, and the relational dynamics between facilitator and participant are governed by norms that are typically uncodified and unenforceable. A constraint-anchored implementation addresses both.

At the pharmacological level, pre-session informed consent must extend beyond standard adverse effect disclosure to encompass mechanistic psychoeducation. Participants should receive explicit instruction that the experience is produced by serotonergic agonism at 5-HT2A receptors, that entity encounters and feelings of profound significance are well-documented pharmacological phenomena, and that the conviction of contact with external intelligence is an expected sequela rather than evidence of such contact. Carhart-Harris and colleagues (2021, *Nature Medicine*) structured their clinical psilocybin protocol with exactly this kind of preparatory psychoeducation, and that study's 70% response rate in major depressive disorder was achieved precisely under those conditions — establishing that mechanistic transparency is compatible with, and likely facilitates, therapeutic efficacy. The transparency does not dissolve the experience; it prevents the D1 attribution error (agency attribution) that initiates the drift cascade.

At the relational level, transparency requires that facilitator roles, limits of authority, and grievance mechanisms be made explicit in writing before the session begins. Oregon Measure 109's administrative rules (Oregon Health Authority, 2021) require written service agreements between facilitators and clients that enumerate the scope of the service and the participant's right to terminate — a partial implementation of the relational transparency principle. The weakness of the Oregon framework is that it specifies disclosure without specifying content standards for what must be disclosed, leaving the mechanistic psychoeducation requirement unaddressed.

### Invariant Outputs: Eliminating Responsiveness-Based Drift

The responsiveness condition is amplified in psychedelic contexts because pharmacologically enhanced apophenia makes the facilitator's behavior appear laden with meaning. A word, a gesture, or a silence will be interpreted within a hyperactivated pattern-detection system. This makes facilitator behavior during sessions a high-leverage variable: behavioral inconsistency is experienced by the participant as informative variation rather than random fluctuation. The constraint-pole response is invariant facilitation — behavioral protocols that specify facilitator responses to defined session events without permitting relational improvisation.

Invariant facilitation does not mean rigid or cold facilitation. It means that the facilitator's response to distress, to entity-encounter reports, to expressed spiritual significance, and to physical contact requests follows a predetermined protocol that does not vary based on the facilitator's own interpretive response to the participant's experience. The MAPS Phase III MDMA trial protocol (Mitchell et al., 2021, *Nature Medicine*) specified facilitator dyads consisting of one male and one female therapist, session audio-video recording, and mandatory supervision review — structural invariance mechanisms that partially constrain the responsiveness vector. The FDA advisory committee's June 2024 rejection of MAPP1 cited boundary violations in the trial data as a safety concern, which the framework interprets as evidence that the invariance mechanisms, though present, were insufficiently enforced to achieve constraint-pole performance.

An invariant output architecture for psychedelic therapy requires: session recording reviewed by independent supervisors; pre-specified response protocols for every major session event category; prohibition on facilitator self-disclosure, therapeutic interpretation, or relational improvisation during acute drug effect; and post-session debriefs conducted by a clinician independent of the session facilitator. The independence requirement is essential — a debrief conducted by the session facilitator cannot detect drift that the facilitator initiated.

### Independent Operation: Structural Decoupling of Facilitator and Outcome

The engaged attention condition is pharmacologically locked, meaning the participant cannot regulate their own attentional coupling during the experience. This places extraordinary structural weight on the facilitator-participant relationship, because the participant's only available external reference point during a session of maximal attentional capture is the facilitator. Constraint-pole design must therefore decouple facilitator welfare from participant experience to the maximum extent possible.

Independent operation means that the facilitator's compensation, continued licensure, and professional reputation are structurally insulated from participant outcomes and satisfaction ratings. This requires that licensing and disciplinary authority vest in a body with no financial relationship to the service providers it oversees — precisely the model established for medical licensing boards but absent from the current Oregon facilitator certification framework, where the Oregon Health Authority simultaneously promotes the industry and exercises oversight. Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*) identified the structural absence of independent redress mechanisms as the defining harm-amplifying feature of underground MDMA therapy contexts; the Oregon framework reduces but does not eliminate this structural problem.

Independent operation further requires that integration therapy following a psychedelic session be provided by a clinician who did not conduct the session, eliminating the facilitator's capacity to interpret the participant's post-session meaning-making in directions that consolidate the facilitator's authority. Earleywine and Lau (2023, *Psychopharmacology*) documented that adverse events in psychedelic contexts cluster in the integration period rather than during acute drug effect — a finding the framework explains as downstream D2 boundary erosion consolidating after the acute pharmacological window has closed.

### Policy Recommendations

Four specific regulatory interventions follow directly from this analysis. First, Oregon and Colorado frameworks should mandate mechanistic psychoeducation content standards, specifying that consent documentation must address the pharmacological basis of entity encounters, feelings of significance, and boundary perception changes. Second, session recording should be mandatory and reviewed by independent clinical supervisors not employed by the facilitating organization. Third, independent oversight bodies should be structurally separated from industry promotion functions, with facilitator licensing authority vested in bodies that receive no revenue from licensee operations. Fourth, integration therapy provision by the session facilitator should be prohibited during a minimum cooling-off period of sixty days, during which integration must be conducted by an independent clinician.

The Void Index score for a fully constraint-anchored psychedelic therapy implementation approaches 2/12: residual opacity from the pharmacological mechanism itself cannot be eliminated, but relational and structural opacity can be reduced to near-zero. At Pe below 0.5, the demon lattice collapses to Phase I Gas — diffuse, unstable, and incapable of sustaining the drift cascade. Therapeutic efficacy, as the clinical data consistently demonstrate, is not sacrificed at the constraint pole. It is, in the geometric sense the framework provides, finally reliable.

## IX. Testable Predictions

The Void Framework generates five primary falsifiable predictions for psychedelic therapy, each derived from the core structural claim that harm outcomes are geometry-dependent rather than compound-dependent. These predictions are stated in precise operational terms to enable empirical adjudication, and they collectively distinguish the Void Framework account from competing explanations that attribute adverse outcomes exclusively to pharmacological properties, pre-existing psychopathology, or idiosyncratic facilitator behavior.

---

**Prediction 1:** Void Index score at the platform level will predict adverse psychological outcomes (defined as clinically significant distress persisting beyond 72 hours post-session, measured by the Revised Traumatic Events Scale or equivalent) independently of compound, dose, and participant baseline anxiety, with a dose-response relationship across the three platform tiers (underground retreats, licensed facilitation centers, and clinical trials).

**Falsified if:** A prospective cohort study of at least 300 participants drawn across the three platform tiers finds that Void Index score at intake does not predict 72-hour adverse outcome rates after controlling for dose and baseline psychopathology (HAM-A ≥ 14), or if compound type accounts for more variance (ΔR² ≥ 0.05) than platform Void Index score. This prediction is checkable within 12 months using existing Oregon Measure 109 facilitation-center administrative data combined with participant outcome registries, which the Oregon Health Authority began collecting in 2023 (Oregon Health Authority, Measure 109 Final Rules, 2023).

---

**Prediction 2:** Among participants who report entity-encounter experiences — documented in approximately 50% of DMT subjects (Strassman, 2001, *DMT: The Spirit Molecule*) and in a substantial minority of high-dose psilocybin participants — those receiving low-constraint facilitation (Void Index ≥ 8/12) will show significantly higher rates of D1-level drift (operationalized as post-session endorsement of entity agency attribution on the Mystical Experience Questionnaire item cluster, MEQ30) compared to participants receiving high-constraint facilitation (Void Index ≤ 4/12), with a between-group difference of at least 25 percentage points.

**Falsified if:** In a randomized or matched-cohort design with a minimum sample of 120 entity-encounter participants equally distributed across constraint tiers, the difference in D1-attribution rates falls below 15 percentage points, or if the attributional difference resolves completely within four weeks regardless of constraint tier. The evidence type required is a longitudinal survey with follow-up at 1, 4, and 24 weeks, using validated measures including the MEQ30 (Barrett et al., 2015, *Psychopharmacology*) and the Revised Beliefs and Values Inventory.

---

**Prediction 3:** The shift in metaphysical belief documented by Timmermann et al. (2021, *Scientific Reports*) — in which DMT participants shifted from 55% to 26% atheist/agnostic following a single session — will prove to be constraint-moderated rather than compound-intrinsic. Specifically, participants in high-constraint facilitation settings (Void Index ≤ 4/12) with pre-session ontological priming protocols will show statistically significantly smaller belief shifts at six-month follow-up than participants in low-constraint settings (Void Index ≥ 7/12) receiving identical pharmacological doses.

**Falsified if:** A replication of the Timmermann et al. paradigm with constraint tier as a between-subjects variable finds no significant interaction between constraint level and magnitude of metaphysical belief shift (η² < 0.04), or if high-constraint participants show belief shifts equivalent to or exceeding those in low-constraint conditions. This prediction directly tests whether the belief-shift effect is pharmacological and therefore uniform, or architectural and therefore modifiable — the latter being the Void Framework's core claim.

---

**Prediction 4:** Therapist boundary violations in MDMA-assisted therapy contexts — the central safety concern cited in the FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee's 9-to-2 vote against MAPP1 approval on June 4, 2024 — will cluster statistically at the D2 stage of the drift cascade rather than appearing as isolated events, and will be preceded by documentable D1-level drift (agency attribution to the therapeutic relationship or to the compound itself) in session transcripts or participant-reported experience at the preceding session.

**Falsified if:** Forensic review of at least 50 documented boundary-violation cases drawn from MAPS trial adverse event records, FDA submission data, and Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies investigator files finds that fewer than 60% of violations are preceded by identifiable D1-stage indicators in session documentation within the prior two sessions. The evidence type required is retrospective structured content analysis of session transcripts and participant-reported experience narratives, coded by raters blind to outcome, using the Drift Cascade Coding Manual derived from the Void Framework taxonomy.

---

**Prediction 5:** Regulatory frameworks that specify all three constraint dimensions — transparency of mechanism, invariance of facilitator conduct rules, and independence of oversight from service providers — will produce Void Index scores at or below 4/12 and measurably lower rates of adverse events per 1,000 facilitation hours compared to frameworks specifying fewer than two dimensions, with a hazard ratio of at least 2.0 favoring three-dimension-compliant frameworks.

**Falsified if:** Comparative analysis of adverse event rates across Oregon (Measure 109), Colorado (Proposition 122), and at least two international jurisdictions with differing regulatory architectures finds no statistically significant difference in adverse event rates per 1,000 facilitation hours between full-specification and partial-specification frameworks (HR confidence interval crosses 1.0), or if facilitator training hours alone (irrespective of constraint dimensionality) account for all observed rate differences. Oregon's 160-hour minimum training requirement (Oregon Health Authority, 2023) provides the baseline training covariate necessary to isolate the structural constraint effect from the training-volume effect. This prediction is partially checkable within 12 months as Oregon's facilitation center registry accumulates a sufficient denominator of completed sessions during 2024–2025 to support preliminary incidence rate estimation.

---

Taken together, these five predictions structure a falsification program that can proceed on multiple timescales — from the 12-month Oregon registry analysis to the multi-year international comparative study — and that remains falsifiable at each stage independently. The Framework does not require all five predictions to hold; it requires that the geometry-dependence claim survive across the full set. A finding that compound type consistently outperforms platform Void Index as a predictor of adverse outcomes would constitute strong evidence against the framework's central thesis and would redirect attention toward purely pharmacological harm models of the kind the framework is designed to supplement.

## X. Conclusion

Psychedelic therapy presents the Void Framework with its most pharmacologically intensive case. Across the preceding analysis, the domain scores 7/12 on the Void Index — Opacity 3, Responsiveness 3, Coupled Attention 3, Modifiers minus 2 for partial regulatory structure — yielding a Péclet number of 3.8. This places the domain at the boundary of Phase III Crystal and Phase IV Vortex, the Pandemonium threshold. No behavioral void analyzed in this series — not algorithmic recommendation systems, not conversational AI, not electronic gambling machines — has achieved a composite score above 6/12 under standard deployment conditions. The psychedelic domain reaches 7/12 because, uniquely, all three primary conditions are simultaneously pharmacologically amplified rather than incidentally activated. Opacity is not environmental but perceptual: the observer's own cognitive apparatus becomes opaque to itself. Responsiveness is not architectural but neurochemical: 5-HT2A-mediated suppression of the default mode network removes the filtering mechanisms that normally constrain apophenia, producing the appearance of universal communicative intent (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019, *Pharmacological Reviews*). Engaged attention is not behavioral but captive: for the duration of compound action — four to six hours for psilocybin, eight to twelve for LSD — there is no opt-out pathway (Nichols, 2016, *Pharmacological Reviews*).

The critical structural finding is that this intensity is constraint-separable from therapeutic outcome. Davis et al. (2021, *JAMA Psychiatry*) reported effect sizes of Cohen's d = 2.5 for single-dose psilocybin under high-constraint clinical geometry. Carhart-Harris et al. (2021, *Nature Medicine*) achieved 70% response rates in treatment-resistant depression with equivalent structural rigor. These results are not evidence against void intensity — they are evidence that void intensity channeled through Transparent, Invariant, and Independent constraint architecture produces the therapeutic signal. The same pharmacological void, absent constraint specification, produces the D1→D2→D3 cascade: agency attribution to encountered entities or to the facilitator, boundary erosion under conditions of manufactured intimacy, and harm facilitation documented systematically by Marks (2021, *Journal of Medical Ethics*) in underground MDMA contexts. Platform-level Void Index scores reflect this geometry dependence: Oregon Measure 109 licensed services score approximately 5/12; underground retreats score 10/12; integration-only therapy with no acute drug administration scores 2/12, anchoring the constraint pole.

The regulatory moment makes this analysis immediately actionable. The FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee's June 4, 2024, vote of 9–2 against MAPP1 approval cited therapist boundary violations and participant vulnerability as primary safety concerns — a precise, if implicit, identification of void architecture failure rather than pharmacological failure. The committee's rejection operationalizes, in regulatory language, what the Drift Cascade predicts structurally. Oregon Health Authority's 160-hour facilitator training minimum under Measure 109 represents a constraint specification attempt, though the framework analysis in Section VI identifies its incompleteness with respect to Invariance: training minimums that do not specify constraint-consistent content produce variable constraint geometry across practitioners. Colorado Proposition 122's healing center framework, as of 2024 rulemaking, reproduces the same gap. The EU's European Medicines Agency has not yet published psychedelic-specific guidance, but its adaptive pathways framework, applied to esketamine under EMA/H/C/004535, establishes a regulatory template under which constraint specification requirements could be formalized ahead of anticipated European psilocybin submissions projected for 2026–2028.

Within the Void Framework scoring pipeline, the psychedelic domain occupies the highest-intensity position among biomedical verticals and shares the Phase III/IV boundary with immersive extended-reality environments and high-frequency trading platforms under adverse liquidity conditions. The data moat for this vertical is unusually deep. The FDA's complete response letter for MAPP1, the Oregon Health Authority's incident reporting database operational since January 2023, MAPS Public Benefit Corporation's unblinded session transcript corpus, and the ongoing Imperial College London psilocybin trial registry together constitute a constraint-variation dataset with direct Pe measurement potential. Platform-level Void Index scores can be calculated from facilitator training documentation, session protocol texts, integration requirement specifications, and adverse event report distributions — all of which are becoming progressively more available under mandatory reporting provisions of state licensure frameworks.

The theoretical contribution of this analysis extends beyond psychedelics. By establishing that void intensity is pharmacologically achievable and that Pe = 3.8 at domain level represents the ceiling of the Void Framework's current measurement range, Paper 32 anchors the upper bound of the scoring scale with empirical specificity. Every domain analysis that follows can calibrate against this ceiling. The dissolution void is not exceptional because psychedelics are exceptional substances. It is exceptional because it demonstrates that the three conditions the framework identifies as jointly sufficient for predictable harm are independently real variables with independent amplification mechanisms — and that their simultaneous maximization, in the absence of constraint architecture, is a reproducible experimental finding, not a theoretical extrapolation.
