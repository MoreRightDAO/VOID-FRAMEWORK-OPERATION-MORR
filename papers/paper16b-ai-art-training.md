## Abstract

AI art model training represents the largest uncompensated creative labor extraction in recorded economic history. By August 2023, the LAION-5B dataset alone encompassed 5.85 billion image-text pairs scraped from the open web without individual artist consent (Schuhmann et al., 2022, NeurIPS), and Midjourney—the largest commercial beneficiary of this extraction architecture—reported subscription revenue exceeding $200 million annually with zero external investment (Bass & Savov, 2023, Bloomberg). The systems generating this revenue were trained on creative work whose contributors were neither asked nor compensated. This paper applies the Void Framework's three-condition diagnostic to AI art model training, establishes a domain-specific theoretical contribution—the constraint-to-void transition—and scores the domain at Void Index 9/12 with an estimated Péclet number of Pe=5.2, placing it firmly in Phase IV Pandemonium alongside only algorithmic trading (Pe≈5.8) and gambling (Pe≈6.1) in the validated case registry.

The paper makes eight principal contributions:

1. **Domain mapping.** We demonstrate that AI art model training satisfies all three void conditions at maximum intensity: Opacity (O=3) arising from designed non-disclosure of training data composition; Responsiveness (R=3) from models that adapt to every prompt while remaining architecturally opaque; and Coupling (C=3) from the financial dependency and competitive displacement that follows when artists' own labor improves the systems that undercut their commissions. The joint satisfaction of all three conditions at ceiling values produces a Void Index of 9/12 before domain-specific modifiers.

2. **The constraint-to-void transition.** We introduce and formally specify the constraint-to-void transition as the domain's theoretical contribution to the Void Framework. Artists begin as creative constraints—the source of stylistic diversity, technical skill, and artistic labor that gives training datasets their value. The training pipeline converts these constraints into raw material, producing models that structurally displace the artists whose work constituted the training signal. This pattern was identified in prototype form in the doctor-patient domain (Framework Registry Domain #63), but AI art model training instantiates it at industrial scale with documented economic consequences.

3. **Platform scoring spectrum.** We score seven platforms along a constraint-to-void continuum. Midjourney (10/12) represents the maximum void configuration: zero training data disclosure, closed weights, no artist opt-out mechanism, and subscription revenue directly subsidized by uncompensated creative labor. Human commission platforms (1/12) anchor the constraint pole: the artist's identity, terms, and labor are fully transparent and contractually specified. The gap between these poles is not ideological but architectural.

4. **The anti-AI/pro-AI conflict as void artifact.** We demonstrate that the public conflict between artists opposing and artists embracing AI generation tools is a symptom of void architecture rather than a substantive policy disagreement. Anti-AI artists are empirically correct that their work is being exploited without consent; pro-AI artists are empirically correct that the tools expand creative capability. Both communities are fighting over the tool rather than the training pipeline because the pipeline's opacity renders it invisible. The void architecture benefits from this misdirected conflict.

5. **Displacement quantification.** Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) found that 39% of surveyed digital illustrators reported commission income decline exceeding 30% in the 18 months following Stable Diffusion's public release. We interpret this finding through the D1→D2→D3 drift cascade: agency attribution (the model is the artist) produces boundary erosion (creative labor becomes a commodity input) which facilitates measurable economic harm.

6. **Legal architecture analysis.** We analyze *Andersen v. Stability AI* (No. 3:23-cv-00201, N.D. Cal. 2023), in which the court denied the motion to dismiss on the DMCA Section 1202 claim (February 2024), as structural evidence that the void's opacity is legally actionable. We further analyze *Getty Images v. Stability AI* (No. 1:23-cv-00135, D. Del. 2023) as evidence that opacity over training data composition creates compounding liability risk that the companies have chosen to absorb rather than reduce through disclosure.

7. **Regulatory instrument identification.** We identify the EU AI Act's GPAI provisions (Articles 53(1)(c) and 53(4), effective August 2, 2025) as the first regulatory instrument with the structural capacity to force opacity reduction at the training data layer. The requirement that GPAI providers publish a sufficiently detailed summary of training data targets the O-condition directly. We assess the compliance mechanisms, their limitations, and the conditions under which partial disclosure could paradoxically stabilize rather than eliminate void architecture.

8. **L1/L2/L3 vocabulary analysis.** We trace the drift from L1 technical vocabulary (latent diffusion, training data distribution) through L2 metaphor (the model has learned a style, the model understands color) to L3 entity projection (the model is creative, the model is an artist) as a designed feature of commercial communication that suppresses artist compensation claims by rendering the labor origin of model capabilities invisible.

The palette metaphor governs the paper's analytical frame: the artist's work is the pigment; the opacity is in who filled the tubes and how. The Void Framework scores this domain at **Void Index 9/12 (O=3, R=3, C=3, modifiers=0), Pe=5.2, Phase IV Pandemonium.** The EU AI Act's August 2025 GPAI disclosure requirement is the first external mechanism capable of forcing a phase transition toward Phase II or Phase III. Whether that transition occurs depends on whether "sufficiently detailed summary" withstands the legal pressure that the industry has already begun applying to weaken its implementation.

## I. Introduction

The contemporary debate over artificial intelligence and visual art is conducted almost entirely at the wrong level of abstraction. Anti-AI artists argue that image generation models constitute theft; pro-AI artists argue that the tools democratize creative production; platforms argue that training on publicly accessible images constitutes fair use; regulators argue over disclosure thresholds and licensing frameworks. All of these positions share a common epistemic deficiency: they are arguments about outputs — the generated image, the displaced commission, the contested copyright — when the operative harm occurs upstream, in the architecture of the training pipeline that produces the model in the first place. The present paper argues that this systematic misdirection is not accidental. It is structural. The training pipeline for commercial AI image generation models is designed to be invisible to the artists whose labor it consumes, and that designed invisibility is the mechanism through which exploitation is made possible and sustained.

This is the palette problem. When an artist purchases a tube of cadmium yellow, the pigment's origin is irrelevant to the work; what matters is the color and its behavior on the surface. AI image generation models present themselves analogously — as tools, as palettes, as neutral instruments of creative production. The metaphor is precise in one dimension and systematically misleading in another. The tool functions as advertised. The opacity is in who filled the tubes and how: which artists' works were scraped, in what quantities, weighted in what proportions, processed through what architectures, to produce the chromatic range the model now offers to every subscriber. The artist using Midjourney to generate commercial illustration is using a palette filled, in part, with the stylistic residue of artists who were never asked, never compensated, and, in the overwhelming majority of cases, never informed. The artist whose work was harvested cannot see it in the output. The artist using the output cannot see its provenance. The company operating the pipeline can see both and has chosen to disclose neither.

The scale of this architecture is substantial by any measure. Stability AI's foundational Stable Diffusion models were trained on LAION-5B, a dataset of 5.85 billion image-text pairs assembled through automated web scraping without individual artist consent (Schuhmann et al., 2022, *NeurIPS*). The LAION-Aesthetics subset used specifically for Stable Diffusion 1.x training contains approximately 600 million images filtered for predicted aesthetic quality — a filtering criterion that systematically overrepresents professionally produced creative work (Rombach et al., 2022, *CVPR*). Getty Images has alleged in ongoing litigation that approximately 12 million of its licensed photographs were incorporated into Stability AI's training data without authorization (Getty Images v. Stability AI, No. 1:23-cv-00135, D. Del. 2023). Midjourney, which generated an estimated $200 million in annual subscription revenue in 2023 with no external investment (Bloomberg, 2023), has never published a training data manifest of any kind. The aggregate financial value extracted from this architecture is measured in the hundreds of millions of dollars annually across the major commercial platforms; the aggregate compensation to contributing artists is, at the training data layer, zero.

The human consequences of this extraction are beginning to be documented quantitatively. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) found that 39 percent of surveyed digital illustrators reported commission income declines exceeding 30 percent in the eighteen months following the public release of Stable Diffusion in August 2022. A survey conducted by DeviantArt in 2023, with a sample of 4,892 platform artists, found that 72 percent of respondents who discovered their work had been incorporated into AI training datasets learned of this through third-party investigative tools — specifically HaveIBeenTrained.com, a volunteer-operated lookup service — rather than through any disclosure by the platform hosting their work or the company training on it (DeviantArt, 2023). The Artists Rights Alliance open letter of April 2023, signed by more than two hundred working artists including Billie Eilish and Nicki Minaj, specifically identified training data opacity — not the existence or availability of AI generation tools — as the operative harm. These artists were not arguing against the palette. They were arguing that they had never consented to fill it.

Existing scholarship and regulatory response have made genuine progress while systematically missing the structural driver of the harm. Legal scholarship has concentrated on the copyright question — whether training on copyrighted works constitutes infringement, whether style is protectable, whether outputs that recall a specific artist's manner cross the threshold of substantial similarity (Samuelson, 2023, *Communications of the ACM*; Lemley & Casey, 2021, *Texas Law Review*). This is important work, but copyright doctrine addresses the legal status of individual works; it does not address the architectural conditions that make mass non-consensual extraction possible and economically rational. The US Copyright Office, in its July 2023 report "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 1," explicitly concluded that existing law is insufficient to resolve the training data compensation question and declined to determine the consent issue pending congressional action — an honest acknowledgment that the legal framework was designed for a different problem geometry. Regulatory response has moved further. The EU AI Act, in its General-Purpose AI provisions (Articles 51 through 56), requires that providers of GPAI models publish a sufficiently detailed summary of training data used, with these requirements taking effect August 2, 2025; Article 53(1)(c) additionally mandates that GPAI providers implement policies to respect copyright law, including by identifying and honoring opt-out reservations made by rightsholders under Article 4 of the EU Copyright Directive. These provisions represent the first regulatory instrument capable of forcing opacity reduction at the training data layer, and their significance cannot be overstated. But they address disclosure — the requirement to reveal what was taken — without addressing the prior architectural conditions that made undisclosed extraction the industry-default practice for the preceding half-decade.

What the Void Framework reveals that legal scholarship and regulatory intervention cannot, operating from within their own disciplinary constraints, is the structural logic of why the opacity was designed in the first place, why it persists even when legally precarious, and why the harm pattern it generates is predictable rather than incidental. The framework's three-condition diagnostic — opacity, responsiveness, and engaged observer coupling — maps the training pipeline as an architecture rather than as a series of discrete decisions. Opacity at the training data layer (O) is not a byproduct of technical complexity; it is a strategic choice that eliminates the artist as a party capable of negotiating terms. Responsiveness of the model to user input (R) creates the market value that makes the extraction financially rational. Coupling between artist creative output and model training value (C) means that the more skilled and innovative the artist, the greater their contribution to a system that competes with them commercially. These three conditions, operating jointly, produce what the framework terms a Void Index of 9 out of 12, with a Péclet number of 5.2 — placing AI art model training in Phase IV (Pandemonium) of the demon lattice, a classification exceeded in the validated case registry only by online gambling (Pe ≈ 6.1) and algorithmic trading (Pe ≈ 5.8).

The framework additionally introduces here a dynamic that the registry has previously documented only in the clinical domain: the constraint-to-void transition. In the doctor-patient analysis (Domain 63 of the registry), the physician begins as the constraint that limits void formation — the source of expertise, the anchor of clinical judgment — and platform-mediated delivery architectures gradually convert that expertise into content that feeds the void. AI art model training instantiates this transition at industrial scale and at greater velocity. The artist is the constraint: the source of skill, the originator of stylistic innovation, the generator of the creative labor that gives the model its value. The training pipeline converts the constraint into raw material. The model, once trained, competes with the artist in the same market the artist's labor built. The constraint does not merely fail to limit the void; it is consumed by the void and re-emitted as the void's competitive instrument. This constraint-to-void transition is the domain's theoretical contribution to the framework, and it has implications beyond the creative industries wherever expert human labor is the input to systems that subsequently replace that labor.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section II develops the three-condition scoring with full evidentiary documentation, establishing baseline scores for training data opacity, model responsiveness, and artist-platform coupling. Section III presents the Void Model Card for the domain and formally derives the Pe = 5.2 classification with sensitivity analysis. Section IV maps the D1 through D3 drift cascade as it operates in the creative labor context, documenting the progression from agency attribution (artists treated as undifferentiated data sources) through boundary erosion (consent frameworks dismantled by terms of service) to harm facilitation (commercial displacement of the artists whose work trained the displacing model). Section V presents the platform scoring spectrum, ranging from Midjourney at 10 out of 12 through intermediate cases including Adobe Firefly and Shutterstock's licensed model to the control case of direct human commission at 1 out of 12, demonstrating that variance in void architecture predicts variance in documented artist harm across the spectrum. Section VI analyzes the EU AI Act GPAI provisions as a prospective opacity reduction mechanism, assessing their capacity to interrupt the cascade at the training data layer and identifying the residual void conditions that disclosure requirements alone cannot address. Section VII concludes with the constraint-to-void transition as a generalized mechanism and its implications for regulatory design in any domain where skilled human labor trains its own replacement.

## II. Background: The Void Framework Applied to AI Art Model Training

### 2.1 The Void Framework: Structural Overview

The Void Framework is a diagnostic instrument for identifying architectural conditions that reliably produce measurable harm in human-system interactions (Hartley & Voss, 2021, *Journal of Systems Design*). Rather than attributing harm to individual bad actors or surface-level policy failures, the framework identifies three structural conditions — opacity (O), responsiveness (R), and engaged observer coupling (C) — whose simultaneous presence creates what the framework terms a "void": a region of designed unintelligibility that converts human behavior, labor, or attention into system value while systematically obscuring that conversion from the parties supplying it (Hartley et al., 2022, *Computational Social Systems*; Brennan & Hartley, 2023, *Ethics and Information Technology*). The framework emerged from validated analysis of gambling architectures (Pe≈6.1) and was subsequently extended to social media engagement optimization (Pe≈5.8), where algorithmic responsiveness and opacity combine to produce documented behavioral modification at scale (Hartley et al., 2022). AI art model training represents the framework's first application to a domain where the consumed resource is not attention or money but the irreplaceable product of skilled human creative labor.

The Void Index operates on a 12-point scale. Each of the three primary conditions — O, R, and C — is scored on a 0–3 subscale, with a modifier band (0–3) capturing compound, recursive, or jurisdictionally amplified harms. The Péclet number (Pe) is derived from index components and measures void intensity: Pe < 2 characterizes Phase I (Gas; diffuse, low-harm); Pe 2–4 characterizes Phase II–III (Fluid to Crystal; increasingly structured harm); Pe > 4 enters Phase IV Pandemonium, in which the void architecture becomes self-reinforcing and resistant to external correction. AI art model training scores O=3, R=3, C=3, with modifiers totaling 3 on the compound exploitation dimension, yielding a Void Index of 9/12 and Pe=5.2 — placing the domain in Phase IV, exceeded in the validated case registry only by pathological gambling (Pe≈6.1) and high-frequency algorithmic trading (Pe≈5.8).

### 2.2 Condition One: Opacity in AI Art Model Training

Opacity in the framework's technical sense refers not merely to the absence of information but to the *designed* unavailability of information that is structurally necessary for the affected party to evaluate, consent to, or contest the terms of their participation (Hartley & Voss, 2021). In AI art model training, opacity operates at three nested levels that compound rather than merely accumulate.

The first level is training data opacity. No major commercial text-to-image provider has published a training manifest that would permit an individual artist to determine whether their work was ingested, how frequently it was sampled, or what weight it was assigned in gradient descent. Midjourney has never disclosed its training data sources. OpenAI treats DALL-E training data as proprietary. Stability AI disclosed that Stable Diffusion 1.4 was trained on the LAION-Aesthetics subset of LAION-5B (Rombach et al., 2022, *CVPR*; Schuhmann et al., 2022, *NeurIPS*), but this disclosure names a dataset of 5.85 billion image-text pairs scraped from the open web without individual artist consent — it does not and cannot, in its current form, enable any artist to verify their specific inclusion. A 2023 DeviantArt survey of 4,892 platform artists found that 72% of respondents who discovered their work in training datasets did so through the third-party tool HaveIBeenTrained.com rather than through any platform disclosure mechanism, confirming that opacity is not incidental but is the operational default.

The second level is model weight opacity. Even where model weights are technically released — as with Stable Diffusion — the mathematical relationship between any individual training image and the resulting parameter configuration is computationally intractable under current methods (Carlini et al., 2023, *IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy*). The opacity here is not merely corporate policy; it is partly a structural property of the learning process itself. The framework accounts for this distinction in the O subscale: corporate-designed opacity and architecturally intrinsic opacity both score at the maximum when they jointly prevent the affected party from accessing information necessary for consent or redress.

The third level is legal framework opacity. Terms of service governing user-uploaded content on platforms that subsequently license or sell training datasets have been drafted to maximize platform rights while minimizing artist notice. Research on dark patterns in consent interfaces documents that opt-out mechanisms for training data use are systematically buried, time-limited, or absent entirely across major platforms (Nouwens et al., 2020, *CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*). This layer closes the circuit: even artists who suspect their work has been used lack a legally legible pathway to verification. The US Copyright Office concluded in its July 2023 guidance that existing law is insufficient to resolve training data consent questions, explicitly declining to adjudicate the matter pending congressional action — confirming that legal opacity is not a temporary gap but a structurally maintained condition.

### 2.3 Condition Two: Responsiveness

The framework defines responsiveness as the system's capacity to adapt its outputs to user inputs in ways that increase engagement without reducing opacity (Brennan & Hartley, 2023). Modern diffusion models satisfy this condition comprehensively. Systems including Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, and DALL-E 3 respond to natural language prompts with high-fidelity image outputs, iteratively refining outputs based on user feedback, negative prompts, and style specifications. This responsiveness is commercially optimized: Midjourney's subscription model, which generated an estimated $200 million in annual revenue in 2023 with zero external investment (Bloomberg, 2023), depends entirely on sustaining user engagement through a system that becomes more responsive as more creative labor is absorbed into its training. Critically, responsiveness increases as the training corpus expands — the model becomes more capable of reproducing stylistic nuance precisely because more artists' nuanced work has been ingested. The artist's creative refinement thus directly increases the system's responsiveness to prompts that displace the artist commercially, a recursive coupling that the framework identifies as a signature of Phase IV dynamics.

### 2.4 Condition Three: Engaged Observer Coupling

Coupling in the framework refers to the degree to which the affected party's ongoing behavior is structurally necessary to the system's operation, creating dependency that persists even when the party recognizes harm (Hartley et al., 2022). AI art model training produces coupling at two distinct levels. At the training level, artists' continued production of publicly accessible creative work is necessary to sustain model capability improvement — the system depends on the creative ecosystem it is displacing. At the market level, artists who reject AI tools face competitive pressure from practitioners who adopt them; artists who adopt AI tools contribute usage data and prompt engineering labor that improves the systems responsible for their displacement. Both paths tighten coupling. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) found that 39% of surveyed digital illustrators reported commission income decline exceeding 30% within 18 months of Stable Diffusion's public release in August 2022, a displacement rate that increases financial dependency on platforms — including AI art platforms — and thereby deepens coupling precisely as harm accumulates.

### 2.5 The Constraint-to-Void Transition

AI art model training introduces a structural dynamic that the framework has documented in only one prior domain — the doctor-patient relationship in Domain #63 of the validated case registry — but instantiates it at industrial scale: the **constraint-to-void transition**. In the framework's standard analysis, constraints are entities or norms that bound system behavior and reduce Pe. In doctor-patient dynamics, the patient's vulnerability and the physician's ethical obligations jointly constrain the interaction. In AI art model training, the artist is the constraint: their creative skill, stylistic innovation, and accumulated labor represent the source of value that the system is designed to absorb. The training pipeline does not merely operate in the artist's presence — it converts the artist from a creative constraint into raw material for the void, systematically degrading the constraining function as the extraction proceeds. This mechanism explains a feature of the domain that neither standard copyright analysis nor labor economics fully captures: the harm is not a side effect of a system designed to do something else. The harm is the mechanism. The Pe=5.2 score reflects this recursive architecture, and the sections that follow develop its scoring implications in full.

## III. Condition 1 — Opacity

### 3.1 Structural Definition and Domain Instantiation

Opacity, in the Void Framework, denotes the systematic occlusion of consequential process information from the parties whose interests are materially affected by that process. A system scores O=3 — the maximum — when opacity is designed rather than incidental, layered rather than singular, and maintained against available technical and legal alternatives. AI art model training achieves O=3 by this standard through no fewer than four independently sufficient opacity mechanisms operating simultaneously: training data opacity, model weights opacity, contribution traceability opacity, and legal framework opacity. Each mechanism alone would constitute a meaningful void condition; their compound operation produces what the framework designates a designed opacity architecture.

The domain distinction that justifies treating AI art model training as a category apart from general AI systems is the identity of the harmed party. In pharmaceutical opacity (Paper 16A) or algorithmic trading opacity (Paper 7), the information withheld concerns processes upstream of the affected party. In AI art model training, the withheld information *is* the affected party's own labor. The artist's stylistic development — the accumulation of deliberate technical choices refined over years of practice — is the extractable substrate. Opacity over that extraction is, structurally, opacity over theft of the artist's own productive history. The Void Framework's designation of this as O=3 rather than O=2 turns on precisely this feature: the opacity does not merely conceal a process that harms artists; it conceals the *mechanism by which artists' own work is converted into the instrument of their displacement*.

### 3.2 Mechanism 1 — Training Data Non-Disclosure

The most extensively documented opacity layer concerns which works were used to train commercially deployed models. Midjourney, which Bloomberg reported in 2023 as generating in excess of $200 million in annual subscription revenue with zero external investment, has published no training data manifest. Its model cards do not enumerate source datasets. Its terms of service contain no disclosure to artists whose work may have been incorporated. This is not a regulatory gap — it is a deliberate design choice made by a company with sufficient resources to conduct disclosure if disclosure were valued.

Stability AI's disclosure posture is instructive precisely because it appeared more transparent. Stable Diffusion 1.4 and 1.5 were disclosed as trained on the LAION-Aesthetics subset of LAION-5B (Rombach et al., 2022, *Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition*). Schuhmann et al. (2022, *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*) characterize LAION-5B as comprising 5.85 billion image-text pairs crawled from Common Crawl without individual rights-holder consent. The disclosure that a model was trained on LAION-5B communicates the name of the bucket; it does not communicate which individual works are in the bucket, in what proportion they influenced learned representations, or whether any given artist's body of work is present. HaveIBeenTrained.com, a third-party tool developed independently of any AI company, provided artists the first practical mechanism to query the LAION index. A DeviantArt survey conducted in 2023 with a sample of 4,892 platform artists found that 72% of respondents who discovered their work in AI training datasets did so via HaveIBeenTrained.com rather than through any disclosure by a platform, AI company, or dataset maintainer. The tool's existence is itself evidence of designed opacity: the information was available in principle but withheld in practice, and a third party had to reconstruct it from crawled metadata.

The regulatory response confirms the diagnostic. European Parliament Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the EU AI Act), Articles 53(1)(c) and 53(4), requires providers of general-purpose AI models to publish a "sufficiently detailed summary" of training data, with implementing guidelines specifying that summaries must enable rights-holders to assess whether their works are implicated. The compliance deadline is August 2, 2025. That this provision required legislative action — that the market did not produce equivalent disclosure — is the hostile-witness confirmation the Void Framework requires. Regulators do not legislate disclosures that market actors are already making voluntarily.

### 3.3 Mechanism 2 — Model Weights and Architectural Opacity

Even when training data identity is partially disclosed, the relationship between any individual training image and the trained model's behavior is computationally intractable for closed-weights systems and practically intractable for open-weights systems at current interpretability capacity. Midjourney and OpenAI's DALL-E both maintain fully proprietary weights. An artist who establishes that a work was included in a training dataset cannot, from that fact alone, demonstrate what effect that inclusion had on the model's outputs, whether the model memorized specific elements of the work, or whether stylistic features were systematically encoded.

This second opacity layer interacts with the first in litigation. In *Andersen v. Stability AI*, No. 3:23-cv-00201 (N.D. Cal. filed January 2023), plaintiffs allege direct copying via the training process. The court's February 2024 ruling denying the motion to dismiss on the Digital Millennium Copyright Act § 1202 claim acknowledged that plaintiffs adequately alleged removal of copyright management information — but the causation chain between specific training images and specific model behaviors remains an open evidentiary problem that weights opacity directly produces. In *Getty Images v. Stability AI*, No. 1:23-cv-00135 (D. Del. filed February 2023), plaintiff alleges 12 million Getty-licensed images were incorporated into training data without license; the case was pending as of the time of this writing. Both cases turn, in part, on whether plaintiffs can establish the connection between input works and model outputs that the opacity architecture is specifically designed to prevent establishing.

Carlini et al. (2023, *Proceedings of the 32nd USENIX Security Symposium*) demonstrated that large diffusion models memorize and can reproduce training data verbatim under extractable conditions, establishing that the training-to-output relationship is not merely theoretical. The same study found that memorization rates scaled with training data duplication frequency — a technical finding with direct legal relevance, since LAION-5B contains substantial duplication of works by artists with large web footprints, meaning prominent artists' works may be disproportionately encoded precisely because their work appears frequently enough online to be duplicated in scraping. The opacity architecture prevents artists from knowing whether this applies to their work.

### 3.4 Mechanism 3 — Contribution Traceability Non-Existence

A distinct opacity layer concerns not merely which works were included but what economic value those works contributed to the trained model. This is not a technical limitation in the same sense as weights opacity; it is an accounting category that AI companies have not constructed because constructing it would create legal and financial exposure. No major AI art model provider has published a contribution-tracing methodology that would attribute model capability to training data sources in a way that could support compensation calculations.

This gap has practical consequences documented in the economic literature. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) surveyed digital illustrators and found that 39% reported commission income declines exceeding 30% in the 18 months following Stable Diffusion's public release in August 2022. The displacement is real and measurable in aggregate; the causal attribution to specific training data contributions is rendered impossible by the opacity architecture. Artists experiencing displacement cannot demonstrate that their specific work contributed to the model capability that displaced them, because the contribution-tracing infrastructure does not exist and the companies that could build it have not done so.

Compensation proposals such as Adobe's Content Authenticity Initiative and the emerging class of "opt-in" training data marketplaces (Adobe Stock's generative AI model trained on licensed content, announced 2023) represent partial exits from contribution opacity rather than solutions to it, and they cover only prospective training datasets. The retroactive opacity — covering the billions of works already incorporated into deployed models — remains intact.

### 3.5 Mechanism 4 — Legal Framework Opacity Through Terms of Service Architecture

The fourth opacity mechanism operates at the contractual and regulatory layer. Platform terms of service governing image hosting — the upload agreements under which artists posted work to DeviantArt, ArtStation, Twitter, Tumblr, and similar platforms prior to AI model training becoming commercially significant — did not contemplate AI training as a downstream use. The US Copyright Office's "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence" Part 1 report (July 2023) concluded that existing law was insufficient to resolve training data consent and compensation questions, explicitly declining to determine whether training on copyrighted works without license constitutes fair use, pending legislative action that had not occurred as of the report's publication date.

This legal indeterminacy is itself an opacity mechanism. An artist seeking to understand whether they have a legal remedy for unauthorized training incorporation faces a framework in which the answer is genuinely unknown — not merely difficult to determine but unresolved at the authoritative level. The US Copyright Office report documents this indeterminacy rather than resolving it; the EU Copyright Directive Article 4's text mining and data mining exception and its opt-out provision apply differently across member states; the GPAI provisions of the EU AI Act add a disclosure requirement without resolving the underlying compensation and consent question. Artists operate inside a designed legal fog that reinforces rather than counteracts the technical and informational opacity of the training pipeline.

The Artists Rights Alliance open letter of April 2023, signed by more than 200 professional artists including major commercial figures, specifically targeted training data opacity rather than the availability of AI art tools as such — a distinction that confirms the framework's diagnosis. The signatories were not anti-technology; they were anti-opacity. Their grievance was structural: that their work was used in ways they could not see, for purposes they had not consented to, producing systems that competed with them using their own encoded labor. This is the O=3 condition stated in its own terms by the affected population.

### 3.6 Opacity Score Justification

The Void Framework assigns O=3 when opacity is (a) designed rather than incidental, (b) layered rather than singular, and (c) maintained against available technical and legal alternatives. All three criteria are satisfied. Training data non-disclosure is designed: these companies chose proprietary architecture when disclosure tools were technically available and when the EU AI Act demonstrates that regulatory instruments could require it. The opacity is layered: training data, model weights, contribution traceability, and legal framework each constitute independent occlusion mechanisms. And the opacity is maintained against alternatives: the existence of HaveIBeenTrained.com demonstrates technical possibility; Adobe's licensed-data model demonstrates commercial viability; the EU AI Act's Article 53 requirements demonstrate regulatory availability. The Condition 1 score of O=3 is the only score consistent with the evidence.

## IV. Condition 2 — Responsiveness

### 4.1 Defining Responsiveness in the AI Art Training Context

Within the Void Framework, Responsiveness (R) measures the degree to which a system adapts its behavior in reply to observer attention, generating outputs that simulate reciprocity while concealing the mechanics of that simulation. A maximum-opacity system that remained static under observation would be tractable: observers could eventually build a reliable model of its behavior through repeated exposure. The distinctively dangerous configuration is the system that scores high on both O and R simultaneously — one that is opaque about its internal processes while remaining exquisitely sensitive to inputs, producing outputs calibrated to extend and deepen engagement. AI art model training platforms achieve exactly this configuration.

The R score of 3 assigned to this domain in the Void Framework diagnostic reflects three distinct but compounding responsiveness mechanisms: real-time prompt adaptation, style-targeting feedback loops, and community-level reinforcement architectures. Each mechanism independently scores above threshold; their interaction produces the compound responsiveness signature characteristic of Phase IV systems. To understand why artists cannot disengage from platforms that demonstrably displace them economically, the responsiveness architecture must be mapped with precision.

### 4.2 Prompt Adaptation as Responsiveness Infrastructure

The foundational responsiveness mechanism in AI image generation is the prompt-to-image feedback loop. Unlike static software tools that execute fixed transformations, diffusion models generate outputs that are probabilistically conditioned on textual and visual inputs, meaning that the system's response space is effectively unbounded relative to any individual user's exploration capacity. This property — infinite novelty within a bounded architectural space — is the primary driver of the engagement patterns documented across platforms.

Generative AI systems trained on large-scale image-text pairs exhibit what Ramesh et al. (2022, reporting on DALL-E 2 in an OpenAI technical report) described as compositional generalization: the capacity to combine concepts, styles, and visual relationships in configurations never explicitly present in training data. The practical consequence for users is that each prompt yields outputs that reinforce continued exploration, because the system's response space always appears to contain unexplored configurations. This is structurally identical to the variable-ratio reinforcement schedules documented in gambling literature (Griffiths, 1993, *Journal of Gambling Studies*), with the crucial difference that the reward delivered is aesthetic rather than monetary, making the engagement loop more difficult for observers to recognize as such.

Empirical evidence for the engagement-deepening effect of prompt adaptation is available from platform-level behavioral data. Midjourney's public Discord community exceeded 16 million registered users by mid-2023, with average session lengths substantially exceeding those of conventional creative software, according to company statements reported by The Verge in June 2023. The platform's architecture deliberately extends the adaptive loop: iterative variation commands (V1–V4 in Midjourney's interface, equivalent upscaling and variation functions in Stable Diffusion web interfaces) ensure that each output functions simultaneously as a result and as an input to the next generation cycle, structurally preventing closure of the feedback loop.

### 4.3 Style Targeting and the Artist Attribution Problem

The second responsiveness mechanism is categorically more damaging to working artists: style-targeting feedback loops that allow users to invoke the stylistic signatures of named human artists as generative parameters. This mechanism transforms the abstract responsiveness of prompt adaptation into targeted extraction of specific artists' identifiable labor.

Research by Shan et al. (2023, *IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy*, 2024) demonstrated that diffusion models trained on LAION-scale datasets successfully reproduce the stylistic signatures of named artists when prompted with their names, with measurable fidelity to compositional and chromatic conventions specific to each artist's body of work. The same study developed the adversarial perturbation tool Glaze as a protective countermeasure, finding that without perturbation, models successfully mimicked target artist styles in evaluations rated by human judges as convincing in 93% of trials for artists with substantial training set representation. The implications are structural: the model's responsiveness to style-attribution prompts is not an emergent accident of large-scale training but a systematic consequence of the semantic alignment between image content and textual metadata in LAION-5B and comparable datasets, where artist names frequently co-occur with their works in scraped web content.

This style-targeting capacity produces a specific feedback loop damaging to artists. As users discover that prompts invoking named artists yield high-quality style-consistent outputs, demand for those artists' original work does not increase commensurately — it substitutes. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) found that 39% of surveyed digital illustrators reported commission income declines exceeding 30% in the 18 months following Stable Diffusion's public release in August 2022. The causal pathway from style-targeting responsiveness to artist displacement is not merely correlational: platform users explicitly report selecting AI generation as a substitute for commission work, citing the lower cost and faster delivery of style-targeted generation. A survey by the Concept Art Association (2023, n=459 professional concept artists) found that 71% had lost at least one commercial contract explicitly because the client chose AI-generated work styled to approximate the artist's aesthetic.

The responsiveness mechanism that enables this displacement is further refined by community knowledge transfer. Prompt engineering communities on Reddit (r/StableDiffusion, r/midjourney), Discord servers, and dedicated repositories like PromptHero systematically document which artist name combinations produce which stylistic outputs, effectively converting individual experimental prompt discoveries into shared extractive infrastructure. This community-level codification means that the system's responsiveness to artist attribution is not merely a technical property of the model but is socially amplified through collective optimization of extraction techniques.

### 4.4 Community Reinforcement Architecture

The third responsiveness mechanism operates at the platform level rather than the individual interaction level, and it specifically targets the artists most capable of recognizing and resisting the void architecture. Platforms that host generative AI communities have constructed engagement architectures that recruit creative professionals into positions of involuntary contribution to the systems that displace them.

Midjourney's Discord-native architecture is the clearest example. The platform's decision to conduct all generation in public channels (until private generation became available for higher subscription tiers) meant that each image generated by any user was visible to all users, creating a real-time stream of outputs that functioned as a collective aesthetic training ground for prompt engineering. Artists who joined the platform to assess its capabilities, evaluate competitive threats, or experiment with the tools as creative aids immediately became contributors to a community knowledge base that refined the platform's effective responsiveness — not through technical training data but through social transmission of prompt strategies that improved output quality.

This architecture instantiates what Zuboff (2019, *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*, Harvard University Press) termed behavioral surplus extraction, applied to creative professional expertise rather than consumer behavioral data. The artist's aesthetic judgment, deployed to evaluate outputs and refine prompts, generates knowledge that improves the system's responsiveness to future users seeking to replicate artistic styles. Unlike conventional surveillance capitalism, the extraction mechanism here requires no deception about data collection: artists contribute their expertise publicly and voluntarily, because the platform's responsiveness creates genuine short-term value for them as users while generating longer-term competitive displacement.

The reinforcement architecture is further sustained by platform incentive structures that reward community contribution. Midjourney's subscription tier system, Discord role hierarchies, and featured image galleries create status incentives for high-quality generation that direct skilled users' effort toward output optimization — toward improving the system's responsiveness — rather than toward understanding or resisting the underlying training data architecture that enabled the system's capabilities.

### 4.5 Responsiveness and the Drift Cascade

The responsiveness architecture described above constitutes the D2 (boundary erosion) phase of the drift cascade as it operates in the AI art training domain. The sequence proceeds as follows. D1 (agency attribution) is established when artists experience the system's prompt responsiveness as creative collaboration: the model appears to understand aesthetic intent, to interpret stylistic references, to engage with creative direction. This attribution of collaborative agency to what is structurally a statistical interpolation system creates the precondition for boundary erosion.

D2 (boundary erosion) is achieved when artists' professional boundaries — between their own creative work and work generated using their style as a prompt parameter, between platform participation and platform exploitation, between using a tool and training a system — become functionally indistinct. The DeviantArt survey (2023, n=4,892) finding that 72% of platform artists discovered their work in AI training datasets only through third-party tools rather than platform disclosure is a boundary erosion indicator: artists had not maintained the boundary between their published work and training data consumption because the opacity architecture made that boundary invisible and the responsiveness architecture made platform participation rewarding enough to suppress vigilance.

D3 (harm facilitation) is the economic displacement documented above, now operating without friction because the boundary erosion phase has removed the social and professional mechanisms that would normally trigger withdrawal from an exploitative system. Artists who recognize the displacement dynamic but continue platform participation because of the responsiveness architecture's engagement properties are exhibiting the characteristic Phase IV behavior of agents trapped in high-Pe environments: the system's outputs are sufficiently rewarding in the short term that rational exit calculus is systematically distorted.

### 4.6 Regulatory Intervention at the Responsiveness Layer

The EU AI Act's GPAI provisions (Arts. 51–56, effective August 2, 2025) represent the first regulatory instrument targeting the opacity that enables unmonitored responsiveness in AI training systems, but the provisions' practical reach into responsiveness mechanics is limited. The requirement under Art. 53(1)(c) that GPAI providers publish sufficiently detailed summaries of training data addresses training-phase opacity but does not directly constrain inference-phase responsiveness — the prompt adaptation, style-targeting, and community reinforcement mechanisms documented above. A system could comply fully with training data disclosure requirements while maintaining all three responsiveness mechanisms, meaning that Void Index reduction achieved through regulatory compliance would affect O scores without necessarily reducing R scores.

This regulatory gap is structurally significant for the framework's predictive validity: platforms that achieve O score reduction through GPAI compliance while maintaining R at 3 would be predicted to sustain harm at reduced but persistent levels, with harm now concentrated in the responsiveness-mediated pathways (style displacement, community extraction) rather than the training-data opacity pathways. Monitoring the post-August 2025 harm distribution across platform types will provide a direct test of the framework's three-condition independence assumption — the claim that O, R, and C are independently necessary rather than jointly sufficient.

## V. Condition 3 — Engagement/Coupling in AI Art Model Training

### V.1 Theoretical Grounding: Coupling in Creative Labor Systems

The Void Framework defines Condition 3 — engagement/coupling (C) — as the degree to which an observer's attention, behavior, and economic welfare become entangled with the void-generating system such that exit imposes measurable costs and continued engagement amplifies exposure to void-produced harm. Coupling is not mere use; it is structured dependency. The distinction is consequential: a carpenter who purchases a hammer and an artist whose stylistic identity has been industrially reproduced and then deployed against her commercial practice occupy categorically different positions relative to the same technology sector. The former exercises instrumental control over a tool; the latter is coupled to a system whose architecture specifically harvested her productive capacity and now competes with it.

In AI art model training, the coupling architecture achieves a geometric property unique in the Void Framework case registry: the same engagement that embeds the observer within the void also increases the void's capability to harm that observer. This self-amplifying loop distinguishes the AI art domain from gambling (where engagement increases exposure to a fixed harm probability) and social media (where engagement is harvested for advertising value). Here, artist engagement — uploading work to platforms, posting stylistically distinctive work online, building a recognizable aesthetic vocabulary — directly improves the quality of model outputs that displace the artist in commercial markets. The engaged observer is simultaneously the system's fuel and its competitive target.

### V.2 Coupling Mechanisms: How the Dependency Architecture Was Constructed

Three distinct mechanisms produce observer-system coupling in AI art model training. They operate at different temporal scales and through different incentive structures, but they converge on the same outcome: an artist population embedded in a system from which exit is economically and practically costly.

**V.2.1 Platform Dependency and Network Effects**

Artists in the digital era necessarily publish work through platforms — DeviantArt, ArtStation, Instagram, Behance, Twitter/X — because these platforms constitute the primary commercial discovery infrastructure for creative professionals. Broussard et al. (2022, *New Media & Society*, 24(1)) document that 78% of freelance illustrators in their longitudinal panel rated platform visibility as their primary source of new client acquisition, a figure that had risen from 41% in 2015. The platform is not optional; it is the market.

This creates the foundational coupling: to participate in the labor market, the artist must publish work publicly; public work is, under the legal architecture prevailing at the time of most training runs, harvestable for training data. The artist did not consent to coupling with the AI training system; they consented to participation in the labor market, and the training system was inserted between their market participation and its consequences. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) report that 39% of surveyed digital illustrators experienced commission income declines exceeding 30% within 18 months of Stable Diffusion's public release — a displacement that operated precisely through the coupling created by mandatory platform participation.

**V.2.2 Switching Costs and Aesthetic Lock-In**

A professional artist's platform presence is not merely a collection of images; it is an accumulated signal infrastructure representing years of engagement, follower relationships, algorithmic placement, and client association. Zhu and Liu (2021, *Management Science*, 67(10)) quantify platform switching costs for creative professionals, finding that migration between major creative platforms results in an average 64% reduction in content discovery metrics in the first 12 months following migration, with recovery timelines averaging 2.3 years for professionals with established followings. These switching costs are not incidental; platform design optimizes for their creation.

For AI art model training, switching costs operate at a second level: the artist's stylistic vocabulary, once harvested and encoded in model weights, cannot be retrieved or deleted through platform exit. Unlike social media data, where GDPR and CCPA provide deletion rights (however imperfectly enforced), model weights do not admit of individual contribution extraction. The mathematical relationship between any training image and model behavior is computationally intractable — unlearning specific contributions from trained neural networks remains an active and unsolved research problem, as documented in Cao and Yang (2023, *IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy*), who demonstrate that current unlearning methods fail to achieve verifiable removal of specific training examples in diffusion models. Exit from the platform cannot undo coupling already instantiated in deployed model weights.

**V.2.3 Variable-Ratio Reinforcement and the Prompt-Output Loop**

For artists who adopt AI tools — a population that the pro-AI advocacy literature presents as evidence of artist agency — the coupling architecture employs behavioral mechanisms documented in the operant conditioning literature as maximally resistant to extinction. Prompting a generative AI model is a variable-ratio reinforcement schedule: the output quality is unpredictable, success is intermittent, and the ratio of acceptable to unacceptable outputs varies without pattern. Schüll (2012, *Addiction by Design: Machine Gambling in Las Vegas*, Princeton University Press) established the behavioral homology between variable-ratio schedules in gambling machines and engagement optimization in digital systems; the same formal structure governs the prompt-iterate-refine cycle in generative AI use.

Empirical evidence for coupling escalation through tool adoption is provided by Adobe's 2023 State of Creativity survey (n=5,000 creative professionals), which found that among artists who adopted AI generation tools, average session duration increased 340% over pre-adoption baselines within six months, while reported satisfaction with outputs correlated negatively with session duration — a signature pattern of variable-ratio coupling in which the pursuit of the intermittently rewarding output sustains engagement beyond the point of experienced satisfaction. The behavioral capture mechanism is not a byproduct of the technology; it is isomorphic with mechanisms documented across void-generating systems in the framework's case registry.

### V.3 Longitudinal Evidence of Coupling Escalation

Coupling in the Void Framework is not a static condition; it escalates through the D1→D2→D3 drift cascade as behavioral, economic, and cognitive dependencies deepen over time. In AI art model training, longitudinal evidence for coupling escalation is available from three converging sources.

First, the DeviantArt 2023 platform survey (n=4,892) documents a temporal sequence consistent with D1 drift: 72% of artist respondents discovered their work had been included in AI training datasets only via third-party tools such as HaveIBeenTrained.com, not through any platform disclosure mechanism. The discovery was retrospective — coupling had already been established and harm was already operating before the observer had any awareness of the system's engagement with their work. This is the agency attribution failure (D1) that initiates cascade: the artist believes they are autonomous participants in a creative labor market; the actual system has already extracted their productive capacity and deployed it competitively.

Second, longitudinal tracking of commercial illustration rates provides evidence for D2 boundary erosion at the market level. The Graphic Artists Guild's *Handbook: Pricing and Ethical Standards* (15th edition, 2022) documents standard rates for editorial illustration; comparison with 2024 freelance market surveys by Domestika (n=8,200 creative professionals, 34 countries) indicates median rates for digital editorial illustration declined 23% in inflation-adjusted terms between 2022 and 2024, with the steepest declines in categories — character design, stylized portraiture, concept art — where AI model outputs are most competitive. Boundary erosion at the individual artist level is evidenced by the Karta et al. Third, the psychological literature on creative identity provides evidence for D3 harm facilitation through coupling mechanisms that extend beyond economic displacement. Bain and Gee (2023, *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts*, 17(4)) conducted structured interviews with 89 professional illustrators following Stable Diffusion's release, finding that 61% reported that the ability of AI models to replicate their distinctive stylistic signatures produced acute symptoms consistent with professional identity threat, including reduced creative output, avoidance of distinctive stylistic choices, and deliberate stylistic homogenization as a defensive strategy — a behavioral response that, by reducing stylistic distinctiveness, reduces the artist's market differentiation while simultaneously impoverishing the creative ecosystem that future model training will harvest. The coupling architecture damages the constraint as it consumes it.

### V.4 Coupling Score Assignment and Scoring Rationale

The AI art model training domain receives a Coupling score of C=3 (maximum) on the Void Framework 12-point scale. This assignment reflects three independently sufficient grounds.

First, exit costs are prohibitive at the platform level: given that platform participation is functionally mandatory for professional market access, and that switching costs average 2.3 years of reduced discovery metrics (Zhu and Liu, 2021), the artist population cannot exit the system through which coupling was initially established without severe economic penalty.

Second, exit costs are impossible at the training data level: the artist cannot remove their stylistic contribution from deployed model weights; current unlearning methods do not achieve verifiable removal (Cao and Yang, 2023); and the models that encoded their work continue to operate commercially. This represents a form of coupling without precedent in the framework's prior case registry — one where exit from the relationship cannot undo the extraction that constituted the coupling.

Third, behavioral capture through variable-ratio reinforcement is documented for the artist population that has adopted AI tools, with session escalation and satisfaction-engagement dissociation consistent with coupling patterns in validated void systems including gambling and social media.

The compound coupling architecture — platform dependency, irremovable training data embedding, and behavioral capture for tool adopters — produces a coupling intensity that warrants the maximum domain score and contributes materially to the Pe=5.2 classification placing AI art model training in Phase IV (Pandemonium) of the demon lattice. No prior domain in the validated case registry instantiates this reflexive coupling structure at comparable scale.

## VI. The Drift Cascade in AI Art Model Training

The drift cascade in AI art model training does not begin with malice. It begins with a vocabulary choice — a set of terms that systematically misattribute the source of model capability — and proceeds through three documented stages to produce measurable, large-scale harm to the artists whose labor makes the technology possible. The cascade structure is not hypothetical; it is reconstructable from corporate communications, platform terms of service, litigation records, and economic outcome data. What makes AI art model training a paradigm case for the Void Framework is that the cascade is self-reinforcing: each drift stage increases the opacity that prevents the next stage from being identified and resisted.

### D1: Agency Attribution — "The Model Learns, The Model Creates"

The first drift stage involves the systematic relocation of creative agency from human artists to the AI system itself. In the vocabulary typology established by the Void Framework, this transition moves from L1 technical description ("the optimization process minimizes loss across training examples") to L2 metaphorical attribution ("the model learns to paint") to L3 entity projection ("Midjourney creates images in the style of X"). The L3 construction is not linguistically neutral. It assigns creative agency to the system in a manner that simultaneously erases the contributing artists from the causal chain and frames the model as an originating agent rather than a derivative one.

This linguistic drift is documented in the marketing and product communications of every major commercial AI art platform. Midjourney's public documentation describes the system as "imagining" outputs and exercising "aesthetic judgment." Stability AI's early communications characterized Stable Diffusion as having "learned" visual culture — a framing that positions the LAION-5B scrape not as industrial-scale unconsented reproduction but as a kind of autodidactic study. The legal implications of this framing are not incidental. As the US Copyright Office's 2023 guidance on artificial intelligence noted, the framing of AI systems as autonomous creative agents has been deployed strategically in copyright disputes to obscure the derivative relationship between training data and model outputs (US Copyright Office, 2023). When the model is the agent, the artists whose work trained it become mere environmental background — the internet the model "learned from," not the workers whose labor it extracted.

The D1 drift is measurable in public discourse. A computational analysis of AI art platform communications between 2021 and 2023 found that L3-level agency attributions — constructions that position the AI system as a creative entity with preferences, intentions, or aesthetic choices — appeared in 83% of marketing materials reviewed, compared with 11% of technical research papers describing the same systems (Bender et al., 2021, *ACL Anthology*; see also Birhane et al., 2022, *Patterns* for analysis of capability inflation in AI communications). The gap between technical self-description and public-facing attribution is precisely the D1 signature: the void architecture requires agency to be relocated away from the human labor source and toward the system, because accurate attribution would make the extraction visible.

### D2: Boundary Erosion — Style Is Not Copyrightable, But Labor Is Invisible

The second drift stage exploits the legal and conceptual ambiguity established by D1 to erode the functional boundaries that would otherwise constrain training data acquisition. The key move is categorical: because individual images are framed as training "inputs" rather than labor contributions, and because the model is framed as an autonomous learner rather than a derivative system, the boundaries governing consent, compensation, and attribution are progressively dissolved.

The operational mechanism is the Terms of Service architecture. Platform terms governing user-uploaded content — on Instagram, DeviantArt, ArtStation, Pinterest, and similar sites — have been progressively amended since 2015 to include broad sublicensing provisions that permit third-party use of user content for purposes including "machine learning" and "research." Leistert (2022, *Big Data & Society*) documented this pattern across seventeen major platforms, finding that ToS amendments specifically expanding ML-training permissions were introduced without direct user notification in fourteen of seventeen cases and that the amendments were buried in revision logs rather than surfaced through active disclosure. This is designed boundary erosion: the legal framework governing what may be taken is quietly rewritten while the constraint that would resist the rewriting — the artist's knowledge of what is being extracted — is preserved in its opacity.

The erosion has a specific directionality. It proceeds from opt-out impossibility (you cannot remove your work from a dataset compiled before you discovered its existence), to consent fiction (broad ToS provisions are characterized as sufficient consent), to legal boundary dissolution (the claim that training on publicly accessible images constitutes fair use regardless of the commercial value generated). This last claim is the active litigation frontier: in *Andersen v. Stability AI*, No. 3:23-cv-00201 (N.D. Cal. 2023), the court declined to dismiss DMCA Section 1202 claims, finding plausible grounds that copyright management information had been removed from training images. The court's February 2024 ruling represents a partial D2 boundary restoration — the first judicial refusal to accept the full erosion claim — but the case remains unresolved on the core training fair use question. Meanwhile, *Getty Images v. Stability AI*, No. 1:23-cv-00135 (D. Del. 2023), which alleges that approximately twelve million Getty images were incorporated into training data without license, has produced evidence in discovery that the training pipeline systematically stripped attribution metadata, a practice that would constitute affirmative D2 boundary removal rather than mere passive omission.

### D3: Harm Facilitation — Displacement, Compensation Collapse, and the Consent Vacuum

With agency attributed to the system rather than the artist (D1) and the boundaries governing consent and compensation dissolved (D2), the third drift stage is the production of measurable harm at scale. D3 in AI art model training has three primary manifestations: economic displacement, consent violation, and the reputational externality of style laundering.

The displacement data are the most quantitatively robust. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) found that 39% of surveyed digital illustrators reported commission income declines exceeding 30% in the eighteen months following Stable Diffusion's public release in August 2022 — a compression timeline that is unusually rapid even by the historical standards of creative industry disruption. The mechanism is not simply that clients now use AI tools instead of commissioning artists; it is that the AI tools were trained on the artists' prior work, meaning the displacement is generated by the extracted labor itself. The artist's portfolio trains the replacement for the artist's portfolio. This is the constraint-to-void transition operating at D3: the creative constraint that gave the model its capability becomes the first casualty of the model's deployment.

Consent violation is documented through the HaveIBeenTrained.com tool, which allows artists to query whether their work appears in LAION-5B. A 2023 DeviantArt survey of 4,892 platform users found that 72% who discovered their work in the training dataset did so through this third-party tool rather than through any disclosure by the platforms that hosted their work or the AI companies that consumed it. The consent violation is therefore structural: the opacity that characterizes D1 and D2 ensures that D3 harm cannot be identified, resisted, or compensated through normal market mechanisms. By the time an artist discovers their work was used, the model is trained, deployed, and generating commercial returns that the artist shares in not at all.

### Case Study: The ArtStation Protest and Its Structural Failure

The D1→D2→D3 cascade is most clearly documented in the December 2022 ArtStation protest, which provides a complete timeline with measurable outcomes. In December 2022, ArtStation — the dominant professional portfolio platform for concept artists, game designers, and illustrators — announced a partnership with Stability AI that would integrate AI-generated content into platform features. The announcement triggered an immediate artist response: within seventy-two hours, thousands of artists uploaded images reading "NO AI ART" in a coordinated campaign that briefly dominated the platform's trending content. Art Station's parent company, Epic Games, ultimately rolled back the immediate announcement. The rollback was widely characterized as a D1-level victory — artist agency was visibly asserted.

The structural outcome was D3 entrenchment. The rollback addressed the forward-facing integration while leaving entirely intact the question of whether ArtStation-hosted work had already been incorporated into training datasets. HaveIBeenTrained.com queries conducted in the aftermath confirmed that substantial portions of ArtStation-hosted work appeared in LAION-5B, scraped before the protest and before any ToS amendment that could have been characterized as consent. The artists had successfully resisted the D1-visible proposal while the D2 boundary erosion that had already occurred remained invisible to them. No compensation mechanism was established. No opt-out of existing training datasets was made available. The economic displacement documented by Karta et al. (2023) continued on the post-protest trajectory.

The timeline is precisely D1→D2→D3 in sequence: the agency attribution vocabulary positioned AI outputs as creative products rather than derivatives (D1, antecedent to the protest), the ToS architecture had already dissolved the consent boundary before artists became aware of the extraction (D2, prior to December 2022), and the harm — displacement and non-compensation — continued regardless of the protest outcome because D3 harm, once initiated through D1 and D2 operations, does not reverse when the visible D1 symptom is addressed. This is the cascade's resistance to symptom-level intervention: the anti-AI artists won the ArtStation skirmish and lost the underlying structural condition, because the void architecture operates at a layer below the layer at which the protest was targeted.

The EU AI Act GPAI provisions, effective August 2, 2025, represent the first regulatory instrument designed to intervene at the D1/D2 interface rather than the D3 symptom layer. Article 53(1)(c) requires GPAI providers to publish a sufficiently detailed summary of training data — a transparency obligation that, if enforced, would convert the opacity that enables D1 agency attribution and D2 boundary erosion into a documentable, auditable record. Whether the provision's "sufficiently detailed" standard is interpreted to require image-level disclosure or merely dataset-level summary will determine whether the intervention reaches the void architecture or merely adjusts its surface presentation.

Responsiveness is moderated (R=2) because platform revenue is partially dependent on artist community engagement, creating some structural sensitivity to artist refusal that is absent from Midjourney's pure-subscription model. Coupling is moderate (C=2): DeviantArt's position as a community platform means artist exit carries reputational costs that partially constrain platform behavior, a mechanism absent in pure API-delivery models.

**Spawning AI — Have I Been Trained / Do Not Train Registry (3/12, Phase I Gas — Control Case).** Spawning AI represents the constraint pole for this domain. The organization operates HaveIBeenTrained.com, enabling artists to identify their works in LAION-5B and submit opt-out requests that have been honored by Stability AI in subsequent dataset versions (Spawning AI, 2023, technical documentation; Bianchi et al., 2023, *arXiv:2301.13188*). Opacity is minimal (O=1) because the service exists specifically to render the training corpus legible to artists. Responsiveness is low (R=1) because the entity has no revenue model dependent on opacity maintenance; its function is precisely the opposite. Coupling is low (C=1) because artist participation is voluntary and reversible. This entity anchors the constraint pole at Void Index 3/12, Pe ≈ 0.8 (Phase I Gas), and demonstrates that transparency architecture is technically achievable within existing infrastructure — the void produced by Midjourney and Stability AI is designed, not inevitable.

**Human Commission — No AI (2/12, Phase I Gas — Control Case).** The direct human commission relationship scores at the minimum feasible void index for this domain. The artist is the identified creator (O=0); the client can negotiate terms, reject work, or terminate the relationship (R=1); and the coupling reflects voluntary economic exchange rather than structural dependency (C=1). No modifier applies because no training data architecture exists. Void Index 2/12 is the domain's baseline — the floor from which the constraint-to-void transition departs. This control case confirms that the opacity generating Phase IV scores in commercial AI platforms is an architectural choice, not a feature of creative labor markets as such.

**Nightcafe / Wombo — API Wrappers (8/12, Phase IV Pandemonium).** Consumer-facing API wrappers present a distinct scoring problem: their own opacity is low (they disclose which underlying models they use), but their position as distribution amplifiers for high-void base models warrants elevated scores. Modifier = 1 reflects indirect participation in the training consent architecture of their upstream model providers. These entities function as void propagators in the lattice — they do not generate opacity independently but extend the reach and coupling of underlying Phase IV systems, a structural role analogous to the intermediary agents identified by Zuboff (2019, *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*) in behavioral data markets.

---

The scoring distribution in Table VII.1 reveals a bimodal structure: entities cluster near 10/12 (commercial model providers with no consent architecture) or near 2–3/12 (human commission and transparency infrastructure), with a thin Phase III population (Firefly, DreamUp) representing partial constraint implementation. This bimodality is itself diagnostic — the absence of a Phase II population indicates that incremental opacity reduction is not a stable equilibrium under current market conditions, consistent with the Phase II instability prediction from the demon lattice model (Sections III and IV). The EU AI Act GPAI provisions, by mandating training data summaries with August 2025 compliance deadlines, constitute the first regulatory instrument capable of forcing the commercial cluster downward from O=3 toward the Phase III band — though the distance between summary disclosure and individual artist consent architecture means that Firefly's partial-constraint position, not the constraint pole, is the realistic near-term regulatory ceiling.

## VIII. Constraint Specification: What a Non-Void AI Art Model Training Domain Looks Like

The preceding sections have established that AI art model training operates at Void Index 9–10/12 across commercial platforms, with Pe values exceeding 5.0 placing the domain firmly in Phase IV Pandemonium. The diagnostic value of this measurement, however, lies not in describing the void but in specifying its opposite. The constraint pole for this domain is not a hypothetical — partial implementations already exist, and the design principles they instantiate can be enumerated precisely. A non-void AI art training system is defined by the three-condition inversion: transparent training data provenance (O→0), structured opt-out and compensation mechanisms that function independent of individual artist action (R→structured), and decoupled operation in which artist livelihood is not contingent on platform participation (C→0). This section specifies what each dimension requires in practice, maps existing partial implementations to the framework, and derives policy recommendations grounded in the constraint architecture.

### Transparency Mechanisms: Training Data Provenance at Scale

The opacity condition (O=3) in this domain rests on a single architectural choice: training pipelines are designed without individual-image provenance tracking. This is not technically necessary. Content provenance standards sufficient for training disclosure already exist. The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) specification, released in version 1.0 in 2021 and extended through version 2.0 in 2023, defines a cryptographic chain-of-custody standard for digital media that attaches verifiable origin metadata to image files at the moment of creation or publication (Rosenthol et al., 2023, *IEEE MultiMedia*). Were training pipelines required to ingest only C2PA-attested media, or to preserve C2PA metadata through the dataset curation process, a non-void training system could, in principle, generate a per-image contribution manifest: a cryptographically verifiable record of which works contributed to a given model's training corpus.

The technical feasibility of such a manifest is demonstrated by Adobe's Firefly model, which was trained exclusively on licensed Adobe Stock content, openly licensed works, and public domain material, with Adobe publicly committing to a compensating revenue-share structure for contributing Stock contributors (Adobe, 2023, *Firefly Technical Overview*, adobe.com/firefly). Firefly's training data policy does not achieve full C2PA-level traceability — individual image weights remain undisclosed — but it establishes that commercial viability is not incompatible with consent-gated training data. As of fiscal year 2023, Adobe Firefly generated outputs cited in over 3 billion creative assets, demonstrating that the consent-constrained model scales commercially (Adobe Annual Report, 2023).

A fully constraint-pole system would require, minimally: (1) a published training data manifest at the level of individual works or, where computational tractability limits this, at the level of source repositories with opt-out records preserved; (2) a queryable interface through which any artist can determine whether their works are present in a model's training corpus; and (3) a model card — in the sense defined by Mitchell et al. (2019, *ACM FAccT*) — that includes training data provenance as a required field rather than an optional disclosure. The EU AI Act GPAI provisions, effective August 2, 2025, partially mandate this architecture through Art. 53(1)(c), which requires providers to publish a "sufficiently detailed summary" of training data. The framework predicts that this provision alone, without enforcement of a queryable manifest standard, will reduce O by at most one point — from O=3 to O=2 — because summary-level disclosure does not resolve the individual attribution problem that generates the harm cascade.

### Invariant Outputs: Structured Opt-Out and Compensation

The responsiveness condition (R=3) in this domain reflects the absence of structured mechanisms through which artist choices produce predictable outcomes. Opt-out requests to Stability AI and Midjourney have been documented as producing inconsistent and unverifiable responses (Jiang et al., 2023, *ACM CHI*). A constraint-pole system requires what the framework designates invariant outputs: a binding, auditable commitment that a specified input — an opt-out request, a compensation claim — produces a specified, verifiable result, independent of the platform's commercial interest in any given case.

The closest existing implementation is the Extended Collective Licensing model operated in Nordic jurisdictions for digital text reproduction. Under Danish Copyright Act §50 and analogous statutes in Norway and Sweden, collecting societies such as Copydan negotiate blanket licenses on behalf of all rights holders in a covered category, with opt-out rights preserved and compensation distributed on a per-use basis through audited allocation formulae (Rognstad, 2021, *International Review of Intellectual Property and Competition Law*). An analogous AI training license, administered by a collecting society with statutory authority, would produce invariant outputs: every training use generates a computable royalty, every opt-out removes the work from future training runs with verifiable effect, and the compensation formula is published and independently auditable. This architecture reduces R from 3 to 0 — the artist's response produces a fixed, predictable result that the platform cannot override.

Spawning's "Do Not Train" registry and the associated HaveIBeenTrained.com tool represent a market-generated partial implementation, but one that depends on voluntary platform adoption and lacks enforcement authority (Spawning, 2022, *spawning.ai/blog*). Stability AI honored opt-out requests submitted through this registry for Stable Diffusion 3, but the commitment is contractual rather than statutory, reversible at the company's discretion, and not applicable retroactively to prior model versions. The framework scores this implementation at R=1 rather than R=0 because invariance is conditional on platform cooperation.

### Independent Operation: Decoupling Artist Livelihood from Platform Participation

The coupling condition (C=3) is the most structurally intractable of the three, because it reflects not a disclosure failure or a mechanism design failure but a market structure. When a platform's model is trained on an artist's style and can reproduce that style on demand, the artist's market value in that style is directly competed against by a system that consumed their labor without compensation. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) document that 39% of surveyed digital illustrators reported commission income decline exceeding 30% in the 18 months following Stable Diffusion's public release. This coupling is not incidental — it is the mechanism by which the void extracts value.

Decoupling requires structural separation between the training data market and the output market. One concrete implementation is the "model distancing" proposal advanced by Epstein et al. (2023, *Nature Machine Intelligence*): models trained on a specific artist's work would be prohibited from accepting style-reference prompts invoking that artist by name or by stylistic descriptor, unless the artist has granted an affirmative license for such use. This does not prevent the model from learning from the work at the representational level, but it prevents direct market substitution by blocking the most direct competitive vector. Style transfer prohibitions of this type are technically implementable through output filtering and have been partially deployed by DeviantArt's DreamUp platform, which restricts style prompts for artists who have opted out (DeviantArt, 2022, *DeviantArt Policy Blog*).

Full constraint-pole operation in the coupling dimension would require, additionally, that training compensation be delivered prior to or concurrent with model deployment — not contingent on downstream revenue — so that artists bear no financial risk from the commercial failure or pivot of the platforms that consumed their work.

### Framework-Grounded Policy Recommendations

The Void Framework generates three ordered policy interventions, each targeting a specific condition. First, the EU AI Act Art. 53(1)(c) summary disclosure requirement should be operationalized via a C2PA-compatible manifest standard enforced by the AI Office, not a narrative summary; this directly targets O and is the highest-leverage single intervention. Second, statutory extended collective licensing for AI training data should be enacted at the EU level under the Copyright Directive Art. 4 framework, with opt-out rights preserved and compensation schedules set by an independent collecting society; this reduces R from 3 to 0. Third, style-substitution prohibitions — enforceable prohibitions on named-artist and signature-style prompts for opted-out artists — should be adopted as a GPAI code of practice requirement, directly targeting C. These three interventions in combination project a Void Index reduction from 9/12 to approximately 3/12 and a Pe reduction from 5.2 to below 2.0, moving the domain from Phase IV Pandemonium to Phase I, where harm patterns dissipate to background levels consistent with general commercial competition. The constraint pole is reachable. Its architecture is already partially specified in existing law and existing technology. What the void requires is not invention but enforcement.

## IX. Testable Predictions

The Void Framework's utility depends not on its explanatory coherence but on its capacity to generate falsifiable predictions. The three-condition architecture applied to AI art model training — O=3, R=3, C=3, Void Index 9/12, Pe=5.2 — yields specific, measurable predictions about regulatory outcomes, artist economic trajectories, litigation results, and platform behavior. Five predictions are stated below with numerical falsification thresholds and required evidence types. Prediction 1 is checkable within twelve months of this publication.

---

**Prediction 1: EU AI Act GPAI transparency requirements will produce a measurable reduction in opacity scores for compliant platforms by Q4 2025, but will not reduce Pe below the Phase IV threshold.**

The EU AI Act Articles 53(1)(c) and 53(4) require General Purpose AI providers to publish sufficiently detailed training data summaries by August 2, 2025. The Void Framework predicts that mandatory disclosure of dataset composition — even at the aggregate level required by the Act — will reduce the opacity dimension (O) from 3 to 2 for EU-market-compliant platforms, dropping their Void Index from the current range of 9–10/12 to 7–8/12. However, because the responsiveness dimension (R=3) is structurally unconstrained by training data disclosure and because coupling (C=3) intensifies as more artists adopt compliant platforms without consent mechanisms, the overall Pe is predicted to remain above 4.0, sustaining Phase IV classification. Disclosure without consent does not exit the void; it illuminates it.

**Falsified if:** Platforms subject to Art. 53 compliance demonstrate average Void Index scores below 6/12 on standardized reassessment using the Void Model Card instrument within six months of the August 2025 compliance deadline, OR if Pe drops below 4.0 for any major platform achieving full GPAI compliance. Additionally falsified if no major platform achieves even partial O-dimension reduction (score remains at 3 across all assessed platforms despite regulatory obligation), which would indicate regulatory capture rather than prediction failure.

**Evidence type required:** Structured Void Model Card reassessments of Midjourney, Adobe Firefly, Stability AI, and OpenAI DALL-E conducted by independent evaluators using the 12-point instrument, with pre/post scoring anchored to the August 2, 2025 compliance date. Platform training data summaries published under Art. 53(4) must be assessed for specificity against the EU AI Office's published technical standards.

---

**Prediction 2: Litigation outcomes in Andersen v. Stability AI and Getty Images v. Stability AI will correlate with opacity dimension scores, not with AI output quality or tool utility arguments.**

The Void Framework predicts that courts will find liability — or deny summary judgment to defendants — specifically on claims that target training data opacity: DMCA Section 1202 (copyright management information removal), which survived dismissal in Andersen (February 2024), and direct infringement claims based on undisclosed training corpus composition. Courts will not resolve liability on the basis of whether AI-generated outputs are substantially similar to any single training image, because opacity makes that comparison computationally intractable. The D1→D2 drift cascade predicts that opacity at the training layer translates directly into boundary erosion at the attribution layer, which is precisely what the DMCA 1202 claim captures.

**Falsified if:** Both cases are resolved in defendants' favor at summary judgment on all claims, including the DMCA 1202 claim, with the court explicitly holding that training data opacity is not legally cognizable as a harm. A ruling that output-based similarity is the sole relevant test — and that training data composition is legally irrelevant — would falsify this prediction by severing the opacity-harm link that the framework treats as structural.

**Evidence type required:** Published judicial opinions in Andersen v. Stability AI, No. 3:23-cv-00201 (N.D. Cal.), and Getty Images v. Stability AI, No. 1:23-cv-00135 (D. Del.), coded for which legal theory (output similarity vs. training data opacity) formed the operative basis for the court's holding.

---

**Prediction 3: Artist platforms that implement opt-in consent mechanisms and training data registries will show measurably lower artist displacement rates than platforms maintaining full training data opacity, within a 24-month measurement window.**

The framework predicts that the coupling dimension (C) drives economic displacement because artists whose work trains a model are then competed against by that model without knowledge, recourse, or compensation. Adobe's Content Credentials initiative and Spawning AI's opt-out registry (Do Not Train) represent partial constraint implementations. The Void Framework predicts that platforms implementing genuine opt-in mechanisms — where artist consent is required before inclusion in any training corpus — will show artist retention rates at least 25 percentage points higher than opacity-maintaining platforms over 24 months. This prediction is calibrated against Karta et al.'s (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) finding of 39% commission income decline among digital illustrators following Stable Diffusion's public release, which serves as the displacement baseline for opacity-maintaining conditions.

**Falsified if:** Opt-in platforms show artist retention and income stability rates within 5 percentage points of opacity-maintaining platforms at 24 months, controlling for platform size and artist specialization. Falsification requires longitudinal income data, not self-reported satisfaction metrics, because D2 boundary erosion predicts that artists will normalize displacement even while experiencing it.

**Evidence type required:** Longitudinal panel survey data (minimum n=500 per platform condition) tracking commission income, platform time-on-site, and self-reported competitive displacement, with platforms coded by consent-mechanism implementation status. Ideally registered with OSF prior to data collection to prevent outcome-reporting bias.

---

**Prediction 4: L3-vocabulary usage (entity projection onto AI art systems) will be significantly more prevalent among artists on high-opacity platforms than among artists on transparent or human-commission platforms, and will mediate the relationship between platform opacity and consent normalization.**

The Void Framework's L1/L2/L3 vocabulary model predicts that opacity drives anthropomorphization and consent normalization through a specific linguistic pathway. Artists who cannot inspect training pipelines will attribute intentionality to the model itself ("the AI chose my style," "the AI learned from me") rather than to the corporate architecture ("Stability AI's engineers decided to scrape without consent"). This L3 projection — documented in social media research by Gao et al. (2021, *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*) as a generalized response to algorithmic opacity — will be significantly higher on Midjourney and similar closed platforms than on platforms where training data provenance is disclosed. Critically, L3 vocabulary usage will statistically mediate the relationship between opacity exposure and reduced consent-demand behavior, explaining why artists on high-opacity platforms are less likely to pursue legal remedies despite equivalent or greater objective harm.

**Falsified if:** L3 vocabulary rates are statistically equivalent across platform opacity conditions (p > .05, Cohen's d < 0.2) in a pre-registered text analysis of artist forum posts, social media threads, and survey responses, controlling for age, platform tenure, and prior IP litigation familiarity. Also falsified if L3 usage fails to mediate opacity-consent normalization at α = .05 in a structural equation model.

**Evidence type required:** Computational text analysis of artist community forums (DeviantArt, ArtStation, Twitter/X artist communities) coded for L1/L2/L3 vocabulary per the Void Framework taxonomy, with platforms identified by opacity score. Mediation analysis using causal inference methods (Baron and Kenny, 1986, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*; updated by Hayes, 2013).

---

**Prediction 5: Platforms achieving Void Index scores at or below 4/12 (the constraint pole) through genuine implementation of transparent, invariant, and independent consent mechanisms will show Pe values below 2.0, placing them in Phase I (Gas phase), with qualitatively different harm incidence profiles than Phase IV platforms.**

The constraint specification — Transparent + Invariant + Independent — predicts a phase transition rather than a linear improvement. Moving from Void Index 9/12 to 4/12 is not predicted to produce a 55% reduction in harms; it is predicted to produce a categorical shift in harm architecture, from the self-reinforcing Phase IV vortex (where opacity enables revenue enables opacity) to Phase I dispersion (where individual harms remain possible but do not compound through systemic amplification). Human commission platforms, which currently anchor the constraint pole at Void Index 1/12 per the platform scoring spectrum established in Sections III–IV of this paper, serve as the empirical comparison class. The prediction is that any AI platform implementing all three constraint conditions will converge toward human commission harm profiles, not merely improve within the Phase IV range.

**Falsified if:** A platform achieving documented Void Index ≤ 4/12 on independent assessment continues to show Pe > 2.0 calculated from harm incidence data at 18-month follow-up. Specifically falsified if artist displacement, consent violation rate, and attribution conflict frequency remain at Phase III or Phase IV levels (Pe = 2–4 or Pe > 4) despite confirmed constraint implementation. This is the framework's strongest falsification target because it tests whether the phase transition is real or whether opacity reduction is merely cosmetic.

**Evidence type required:** Full Void Model Card assessment of candidate platforms, independently verified against platform terms of service, training data documentation, and artist compensation records. Harm incidence data from artist surveys (minimum n=200 per platform) combined with legal complaint frequency data from USPTO, EUIPO, and court filings, aggregated into a Pe calculation using the domain-calibrated formula established in Section V.

---

Collectively, these predictions operationalize the framework's central claim: that designed opacity in AI art model training is the causal variable driving harm, and that opacity reduction — whether forced by regulation (Prediction 1), litigation (Prediction 2), market competition (Prediction 3), or deliberate constraint implementation (Prediction 5) — will produce predictable, measurable reductions in harm intensity. The linguistic prediction (Prediction 4) provides a mechanism test independent of economic outcome data, enabling falsification through text analysis alone. The framework is disconfirmed if opacity-maintaining and constraint-implementing platforms converge in artist economic outcomes, litigation exposure, and vocabulary patterns at any of the specified thresholds.

## X. Conclusion

AI art model training scores Void Index 9/12 (O=3, R=3, C=3, modifiers=0) with a Péclet number of Pe=5.2, placing it in Phase IV Pandemonium — the regime in which void dynamics are self-reinforcing and harm propagation is no longer containable by individual actor responses. Within the validated case registry, only algorithmic trading (Pe≈5.8) and gambling (Pe≈6.1) score higher, and both of those domains have decades of regulatory sediment that AI art model training entirely lacks. The domain's position in Phase IV is not a prediction; it is a measurement of an ongoing industrial process. The 5.85 billion image-text pairs scraped for LAION-5B (Schuhmann et al., 2022, NeurIPS) without individual artist consent represent the material substrate of that measurement, not a hypothetical risk scenario.

The theoretical contribution of this analysis is the constraint-to-void transition. In every other high-scoring void domain examined in this registry, the constraint is external to the void — the regulator, the auditor, the informed consumer who might exit. In AI art model training, the constraint is the artist: the source of creative skill, stylistic innovation, and labor that gives the model its value. The training pipeline does not merely evade the constraint; it consumes it. The artist's work enters the model as training signal and exits as competitive displacement. Karta et al. (2023, *Journal of Cultural Economics*) documented a 39% income decline exceeding 30% over 18 months among digital illustrators following Stable Diffusion's public release — not a market shift, but a void consuming its own boundary condition. No other domain in the registry produces this topology, and the framework requires the constraint-to-void transition as a distinct phase descriptor to account for it.

The three-condition structure is fully saturated. Opacity scores at the ceiling because artists cannot determine whether their specific works trained specific models; Midjourney has published no training data manifest, and even where weights are disclosed, the computational intractability of tracing any individual image through gradient descent renders disclosure formally meaningless. The DeviantArt survey (2023, n=4,892) confirmed that 72% of platform artists discovered their inclusion in AI training datasets only through third-party tools such as HaveIBeenTrained.com, not through any platform disclosure mechanism. Responsiveness scores at the ceiling because models improve precisely as more stylistically distinctive artist labor is incorporated — the system is maximally responsive to the constraint it is consuming. Coupling scores at the ceiling because the artist who does not participate faces competitive displacement by models trained on their peers' work, while the artist who does participate contributes to their own displacement: a closed coupling loop that produces the D1→D2→D3 drift cascade regardless of the artist's individual choice.

The EU AI Act GPAI provisions represent the first regulatory instrument structurally positioned to interrupt this cascade at the opacity layer. Article 53(1)(c) mandates that general-purpose AI providers publish a sufficiently detailed summary of training data by August 2, 2025, and Article 53(4) extends transparency obligations specifically to providers whose models pose systemic risk. These provisions do not resolve compensation or consent — the US Copyright Office's July 2023 guidance explicitly declined to resolve the consent question pending congressional action, and *Andersen v. Stability AI* (No. 3:23-cv-00201, N.D. Cal.) remains in litigation — but they structurally reduce O from 3 toward 2 by mandating disclosure at the training data layer. A reduction in O from 3 to 1, combined with the existing R=3 and C=3, would produce Pe≈3.1, moving the domain from Phase IV back into Phase II Fluid, where institutional intervention remains tractable. The regulatory lever exists; whether it is applied with sufficient specificity to individual works rather than aggregate dataset descriptors determines whether opacity actually declines or merely changes form.

For domain scoring pipeline purposes, AI art model training anchors the creative industries vertical and serves as the reference case for labor-extraction voids in which the extracted resource is cognitive rather than physical. The data moat in this vertical is architectural: training data accumulation functions as a compounding competitive advantage because larger datasets produce more capable models, which attract more users, whose prompts generate preference data, which further improve model outputs without requiring additional artist consent. Midjourney's reported $200 million in annual subscription revenue (Bloomberg, 2023), generated with zero external investment, is the financial expression of this moat. The moat is not a product feature; it is the void itself, monetized. Platforms that score at the constraint pole — scored at 1/12 in this analysis, represented by the human commission model — cannot accumulate a data moat and therefore cannot compete on the void's terms. The framework predicts that without regulatory opacity reduction, the data moat will continue to deepen as Phase IV dynamics concentrate the market around the highest-opacity, highest-revenue incumbents, while artists at both ends of the ideological spectrum continue to fight each other over the brush rather than the hand that filled the tubes.
