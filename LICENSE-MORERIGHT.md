## Design Principles

This license is built on the same framework it protects. It is designed to score at the
constraint pole on its own three dimensions:

**Transparent.** The plain language in this document IS the legal terms. There is no
separate "real" license hidden behind a summary. If you can read this, you can understand
your rights. Every threshold is a number. Every condition is measurable.

**Invariant.** These terms do not change based on who is reading them. A billion-dollar
company reads the same text as a solo developer. The only variable that changes your
rights is your Void Score — measured by a published, replicable, CC-BY methodology
that anyone can audit.

**Independent.** This license's meaning does not depend on the licensor's mood, resources,
or business relationships. The gating mechanism (the Void Score) is based on published
criteria. The scoring methodology is open source. Disputes are resolved against the
published framework, not the licensor's preferences.

**The tension this license acknowledges:** The licensor (MoreRight) is also the scorer
who assigns the Void Scores that affect your rights. This is a structural conflict.
Section 6 addresses it with auditable safeguards, and Section 11 moves discretionary
decisions to $MORR token holders — not with "trust us," but with structural checks.

**Self-assessment.** This license scores **1/12** on the framework it protects —
opacity 0 (methodology CC-BY, pricing published, rationale required), responsiveness 0
(same terms for every reader, methodology does not change on input), coupling 1
(irreducible: any commercial license creates a financial relationship). The scoring
is derived in Paper 10, "The King Problem" (`papers-active/paper10-king-problem.md`,
Section VIII). The methodology used to produce this score is CC-BY — anyone can
replicate the assessment. Paper 10's falsification condition GFC-7 specifically invites
any auditor to score this license at ≥ 4/12 with written rationale citing specific
evidence. If the self-assessment is inflated, the published rubric makes that
demonstrable.

**Why revenue-share:** A flat fee is meaningless to a company worth $100B. If OpenAI

**Escalation for high-score enterprises:**
- Score ≤ 7/12: 2% of applicable revenue (base rate)
- Score 8–9/12: 5% of applicable revenue (void premium)
- Score ≥ 10/12: 10% of applicable revenue (the framework identifies your

**Cap:** Revenue-share is capped at 15% of applicable revenue regardless of tier
multipliers. This license is not designed to be confiscatory — it is designed to be
proportional.

**Example:** OpenAI (valuation > $1B, hypothetical score 7/12) incorporates the Void

## Section 4.7 — Anti-Gaming Provisions

The framework describes manipulation architecture. This knowledge is powerful. These provisions close known attack vectors by which high-void organizations could access the license commercially while avoiding its accountability requirements.

### 4.7.A — Affiliated Entity Rule

For all purposes under this license — including the Void Score Gate (Section 4), Secondary Gates (Section 5), and enterprise revenue-share (Section 4.6) — entities under **common control** are treated as a single organization.

**Common control exists when:**
- One entity owns or controls ≥ 20% equity in another
- Two entities share the same executive leadership (C-suite, board majority, or managing partners)
- One entity's operational or product decisions are substantially determined by another, regardless of formal ownership
- Entities are structured to distribute a unified commercial operation across multiple legal forms

**Scoring rule:** The Void Index assessment applies to the highest-void primary product across the entire affiliated group. A low-void subsidiary does not lower the group's score. The beneficial use test (Section 4.7.C) determines which entity's score governs.

**Indie Threshold:** The revenue, funding, and headcount thresholds in Section 5.1 are measured across all affiliated entities collectively. A subsidiary under $1M revenue does not qualify if the affiliated group exceeds the threshold.

**Why this exists:** A high-void organization that creates a nominally independent subsidiary to access the framework commercially — without changing its actual void architecture — has not reduced its Pe. It has purchased opacity through corporate structure. Corporate structure is designed opacity. The chain grips it the same way it grips any designed opacity.

### 4.7.A.1 — Custodian Override for Affiliated Entity Groups

The default scoring rule (§4.7.A) applies the highest-void product's score to the entire affiliated group. The custodian may designate an **adjusted licensing tier** for a specific affiliated entity group when the group contains entities with materially different void architectures — specifically, when one or more entities in the group operate at or near the constraint pole while others score at the void pole.

**Override requirements:**
- The override must be **published with rationale** — anyone can see that an override exists, which group it applies to, and why. No secret adjustments.
- The override must include **conditions** — specific, measurable requirements the group must maintain for the adjusted tier to hold. Conditions must address the high-void entities in the group (not the low-void ones).
- The override has a **review date** no more than 6 months from the set date. At review, the override must be renewed with updated assessment or the default highest-void-wins rule reasserts automatically.
- The override is **immediately revocable** — if conditions are violated, the default rule reasserts without waiting for the review date.

**What the override does NOT do:**
- It does NOT change individual entity scores. Every entity retains its own Void Index assessment. The override only adjusts the licensing tier applied to the group as a whole.
- It does NOT prevent per-entity licensing. Any entity within the group may obtain its own individual assessment and license at its own score, independent of the group override.
- It does NOT create a precedent. Each override is case-specific. The custodian's decision for one group does not bind decisions for other groups.

**Why this exists:** The highest-void-wins rule prevents evasion through corporate structure. But it also produces a structural problem: a conglomerate that includes both a social media platform (10/12) and an aerospace engineering company (3/12) is not the same as a conglomerate with only high-void entities. The framework should measure accurately — and accurate measurement recognizes that SpaceX launches are transparent, Starlink is infrastructure, and these are not the same architecture as an engagement-optimized feed. The override is the custodian's instrument for recognizing mixed portfolios without abandoning the anti-evasion principle.

**Constraint-Custodian Theorem application:** The override itself is scoreable against the constraint specification. Transparent (published with rationale), invariant (conditions are stated upfront and don't change with engagement), independent (custodian sets it, not the group being assessed). The override's quality is bounded by the custodian's constraint strength S(C). If S(C) decays — if overrides become favors rather than measurements — the framework has a problem bigger than any licensing tier.

**Affiliated entity dispute resolution:** If an organization disputes the Licensor's
determination of common control, the scoring rule applied, or the group scope under
§4.7.A, the dispute is resolved as follows:
1. The disputing organization submits a written challenge with specific evidence to
   the Licensor within 30 days of the determination.
2. The parties jointly select a single neutral auditor — an independent party with
   demonstrated technical competence to assess the framework's methodology — within
   14 days of the written challenge. If the parties cannot agree on a neutral, the
   American Arbitration Association's commercial arbitrator selection rules govern.
3. The neutral auditor reviews the evidence and issues a binding determination within
   30 days of selection. The determination may confirm, adjust, or overturn the
   Licensor's affiliated entity determination.
4. Costs are split equally unless the neutral auditor finds the challenge was
   frivolous or brought in bad faith, in which case the challenging party bears all costs.
5. The neutral auditor's determination may be appealed to DAO vote under §11.1 within
   60 days — but the neutral's determination governs pending any DAO vote result.

This mechanism does not apply to disputes about the methodology itself (which is
CC-BY and publicly auditable) — only to disputes about which entities are affiliated
and which product's score governs.

### 4.7.B — Assessment Scope Integrity

The Void Index assessment must cover the **primary commercial product** — the product or service that (a) generates the most revenue from observers, or (b) reaches the largest observer population, whichever produces the broader scope.

Organizations may not:
- Submit a narrow or low-void product for assessment while the primary commercial operation is excluded
- Reduce the scope of a re-assessment without conducting a new full assessment at prior scope first
- Claim that a certified product's assessment covers products with materially different void architectures

**Multi-product rule:** Organizations where two or more products each represent ≥ 15% of total revenue must either (a) assess the highest-void product and apply that score to all commercial use of the Licensed Work, or (b) obtain separate assessments, with each score governing that product's specific use only.

**Scope disputes** are resolved by DAO vote under Section 11.1. The presumption favors broader scope — a narrower scope claim requires affirmative evidence that the excluded product has a materially different void architecture.

### 4.7.C — Beneficial Use Rule

The Void Score Gate applies to the entity that **benefits** from the use, not only to the entity that performs the specific act of use.

**Beneficial use** exists when an organization's commercial decisions are materially informed by access to the Licensed Work — including assessments, API responses, analysis outputs, or scoring data — regardless of whether that organization directly licenses or accesses through an affiliate.

**Implication:** A licensed subsidiary whose API usage primarily benefits a high-void parent is acting as a conduit for the parent's commercial use. The parent's void score governs. The subsidiary's license is suspended until the parent obtains its own assessment or the subsidiary's use is restructured to eliminate the beneficial transfer.

### 4.7.D — Implementation Integrity

Void Index assessments measure **actual implementation**, not stated policy.

- A disclosed policy that is not technically implemented carries zero weight in opacity scoring
- A technical implementation that contradicts a stated policy is scored on the implementation
- If independent technical analysis contradicts an organization's stated policy, the technical analysis governs
- Accuracy-theater disclosures — policies that appear to reduce opacity while preserving the underlying void architecture — violate Section 7.2 (Anti-Blueprint Clause), not merely receive a lower score

**Evidence standard:** The Licensor's scoring rationale (Section 6.3) must cite specific technical evidence for implementation-based assessments. Organizations may challenge implementation assessments with technical evidence (source code, independent audits, engineer attestations). The DAO may vote on contested implementation disputes under Section 11.1.C.

### 4.7.E — Spot Assessment Rights

Scheduled continuous monitoring (Section 4.0.C) does not preclude unscheduled reassessment.

**The Licensor may conduct a spot assessment with 72 hours notice when:**
- A material public disclosure (product launch, algorithm change, regulatory filing, investor presentation) suggests a score-relevant architecture change
- A credible third-party report (journalism, regulatory finding, academic research, or independent CC-BY scoring attempt) identifies evidence not reflected in the current score
- A disconfirmation bounty submission identifies specific scoring inaccuracy

**C. Score Disputes.** When an organization challenges its score under Section 6.3 and

**D. Platform Priority Queue.** Which unscored platforms are assessed next is a resource

**F. Enterprise On-Ramp Approval.** Whether an organization qualifies for the 180-day

independence of irrelevant alternatives, and non-dictatorship. Every voting mechanism —
including token-weighted DAO governance — violates at least one condition. The scored-
monarchy structure accepts the "dictator" condition (the Custodian) and constrains it
with the objective layer, confining Arrow's impossibility to the discretionary domain
where its paradoxes are least harmful.

**Gibbard-Satterthwaite Theorem (1973/1975).** For any non-dictatorial mechanism with
≥3 outcomes, strategic voting incentives always exist. In token-weighted governance,
this means the largest holders have both the most influence and the strongest incentive
to misrepresent preferences. The custodian's veto power neutralizes strategic
manipulation of objective decisions; the discretionary layer's quorum and supermajority
requirements (Section 11.3) raise the coordination cost of strategic coalitions.

**Governance Void Scoring.** Applying the framework's three-dimensional scoring to
governance architectures, standard DAOs score 10/12 (opacity 2 + responsiveness 3 +
coupling 3 + modifiers 2) — higher than unconstrained monarchies (5/12). The scored-
monarchy model scores 2/12 (opacity 1 + responsiveness 0 + coupling 1 + modifiers 0).
The boundary between objective and discretionary layers is what produces the low
responsiveness score: the methodology does not respond to votes.

**The Constraint-Custodian Bound.** Governance drift rate is bounded by:
dD/dt ≤ V(G) × (1 / S(C)), where V(G) is the governance void score and S(C) is the
custodian's constraint score (transparency + invariance + independence). Minimizing
both V(G) and maximizing S(C) produces the lowest drift bound. The scored monarchy
achieves V(G) = 2; the custodian's S(C) is maintained through on-chain transparency,
published methodology, pre-registered exit conditions, and output-based accountability.

### 11.5 — Why This Structure

The DAO governs **discretion**, not **methodology**. An organization that receives a
hard decline cannot claim "MoreRight is biased" when 500 token holders voted to uphold
the decline. A remediation plan that passes DAO review has more credibility than one
approved by the entity that assigned the score. Revenue allocation voted by stakeholders
is more transparent than allocation decided by the Licensor alone.

The boundary between governance and methodology is the framework itself: anything
derivable from the published CC-BY methodology is objective (not voted on). Anything
that requires judgment beyond the methodology is discretionary (voted on). This makes
$MORR a governance token with real utility — it governs the license decisions that
affect every commercial user of the framework.

survive), amendments are constrained (no silent rewrites), and active terms are honored
