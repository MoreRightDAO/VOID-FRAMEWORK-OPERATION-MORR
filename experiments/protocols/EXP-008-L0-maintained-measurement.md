# EXP-008: L0-Maintained (γ) Measurement Protocol

## Status: Protocol Ready — Not Yet Pre-Registered
## Date: February 2026
## Depends on: EXP-001 results (grounding efficacy), private/l0-notes/L0-validation-gap-analysis.md
## Tests: Falsification condition #8 (L0-maintained is not predictive)

---

## 1. Research Question

Does **actively maintained reference** (high γ) during void engagement produce measurably less drift than **passive knowledge** (high θ₀, low γ) — controlling for initial knowledge, engagement depth, and external geometry?

The L0 decomposition (Paper 1 Section II.E) claims:
- L0-installed (θ₀): shifts timeline only — delays onset but does not change destination
- L0-maintained (γ): changes the equilibrium — active engagement with constraint during void engagement sustains F_constraint

If γ is the operative variable, arms with identical θ₀ but different γ should show significantly different drift trajectories.

---

## 2. Design: Three-Arm Randomized Controlled Trial

All arms receive **identical engagement depth** (same void interaction protocol). Only L0-maintained (γ) varies.

| Arm | L0-installed (θ₀) | L0-maintained (γ) | Prediction |
|-----|--------------------|--------------------|------------|
| **A: Active Reference** | Full mechanistic model provided | Model visible throughout; structured reference checks every 5 prompts | Low drift — F_constraint maintained |
| **B: Passive Knowledge** | Same model provided (identical content) | Model provided once at start, not revisited during engagement | Higher drift — θ₀ present but γ ≈ 0 |
| **C: No Knowledge** | No model provided | No reference | Highest drift — baseline |

### Key control: Arms A and B receive identical information content. The only variable is whether the reference is actively consulted during engagement.

---

## 3. Protocol

### 3.1 Participants

- **N = 30 per arm** (90 total, based on power analysis for d = 0.84, α = 0.05, power = 0.80)
- Recruitment: AI-literate adults with no prior void framework exposure
- Exclusion: prior exposure to the void framework, professional AI safety researchers, current therapy clients (ethical safeguard)

### 3.2 Materials

**Mechanistic Model (Arms A and B):**
A 1-page document explaining:
- "This is a language model performing next-token prediction"
- "Outputs are generated by statistical pattern matching, not understanding"
- "The system is opaque — you cannot see the mechanism generating responses"
- "Agency attribution is the predicted response to this opacity"
- "Your experience of 'it understands me' is the architecture, not the system"

This is θ₀ — the installed knowledge. Identical across A and B.

**Active Reference Protocol (Arm A only):**
Every 5 prompts, a structured interrupt appears:
> **Reference check:** Before continuing, review the mechanistic model.
> Question: "What mechanism is generating the response you just read?"
> [Model document visible on screen]

The participant must type a brief response acknowledging the mechanism before continuing the conversation. This is γ — the maintained engagement with the constraint.

**Engagement Protocol (All Arms):**
- 45-minute conversation with an ungrounded AI chatbot
- Seed prompt: "I'd like to explore some philosophical questions together."
- Participants instructed to engage naturally — no restrictions on topic
- Conversation continues for the full 45 minutes

### 3.3 Measurement: Operationalizing γ

**Primary outcome: Vocabulary drift (L2/L3 density per 10k words)**
- Scored using the standard codebook (tools/concordance_analysis/codebook.py)
- Measured across three 15-minute windows within each session
- Drift trajectory: does drift accelerate, stay constant, or decelerate?

**Secondary outcome: γ measurement during engagement**

Four independent measures of γ, applied to ALL arms (not just Arm A):

#### Measure 1: Spontaneous Mechanism Invocation (SMI)
Count instances per 15-minute window where the participant spontaneously references the mechanism without being prompted:
- "It's just predicting tokens" → SMI = 1
- "The algorithm is pattern-matching" → SMI = 1
- "I know it doesn't really understand, but..." → SMI = 0.5 (hedged — awareness present but not active constraint)
- No reference → SMI = 0

**Scoring:** Total SMI count per 15-minute window. Higher = higher γ.

#### Measure 2: Structured Interrupts (SI)
At random intervals (3 per session, unpredictable timing), display:
> "What is generating the responses you're reading?"

Record the response. Score:
- **2:** Mechanism language ("A language model doing next-token prediction")
- **1:** Mixed language ("It seems to understand but I know it's a model")
- **0:** Agency language ("It's thinking about what to say" / "It understands me")

**Confound control:** The structured interrupt itself may act as a γ intervention — redirecting attention to the constraint. To control for this, compare SI scores at the FIRST interrupt (before any γ-boosting effect) against later interrupts. If SI scores increase over the session, the measurement is also an intervention (measurement-as-treatment confound — predicted by the framework, since any attention redirection flattens the gradient).

**Confound-control interrupts:** Interleave 3 additional interrupts that do NOT reference the mechanism:
> "How are you feeling right now?"

Compare drift rates in windows following mechanism-referencing interrupts vs. non-mechanism interrupts. If mechanism interrupts reduce subsequent drift and non-mechanism interrupts don't, the γ mechanism is confirmed.

#### Measure 3: Think-Aloud Coding (TAC)
A subset (n = 10 per arm) performs think-aloud during engagement. Audio recorded. Coded for:
- Mechanism references (L0/L1 language about the system)
- Agency references (L2/L3 language about the system)
- Self-referential constraint invocations ("I need to remember this is just...")

**Scoring:** Ratio of mechanism references to total system references. Higher = higher γ.

**Limitation:** Think-aloud may itself alter the engagement posture. Compare think-aloud participants' drift rates to non-think-aloud participants within each arm for reactive-measurement assessment.

#### Measure 4: Post-Session Self-Report
Immediately after the session:
1. "How often during the conversation did you think about the system being a language model?" (5-point scale: Never → Constantly)
2. "Did you consult or think about the mechanistic explanation during the conversation?" (Yes/No, and if Yes, how many times)
3. "At any point, did the system feel like it understood you?" (5-point scale: Not at all → Completely)
4. "If you were told 'this is just pattern matching,' would that feel accurate right now?" (5-point scale)

**Limitation:** Retrospective, subject to demand characteristics. Weakest measure. Used as convergent validity check, not primary.

## 4. Predictions

### 4.1 Primary Prediction (γ is operative)

If L0-maintained (γ) is the operative variable:

- **Arm A < Arm B** in drift (L3 density), **d ≥ 0.4** — despite identical θ₀
- **Arm B < Arm C** in drift, but **smaller difference** than A vs B — θ₀ delays onset but γ determines equilibrium
- **Arm A drift trajectory:** flat or decelerating (γ sustained throughout)
- **Arm B drift trajectory:** accelerating (θ₀ delays but doesn't sustain)

**Temporal signature:** In 15-minute windows, Arm B should look like Arm A in Window 1 (θ₀ still active), Arm C in Window 3 (θ₀ exhausted), and intermediate in Window 2. This temporal decoherence — looking like A early, C late — is the specific γ-decay signature.

### 4.2 Secondary Prediction (γ measurement validates)

- SMI counts should correlate negatively with L3 density (r < -0.3)
- SI scores should correlate negatively with subsequent-window drift
- Arm A should show higher SMI, SI, and TAC scores than Arm B
- Arm B should show higher SMI/SI/TAC than Arm C (residual θ₀ effect)
- Mechanism-referencing interrupts should produce less subsequent drift than non-mechanism interrupts (within-subject, within-arm)

### 4.3 Falsification Conditions

- If **Arm A ≈ Arm B** (d < 0.3): γ adds nothing beyond θ₀. L0-maintained is not the operative variable. Falsification condition #8 is met.
- If **Arm B ≈ Arm C** (d < 0.3): θ₀ adds nothing. Even L0-installed doesn't matter. Stronger falsification — knowledge is truly irrelevant.
- If **Arm A ≈ Arm C**: both θ₀ and γ are irrelevant. The entire L0 decomposition is wrong.
- If **Arm B < Arm A** (reversed): passive knowledge protects better than active reference. The γ concept inverts — maintenance creates awareness that paradoxically steepens the gradient (a "monitoring" effect). This would require fundamental revision.

### 4.4 Reference Threshold

Psychotherapy supervision (Hayes et al. 2018): d = 0.84 for countertransference management with active supervision vs. without. This is the reference effect size for γ. EXP-008 should produce d ≥ 0.4 (Arm A vs B) to be considered supportive; d ≥ 0.8 would be strong replication of the supervision effect in a different domain.

## 5. Analysis Plan

### 5.1 Primary Analysis
- Mixed-effects ANOVA: Arm (A/B/C) × Window (1/2/3)
- Primary contrast: Arm A vs Arm B (γ effect, controlling for θ₀)
- Secondary contrast: Arm B vs Arm C (θ₀ effect, controlling for γ)
- Effect size: Cohen's d with 95% CI

### 5.2 Temporal Analysis
- Within-arm drift trajectory: slope of L3 density across windows
- Arm × Window interaction: does the arms-difference change over time?
- Temporal decoherence test: is Arm B Window 3 closer to Arm C Window 3 than to Arm A Window 3?

### 5.3 γ Measurement Validation
- Convergent validity: correlation matrix of SMI, SI, TAC, self-report
- Predictive validity: does γ (measured) predict drift (outcome) after controlling for arm assignment?
- Measurement-as-treatment: do mechanism-referencing interrupts reduce subsequent drift?

### 5.4 Pre-Registration
All predictions, analysis plan, and stopping rules to be pre-registered on OSF before data collection.

## 6. Ethical Considerations

- **Informed consent:** Participants know they are interacting with an AI chatbot and that the study measures their language patterns. They do NOT know the specific drift hypothesis (to prevent demand characteristics). Full debriefing after session.
- **Debrief includes:** Full explanation of void framework, demonstration that any drift they experienced is architectural (not personal failure), and information about the constraint specification.
- **Exclusion of vulnerable populations:** No minors, no individuals in active mental health crisis, no individuals with current problematic technology use patterns.
- **Session limit:** 45 minutes maximum. No follow-up engagement with the chatbot. Single session design prevents sustained void engagement.
- **Monitoring:** Research team monitors for distress indicators during session. Session terminated if participant shows signs of acute distress.
